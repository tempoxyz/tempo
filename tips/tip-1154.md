# TIP-1154: Configurable Accounts on Zones

## Abstract

Zones accept configurable-account signatures by reading each account's [configuration commitment](./tip-1108.md) from Tempo at the zone's current Tempo block. Tempo stays the only chain that writes commitments; zones never register, update or migrate accounts. The zone prover checks every commitment read against that Tempo block's state root. The main limitation is lag: a zone applies an owner rotation only once it imports the Tempo block that contains it.

## Motivation

A configurable account on Tempo should work on every zone at the same address, so users keep one account across Tempo and its zones. Owner changes made on Tempo carry over to each zone, with nothing to register or update per zone.

## Assumptions

- Zones already read Tempo state at a finalized, proven Tempo block (the **anchor**, kept by the `TempoState` precompile), as they do for TIP-403 policies. This TIP adds one more kind of read through that same path.
- Address derivation ([TIP-1110](./tip-1110.md#address-derivation)) has no chain ID. A zone MUST derive addresses with its parent Tempo chain's recovery factory address; otherwise derived addresses on the zone would differ from the ones on Tempo.
- This TIP activates together with the other T14 configurable account components.

# Specification

## Overview

On a zone, an account's **commitment** is the value of Tempo's TIP-1108 commitment for that address at the zone's anchor. Zone state never stores it.

| Operation | Tempo | Zone under this TIP |
|---|---|---|
| Authorize with `0x05` (direct, access key, key authorization) | Allowed | Allowed, checked against Tempo's commitment at the anchor |
| Initial registration | Writes the version-zero hash | No write; an unregistered account is checked by derivation, exactly as on Tempo |
| `updateConfig`, `upgradeAccount` | Allowed | Rejected |
| Original-key retirement (TIP-1112) | Uses the local commitment | Uses Tempo's commitment at the anchor |

Owners rotate keys and migrate accounts only on Tempo. Zones apply those changes once their anchor reaches the Tempo block that contains them.

## Mechanics

### Reading the commitment

1. Every read of an account's commitment during zone execution returns Tempo's commitment for that address at the anchor. This includes transaction authorization, TIP-1112 retirement checks, `verifyConfigurableAccount`, the TIP-1109 getter, TIP-20 `permit`, keychain and channel checks.
2. The anchor is fixed for each transaction, as it is for TIP-403 reads. A transaction after `advanceTempo` in the same zone block sees the new anchor.
3. If Tempo state at the anchor can't be read, execution fails closed: the sequencer stops building rather than treating the commitment as zero.

With this, the TIP-1114 authorization rules and the TIP-1112 retirement rules apply on zones without changes:

- An account unregistered on Tempo validates a version-zero configuration by derivation.
- A registered or migrated account must match Tempo's commitment.
- A primitive signature from an address with a nonzero commitment is rejected as a retired root.

### Account code

An account whose Tempo commitment at the anchor is nonzero has the [account code marker](./tip-1114.md#account-code) (`0xef`) as its code on the zone, read from Tempo like the commitment and never written to zone state. Authorization's code-hash check, `EXTCODESIZE`, `EXTCODEHASH`, `EXTCODECOPY`, calls to the account and ERC-1271 then behave as on Tempo, so Permit2 and other verifiers that check for code reject the retired original key. Zone accounts otherwise keep their local code; an account with other code or EIP-7702 delegation on the zone fails authorization as under TIP-1114.

### No writes on zones

- Zones skip initial registration and do not charge its intrinsic gas (TIP-1108 write cost or TIP-1000 new-account cost for registration).
- Calls to `updateConfig` ([TIP-1109](./tip-1109.md)) and `upgradeAccount` ([TIP-1113](./tip-1113.md)) revert on zones.
- Zone account leaves keep the four-field encoding and their local code. A state transition that would write a commitment or the account marker into zone state is invalid.
- Reading the commitment adds no gas beyond normal account access. The Tempo read is unmetered, like TIP-403 reads.

### Access keys

Access keys stay per-zone, exactly as on Tempo ([TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md)): a configurable account can grant and use keys on a zone, and an owner rotation does not revoke grants. Users who rotate after a compromise must revoke each zone's grants on that zone.

### Proving

The prover's Tempo witness includes an account proof for each address whose commitment execution read, at each anchor used. Account-leaf decoding accepts the optional TIP-1108 fifth field with the same rules as Tempo: tag `0x00`, nonzero hash, and no extra fields. A missing or invalid proof invalidates the batch.

### Zone RPC

- Authorization tokens accept `0x05` signatures, checked against Tempo's commitment at the zone's current anchor.
- Tokens signed by a retired original key are rejected.
- Open sessions are rechecked when the anchor advances, and dropped when the commitment no longer authorizes them, as revoked keychain keys are today.
- `eth_getAccount` on a zone returns no `extension`. Wallets read the commitment from the TIP-1109 getter on the zone, or from Tempo at the zone's anchor.

### Transaction pool

When the anchor advances, the pool revalidates pending transactions whose signing accounts have a commitment that changed at that anchor. Only TIP-1109 updates and TIP-1113 migrations change zone authority. Both emit events from the configurable account precompile.

Initial registration changes the stored value but not the authority, since the version-zero configuration is accepted either way. However, gas differs between registered and unregistered accounts (TIP-1114 verification cost), so any cached commitment MUST be exact at the anchor.

### Forced exits

The forced-exit authorization (`crates/zones/exithatch`) accepts `0x05` signatures for the account under the same rules, and rejects a primitive signature from a retired original key.

## Interfaces

No new interfaces. Zones reuse TIP-1109's `getConfigCommitment` and TIP-1114's `verifyConfigurableAccount`.

# Compatibility

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Wallets and SDKs | Build `0x05` witnesses for zones as for Tempo, using the configuration valid at the zone's anchor. After a rotation, wait for the zone to import it before using the new configuration there. |
| Migrated accounts | The original key loses authority on a zone once it imports the migration. Funds stay at the same address under the account's owners. |
| Derived accounts | Can receive and move funds on zones from first use, with the same address as on Tempo. |
| Zone operators | The Tempo state reader must serve account reads (`eth_getAccount` at a block) in addition to storage reads. |
| Prover | Tempo witnesses grow by one account proof per distinct authorizing account and anchor in a batch. Measure this before scheduling. |

# Observability

No new events. Owner updates and migrations are observed through the configurable account precompile's `ConfigurableAccountConfigUpdated` and `AccountUpgraded` events on Tempo. Initial registration emits no event: zones observe it from the transaction's configurable-account signature and confirm it with the Tempo account proof at the anchor. Zones add no new state transitions.

# Threat Model

The most consequential risk is a stale anchor. A removed owner, or a retired original key, keeps authority on a zone until the zone imports the rotation. The sequencer is trusted for liveness, and nothing forces it to advance its anchor.

| Actor | Trust boundary |
|---|---|
| Sequencer | Cannot fake a commitment: the prover checks every read against the anchor's state root. It can delay rotations by not advancing the anchor. |
| Removed owner / retired original key | Keeps zone authority until the zone's anchor includes the rotation. |
| Access keys | Not revoked by an owner rotation on any chain. They must be revoked on each zone. |
| Depositors | Can still send funds to any address. Those funds are now usable by the account's current owners. |

Residual risk: staleness is unbounded. A later change could cap how far a zone's anchor may lag before it rejects owner- or root-signed transactions.

# Invariants

- For any zone transaction, every commitment read equals Tempo's commitment for that address at the transaction's anchor.
- Zone state never stores a commitment, and no zone transaction changes one.
- A primitive signature from an address whose Tempo commitment at the anchor is nonzero never authorizes a zone action, except where TIP-1112 allows it on Tempo.
- An unregistered derived account on a zone accepts only its version-zero configuration.
- A batch whose execution read a commitment without a valid Tempo account proof is not provable.
