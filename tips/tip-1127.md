# TIP-1127: Native Zone Payment Lifecycle

## Abstract

This proposal makes existing ZonePortal accounts native payment endpoints while
retaining their addresses, storage, queue commitments, events, and ABI. Deposits,
withdrawals, refunds, and proof settlement execute under the bounded EVM2 call
rules in TIP-1125. The Zone chain applies the same fork schedule to private
transfers, inbox credits, outbox requests, and proof production. Activation has
no network date until historical replay, migration, genuine proof settlement,
and the combined payment-lane devnet gates pass.

## Motivation

TIP-1091 initializes Solidity-compatible portal accounts through a native
factory. Their ERC-1167 proxies still delegate to Solidity because the current
REVM integration cannot call dependent contracts from a precompile. That proxy
path consumes the general lane for required payment operations. EVM2 can run
TIP-20, the policy registry, verifier, and bounded callbacks in child frames,
allowing the complete Zone lifecycle to use payment capacity.

## Assumptions

- TIP-1125 supplies transaction-owned paid work reservations and atomic EVM2
  child frames. Neither a nested portal nor a Zone account-abstraction bundle
  can create a fresh reservation.
- The deployed T13 portal ABI and storage layout are authoritative for existing
  accounts. Its historical `submitBatch` overload remains decodable for replay.
- A configured cryptographic proof backend and its verifier have one specified
  version at production activation. NoProof development mode is permitted only
  with an explicit development verifier on a disposable chain.
- TIP-20 token and TIP-403 policy state are read at the block being executed.
  Bridge custody does not authorize a token issuer or waive a policy.

## Threat Model

Users may send malformed encryption points, stale keys, forged refunds,
noncanonical calldata, duplicate withdrawals, or hostile callback targets.
Sequencers may reorder or omit deposits, reuse a certificate, submit a false
height or queue cursor, or bind a proof to another Zone or Tempo fork. A token
administrator may pause transfers or change policy while a queue is pending.
All external code, including callbacks and verifier dependencies, executes
under the call and work budget. A factory address prefix alone gives no Zone
authority.

# Specification

## Fork and identity

The proposed native activation fork follows T14; its final identifier and
network schedule are assigned before scheduling this TIP. Before activation,
historical blocks execute the existing proxy and Zone rules. After activation,
the factory's next Zone ID, the portal's initialized flag, and the exact
canonical proxy runtime together identify a native portal. Recognition is
constant work for one requested address. A candidate with an unregistered ID,
missing initialization, or different bytecode fails before a native custody
operation. Dynamic, unbounded scans of all Zones are forbidden.

Native dispatch is selector aware. The payment selectors below enter native
execution; existing administration selectors continue through the proxy and
their historical implementation under the general lane. The implementation
may instead port an administration selector natively if it preserves its
existing ABI and charges its work. An arbitrary proxy, EIP-7702 delegate,
fallback selector, or forged native-looking address cannot gain payment
admission. Pool, builder, validator, execution, RPC estimation, and prover use
the same stateful identity and fork-aware classifier.

The ABI and error signatures in the deployed `ZonePortal.sol` and TIP-1091 are
the portal interface at activation. This TIP adds `PortalNotRegistered()` for
native payment dispatch. The payment functions are `deposit`,
`depositEncrypted`, `processWithdrawals`, `claimRefund`, and the T13
`submitBatch` overload. `deposit` and `depositEncrypted` have identical
parameter and return types. A caller cannot use the retired settlement overload
to bypass the active height or proof checks. Zone-side private TIP-20 transfer,
inbox advance, and outbox request selectors receive the corresponding Zone
payment classification.

## Deposit and private credit

Both deposit selectors accept one enabled TIP-20 token, a `uint128` gross
amount, a valid encryption key index, a 64-byte AES-GCM ciphertext with a
separate nonce and tag, and a nonzero Tempo refund recipient. The compressed
ephemeral secp256k1 public key must have SEC1 parity 2 or 3 and a valid X
coordinate. The latest sequencer key remains valid; an older key expires at
the next key's activation block plus 86,400 Tempo blocks. Access enforcement
allows an Account depositor, or a CallbackGateway depositor when gateway mode
is enforced. The refund recipient still needs Account permission. TIP-403 must
authorize that recipient under the token's active transfer policy.

The fee is `100000 * zoneGasRate`; the reserved bounce fee is
`ceil(bouncebackGas * block.basefee / 10^12)`. The gross amount must cover both.
The portal calls TIP-20 `transferFrom` to collect the gross amount, then pays
the fee to the existing administrator. The deposit queue records the net
amount. These calls are zero-value EVM2 child frames with the portal as caller.
Preparation, both token calls, queue hash update, count increment, and
`DepositMade` are one outer EVM journal operation. A failure at any stage
reverts token balances, allowances, queue state, and logs.

The queue hash is
`keccak256(abi.encode(DepositType.Deposit, Deposit, previousQueueHash))` using
the deployed type discriminator and field order. At most 210 public deposits
may remain unprocessed. A withdrawal callback in status 2 may consume one of
the 20 reserved places and advances to status 3; later callback deposits face
the ordinary public limit. The private sequencer processes entries in order,
verifies the committed encryption/decryption material, applies TIP-403 and
Zone access policy, credits the private balance once, and advances its
processed hash and number. Failed credits take the specified refund path and
cannot silently mint an unbacked private token.

## Private transfer and withdrawal

Zone TIP-20 transfer is admitted only when its sender and recipient privacy
authorization and token policy pass. A private transfer changes exactly two
private balances and emits no public recipient or sender tag. Outbox requests
commit their token, amount, sender tag, recipient, memo, callback gas limit,
fallback nonce, callback data, and encrypted sender in the existing FIFO hash
chain. The requested amount is locked or burned once under the existing Zone
custody model. Revert and retry cannot create another request or consume the
fallback nonce twice.

An accepted proof makes only its ordered withdrawal prefix spendable on Tempo.
`processWithdrawals` verifies the head and remaining queue hash before each
effect. A plain payout uses a paid TIP-20 child call. A callback is bounded by
the lesser of its requested gas and 10 million gas. A callback failure is
recoverable only after its child frame has rolled back. The portal then
records the fixed bounce-back credit to the authenticated refund path, charges
the failed work, and consumes the withdrawal once. A fatal database error,
profile limit, or native adapter error aborts the outer transaction rather
than becoming a successful bounce. The callback reentrancy state admits at
most one reserved deposit; it cannot permit a second payout or replay.

`claimRefund` debits a recorded token/account refund before its paid TIP-20
transfer and commits both effects together. Refunded amounts and unprocessed
deposits remain portal liabilities. Neither a callback nor Earn integration
may treat locked backing as free funds.

## Proof settlement

The T13 settlement arguments bind the Zone ID and chain, sequencer-set version,
threshold signatures, previous and next Zone heights, Tempo anchor and block
hash, processed deposit hash and cursor, token-enablement hash and cursor,
withdrawal queue root and batch index, and verifier configuration. The exact
T13 ABI field order, event fields, and signature domain remain authoritative.
At most eight distinct authorized signers satisfy the threshold; duplicate or
stale signatures do not count. The anchor must resolve through the active
historical block-hash rules. All cursors advance monotonically from the stored
values and cannot skip or apply a queue entry twice.

Verifier execution is a paid dependency call with bounded proof input and
return data. A false result, reverted verifier, stale or wrong-chain proof,
invalid signer set, or changed anchor reverts the entire settlement. The
portal submits the verifier configuration committed by the threshold-signed
attestation. Every quorum signer derives that commitment from one shared Zone
manifest proof policy; a node whose local prover setting conflicts with the
manifest MUST refuse to join the settlement quorum. A production portal's
proof-enforcing verifier MUST reject the NoProof payload `[2]` with empty
proof. A disposable development portal MAY accept it under an explicit
NoProof verifier, but such settlement does not satisfy the production
activation gate. Historical pre-fork NoProof blocks remain executable under
their historical rules.

## Resource profile

The proposed limits are consensus constants subject to measured refinement
before scheduling. A deposit's canonical ABI is at most 420 bytes, with one
64-byte ciphertext, one point validation, and at most two TIP-20 child calls.
Each such call may forward at most 2 million execution gas and expose at most
2 million state gas; the non-refundable deposit reservation is at most 8
million work units. Proof input is at most 65,536 bytes and settlement accepts
at most eight signatures. A withdrawal batch has at most 19 entries, at most
4,096 callback data bytes and 512 encrypted-sender bytes per entry, and at
most 64 native child calls across the transaction. Callback execution is
individually capped at 10 million gas. All native child reservations, including
nested callbacks and refund transfers, share a transaction work ceiling of
200 million units. Exact gas charges for point validation, ABI parsing,
hashing, log data, and proof verification must be benchmarked and fixed in the
hardfork schedule before activation. Unbounded decoder allocation is forbidden.

A user-provided lower callback gas limit lowers the reserved maximum for that
call; it cannot raise any protocol cap. A batch that exceeds its aggregate
reservation fails before another child executes and cannot run in the payment
lane by falling back to the general lane. The parent gas limit and block's
execution/state ceilings remain binding. State gas spilled into execution gas
is paid and reconciled under TIP-1125, including failed children.

## Migration and historical execution

Existing portal storage stays at the same address and slot numbers. Native
reads use the deployed Solidity packing for roles, keys, token configs,
refunds, queue heads/tails, hashes, sequencer state, leadership, and pause.
Activation performs no whole-chain scan or balance mint. The first native call
checks the factory registration and portal identity in bounded time and then
uses the existing state. In-flight deposits, withdrawals, refunds, and
enabled-token queues retain their commitments and can finish after activation.
If a pending queue item cannot be represented identically, scheduling is
blocked until its migration rule and proof fixture are specified.

Tempo and Zone replay from genesis must match historical state roots, receipts,
logs, fees, and system calls on both sides of the fork. A fresh peer must sync
across activation. Restarts, reorgs, retries, and simulations use the executed
block's fork, never the latest head's fork.

# Tooling

Tempo, Zones, SDKs, and provers generate the complete portal ABI from one
definition, including both historical settlement overloads. SDKs construct
canonical bounded deposit calldata, expose the active key and grace deadline,
split withdrawal batches to the resource profile, and expose refund and proof
status. Devnet tooling seeds a live Zone, deposit, and queued withdrawal before
the scheduled fork, activates native execution, and settles genuine proofs
after it. Test vectors must include an actual pre-fork Solidity deposit queue
hash/event and its post-fork native equivalent.

# Observability

`DepositMade`, `BatchSubmitted`, withdrawal, refund, and token-enable events
retain their deployed topics and fields. Tempo and Zones report payment and
general-lane gas per operation, native child work reservations and actual gas,
proof backend/version, queue cursor lag, bounce reasons, and pending refunds.
Public telemetry must not disclose private recipients or sender tags.

# Invariants

- Private token supply and balances reconcile to portal backing plus explicit
  in-flight and refundable liabilities, accounting for fee transfers once.
- Every deposit enters the queue once, every private credit consumes a committed
  entry once, and every withdrawal either pays out once or creates one bounce.
- No rejected proof advances Zone height, queue cursor, withdrawal root, or
  sequencer-set state.
- Native child failures cannot retain token moves, allowances, logs, or queue
  writes unless this TIP explicitly commits a bounded bounce.
- Every required Zone payment and its settlement uses zero general-lane gas,
  measured from validator resource accounting.

Activation requires a devnet run across the scheduled fork with real funded
pre-fork positions, one existing Zone, accepted genuine proofs, settlement
receipts, balance and backing reconciliation, a genesis-sync peer, failure
cases, and a 30-minute mixed Earn/Zone workload compared with the current
stack. The run must saturate the general lane separately and still show Zone
payment progress and zero general-lane attribution for these flows.
