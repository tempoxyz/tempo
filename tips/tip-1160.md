# TIP-1160: Zone TIP-20 Transfers and L1 Pause Mirroring

## Abstract

Before T15, Zone TIP-20 balances move only through the protocol paths: deposits credited by
`ZoneInbox` and withdrawals burned by `ZoneOutbox`. From T15, a Zone's portal admin can opt the
Zone into zone-internal `transfer` and `transferFrom` with a new `ZonePortal.transfersEnabled`
flag, which the Zone reads from finalized Tempo state. T15 also removes the Permit2 and Multicall3
predeploys from Zones, since transfers would make them reachable, and mirrors each TIP-20 token's
Tempo pause flag into Zone execution, so a token its issuer pauses on Tempo is paused on every
Zone.

## Motivation

Today every Zone TIP-20 transfer reverts with `Unauthorized`, so a payment needs a withdrawal to
Tempo and a fresh deposit. Enabling transfers changes what an existing Zone guarantees to its
operator and to issuers. Funds that could previously only enter and leave through the portal can
now change hands inside the Zone, where only the sequencer and prover see them. Transfers are
therefore opt-in per Zone, and the decision sits with the portal admin, who already controls the
Zone's access policy.

Transfers also expose a gap in how the Zone follows issuer controls. The Zone mirrors the TIP-403
registry from Tempo, so policy changes reach the Zone, but each token's `paused` slot is
Zone-local storage that nothing writes: `pause` and `unpause` are blocked on the Zone. A token
its issuer paused on Tempo stays live on every Zone. With transfers enabled, paused funds could
keep circulating. Mirroring the flag closes that gap with the mechanism the Zone already uses for
TIP-403 state.

Finally, two predeploys that were harmless while no balance could move become dangerous once
transfers are enabled. Permit2's allowance and nonce getters are public, which would expose who
approved whom, and anyone can make Multicall3 call a token as itself, so any balance or allowance
Multicall3 held would be anyone's. Removing both is simpler and safer than teaching every
fund-moving path to recognize them.

## Assumptions

- Zones read Tempo state at the finalized anchor recorded in `TempoState`. A
  `setTransfersEnabled` call or an issuer's pause takes effect on a Zone only once the Zone has
  imported the Tempo block that recorded it. Until then the Zone follows the previous value.
- The portal admin is trusted to decide whether its Zone allows transfers. Abdicating
  `Capability.AccessPolicy` (giving up the right to change the Zone's access policy, effective
  after a fixed delay) freezes the flag at its current value.
- Contract creation and authorization lists are disabled on Zones, so once T15 clears the code
  at the Permit2 and Multicall3 addresses, no code can return there, and no other contract can
  move funds on a user's behalf. Funds move only through TIP-20 calls, the outbox, and fees.
- Transfers need T15 active on both the Zone (`t15Time` in its genesis) and Tempo, where the
  flag lives. A Zone whose genesis does not schedule T15 rejects transfers even after its portal
  opts in. Operators schedule the Zone's T15 no earlier than Tempo's. Tempo verifies Zone proofs
  against the enclave measurements approved for the fork active on Tempo, so a prover running the
  T15 Zone rules needs its measurements approved for T15.

# Specification

## Overview

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zone token | A TIP-20 token enabled on a Zone, deployed at the same address as on Tempo. |
| Anchor | The finalized Tempo block number recorded in `TempoState.tempoBlockNumber`. |
| Portal admin | The `ZonePortal` admin of the Zone. |
| Removed predeploys | Permit2 (`PERMIT2_ADDRESS`) and Multicall3 (`MULTICALL3_ADDRESS`). |
| Transfer-family call | `transfer`, `transferWithMemo`, `transferFrom` and `transferFromWithMemo` on a Zone token. |

Only the portal admin authorizes a Zone's transfers, on Tempo. The Zone sequencer cannot enable
them, and block validation re-executes the admission check, so a sequencer cannot include a
transfer that the Zone's flag forbids.

## Mechanics

### 1. Transfer opt-in

`ZonePortal` gains `bool transfersEnabled`, packed into slot 28 at byte offset 9, after
`tokenEnablementCursorInitialized`. It defaults to `false`, so every existing portal starts
opted out.

`setTransfersEnabled(bool enabled)`:

1. Reverts with `NotAdmin` unless `msg.sender` is the portal admin.
2. Reverts with `CapabilityAbdicated(AccessPolicy)` once an `AccessPolicy` abdication is effective.
3. Writes `transfersEnabled = enabled` and emits `TransfersEnabledUpdated(enabled)`, even when the
   value is unchanged.

The call does not check the portal's pause state.

### 2. Transfer admission on the Zone

The Zone's TIP-20 rules run before the call reaches the upstream TIP-20 precompile. For a
transfer-family call, the checks run in this order:

1. Before T15: revert with `Unauthorized`.
2. If the Zone portal's `transfersEnabled` at the anchor is `false`: revert with `Unauthorized`.
3. Forward the call unchanged to the upstream TIP-20 precompile.

Upstream then applies its normal TIP-20 checks unchanged, including TIP-1022 virtual-address
resolution, the pause check (section 4), the TIP-403 policies (mirrored from Tempo), the
allowance, the TIP-1028 receive policy, and the balance check.

Upstream behaves differently on a Zone in one place. On Tempo, funds a receive policy blocks are
diverted to `ReceivePolicyGuard`, where the policy's recovery authority can claim them. On a Zone
the recovery authority may be an address that cannot act there, such as an L1 contract, and could
never claim them, so the precompile reverts with `PolicyForbids` instead. This already applies
on every Zone at every fork: before T15 no Zone transfer reached it, and `ZoneInbox` rejects
blocked deposits before the mint.

The fixed 100,000 gas charge for transfer-family calls, `approve` and `permit` is unchanged.

### 3. Predeploy removal

Zone genesis omits Permit2 and Multicall3, and before every T15 block the Zone executor clears
any code at `PERMIT2_ADDRESS` and `MULTICALL3_ADDRESS`, keeping each account's balance, nonce and
storage. The step changes nothing once the code is gone, since the code cannot return (see
Assumptions). Calls to either address from T15 behave as calls to an account without code.

### 4. L1 pause mirroring

From T15, Zone execution reads the `paused` slot (`tip20::slots::PAUSED`) of any TIP-20-prefixed
address from Tempo state at the anchor, not from Zone storage. Like a TIP-403 registry read, the
EVM's `SLOAD` charge covers it, with no extra host charge. The Zone never writes this slot:
`pause` and `unpause` stay blocked for every caller, and at any fork the Zone executor fails a
block in which a transaction would persist a change to a mirrored slot (any TIP-403 registry
slot, or a TIP-20 `paused` slot).

A token paused on Tempo therefore blocks on the Zone exactly what it blocks on Tempo:

| Zone operation | Effect while the token is paused on Tempo |
|---|---|
| Transfer-family calls | Revert with `ContractPaused` |
| Deposit (`ZoneInbox` mint) | Reverts, so the deposit bounces back to Tempo |
| Withdrawal bounce-back (`ZoneInbox` refund) | Held as a pending refund; `claimRefund` reverts with `ContractPaused` |
| `requestWithdrawal` (burn) | Reverts |
| Transactions paying fees in the token | Invalid |
| `approve`, `permit`, reads | Unaffected |

Before T15, the Zone reads the Zone-local slot, which is always unpaused.

## Interfaces

```solidity
interface IZonePortal {
    /// @notice Emitted when zone-internal TIP-20 transfers are enabled or disabled.
    event TransfersEnabledUpdated(bool enabled);

    /// @notice Whether zone-internal TIP-20 transfers are enabled. Defaults to false.
    function transfersEnabled() external view returns (bool);

    /// @notice Enable or disable zone-internal TIP-20 transfers.
    /// @dev Only callable by the admin. Reverts with NotAdmin for any other caller and with
    ///      CapabilityAbdicated(AccessPolicy) once an AccessPolicy abdication is effective.
    function setTransfersEnabled(bool enabled) external;
}
```

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| `transfersEnabled()` | `0xbef97c87` |
| `setTransfersEnabled(bool)` | `0x26b9ce13` |

No Zone TIP-20 interface changes. The errors already exist: `Unauthorized`, `PolicyForbids`,
`ContractPaused`.

# Compatibility

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Existing Zones | Nothing until the portal admin calls `setTransfersEnabled(true)`. Transfers keep reverting with `Unauthorized`. The `set-transfers-enabled` xtask sends the call or builds a Safe proposal; `just set-transfers-enabled` sends it. |
| Zone users and wallets | Can send TIP-20 within an opted-in Zone. A transfer may revert while the opt-in waits for the Zone to import the block that recorded it. Wallets should read `transfersEnabled()` on the portal before offering transfers. |
| Issuers | A Tempo pause now also pauses the token on every Zone, including deposits and withdrawals. |
| Permit2 and Multicall3 users on Zones | Both contracts have no code from T15. Existing Permit2 approvals stay in storage but can no longer be used, and batched reads through Multicall3 must become separate calls. |
| Zone node operators | Must run a T15-capable release before T15 activates. The L1 state cache invalidates a token's cached pause flag on its `PauseStateUpdate` event, as it already does for TIP-403 registry entries on `TransferPolicyUpdate`. |
| Zone checker | Ignores `TransfersEnabledUpdated`; it does not change L1 liabilities. |
| Sequencer allowlist | Optional and builder-local; see [Sequencer transfer allowlist](#sequencer-transfer-allowlist). |

## Sequencer transfer allowlist

Sequencers can restrict transfers further with the deposit allowlist
([tempoxyz/tempo#8634](https://github.com/tempoxyz/tempo/pull/8634)). This is block-builder
policy: Zone followers and the prover accept any block that passes the rules above, so it adds
no consensus rule and needs no hardfork. One flag, `--deposits.allowlist`, gates deposits and
transfers together on purpose: users keep the same address on Tempo and the Zone, and gating
deposits while leaving transfers open would let a listed account pay an unlisted one privately.
With the flag and a written list:

- While leading, the sequencer bounces a deposit whose L1 sender or decrypted Zone recipient is
  unlisted, so a listed depositor cannot fund an unlisted Zone account.
- Submission screens the calldata of each call, including each call of an AA batch. It rejects
  the transaction when a `transfer` or `transferWithMemo` names an unlisted sender or `to`, when a
  `transferFrom` or `transferFromWithMemo` has an unlisted caller, `from` or `to`, or when a
  `requestWithdrawal` names a `zoneFallbackRecipient` other than the withdrawer unless both are
  listed. Every party is checked against one version of the list. The error is
  `transfer sender or recipient is not allowlisted`. A screened call whose arguments do not decode
  is rejected with `malformed transfer calldata`, since the precompile would revert it.
- A transfer whose `from` or `to` is `ZoneOutbox` skips the `from` and `to` check (a
  `transferFrom` caller is still checked), and fees are not transfers, so an account removed from
  the list can still pay fees and withdraw.
- While leading, the sequencer runs the same calldata screen against its current list before
  executing each pool transaction. It leaves out, without executing, any the list now rejects and
  drops them from its pool, which catches list changes after admission.
- Every RPC follower mirrors the leader's list in memory, with no flag and no file: at startup,
  after each write it relays (when started with `--deposits.allowlist.admin`), and every second,
  or every 30 seconds while the leader reports no written list. It rejects the same transactions
  on submission and evicts pooled ones the new list rejects. While it holds no written list it
  admits every transfer whose calldata decodes, and the leader still enforces its own.
- Without a written list, the sequencer admits every transfer whose calldata decodes and credits
  every deposit. Simulations (`eth_call`, `eth_estimateGas`) do not check the list.

Screening calldata is enough because, from T15, no contract can move funds on a user's behalf
(see Assumptions). `ReceivePolicyGuard` claims need no screen: on a Zone a blocked inbound
reverts instead of moving funds into the guard, so it never holds funds there.

# Observability

- `TransfersEnabledUpdated(bool enabled)` on the portal: when a Zone opted in or out, and who
  changed it (the transaction sender).
- The existing `PauseStateUpdate` event on the Tempo token shows when a pause starts and ends.
  Whether it has reached a Zone follows from the Zone's anchor in `TempoState`.
- Rejected transfers revert with `Unauthorized` (pre-T15 or opted out), `PolicyForbids` (TIP-403
  or receive policy) or `ContractPaused`.
- The Zone executor fails a block with `L1Write`, naming the address (and the slot, for a
  storage write), if a transaction tried to persist a change to a mirrored slot.

# Threat Model

The most consequential risk is funds moving inside a Zone after an issuer or operator has tried
to stop them. The pause mirror and the mirrored TIP-403 registry hold Zone transfers to the
issuer's current Tempo controls, delayed only by the anchor's lag.

| Actor | Controls | Enforcing check | If compromised or wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portal admin | The opt-in flag | `onlyAdmin`; `AccessPolicy` abdication freezes it | Can enable or disable transfers on its own Zone, not on others. |
| Sequencer | Ordering and inclusion | Validation and the prover re-run the admission checks | Can censor transfers, cannot admit forbidden ones. |
| Issuer | Pause, TIP-403 policies on Tempo | Mirrored reads at the anchor | Controls take effect after the anchor lag, not instantly. |
| Approved spender | `transferFrom` simulations | None (accepted) | Learns `min(balance, allowance)` of the owner. The owner bounds this by approving exact amounts. |

Residual risks:

- Simulations of transfers to arbitrary recipients cause uncached Tempo storage reads. Outside
  canonical execution these are time- and count-bounded, and the builder prewarms them
  ([tempoxyz/tempo#8722](https://github.com/tempoxyz/tempo/pull/8722)).
- After a pause, pooled transactions that pay fees in the paused token stay in the pool until a
  block rejects them.
- The allowlist submission error tells a listed sender that a counterparty is not listed, so a
  listed account can probe addresses one at a time. An unlisted `transferFrom` caller is rejected
  whatever parties it names, so an unlisted account cannot.

# Invariants

- Before T15, every transfer-family call on a Zone token reverts with `Unauthorized`.
- From T15, no transfer-family call succeeds unless the portal's `transfersEnabled` at the anchor
  is `true`.
- From T15, no Zone block ends with code at `PERMIT2_ADDRESS` or `MULTICALL3_ADDRESS`.
- No Zone transfer or mint ever credits `ReceivePolicyGuard`.
- From T15, the Zone's view of a token's `paused` flag equals Tempo's at the anchor. At every
  fork, Zone execution never persists a write to a TIP-20 `paused` slot or to the TIP-403
  registry.

Tests must cover: opted-out and opted-in transfers on both sides of T15, `setTransfersEnabled` by
a non-admin and after an `AccessPolicy` abdication, Permit2 and Multicall3 code cleared at the
first T15 block and absent from Zone genesis, receive-policy and TIP-403 rejections, deposits,
withdrawals and transfers while the token is paused on Tempo, and the rejection of a block that
writes a mirrored slot.

# Alternatives Considered

- **Keep Permit2 and Multicall3 and reject them as transfer parties.** Rejected: every path that
  moves funds (transfers, `approve`, the outbox's internal `transferFrom`, and any future one)
  would need to recognize both addresses, and a sequencer allowlist would still have to screen
  calls through Multicall3. Clearing their code removes them for good (see Assumptions).
- **Copy the pause flag into Zone storage during `advanceTempo`**, the system call that opens
  each Zone block by importing finalized Tempo headers. Rejected: it needs a system write for
  every token on every import. The Zone's database adapter already answers TIP-403 registry reads
  from Tempo state at the anchor; routing the pause slot through it costs nothing until a call
  reads the slot.
