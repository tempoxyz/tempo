# TIP-1179: Zone Virtual Address Resolution

## Abstract

TIP-1022 virtual addresses forward TIP-20 payments to a master registered on Tempo, but Zones do
not run the registry, so Zone payments to virtual addresses fail. From T15, a Zone reads Tempo's
address registry at its anchor, so Zone transfers and deposits to a registered virtual address
credit its master under the master's receive policy. The Zone cannot register masters of its own,
and a master that is a contract on Tempo cannot move funds credited to it on a Zone.

## Motivation

A business that takes payments from many users inside a Zone, such as an exchange or a fintech
offramp, needs to tell which user each payment came from. On Tempo, virtual addresses solve this:
the business gives each user a distinct address, all of them forward to one treasury wallet, and
the receiving address identifies the user. There is no wallet per user to secure and no sweeping
into the treasury. Custody platforms already credit deposits by destination address, so this
matches how they reconcile.

Inside a Zone the same payments fail today. Matching each payment by sender works when users pay
from wallets the business provisioned, but breaks when the sender is a router, a solver, a
relayer, or a wallet the business does not know. Resolving virtual addresses on the Zone gives
Zone users the same per-user addresses they have on Tempo.

The Zone reuses Tempo's registry rather than keeping its own, so a virtual address names the same
master everywhere. [Alternatives Considered](#alternatives-considered) covers the other options.

## Assumptions

| Assumption | If it does not hold |
|---|---|
| Zones read Tempo state at the *anchor*: the finalized Tempo block number recorded in `TempoState.tempoBlockNumber`. A registration on Tempo takes effect on a Zone only once the Zone imports the Tempo block that recorded it. | Until then the Zone treats the master ID as unregistered: transfers to its virtual addresses revert and deposits to them bounce back. Nothing is credited to the wrong account. |
| TIP-1022 registrations are permanent: a master ID, once registered, always resolves to the same master. | A Zone could credit a master that no longer owns the ID. |
| Contract creation is disabled on Zones, and a Zone has no precompile at the registry address. | A Zone could register masters of its own, and one virtual address could name different masters on Tempo and on the Zone. |
| T15 is active on the Zone (`t15Time` in its genesis), and operators schedule it no earlier than Tempo's. | A Zone before T15 keeps treating every virtual address as unregistered. |

# Specification

## Overview

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Virtual address | A TIP-1022 address whose bytes `[4:14]` are the magic `0xFD` repeated ten times. Bytes `[0:4]` are the master ID and bytes `[14:20]` are the user tag. |
| Master | The account that registered a master ID with `registerVirtualMaster` on Tempo. |
| Address registry | The TIP-1022 `AddressRegistry` precompile at `0xFDC0000000000000000000000000000000000000`. |
| Anchor | The finalized Tempo block number recorded in `TempoState.tempoBlockNumber`. |
| Zone fallback recipient | The `zoneFallbackRecipient` of a withdrawal: the Zone account that receives the funds if the withdrawal bounces back. |

From T15:

| Path | Before T15 | From T15 |
|---|---|---|
| Zone transfer to a registered virtual address | Reverts with `Unauthorized` | Credits the master, subject to its receive policy |
| Zone transfer to an unregistered virtual address | Reverts with `Unauthorized` | Reverts with `VirtualAddressUnregistered` |
| Deposit to a registered virtual address | Fails to mint and bounces back | Credits the master, subject to its receive policy |
| Deposit to an unregistered virtual address | Fails to mint and bounces back | Unchanged |
| Withdrawal with a virtual Zone fallback recipient | Accepted | Reverts with `InvalidFallbackRecipient` |
| Registering a master on the Zone | Not possible | Not possible |

Zone transfers reach resolution only once TIP-1160's admission check passes: T15 is active and the
portal's `transfersEnabled` is true at the anchor. Before that, every Zone transfer reverts with
`Unauthorized`.

Only Tempo authorizes masters. A Zone sequencer cannot register, change or remove one, and block
validation re-executes every resolution against the registry at the anchor.

Out of scope:

- TIP-1094 Zone deposit addresses. They use different magic bytes and a separate resolver, and
  the two never chain.
- Masters that are contracts on Tempo ([Threat Model](#threat-model)).

### Example

A business registers master ID `0xA1B2C3D4` on Tempo from its treasury address `T`, in Tempo block
2,000. It gives a user the virtual address `0xA1B2C3D4FDFDFDFDFDFDFDFDFDFD000000000007`. The Zone's
anchor trails Tempo by 5 blocks.

1. At anchor 1,998, the user sends 50 USDX inside the Zone to their virtual address. The Zone
   reads the master ID as unregistered, and the transfer reverts with `VirtualAddressUnregistered`.
2. The Zone imports Tempo block 2,000. The user sends the same transfer, and the Zone credits
   `T` with 50 USDX. The receipt shows two `Transfer` events: from the user to the virtual
   address, and from the virtual address to `T`.
3. Another user deposits 20 USDX from Tempo to the same virtual address with an encrypted
   deposit. The Zone credits `T`.
4. A deposit to a virtual address whose master ID nobody registered fails its mint on the Zone
   and bounces back to the depositor on Tempo.

## Mechanics

### 1. Mirroring the registry

From T15, the Zone's database answers every storage read of the address registry from Tempo's
state at the anchor, through the same overlay that serves the TIP-403 registry and, from T15,
TIP-20 pause flags (TIP-1160). The upstream TIP-20 precompile resolves virtual recipients by
reading the registry, so Zone transfers resolve without other changes.

Mirrored storage is read-only on the Zone. The block executor rejects any transaction or block
that would persist a change to a mirrored slot, with `L1Write`. The set of mirrored slots depends
on the fork: the TIP-403 registry at every fork, and from T15 the address registry and each
TIP-20 pause flag.

When Tempo records a `MasterRegistered` event, Zone nodes invalidate their cached registry reads,
because the cache may hold the new master ID as unregistered.

### 2. The registry account

Zone genesis has no account at the registry address. The EVM's block cache treats the storage of
an account that does not exist as zero and does not read it through the overlay, so without an
account every master ID would read as unregistered.

When T15 activates, the Zone block executor deploys the `0xEF` marker code at the registry
address in pre-execution, unless the account already has code. This is the same deployment Tempo
performs for precompiles at their activation forks. Only the code is written; the registry's
storage stays mirrored. The prover replays the same executor, so the account exists in both
block production and proofs.

### 3. Deposits

`ZoneInbox` mints each deposit's amount to its recipient. From T15, a virtual recipient is
resolved before the receive policy check, so the master's TIP-1028 receive policy governs the
credit. A deposit whose recipient is an unregistered virtual address fails its mint, like any
other failed mint, and bounces back to Tempo.

### 4. Withdrawal fallback recipients

When a withdrawal bounces back from Tempo and the Zone cannot credit it, the funds are held in the
refund registry for the withdrawal's Zone fallback recipient, and only that address can claim
them. A virtual address cannot sign, so funds held for one could never be claimed. From T15,
`requestWithdrawal` reverts with `InvalidFallbackRecipient` when `zoneFallbackRecipient` is a
virtual address. This matches the portal's existing rule for a deposit's Tempo refund recipient.

Before T15 a virtual fallback recipient is accepted, and funds held for it can never be claimed.
Changing that would alter T14, which is already scheduled, so the rule starts at T15.

### 5. Sequencer allowlists

A Zone may run a sequencer allowlist that screens deposits and transfers. Allowlists check the
literal address, while the Zone credits a virtual address's master, so listing a virtual address
could pass funds to an unlisted master. Sequencers therefore reject an allowlist write that adds
a virtual address, and drop any virtual address found in a saved list when it loads. On a Zone
with an allowlist, virtual addresses cannot receive deposits or transfers, even when the master
is listed.

Allowlists are sequencer policy, not consensus: block validation does not check them.

## Interfaces

No new interfaces. T15 uses:

- `IAddressRegistry` (TIP-1022): `registerVirtualMaster(bytes32 salt)`, `getMaster(bytes4 masterId)`,
  the `MasterRegistered(bytes4 masterId, address masterAddress)` event and the
  `VirtualAddressUnregistered()` error.
- `IZoneOutbox.InvalidFallbackRecipient()`, which `requestWithdrawal` already uses for a zero
  fallback recipient.

# Compatibility

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Masters on Tempo | Their virtual addresses now also receive on every Zone at T15. A master registers once on Tempo; there is no Zone registration. |
| Zone users and wallets | Can pay virtual addresses inside a Zone. A payment may still revert while the Zone waits to import the registration. |
| Withdrawal clients | Must not pass a virtual address as `zoneFallbackRecipient`. |
| Zone operators with an allowlist | An allowlist write that adds a virtual address fails. Virtual addresses already in a saved list are dropped, with a warning, when the sequencer restarts. |
| Zone node operators | Must run a T15-capable release before T15 activates. |
| TIP-1160 | Its statements that virtual addresses never resolve on a Zone no longer hold from T15. |

# Observability

- `MasterRegistered` on Tempo shows when a master registered. Whether it has reached a Zone
  follows from the Zone's anchor in `TempoState`.
- A Zone transfer to a registered virtual address emits two `Transfer` events: from the sender to
  the virtual address, and from the virtual address to the master. The Zone's private RPC shows
  each event only to the parties named in it.
- Rejections: `VirtualAddressUnregistered` (unregistered master ID), `PolicyForbids` (the master's
  receive policy), and `InvalidFallbackRecipient` (virtual fallback recipient).

# Threat Model

The most consequential risk is a Zone crediting a different account than Tempo would for the same
virtual address. Reading Tempo's registry, and giving the Zone no way to write it, keeps both
answers the same.

| Actor | Controls | Enforcing check | If compromised or wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Master | Its registration on Tempo | TIP-1022 registration rules on Tempo | Receives payments to its virtual addresses on every Zone. |
| Sequencer | Ordering and inclusion | Validation and the prover re-run resolution at the anchor | Can censor payments, cannot redirect them. |
| Zone transaction sender | Calls on the Zone | No registry precompile on the Zone; mirrored slots are read-only | Cannot register or change a master. |

Residual risks:

- **Masters that are contracts.** If a master on Tempo is a contract, such as a multisig, its Zone
  account has no code and no key, so funds credited to it on the Zone cannot move. Before T15,
  such payments reverted or bounced. The Zone cannot read an account's code on Tempo today, so it
  cannot refuse these masters. Masters that receive on Zones should be externally owned accounts.
- **Public registration.** Registration emits `MasterRegistered(masterId, masterAddress)` on Tempo.
  It reveals which address owns a master ID, but not users, balances or Zone activity. User tags
  are derived offchain and never registered. A master that wants no link between that address and
  its other activity should register from a dedicated address that does nothing else on Tempo,
  and withdraw to a different address.

# Invariants

- From T15, a Zone resolves a virtual address to the same master that Tempo's registry records at
  the anchor, or treats it as unregistered if Tempo's registry has no master for its ID.
- No Zone transaction or block persists a change to the address registry's storage.
- Before T15, every virtual address is unregistered on a Zone.
- From T15, no withdrawal is recorded with a virtual `zoneFallbackRecipient`.
- No sequencer allowlist contains a virtual address.

Tests must cover, against a Tempo node: a Zone transfer to a virtual address before and after its
master registers on Tempo, a deposit to a registered and to an unregistered virtual address, a
withdrawal with a virtual fallback recipient, and a sequencer allowlist that refuses a virtual
address.

# Alternatives Considered

- **A registry on the Zone in the same address space.** Rejected: one virtual address could then
  name different masters on Tempo and on the Zone, and whichever registry takes precedence, the
  other one's master can lose its ID.
- **Zone-only virtual addresses with their own magic bytes and registry.** This would hide which
  address owns a master, but it needs a new address format, a second resolver in the Zone TIP-20,
  and custody integrations that recognize it. The dedicated-master practice above gives most of
  the privacy without them. A future TIP can add it if needed.
- **Match payments by sender.** Works when users pay from wallets the recipient provisioned. It
  fails when the sender is a contract or an unknown wallet, and needs custom reconciliation
  instead of the destination-address model custody platforms use.
- **Resolve masters in sequencer allowlists.** Would let allowlisted Zones receive at virtual
  addresses. It needs registry reads during transaction admission, so it is left for a later
  change.
