# TIP-1153: Account Reservations

## Abstract

A new precompile lets an account reserve part of its own TIP-20 balance for a payee under an id it chooses, capture that hold later in one or several parts or release it, pay without a hold, and owe a capped debt when a payment exceeds what it has available. Reserved funds never leave the account: a hold moves them from the account's spendable TIP-20 balance into its reserved amount, which `balanceOf` still counts, so debits cannot spend them and need no reservation check, and from T15 every TIP-20 credit repays outstanding debt first. Payees can refund against the captured total of an id. A lender can open a credit line that pays for part of an owner's holds out of the lender's own reserved balance. The precompile holds no funds and does not interpret card network messages.

## Motivation

Card authorization needs three things a plain transfer cannot give: an amount set aside at authorization time without moving it, a final amount that may differ from the authorization (tips, partial shipments, offline and late presentments), and refunds tied to the original payment. Today an issuer either prefunds settlement or pulls funds into an escrow contract at authorization, which takes custody of user balances and costs a transfer per authorization.

Holding funds in place keeps custody with the cardholder, lets the cardholder keep earning on held balances, and needs no issuer float. Capped debt lets an issuer approve a capture slightly above the hold (tips, currency swings) without prefunding: the shortfall is recovered from the next funds the cardholder receives.

Alternatives considered: an escrow contract (takes custody, and every hold is a transfer), TIP-20 allowances (do not lock funds, so a later transfer can spend them), and TIP-1034 channels (lock funds in the reserve's custody and settle by vouchers, which does not fit network-driven captures or refunds).

## Assumptions

- The cardholder's account is driven for card flows by an issuer-held access key (TIP-1011) whose call scope is limited to this precompile's `hold` (or `holdCard`, see TIP-1169), `holdStandIn`, `holdWithLine`, `adjust`, `capture`, `capturePart`, `charge`, `chargeStandIn` and `release` selectors, with periodic spending limits per token. That key is the mandate; this TIP adds no separate mandate object.
- The debt cap, the offline cap and the capture tolerance are set by the account's root key. If a root key is compromised, the attacker can raise caps or lift tolerances; this is no worse than the root key's existing ability to transfer the whole balance.
- Payees (issuers or their settlement accounts) are trusted by the owner to the extent of the holds and caps the owner grants them.
- Hold ids are unique per owner per authorization. Card programs use `keccak256(network authorization id)`.

# Specification

## Overview

Per (owner, token) the precompile tracks `reserved` (the sum of the owner's live holds) and `debt` (owed to a single creditor). From T15 the TIP-20 balance slot holds only the owner's available (spendable) balance: placing or raising a hold moves the amount from it into `reserved`, and lowering, releasing or capturing a hold moves the amount back. TIP-20 `balanceOf` returns the available balance plus `reserved`, as before.

An authorization record per (owner, id) moves through: **held** (after `hold`; may be `adjust`ed and captured in non-final parts, which keep it held and add to its captured total), then either **released** (by `release` or `releaseExpired`: the record is deleted when nothing was captured, and otherwise kept as settled with the parts' captured total) or **settled** (after a final capture, `charge` or a release after non-final captures; keeps token, payee and captured/refunded totals). A held or settled id takes `refund`s up to its captured total, and a settled id can take further `charge`s for the same token and payee.

| Operation | Caller | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hold`, `holdStandIn`, `adjust`, `release` | owner | Change `reserved` |
| `capture`, `capturePart`, `charge`, `chargeStandIn` | owner | Pay the payee, possibly creating debt |
| `captureFor`, `captureForPart` | the hold's payee | Pay itself up to the held amount |
| `setDebtCap` | owner, root key only | Bound debt towards a creditor |
| `setCaptureTolerance` | owner, root key only | Bound how far final captures for a payee exceed the hold |
| `setOfflineCap` | owner, root key only | Bound debt towards a payee from stand-in approvals |
| `releaseExpired` | anyone | Release a hold once expired |
| `refund` | the record's payee | Return funds to the owner, repaying a credit line first |
| `openLine` | lender | Open a credit line in one token |
| `offerCredit` | lender, root key only | Offer an owner credit for one payee, or lower or withdraw it |
| `grantCredit` | owner, root key only | Accept an offer; reserve the lender's balance for the undrawn limit |
| `holdWithLine` | owner | Place a hold with part of it on a credit line |
| `repayCredit` | owner | Repay a credit line from the available balance |

## Mechanics

### Amounts and time

All amounts are TIP-20 base units. ABI amounts are `uint128` but are stored as 96-bit values (enough for 7.9e28 base units, far above any TIP-20 supply cap in practice); a zero amount or one above `2^96 - 1` reverts `InvalidAmount`. `expiresAt` is a unix timestamp in seconds; a hold is expired once `block.timestamp >= expiresAt`. `hold` with `expiresAt <= block.timestamp` or `expiresAt > block.timestamp + MAX_HOLD_DURATION` reverts `InvalidAmount`.

`MAX_HOLD_DURATION` is 31 days (2,678,400 seconds). Card network rules expire authorizations within 30 days, so a longer hold serves no card flow; the bound limits how long funds can stay locked and how long a hold outlives a change of the token's policy. A hold cannot be extended: `adjust` changes only its amount.

### hold(token, id, amount, expiresAt, payee)

1. Validate `amount` and `expiresAt` as above, including the `MAX_HOLD_DURATION` bound.
2. `token` must have the TIP-20 prefix (`InvalidToken`). `payee` is resolved through the address registry (TIP-1022 virtual addresses resolve to their master) and must not be zero or a TIP-20 address (`InvalidRecipient`).
3. The token's TIP-403 transfer policy must authorize the owner as sender and the payee as recipient (`PolicyForbids`).
4. No record may exist for (owner, id), live or settled (`HoldExists`).
5. `amount` must not exceed the owner's available balance (`InsufficientAvailable`).
6. Move `amount` from the available balance to `reserved`, store the record, emit `HoldPlaced`.

### holdStandIn(token, id, amount, expiresAt, payee)

Records an approval the payee made in stand-in, while it could not reach the chain: card networks let an issuer approve offline within a limit and present the approval later, and by then the owner's available balance may have fallen below it. Checks as `hold` steps 1 to 4, then reserves `min(amount, available)` (possibly zero) instead of reverting `InsufficientAvailable`, stores the record as a stand-in hold and emits `HoldPlaced` with the reserved amount followed by `StandInHoldPlaced(owner, id, token, approved)` with `amount`. The hold then behaves as any hold, with the reserved amount as its amount, except that its captures pay as a stand-in payment in [Paying](#paying): a final capture of the approved amount may leave the part not reserved as debt up to the larger of the debt cap and the [offline cap](#setofflinecappayee-token-cap). Ordinary holds keep the debt cap alone, so an issuer key cannot use the offline cap for approvals it made online.

### adjust(id, amount)

Sets the live hold's amount, what it still reserves after any non-final captures, to `amount` (`HoldNotFound` without a live hold, `InvalidAmount` for zero). Captured parts are unaffected: an adjust after non-final captures sets only the remainder, so an issuer turning a new total authorization amount into a hold amount subtracts what was already captured. An increase moves the increase from the available balance to `reserved` and needs it to fit (`InsufficientAvailable`). A decrease moves the decrease back, but never more than `reserved` (see [Compliance burns](#compliance-burns)). Works on expired but unreleased holds. Emits `HoldAdjusted` with the hold's token and new total.

### capture(id, amount)

1. Requires a live hold (`HoldNotFound`) and a valid `amount`. Works on expired but unreleased holds. When `amount` exceeds what the hold still reserves and the owner bounded captures for the hold's payee and token, the captured total after this capture may exceed the hold's authorized total (its captured total plus what it still reserves) by at most the tolerance (`ToleranceExceeded`); see [setCaptureTolerance](#setcapturetolerancepayee-token-bounded-tolerancebps).
2. The token's TIP-403 transfer policy must still authorize the owner as sender and the hold's payee as recipient (`PolicyForbids`). The policy may have changed since the hold was placed, and settlement moves funds; a hold whose parties became forbidden can only be released or expire.
3. The hold's amount (what remains after any non-final captures) moves from `reserved` back to the available balance first (never more than `reserved`), so the payment spends the reservation it consumes.
4. The payment is made as in [Paying](#paying) to the hold's payee.
5. The hold ends: its reserved amount is zero, it is no longer live, and its captured total becomes the sum of its non-final captures plus `amount` (debt included). Emits `HoldCaptured(owner, id, token, amount, debt)` with this capture's `amount`.

### capturePart(id, amount, isFinal)

Captures a live hold in several parts, for split shipments. With `isFinal` set it is `capture`: any amount, including above what remains held, ends the hold. Otherwise it is a non-final capture:

1. Requires a live hold (`HoldNotFound`), the same signing-key check as `capture` (`Unauthorized`) and a valid `amount` at most what the hold still reserves (`InvalidAmount`), whatever the owner's debt cap; only the final capture may take more than was held. Works on expired but unreleased holds.
2. The token's TIP-403 transfer policy must authorize the owner as sender and the hold's payee as recipient (`PolicyForbids`), as for `capture`.
3. `amount` of the hold moves from `reserved` back to the available balance (as [Compliance burns](#compliance-burns) describe, a hold placed before the latest burn takes it from `unbacked` first) and is paid as in [Paying](#paying).
4. The hold's amount drops by `amount` and its captured total rises by `amount`; the hold stays live with its expiry unchanged and may take more parts, be adjusted, finally captured or released. A part equal to what remains leaves a live hold reserving nothing. Emits `HoldPartCaptured(owner, id, token, amount, debt)`.

The final capture records the settled total, and refunds are capped by the captured total across all parts. Releasing a hold after non-final captures releases only what it still reserves and keeps the record, settled with the parts' captured total. Only the final capture may exceed the hold, so the excess, which is new key spend and possibly debt, is decided once per authorization. Non-final parts emit their own event so that `HoldCaptured` keeps meaning that the hold ended; the parts' amounts and the final capture's sum to the captured total. Finality is a call argument rather than a separate selector so a card program can pass the network's final-clearing indicator through; `capture` and `captureFor` are the final forms and keep their selectors.

### captureFor(owner, id, amount)

Called by the hold's payee to settle `owner`'s live hold `id`, for example after the owner revoked or rotated the access key that placed it.

1. Requires a live hold of `owner` (`HoldNotFound`), and the caller must be its resolved payee (`Unauthorized`). Works on expired but unreleased holds. When `owner` is the transaction origin and an access key signed the transaction, it must be the key that placed the hold (`Unauthorized`), as for `capture`; this applies only to an owner that is its own payee.
2. `amount` must be valid and at most the hold's amount (`InvalidAmount`), whatever the owner's debt cap.
3. The hold then settles as in `capture` steps 2 to 5: TIP-403 re-check, release, payment as in [Paying](#paying) and `HoldCaptured`. Refunds work as after `capture`. A compliance burn can leave less than the hold to pay; the rest becomes debt within the cap, as for `capture`.

The hold is the owner's prior consent to pay up to its amount to this payee before it is released, and an access key that placed it already counted it against its limit. Letting the payee settle within it keeps an approved purchase payable after the owner revokes or rotates that key, while the payee can never take more than was held, change the hold, or create new key spend. The owner withdraws consent with `release` before a capture, and anyone can release the hold with `releaseExpired` once it expires.

### captureForPart(owner, id, amount, isFinal)

The payee's `capturePart`: the checks of `captureFor` steps 1 and 2 apply, so `amount` never exceeds what the hold still reserves, final or not. With `isFinal` set it is `captureFor`; otherwise it is a non-final capture as in `capturePart` steps 2 to 4.

### charge(token, id, amount, payee)

A payment without a hold, for offline and late presentments. Validates token, payee and TIP-403 (owner as sender, payee as recipient) as `hold` does, at the time of the charge. Reverts `HoldExists` if the id has a live hold, or a settled record with a different token or payee. Pays as in [Paying](#paying), adds `amount` to the id's captured total and emits `Charged`.

### chargeStandIn(token, id, amount, payee)

`charge` for a stand-in approval presented without a hold: the same checks and `Charged` event, paid as a stand-in payment, so the shortfall may become debt up to the larger of the debt cap and the offline cap.

### Paying

For an owner, token, payee and amount:

1. `paid = min(amount, available)` and `shortfall = amount - paid`. For a capture of a hold with a line part, the line then pays `drawn = min(shortfall, part)` of it as in [Line draws](#line-draws), where `part` is the line part the capture ends, and `shortfall` drops by `drawn`.
2. If `shortfall > 0`: if the owner has debt in the token to a creditor other than the payee, revert `DebtCapExceeded` (one creditor per owner and token). If `debt + shortfall` exceeds the cap, revert `DebtCapExceeded`. The cap is `debtCap(owner, payee, token)`, or for a stand-in payment (a capture of a `holdStandIn` hold, or `chargeStandIn`) the larger of it and `offlineCap(owner, payee, token)`. Otherwise the payee becomes (or stays) the creditor and `debt += shortfall`. The payee receives no tokens for the shortfall: it holds a claim that later credits repay.
3. The amount not already counted by the hold (for a final `capture` or `capturePart`, the excess over what remains held; for `charge`, the full `amount`; for `captureFor`, `captureForPart` and non-final captures, nothing, since they never exceed the hold) counts against the signing access key's limit as described in [Mandate limits](#mandate-limits), debt included.
4. `paid` moves from owner to payee as a TIP-20 transfer: it reverts if the token is paused, and if the payee's TIP-1028 receive policy rejects the owner (`PolicyForbids`), because the record cannot represent a guarded payment.

### Mandate limits

An open hold is committed spend of the card mandate. Whenever the owner is the transaction origin and an access key signed the transaction (TIP-1011), the amounts below count against that key's spending limit for the hold's token, reverting with the keychain's `SpendingLimitExceeded()` when over. Root-key transactions and calls from other accounts count nothing.

| Operation | Limit effect |
| --- | --- |
| `hold` | Spends `amount` |
| `holdStandIn` | Spends the amount it reserves |
| `holdWithLine` | Spends `amount`, its line part included |
| `adjust` up | Spends the increase |
| `capture`, final `capturePart` | Spends `amount - held` when positive; gives back `held - amount` when positive, `held` being what remains after non-final captures |
| `captureFor`, final `captureForPart` | Spends nothing; gives back `held - amount` when positive |
| Non-final `capturePart`, `captureForPart` | Spends nothing, gives back nothing: the paid part stays committed |
| `charge`, `chargeStandIn` | Spends `amount` |
| `adjust` down | Gives back the decrease |
| `release`, `releaseExpired` | Give back the held amount (what remains after non-final captures) |
| `refund` | Gives back the refunded amount |
| `repayCredit` | Spends the repaid amount, as a TIP-20 transfer by the owner would |

The authorization records the access key that placed the hold or first charge (zero for the root key) and the end of that key's limit period after the charge (zero for one-time limits). Another access key of the same owner cannot `adjust`, `capture`, `capturePart`, `charge` or `release` that id (`Unauthorized`); the root key can, and its own changes count nothing. Spend is always charged to the signing key, while amounts are given back only to the recorded key, and only while its limit row is still in the recorded period: once a spend has rolled the key's period, nothing is given back, because the new period already started at the full limit and returning older spend would let the key exceed its limit within one period. Gives never raise the remaining limit above its configured maximum, and do nothing for revoked or expired keys, keys without enforced limits, or limit rows whose maximum is zero: an owner that freezes a key with `updateSpendingLimit(key, token, 0)` keeps it frozen through later releases and refunds, and keys created before T3 (whose rows store no maximum) get nothing back. TIP-20 fee refunds keep their existing limit behavior. The authorization also records how much the key's limit counted in that period (`counted`); after every change it is lowered to at most what the authorization still commits (the live hold plus captured less refunded; on release, captured less refunded) and only that drop is given back, so amounts the root key added are released first, and a spend by the recorded key in a new period moves the recorded period forward and restarts `counted` at that spend. Consequences: spend counted in an earlier period than the latest is not returned (the safe direction), and amounts the root key adds to an access key's authorization (hold increases, captures above the hold, further charges) are never returned to that key.

### release(id) and releaseExpired(owner, id)

Both require a live hold (`HoldNotFound`). `releaseExpired` is callable by anyone and reverts `HoldNotExpired` before expiry. The hold's amount (what remains after non-final captures) moves from `reserved` back to the available balance and `HoldReleased` is emitted. Without non-final captures the record is deleted and the id can be held again; otherwise the record is kept as settled, with its captured total the sum of the parts, and takes refunds and further `charge`s like any settled record.

### setDebtCap(creditor, token, cap)

Root key only: reverts `Unauthorized` when the owner is the transaction origin and an access key signed the transaction. The creditor is resolved through the address registry like a payee (a TIP-1022 virtual address sets the cap of its master, which is the creditor a payment to that virtual address creates); the call reverts `Unauthorized` when the resolved creditor is zero or the owner itself, and `InvalidToken` for a non-TIP-20 token. `debtCap` resolves the creditor the same way. Lowering a cap below the outstanding debt is allowed and blocks further debt.

### setCaptureTolerance(payee, token, bounded, toleranceBps)

Root key only, with the payee resolved and checked as the creditor of `setDebtCap` (`Unauthorized` for an access-key signer, a zero payee or the owner itself; `InvalidToken` for a non-TIP-20 token). With `bounded` set, a final capture (`capture` or a final `capturePart`) of the owner's hold for that payee in that token reverts `ToleranceExceeded` when `captured * 10000 > authorized * (10000 + toleranceBps)`, where `captured` is the hold's captured total including this capture and `authorized` is its captured total before this capture plus what it still reserves. With `bounded` unset, which is the default, captures are bounded only by the debt cap and the access key's limit; `toleranceBps` must then be zero (`InvalidAmount`). `captureTolerance` resolves the payee the same way.

A capture above the hold is how tips and other final amounts above the authorization settle, and its excess is new key spend and possibly debt. The card mandate bounds that excess with an overcapture tolerance (for example 20% for restaurant tips), and an issuer key could otherwise capture any excess within its remaining limit. The tolerance applies to the whole authorization: an adjust raises the authorized total, and non-final parts count towards the captured total. Only final captures need the check, because non-final captures and `captureFor` never exceed the hold. `toleranceBps` is a `uint32` so that no card flow's tolerance is out of range; the products are computed in 256 bits and cannot overflow. Lowering or setting a tolerance applies to every live hold of that payee and token at its next final capture.

### setOfflineCap(payee, token, cap)

Root key only, with the payee resolved and checked as the creditor of `setDebtCap` (`Unauthorized` for an access-key signer, a zero payee or the owner itself; `InvalidToken` for a non-TIP-20 token). Sets how much debt in `token` towards `payee` the owner accepts from stand-in payments, where it applies when larger than the debt cap; it changes nothing for other payments. Zero by default. `offlineCap` resolves the payee the same way. Lowering it below the outstanding debt is allowed and blocks further stand-in debt beyond the debt cap.

A card program's offline limit is how much the issuer may approve while the chain is unreachable. Those approvals bind the issuer before the chain sees them, so the owner's consent to owe up to that limit is set ahead of time by the root key, separately from the debt cap, which bounds what ordinary captures may owe. Only stand-in holds and charges use it, so an issuer key cannot turn the offline cap into debt room for purchases it approved online.

### refund(owner, id, amount)

Called by the record's payee (`HoldNotFound` without a record, `Unauthorized` for another caller). Cumulative refunds may not exceed the captured total across all captures, charges and non-final parts (`RefundExceedsCapture`); a live hold with non-final captures takes refunds of them and stays live. The token's TIP-403 policy must authorize the payee as sender and the owner as recipient. If the owner owes debt in the token to the refunding payee, `min(debt, amount)` cancels that debt (emitting `DebtRepaid`). If a credit line paid part of the id's captures, the next part of the rest, up to what the line paid and was not yet refunded and what the owner still owes on the line, repays the line as in [Line repayments](#line-repayments). Only what is left moves from payee to owner as a TIP-20 transfer; the line repayment and that transfer count against the payee's own access key limit. Emits `Refunded` with the full amount.

## Credit lines

A credit line lets a lender pay for an owner's holds instead of the owner's balance: a card on a bank's line, or an issuer lending from its omnibus account. A line is `(lender, line)` in one token. Credit is `(owner, lender, line)` with a `limit`, an amount `owed` (drawn and not repaid) and an amount `held` (the line parts of the owner's live holds), bound to one payee. The lender's commitment is a **lien**: part of the lender's own balance moved from its spendable balance slot into its `reserved` amount, as a hold moves the owner's, so `balanceOf` still counts it and no TIP-20 debit can spend it, but with no expiry. Lines have no interest or statements in this version; debt keeps its meaning (one creditor, debt-first credit) and is not a line.

What the lien keeps reserved for each credit is `max(limit - owed, held)` (zero when `owed > limit`), and a line's lien is the sum over its credits. A capture that draws `d` from a line lowers it by at least `d`, which is what the lender pays. The lien never covers more than the lender's offers allow, and never less than the line parts of approved holds, so a lender lowering a limit never strands an approved purchase.

### Consent

Three parties commit something, and each signs for it:

- **The lender** opens the line with `openLine` and offers each owner credit with `offerCredit(line, owner, payee, limit)`, from its root key. Nothing is reserved yet.
- **The owner** accepts with `grantCredit(line, lender, payee, limit)` from its root key. It reverts `CreditNotOffered` unless the lender's standing offer names this owner and `payee` and `limit` is at most the offered limit. A grant at or below the current limit for the same payee needs no offer, so an owner can always lower or close its own credit; any other grant (a higher limit or another payee) needs the offer and consumes it whole, whatever part of it was accepted. So once the lender lowers or closes a line, the owner cannot raise it again, or rebuild the lien, without a fresh offer.
- **The issuer key** places holds with `holdWithLine` within the owner's credit, and only for the payee the credit is bound to: a line funds only the issuer it was granted for, as the reference card model requires.

Without the lender's offer, an owner could name any open line and a payee it controls, lien the lender's balance with no expiry and draw it; without the owner's root key, an issuer key could bind the owner to a lender it chose.

### openLine(line, token)

The caller becomes the lender of `line`, a `bytes32` it chooses, in `token` (`InvalidToken` for a non-TIP-20 token, `LineExists` when the caller already opened that id). Emits `LineOpened`. The token is fixed, so draws and liens never mix tokens.

### offerCredit(line, owner, payee, limit)

Lender, root key only (`Unauthorized` for an access-key signer, a zero `owner` or `owner` equal to the lender; `LineNotFound` without the line). Records the lender's standing offer to `owner`: `payee` resolved as a hold's payee, and `limit` (`InvalidAmount` above `2^96 - 1`). A zero `limit` withdraws the offer and names no payee. If the owner's granted limit exceeds `limit`, it drops to `limit` at once and the lien shrinks to what the credit still needs; what was drawn stays owed and open holds' line parts stay reserved. Emits `CreditOffered`, and `CreditGranted` with the new limit when it lowered one.

### grantCredit(line, lender, payee, limit)

Owner, root key only (`Unauthorized` for an access-key signer or when the owner is the lender; `LineNotFound` without the line). Checks consent as above (`CreditNotOffered`) and that the token's TIP-403 policy authorizes the lender as sender and `payee` as recipient (`PolicyForbids`). Sets the credit's payee and limit, then moves the lien to the new `max(limit - owed, held)`: growth comes from the lender's available balance (`InsufficientAvailable` when short) and a decrease returns to it. Emits `LineReserved` when the lien changes and `CreditGranted`.

### holdWithLine(token, id, amount, linePart, line, lender, expiresAt, payee)

As `hold`, with `linePart` of `amount` on the owner's credit on `lender`'s `line` instead of the owner's balance. On top of the checks of `hold` steps 1 to 4: `0 < linePart <= amount` (`InvalidAmount`); the line exists in `token` (`LineNotFound`); `payee` is the payee the credit is bound to (`CreditNotOffered`); `linePart <= limit - owed - held` (`InsufficientAvailable`); and `amount - linePart` fits the owner's available balance (`InsufficientAvailable`). Reserves `amount - linePart` as a hold does, adds `linePart` to the credit's `held` (the lien already covers it) and stores the hold with `amount` as its amount, so tolerances, `captureFor` bounds, `getHold` and limit accounting treat it as one hold of `amount`. Emits `HoldPlaced` with `amount`, followed by `CreditHeld` with `linePart`.

A hold is on a card or on a credit line, never both: `holdCard` (TIP-1169) puts no part on a line, and `holdWithLine` checks no card. Card-scoped line holds are not supported yet, so a mandate key scoped to `holdCard` instead of `hold` cannot place line holds.

A line hold's balance part is `amount - linePart` and its line part `linePart`; both shrink as the hold settles:

- **`adjust`** up takes the increase from the owner's available balance first and the rest from the credit's headroom (`InsufficientAvailable` when both fall short); down takes the decrease off the line part first, then the balance part. A changed line part emits `CreditHeld` after `HoldAdjusted`.
- **Non-final captures** end the balance part first and then the line part, by the captured amount.
- **Final captures** (including `captureFor`) end both parts.
- **`release`** and **`releaseExpired`** end both parts without drawing.

Ending a line part lowers the credit's `held` and shrinks the lien to the credit's new need; whatever the lien frees and no draw pays returns to the lender's available balance.

### Line draws

In [Paying](#paying), after the owner's available balance (which includes the balance part the capture released), the line pays up to the line part the capture ends. The draw moves `drawn` from the lender's lien to the payee as a TIP-20 transfer from the lender (`Transfer(lender, payee)`, policy and receive-policy checked as any reservation payment), adds it to the credit's `owed` and to the id's line-drawn total, and emits `CreditDrawn`. The rest of the payment, a capture above the hold's line part included, is debt within the cap as before: a line pays only what a hold reserved on it. A draw never takes the lender's spendable balance; after a compliance burn of the lender's reserved funds (see [Compliance burns](#compliance-burns), which treats a line's lien as a hold placed when the lien last grew from zero) the lien can be short, and the draw pays only what it still frees.

### Line repayments

`repayCredit(lender, line, amount)` repays `min(amount, owed)` from the owner's available balance (`InvalidAmount` for zero, `LineNotFound` without the line, `InsufficientAvailable` when short; nothing happens when nothing is owed). The repayment is a TIP-20 transfer from the owner to the lender, checked against the token's policy and counted against the owner's signing access key as a transfer would be. A refund repays a line the same way, from the payee (see [refund](#refundowner-id-amount)). Deposits do not repay lines: unlike debt, a line is repaid only when the owner or a refund chooses to.

After the repayment lowers `owed`, the lien grows towards the credit's new need from the lender's available balance. The repayment reached the lender as an ordinary TIP-20 credit, which repays the lender's own debt first, so the lien grows by at most what the lender then has available. Emits `LineReserved` when the lien grows and `CreditRepaid`.

### TIP-20 debits (T15)

Every TIP-20 debit (transfer, transferFrom and memo variants, system transfers including stablecoin DEX deposits and TIP-1034 channel open and top-up, issuer burns, and fee collection) debits the balance slot, which holds only the available balance, so it can never spend reserved funds and needs no reservation read. A debit above the available balance reverts with TIP-20's `InsufficientBalance(available, amount, token)`. Capture and charge release the hold back to the available balance first and never pay more than it.

### Compliance burns

`burnBlocked` and `burnAt` are the token administrator's seizure paths and ignore reservations: holds are the owner's commitments and must not shield funds from a compliance burn. Before such a burn of `amount` above the owner's available balance, up to `amount - available` moves from `reserved` back to the available balance, so the burn can take reserved funds. A burn above `balanceOf` still reverts `InsufficientBalance`. The holds keep their amounts, so `reserved` can then be below the sum of the owner's live holds; the difference is recorded per owner and token as `unbacked`, with a burn counter (`epoch`) that each hold copies when placed. Holds settle against what remains: `release`, `releaseExpired`, `adjust` down and final captures remove the hold's amount and a non-final capture its own amount, and a hold placed before the latest burn takes it from `unbacked` first and only the rest from `reserved`, so the reservation behind other holds (including holds placed after the burn) stays in place; and a capture pays only the available balance, with any rest becoming debt within the cap or reverting `DebtCapExceeded` as in [Paying](#paying). Limit accounting is unaffected.

### TIP-20 debt-first credit (T15)

After any TIP-20 credit to an account (transfers, mints, receive-guard releases), if the account owes debt in that token, `min(debt, amount)` moves to the creditor with a TIP-20 `Transfer(owner, creditor, repaid)` and the precompile emits `DebtRepaid`. The repayment credit does not itself repay the creditor's own debt. For a credit to a TIP-1022 virtual address the repayment follows the forwarding `Transfer(virtual, master)`.

To find debtors without a read per credit, the precompile counts, per account, the tokens in which it owes debt, in spare bytes of the account's TIP-1028 receive-policy configuration slot in the TIP-403 registry (`reservationDebts`, a `uint32` after the existing fields). Every TIP-20 credit already reads that slot for the receive-policy check, so a credit consults the account's position only when the count is non-zero. The count rises when an account's debt in a token goes from zero to non-zero and falls when it returns to zero; setting a receive policy preserves it.

A repayment is a transfer from the owner to the creditor and obeys the same rules: it happens only while the token is not paused, the token's TIP-403 policy authorizes the owner as sender and the creditor as recipient, and the creditor's TIP-1028 receive policy admits the owner. Otherwise the repayment is skipped: the credit completes and stays with the owner, and the debt is unchanged until a later credit can repay it. Skipping rather than reverting keeps a creditor's policy from blocking all credits to its debtor. TIP-20 reward claims and fee refunds are not credits for this purpose: fee refunds run outside TIP-1060 storage-credit metering, where writing the creditor's balance slot would let it earn unbacked storage credits. Funds that become available by releasing or partially capturing a hold are not credits either: an owner in debt can spend them.

### Activation

The precompile exists and the TIP-20 hooks run only from T15, on Tempo L1. Zones neither expose the precompile nor run the hooks. Before T15 no TIP-20 operation reads reservation state, so gas and behavior are unchanged.

## Gas

The precompile charges standard calldata input cost and storage costs. An account position is one slot; an authorization is five slots (token and amount, payee, expiry and burn epoch, captured and refunded, key and limit period end, counted with the stand-in flag, the line flag and, for a TIP-1169 card hold, its card line); the third is written only on a capture or charge and the last two only when an access key spends on the authorization. A hold also reads the owner's shortfall slot, which only compliance burns write. Under TIP-1000 each new slot costs 250,000 gas, partly recoverable through TIP-1060 storage credits; a hold creates up to five new slots the first time an owner uses a token and id. Limit accounting adds the keychain's key and limit-row reads and one limit-row write per spend or give-back.

From T15 a TIP-20 debit reads no reservation state, since the balance slot already excludes reserved funds. A credit re-reads the recipient's receive-policy slot, which the receive-policy check loaded earlier in the call (a warm SLOAD, 100 gas); only recipients with debt add the position, creditor, pause, TIP-403 and receive-policy reads and the repayment writes. Fee affordability checks read only the balance slot. For an account with no reservations or debt, a transfer therefore costs about 100 gas more than before T15. A final capture above what the hold still reserves also reads the owner's tolerance slot for the payee and token. Credit lines add no read to holds without a line part: the authorization's `line` flag shares its last slot, and the hold's line part lives in a separate three-slot record (lender and line part, line id, drawn total) read only when the flag is set. A line is two slots (token and lien, burn epoch), a credit two (payee and limit, owed and held) and an offer one. A line hold or a capture of one adds the credit and line reads and writes, the lender's position, and on a draw a TIP-20 transfer from the lender; refunds of a capture with a line part add the same for the repayment. TIP-20 debits and credits read nothing more: a lien sits in the lender's `reserved`, outside its balance slot. A stand-in payment whose debt would exceed the debt cap also reads the owner's offline cap slot for the payee and token. `balanceOf` adds one read of the position slot.

Moving held funds out of the balance slot keeps TIP-20 balance changes commutative (`Sinc`/`Sdec`) for parallel block building: debits need no exact read. A recipient's receive-policy slot changes only through policy updates and when its debt count changes, so concurrent credits to recipients without debt read an unchanged value and replay without conflict; credits to a recipient with debt each write its position, so they conflict with one another.

### Alternatives considered

- **Reading the position on every debit and credit.** The earlier design kept reserved funds in the balance slot and checked `balance - reserved` on each debit and the debt on each credit: about 4,200 gas and two cold reads more per transfer for every account, which slowed TIP-20 execution benchmarks by 10 to 20%.
- **Lazy repayment.** Credits stop repaying debt, and debt is collected later. It saves the credit-side read too, but debt stays outstanding while the debtor receives funds; the debtor count gives the same saving while keeping repayment on credit.
- **Flag bits in the balance slot.** Reading a flag from the balance slot on every transfer turns commutative balance updates into exact reads, so concurrent payments to one account conflict, and it breaks the underflow check in `sdec`.

# Compatibility

Before T15 nothing changes. From T15, accounts that never call the precompile behave as before except for about 100 gas per TIP-20 credit and one more read in `balanceOf`. Wallets and indexers should show `available` rather than `balanceOf` as spendable for accounts with holds; `balanceOf` still includes reserved funds, and holds emit no `Transfer`. Indexers tracking balances from `Transfer` events stay correct: repayments emit a `Transfer` to the creditor. A compliance burn can lower `reserved` without a reservations event; indexers read `reserved` after `BurnBlocked` and `BurnAt`.

From T15 every fee affordability check uses the available balance, which is the balance slot, matching fee collection: the fallback fee-token choice, the transaction validity check against the maximum fee, the RPC gas allowance, and transaction pool admission and maintenance. The pool re-checks a fee payer's pending transactions when `HoldPlaced`, `HoldAdjusted` or `LineReserved` names it and the fee token; other operations that lower the available balance emit a TIP-20 `Transfer` from the owner.

# Observability

Every state change emits one of the events above: holds placed (`StandInHoldPlaced` adds a stand-in hold's approved amount), adjusted, released, captured (with the debt created; `HoldPartCaptured` for a non-final part, `HoldCaptured` for the capture that ends the hold), charges (with debt), refunds, and each debt repayment with its creditor; for credit lines, lines opened, every lien change, offers, grants (and limits a lender lowered), holds' line parts, draws and repayments. Access key spending under a card mandate is visible through the existing `AccessKeySpend` event.

# Threat Model

| Actor | Controls | Enforcing check | On compromise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Issuer access key | hold, holdStandIn, adjust, capture, capturePart, charge, chargeStandIn, release on the cardholder account | TIP-1011 call scope, per-token periodic spending limit counting open holds, capture excess and charges, debt cap, offline cap for stand-in payments, capture tolerance | Commits (holds plus payments) up to the limit per period, into debt only up to the caps set by the root key; cannot act on another key's holds |
| Cardholder root key | debt caps, offline caps, all funds | none beyond key ownership | Already controls all funds |
| Payee | refunds, `captureFor`, `captureForPart` | caller must be the record's payee; refunds capped by captured; `captureFor` and `captureForPart` capped by the live hold | Can only return funds, or collect up to what the owner held for it |
| Lender root key | openLine, offerCredit, its own lien | none beyond key ownership | Already controls its balance; can lower credit but never below open holds' line parts |
| Anyone | releaseExpired | expiry | Can only release expired holds |

The most consequential attack is a compromised issuer key draining the cardholder: captures and charges are bounded by the key's spending limit and by the root-key debt cap (for stand-in holds and charges, the larger of it and the root-key offline cap), and the key cannot change any of them. Holds consume the limit, so a compromised key can lock at most its remaining limit per period, until holds expire or the root key releases them; holds cannot move funds by themselves. Releases and refunds return headroom only to the key that placed the authorization and only within the period it was counted in, so a key cannot recycle the same allowance across periods. Holds cannot shield funds from token administrators: compliance burns (`burnBlocked`, `burnAt`) take reserved funds, and captures and charges re-check TIP-403 at settlement, so a payee or owner blocked after a hold was placed cannot be paid. Debt repayments obey pause, TIP-403 and the creditor's receive policy, so debt cannot move funds the token's policy forbids. Holds last at most 31 days. Credit lines add no new power to the issuer key: it can put on a line only what the owner accepted, only for the payee the credit is bound to, within the key's limit, which counts line parts like any hold. A lien has no expiry, but only a grant the lender offered and the owner's root key accepted creates one, and the lender can shrink it to what open holds need at any time.

# Invariants

- For every owner and token, `reserved` never exceeds the sum of the balance parts of the owner's live holds in that token plus the liens of the lines it lends in that token, and equals it less the owner's `unbacked` amount, which only compliance burns raise. A hold's balance part is its amount less its line part.
- For every line, its lien is at most the sum over its credits of `max(limit - owed, held)`, and equals it unless a compliance burn or a repayment the lender's own debt took left it short.
- For every credit, `held` is the sum of the line parts of the owner's live holds on it, and a hold is placed on it only for its payee and within `limit - owed - held`.
- A credit's limit rises only by `grantCredit` within a standing offer the lender made for that owner and payee, which the grant then consumes.
- A line draw never pays more than the line part the capture ends, and never takes the lender's spendable balance.
- `debt <= debtCap(owner, creditor, token)` at the time each debt increase is made, or for a stand-in payment `debt <= max(debtCap, offlineCap)` for that creditor and token, and debt has at most one creditor per owner and token; `creditor == 0` exactly when `debt == 0`.
- For every record, `refunded <= captured`.
- Within one limit period, an access key's remaining limit never exceeds its maximum, and amounts given back to it never exceed what the same authorization counted against it in that period.
- For every token, the sum over accounts of the balance slot plus `reserved` equals `totalSupply`: holds move funds between the two without minting or burning.
- An account's `reservationDebts` equals the number of tokens in which its `debt` is non-zero.
- Every TIP-20 credit to an account with debt pays `min(debt, amount)` to the creditor before the account can spend it, unless the token is paused or the token's or creditor's policy forbids the repayment, in which case the debt is unchanged.
- No live hold expires more than `MAX_HOLD_DURATION` after it was placed.
- Captures and charges never transfer more of the owner's balance than is available at the time of payment; the rest is a line draw (only for a hold's line part) or debt.
- While the owner bounds captures for a payee and token, a final capture never takes a hold's captured total past its authorized total (captured plus still reserved, before that capture) by more than the tolerance.
- `captureFor` and `captureForPart` never capture more than what the hold still reserves and never count against an access key's limit.
- A non-final capture never pays more than what the hold still reserves, leaves its expiry unchanged, and neither spends nor gives back access key limit.
- A live hold's captured total is the sum of its non-final captures, and a settled record's is the sum of all its captures and charges.
