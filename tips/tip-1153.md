# TIP-1153: Account Reservations

## Abstract

A new precompile lets an account reserve part of its own TIP-20 balance for a payee under an id it chooses, capture or release that hold later, pay without a hold, and owe a capped debt when a payment exceeds what it has available. Reserved funds never leave the account: from T15 every TIP-20 debit respects the reservation and every TIP-20 credit repays outstanding debt first. Payees can refund against the captured total of an id. The precompile holds no funds and does not interpret card network messages.

## Motivation

Card authorization needs three things a plain transfer cannot give: an amount set aside at authorization time without moving it, a final amount that may differ from the authorization (tips, partial shipments, offline and late presentments), and refunds tied to the original payment. Today an issuer either prefunds settlement or pulls funds into an escrow contract at authorization, which takes custody of user balances and costs a transfer per authorization.

Holding funds in place keeps custody with the cardholder, lets the cardholder keep earning on held balances, and needs no issuer float. Capped debt lets an issuer approve a capture slightly above the hold (tips, currency swings) without prefunding: the shortfall is recovered from the next funds the cardholder receives.

Alternatives considered: an escrow contract (takes custody, and every hold is a transfer), TIP-20 allowances (do not lock funds, so a later transfer can spend them), and TIP-1034 channels (lock funds in the reserve's custody and settle by vouchers, which does not fit network-driven captures or refunds).

## Assumptions

- The cardholder's account is driven for card flows by an issuer-held access key (TIP-1011) whose call scope is limited to this precompile's `hold`, `adjust`, `capture`, `charge` and `release` selectors, with periodic spending limits per token. That key is the mandate; this TIP adds no separate mandate object.
- The debt cap is set by the account's root key. If a root key is compromised, the attacker can raise caps; this is no worse than the root key's existing ability to transfer the whole balance.
- Payees (issuers or their settlement accounts) are trusted by the owner to the extent of the holds and caps the owner grants them.
- Hold ids are unique per owner per authorization. Card programs use `keccak256(network authorization id)`.

# Specification

## Overview

Per (owner, token) the precompile tracks `reserved` (the sum of the owner's live holds) and `debt` (owed to a single creditor). An owner's available balance is `balance - reserved`.

An authorization record per (owner, id) moves through: **held** (after `hold`; may be `adjust`ed), then either **released** (record deleted by `release` or `releaseExpired`) or **settled** (after `capture` or `charge`; keeps token, payee and captured/refunded totals). A settled id can take further `charge`s for the same token and payee and `refund`s up to its captured total.

| Operation | Caller | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hold`, `adjust`, `release` | owner | Change `reserved` |
| `capture`, `charge` | owner | Pay the payee, possibly creating debt |
| `setDebtCap` | owner, root key only | Bound debt towards a creditor |
| `releaseExpired` | anyone | Release a hold once expired |
| `refund` | the record's payee | Return funds to the owner |

## Mechanics

### Amounts and time

All amounts are TIP-20 base units. ABI amounts are `uint128` but are stored as 96-bit values (enough for 7.9e28 base units, far above any TIP-20 supply cap in practice); a zero amount or one above `2^96 - 1` reverts `InvalidAmount`. `expiresAt` is a unix timestamp in seconds; a hold is expired once `block.timestamp >= expiresAt`. `hold` with `expiresAt <= block.timestamp` reverts `InvalidAmount`.

### hold(token, id, amount, expiresAt, payee)

1. Validate `amount` and `expiresAt` as above.
2. `token` must have the TIP-20 prefix (`InvalidToken`). `payee` is resolved through the address registry (TIP-1022 virtual addresses resolve to their master) and must not be zero or a TIP-20 address (`InvalidRecipient`).
3. The token's TIP-403 transfer policy must authorize the owner as sender and the payee as recipient (`PolicyForbids`).
4. No record may exist for (owner, id), live or settled (`HoldExists`).
5. `amount` must not exceed the owner's available balance (`InsufficientAvailable`).
6. `reserved += amount`, store the record, emit `HoldPlaced`.

### adjust(id, amount)

Sets the live hold's amount to `amount` (`HoldNotFound` without a live hold, `InvalidAmount` for zero). An increase needs the increase to fit in the available balance (`InsufficientAvailable`). Works on expired but unreleased holds. Emits `HoldAdjusted` with the new total.

### capture(id, amount)

1. Requires a live hold (`HoldNotFound`) and a valid `amount`. Works on expired but unreleased holds.
2. The hold's amount is removed from `reserved` first, so the payment spends the reservation it consumes.
3. The payment is made as in [Paying](#paying) to the hold's payee.
4. The hold ends: its reserved amount is zero, it is no longer live, and its captured total becomes `amount` (debt included). Emits `HoldCaptured(owner, id, amount, debt)`.

TIP-403 is not re-checked at capture: the policy was checked when the hold was placed, and a captured authorization must settle.

### charge(token, id, amount, payee)

A payment without a hold, for offline and late presentments. Validates token, payee and TIP-403 as `hold` does. Reverts `HoldExists` if the id has a live hold, or a settled record with a different token or payee. Pays as in [Paying](#paying), adds `amount` to the id's captured total and emits `Charged`.

### Paying

For an owner, token, payee and amount:

1. `paid = min(amount, available)` and `shortfall = amount - paid`.
2. If `shortfall > 0`: if the owner has debt in the token to a creditor other than the payee, revert `DebtCapExceeded` (one creditor per owner and token). If `debt + shortfall > debtCap(owner, payee, token)`, revert `DebtCapExceeded`. Otherwise the payee becomes (or stays) the creditor and `debt += shortfall`. The payee receives no tokens for the shortfall: it holds a claim that later credits repay.
3. The full `amount` counts against the spending limit of the access key signing the transaction when the owner is the transaction origin (TIP-1011), so the mandate bounds commitments, debt included. Holds do not consume the limit.
4. `paid` moves from owner to payee as a TIP-20 transfer: it reverts if the token is paused, and if the payee's TIP-1028 receive policy rejects the owner (`PolicyForbids`), because the record cannot represent a guarded payment.

### release(id) and releaseExpired(owner, id)

Both require a live hold (`HoldNotFound`). `releaseExpired` is callable by anyone and reverts `HoldNotExpired` before expiry. The hold's amount leaves `reserved`, the record is deleted (a live hold never has captures), and `HoldReleased` is emitted. A released id can be held again.

### setDebtCap(creditor, token, cap)

Root key only: reverts `Unauthorized` when the owner is the transaction origin and an access key signed the transaction. Also reverts `Unauthorized` for a zero creditor or the owner itself, and `InvalidToken` for a non-TIP-20 token. Lowering a cap below the outstanding debt is allowed and blocks further debt.

### refund(owner, id, amount)

Called by the record's payee (`HoldNotFound` without a record, `Unauthorized` for another caller). Cumulative refunds may not exceed the captured total (`RefundExceedsCapture`). The token's TIP-403 policy must authorize the payee as sender and the owner as recipient. If the owner owes debt in the token to the refunding payee, `min(debt, amount)` cancels that debt (emitting `DebtRepaid`) and only the rest moves from payee to owner as a TIP-20 transfer, which counts against the payee's own access key limit. Emits `Refunded` with the full amount.

### TIP-20 debit check (T15)

Every TIP-20 debit (transfer, transferFrom and memo variants, system transfers including stablecoin DEX deposits and TIP-1034 channel open and top-up, burns, burnBlocked, burnAt, and fee collection) requires `balance - reserved(owner, token) >= amount`. Otherwise it reverts with TIP-20's `InsufficientBalance(available, amount, token)`, carrying the available balance. Capture and charge pass this check because they release the hold first and never pay more than the available balance.

### TIP-20 debt-first credit (T15)

After any TIP-20 credit to an account (transfers, mints, receive-guard releases, fee refunds), if the account owes debt in that token, `min(debt, amount)` moves to the creditor with a TIP-20 `Transfer(owner, creditor, repaid)` and the precompile emits `DebtRepaid`. The repayment credit does not itself repay the creditor's own debt. TIP-20 reward claims are not credits for this purpose. Funds that become available by releasing or partially capturing a hold are not credits either: an owner in debt can spend them.

### Activation

The precompile exists and the TIP-20 hooks run only from T15, on Tempo L1. Zones neither expose the precompile nor run the hooks. Before T15 no TIP-20 operation reads reservation state, so gas and behavior are unchanged.

## Gas

The precompile charges standard calldata input cost and storage costs. An account position is one slot; an authorization is three slots (token and amount, payee and expiry, captured and refunded), the last written only on settlement. Under TIP-1000 each new slot costs 250,000 gas, partly recoverable through TIP-1060 storage credits; a hold creates two or three new slots the first time an owner uses a token and id.

From T15 each TIP-20 debit reads the sender's position slot and each credit reads the recipient's position slot (one cold SLOAD each, 2,100 gas), plus a balance read for senders with reservations and the repayment writes for recipients with debt. A future change can pack a "has reservations or debt" flag into the balance slot's unused high bits to remove both reads for ordinary accounts.

## Interfaces

Address: `0x5245535600000000000000000000000000000000` (ASCII "RESV").

```solidity
interface IReservations {
    struct Hold { address token; address payee; uint128 amount; uint64 expiresAt; }

    function hold(address token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, uint64 expiresAt, address payee) external;
    function adjust(bytes32 id, uint128 amount) external;
    function capture(bytes32 id, uint128 amount) external;
    function charge(address token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, address payee) external;
    function release(bytes32 id) external;
    function setDebtCap(address creditor, address token, uint128 cap) external;
    function releaseExpired(address owner, bytes32 id) external;
    function refund(address owner, bytes32 id, uint128 amount) external;

    /// Zero struct without a record; a settled record returns token and payee with zero amount and expiry.
    function getHold(address owner, bytes32 id) external view returns (Hold memory);
    function reserved(address owner, address token) external view returns (uint128);
    function available(address owner, address token) external view returns (uint128);
    /// Creditor is zero when there is no debt.
    function debtOf(address owner, address token) external view returns (address creditor, uint128 amount);
    function debtCap(address owner, address creditor, address token) external view returns (uint128);
    function captured(address owner, bytes32 id) external view returns (uint128 captured, uint128 refunded);

    event HoldPlaced(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, address indexed token, address payee, uint128 amount, uint64 expiresAt);
    event HoldAdjusted(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, uint128 amount);
    event HoldReleased(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id);
    event HoldCaptured(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, uint128 amount, uint128 debt);
    event Charged(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, address indexed token, address payee, uint128 amount, uint128 debt);
    event Refunded(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, uint128 amount);
    event DebtRepaid(address indexed owner, address indexed token, address indexed creditor, uint128 amount);

    error HoldExists(); error HoldNotFound(); error HoldNotExpired(); error InsufficientAvailable();
    error RefundExceedsCapture(); error DebtCapExceeded(); error PolicyForbids(); error Unauthorized(); error InvalidAmount();
}
```

`PolicyForbids()`, `Unauthorized()` and `InvalidAmount()` share selectors with the TIP-20 errors of the same name; TIP-20 validation failures inside these calls (`InvalidToken`, `InvalidRecipient`, `ContractPaused`, `InsufficientBalance`) surface as TIP-20 errors.

Storage layout (precompile slots in declaration order):

| Slot | Field | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | positions | `mapping(owner => mapping(token => {uint128 reserved; uint128 debt}))` |
| 1 | creditors | `mapping(owner => mapping(token => address))` |
| 2 | debtCaps | `mapping(owner => mapping(creditor => mapping(token => uint128)))` |
| 3 | authorizations | `mapping(owner => mapping(id => {address token; uint96 held; address payee; uint64 expiresAt; uint96 captured; uint96 refunded}))` |

# Compatibility

Before T15 nothing changes. From T15, accounts that never call the precompile behave as before except for the extra position reads in TIP-20 gas. Wallets and indexers should show `available` rather than `balanceOf` as spendable for accounts with holds; `balanceOf` still includes reserved funds. Indexers tracking balances from `Transfer` events stay correct: repayments emit a `Transfer` to the creditor. Transaction pool fee affordability checks read raw balances, so a transaction whose fee would need reserved funds can enter the pool and then fail fee collection; card flows normally have the issuer sponsor fees.

# Observability

Every state change emits one of the events above: holds placed, adjusted, released, captured (with the debt created), charges (with debt), refunds, and each debt repayment with its creditor. Access key spending under a card mandate is visible through the existing `AccessKeySpend` event.

# Threat Model

| Actor | Controls | Enforcing check | On compromise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Issuer access key | hold, adjust, capture, charge, release on the cardholder account | TIP-1011 call scope, per-token periodic spending limit counting captures and charges, debt cap | Spends up to the limit per period, into debt only up to the cap set by the root key |
| Cardholder root key | debt caps, all funds | none beyond key ownership | Already controls all funds |
| Payee | refunds | caller must be the record's payee; refunds capped by captured | Can only return funds |
| Anyone | releaseExpired | expiry | Can only release expired holds |

The most consequential attack is a compromised issuer key draining the cardholder: captures and charges are bounded by the key's spending limit and by the root-key debt cap, and the key cannot change either. Holds do not consume the limit, so a compromised key can lock the whole balance until holds expire or the root key releases them; holds cannot move funds by themselves. Burns by token administrators (`burnBlocked`, `burnAt`) also respect reservations, so the owner or an expiry must release holds before a compliance burn can take reserved funds.

# Invariants

- For every owner and token, `reserved` equals the sum of the amounts of the owner's live holds in that token, and never exceeds the owner's balance.
- `debt <= debtCap(owner, creditor, token)` at the time each debt increase is made, and debt has at most one creditor per owner and token; `creditor == 0` exactly when `debt == 0`.
- For every record, `refunded <= captured`.
- No TIP-20 debit leaves `balance < reserved`.
- Every TIP-20 credit to an account with debt pays `min(debt, amount)` to the creditor before the account can spend it.
- Captures and charges never transfer more than the available balance at the time of payment; the rest is debt.
