# TIP-1153: Account Reservations

## Abstract

A new precompile lets an account reserve part of its own TIP-20 balance for a payee under an id it chooses, capture or release that hold later, pay without a hold, and owe a capped debt when a payment exceeds what it has available. Reserved funds never leave the account: a hold moves them from the account's spendable TIP-20 balance into its reserved amount, which `balanceOf` still counts, so debits cannot spend them and need no reservation check, and from T15 every TIP-20 credit repays outstanding debt first. Payees can refund against the captured total of an id. The precompile holds no funds and does not interpret card network messages.

## Motivation

Card authorization needs three things a plain transfer cannot give: an amount set aside at authorization time without moving it, a final amount that may differ from the authorization (tips, partial shipments, offline and late presentments), and refunds tied to the original payment. Today an issuer either prefunds settlement or pulls funds into an escrow contract at authorization, which takes custody of user balances and costs a transfer per authorization.

Holding funds in place keeps custody with the cardholder, lets the cardholder keep earning on held balances, and needs no issuer float. Capped debt lets an issuer approve a capture slightly above the hold (tips, currency swings) without prefunding: the shortfall is recovered from the next funds the cardholder receives.

Alternatives considered: an escrow contract (takes custody, and every hold is a transfer), TIP-20 allowances (do not lock funds, so a later transfer can spend them), and TIP-1034 channels (lock funds in the reserve's custody and settle by vouchers, which does not fit network-driven captures or refunds).

## Assumptions

- The cardholder's account is driven for card flows by an issuer-held access key (TIP-1011) whose call scope is limited to this precompile's `hold`, `adjust`, `capture`, `charge` and `release` selectors, with periodic spending limits per token. That key is the mandate; this TIP adds no separate mandate object.
- The debt cap is set by the account's root key. If a root key is compromised, the attacker can raise caps; this is no worse than the root key's existing ability to transfer the whole balance.
- Payees (issuers or their settlement accounts) are trusted by the owner to the extent of the holds and caps the owner grants them.
- Hold ids are unique per owner per authorization. Card programs use `keccak256(network authorization id)`.

# Specification

## Overview

Per (owner, token) the precompile tracks `reserved` (the sum of the owner's live holds) and `debt` (owed to a single creditor). From T15 the TIP-20 balance slot holds only the owner's available (spendable) balance: placing or raising a hold moves the amount from it into `reserved`, and lowering, releasing or capturing a hold moves the amount back. TIP-20 `balanceOf` returns the available balance plus `reserved`, as before.

An authorization record per (owner, id) moves through: **held** (after `hold`; may be `adjust`ed), then either **released** (record deleted by `release` or `releaseExpired`) or **settled** (after `capture` or `charge`; keeps token, payee and captured/refunded totals). A settled id can take further `charge`s for the same token and payee and `refund`s up to its captured total.

| Operation | Caller | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hold`, `adjust`, `release` | owner | Change `reserved` |
| `capture`, `charge` | owner | Pay the payee, possibly creating debt |
| `setDebtCap` | owner, root key only | Bound debt towards a creditor |
| `releaseExpired` | anyone | Release a hold once expired |
| `refund` | the record's payee | Return funds to the owner |

## Mechanics

### Amounts and time

All amounts are TIP-20 base units. ABI amounts are `uint128` but are stored as 96-bit values (enough for 7.9e28 base units, far above any TIP-20 supply cap in practice); a zero amount or one above `2^96 - 1` reverts `InvalidAmount`. `expiresAt` is a unix timestamp in seconds; a hold is expired once `block.timestamp >= expiresAt`. `hold` with `expiresAt <= block.timestamp` or `expiresAt > block.timestamp + MAX_HOLD_DURATION` reverts `InvalidAmount`.

`MAX_HOLD_DURATION` is 31 days (2,678,400 seconds). Card network rules expire authorizations within 30 days, so a longer hold serves no card flow; the bound limits how long funds can stay locked and how long a hold outlives a change of the token's policy. A hold cannot be extended: `adjust` changes only its amount.

### hold(token, id, amount, expiresAt, payee)

1. Validate `amount` and `expiresAt` as above, including the `MAX_HOLD_DURATION` bound.
2. `token` must have the TIP-20 prefix (`InvalidToken`). `payee` is resolved through the address registry (TIP-1022 virtual addresses resolve to their master) and must not be zero or a TIP-20 address (`InvalidRecipient`).
3. The token's TIP-403 transfer policy must authorize the owner as sender and the payee as recipient (`PolicyForbids`).
4. No record may exist for (owner, id), live or settled (`HoldExists`).
5. `amount` must not exceed the owner's available balance (`InsufficientAvailable`).
6. Move `amount` from the available balance to `reserved`, store the record, emit `HoldPlaced`.

### adjust(id, amount)

Sets the live hold's amount to `amount` (`HoldNotFound` without a live hold, `InvalidAmount` for zero). An increase moves the increase from the available balance to `reserved` and needs it to fit (`InsufficientAvailable`). A decrease moves the decrease back, but never more than `reserved` (see [Compliance burns](#compliance-burns)). Works on expired but unreleased holds. Emits `HoldAdjusted` with the hold's token and new total.

### capture(id, amount)

1. Requires a live hold (`HoldNotFound`) and a valid `amount`. Works on expired but unreleased holds.
2. The token's TIP-403 transfer policy must still authorize the owner as sender and the hold's payee as recipient (`PolicyForbids`). The policy may have changed since the hold was placed, and settlement moves funds; a hold whose parties became forbidden can only be released or expire.
3. The hold's amount moves from `reserved` back to the available balance first (never more than `reserved`), so the payment spends the reservation it consumes.
4. The payment is made as in [Paying](#paying) to the hold's payee.
5. The hold ends: its reserved amount is zero, it is no longer live, and its captured total becomes `amount` (debt included). Emits `HoldCaptured(owner, id, token, amount, debt)`.

### charge(token, id, amount, payee)

A payment without a hold, for offline and late presentments. Validates token, payee and TIP-403 (owner as sender, payee as recipient) as `hold` does, at the time of the charge. Reverts `HoldExists` if the id has a live hold, or a settled record with a different token or payee. Pays as in [Paying](#paying), adds `amount` to the id's captured total and emits `Charged`.

### Paying

For an owner, token, payee and amount:

1. `paid = min(amount, available)` and `shortfall = amount - paid`.
2. If `shortfall > 0`: if the owner has debt in the token to a creditor other than the payee, revert `DebtCapExceeded` (one creditor per owner and token). If `debt + shortfall > debtCap(owner, payee, token)`, revert `DebtCapExceeded`. Otherwise the payee becomes (or stays) the creditor and `debt += shortfall`. The payee receives no tokens for the shortfall: it holds a claim that later credits repay.
3. The amount not already counted by the hold (for `capture`, the excess over the hold; for `charge`, the full `amount`) counts against the signing access key's limit as described in [Mandate limits](#mandate-limits), debt included.
4. `paid` moves from owner to payee as a TIP-20 transfer: it reverts if the token is paused, and if the payee's TIP-1028 receive policy rejects the owner (`PolicyForbids`), because the record cannot represent a guarded payment.

### Mandate limits

An open hold is committed spend of the card mandate. Whenever the owner is the transaction origin and an access key signed the transaction (TIP-1011), the amounts below count against that key's spending limit for the hold's token, reverting with the keychain's `SpendingLimitExceeded()` when over. Root-key transactions and calls from other accounts count nothing.

| Operation | Limit effect |
| --- | --- |
| `hold` | Spends `amount` |
| `adjust` up | Spends the increase |
| `capture` | Spends `amount - held` when positive; gives back `held - amount` when positive |
| `charge` | Spends `amount` |
| `adjust` down | Gives back the decrease |
| `release`, `releaseExpired` | Give back the held amount |
| `refund` | Gives back the refunded amount |

The authorization records the access key that placed the hold or first charge (zero for the root key) and the end of that key's limit period after the charge (zero for one-time limits). Another access key of the same owner cannot `adjust`, `capture`, `charge` or `release` that id (`Unauthorized`); the root key can, and its own changes count nothing. Spend is always charged to the signing key, while amounts are given back only to the recorded key, and only while its limit row is still in the recorded period: once a spend has rolled the key's period, nothing is given back, because the new period already started at the full limit and returning older spend would let the key exceed its limit within one period. Gives never raise the remaining limit above its configured maximum, and do nothing for revoked or expired keys, keys without enforced limits, or limit rows whose maximum is zero: an owner that freezes a key with `updateSpendingLimit(key, token, 0)` keeps it frozen through later releases and refunds, and keys created before T3 (whose rows store no maximum) get nothing back. TIP-20 fee refunds keep their existing limit behavior. The authorization also records how much the key's limit counted in that period (`counted`); after every change it is lowered to at most what the authorization still commits (the live hold, or captured less refunded; zero on release) and only that drop is given back, so amounts the root key added are released first, and a spend by the recorded key in a new period moves the recorded period forward and restarts `counted` at that spend. Consequences: spend counted in an earlier period than the latest is not returned (the safe direction), and amounts the root key adds to an access key's authorization (hold increases, captures above the hold, further charges) are never returned to that key.

### release(id) and releaseExpired(owner, id)

Both require a live hold (`HoldNotFound`). `releaseExpired` is callable by anyone and reverts `HoldNotExpired` before expiry. The hold's amount moves from `reserved` back to the available balance, the record is deleted (a live hold never has captures), and `HoldReleased` is emitted. A released id can be held again.

### setDebtCap(creditor, token, cap)

Root key only: reverts `Unauthorized` when the owner is the transaction origin and an access key signed the transaction. The creditor is resolved through the address registry like a payee (a TIP-1022 virtual address sets the cap of its master, which is the creditor a payment to that virtual address creates); the call reverts `Unauthorized` when the resolved creditor is zero or the owner itself, and `InvalidToken` for a non-TIP-20 token. `debtCap` resolves the creditor the same way. Lowering a cap below the outstanding debt is allowed and blocks further debt.

### refund(owner, id, amount)

Called by the record's payee (`HoldNotFound` without a record, `Unauthorized` for another caller). Cumulative refunds may not exceed the captured total (`RefundExceedsCapture`). The token's TIP-403 policy must authorize the payee as sender and the owner as recipient. If the owner owes debt in the token to the refunding payee, `min(debt, amount)` cancels that debt (emitting `DebtRepaid`) and only the rest moves from payee to owner as a TIP-20 transfer, which counts against the payee's own access key limit. Emits `Refunded` with the full amount.

### TIP-20 debits (T15)

Every TIP-20 debit (transfer, transferFrom and memo variants, system transfers including stablecoin DEX deposits and TIP-1034 channel open and top-up, issuer burns, and fee collection) debits the balance slot, which holds only the available balance, so it can never spend reserved funds and needs no reservation read. A debit above the available balance reverts with TIP-20's `InsufficientBalance(available, amount, token)`. Capture and charge release the hold back to the available balance first and never pay more than it.

### Compliance burns

`burnBlocked` and `burnAt` are the token administrator's seizure paths and ignore reservations: holds are the owner's commitments and must not shield funds from a compliance burn. Before such a burn of `amount` above the owner's available balance, up to `amount - available` moves from `reserved` back to the available balance, so the burn can take reserved funds. A burn above `balanceOf` still reverts `InsufficientBalance`. The holds keep their amounts, so `reserved` can then be below the sum of the owner's live holds; the difference is recorded per owner and token as `unbacked`, with a burn counter (`epoch`) that each hold copies when placed. Holds settle against what remains: `release`, `releaseExpired`, `adjust` down and `capture` remove the hold's amount, and a hold placed before the latest burn takes it from `unbacked` first and only the rest from `reserved`, so the reservation behind other holds (including holds placed after the burn) stays in place; and a capture pays only the available balance, with any rest becoming debt within the cap or reverting `DebtCapExceeded` as in [Paying](#paying). Limit accounting is unaffected.

### TIP-20 debt-first credit (T15)

After any TIP-20 credit to an account (transfers, mints, receive-guard releases), if the account owes debt in that token, `min(debt, amount)` moves to the creditor with a TIP-20 `Transfer(owner, creditor, repaid)` and the precompile emits `DebtRepaid`. The repayment credit does not itself repay the creditor's own debt. For a credit to a TIP-1022 virtual address the repayment follows the forwarding `Transfer(virtual, master)`.

To find debtors without a read per credit, the precompile counts, per account, the tokens in which it owes debt, in spare bytes of the account's TIP-1028 receive-policy configuration slot in the TIP-403 registry (`reservationDebts`, a `uint32` after the existing fields). Every TIP-20 credit already reads that slot for the receive-policy check, so a credit consults the account's position only when the count is non-zero. The count rises when an account's debt in a token goes from zero to non-zero and falls when it returns to zero; setting a receive policy preserves it.

A repayment is a transfer from the owner to the creditor and obeys the same rules: it happens only while the token is not paused, the token's TIP-403 policy authorizes the owner as sender and the creditor as recipient, and the creditor's TIP-1028 receive policy admits the owner. Otherwise the repayment is skipped: the credit completes and stays with the owner, and the debt is unchanged until a later credit can repay it. Skipping rather than reverting keeps a creditor's policy from blocking all credits to its debtor. TIP-20 reward claims and fee refunds are not credits for this purpose: fee refunds run outside TIP-1060 storage-credit metering, where writing the creditor's balance slot would let it earn unbacked storage credits. Funds that become available by releasing or partially capturing a hold are not credits either: an owner in debt can spend them.

### Activation

The precompile exists and the TIP-20 hooks run only from T15, on Tempo L1. Zones neither expose the precompile nor run the hooks. Before T15 no TIP-20 operation reads reservation state, so gas and behavior are unchanged.

## Gas

The precompile charges standard calldata input cost and storage costs. An account position is one slot; an authorization is five slots (token and amount, payee, expiry and burn epoch, captured and refunded, key and limit period end, counted); the third is written only on settlement and the last two only when an access key spends on the authorization. A hold also reads the owner's shortfall slot, which only compliance burns write. Under TIP-1000 each new slot costs 250,000 gas, partly recoverable through TIP-1060 storage credits; a hold creates up to five new slots the first time an owner uses a token and id. Limit accounting adds the keychain's key and limit-row reads and one limit-row write per spend or give-back.

From T15 a TIP-20 debit reads no reservation state, since the balance slot already excludes reserved funds. A credit re-reads the recipient's receive-policy slot, which the receive-policy check loaded earlier in the call (a warm SLOAD, 100 gas); only recipients with debt add the position, creditor, pause, TIP-403 and receive-policy reads and the repayment writes. Fee affordability checks read only the balance slot. For an account with no reservations or debt, a transfer therefore costs about 100 gas more than before T15. `balanceOf` adds one read of the position slot.

Moving held funds out of the balance slot keeps TIP-20 balance changes commutative (`Sinc`/`Sdec`) for parallel block building: debits need no exact read. A recipient's receive-policy slot changes only through policy updates and when its debt count changes, so concurrent credits to recipients without debt read an unchanged value and replay without conflict; credits to a recipient with debt each write its position, so they conflict with one another.

### Alternatives considered

- **Reading the position on every debit and credit.** The earlier design kept reserved funds in the balance slot and checked `balance - reserved` on each debit and the debt on each credit: about 4,200 gas and two cold reads more per transfer for every account, which slowed TIP-20 execution benchmarks by 10 to 20%.
- **Lazy repayment.** Credits stop repaying debt, and debt is collected later. It saves the credit-side read too, but debt stays outstanding while the debtor receives funds; the debtor count gives the same saving while keeping repayment on credit.
- **Flag bits in the balance slot.** Reading a flag from the balance slot on every transfer turns commutative balance updates into exact reads, so concurrent payments to one account conflict, and it breaks the underflow check in `sdec`.

## Interfaces

Address: `0x5245535600000000000000000000000000000000` (ASCII "RESV").

```solidity
interface IReservations {
    struct Hold { address token; address payee; uint128 amount; uint64 expiresAt; }

    function hold(address token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, uint64 expiresAt, address payee) external;
    function adjust(bytes32 id, uint128 amount) external;
    function capture(bytes32 id, uint128 amount) external;
    function charge(address token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, address payee) external;
    function release(bytes32 id) external;
    function setDebtCap(address creditor, address token, uint128 cap) external;
    function releaseExpired(address owner, bytes32 id) external;
    function refund(address owner, bytes32 id, uint128 amount) external;

    /// Zero struct without a record; a settled record returns token and payee with zero amount and expiry.
    function getHold(address owner, bytes32 id) external view returns (Hold memory);
    function reserved(address owner, address token) external view returns (uint128);
    function available(address owner, address token) external view returns (uint128);
    /// Creditor is zero when there is no debt.
    function debtOf(address owner, address token) external view returns (address creditor, uint128 amount);
    function debtCap(address owner, address creditor, address token) external view returns (uint128);
    function captured(address owner, bytes32 id) external view returns (uint128 captured, uint128 refunded);

    event HoldPlaced(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, address indexed token, address payee, uint128 amount, uint64 expiresAt);
    event HoldAdjusted(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, address indexed token, uint128 amount);
    event HoldReleased(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, address indexed token);
    event HoldCaptured(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, address indexed token, uint128 amount, uint128 debt);
    event Charged(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, address indexed token, address payee, uint128 amount, uint128 debt);
    event Refunded(address indexed owner, bytes32 indexed id, uint128 amount);
    event DebtRepaid(address indexed owner, address indexed token, address indexed creditor, uint128 amount);

    error HoldExists(); error HoldNotFound(); error HoldNotExpired(); error InsufficientAvailable();
    error RefundExceedsCapture(); error DebtCapExceeded(); error PolicyForbids(); error Unauthorized(); error InvalidAmount();
}
```

`PolicyForbids()`, `Unauthorized()` and `InvalidAmount()` share selectors with the TIP-20 errors of the same name; TIP-20 validation failures inside these calls (`InvalidToken`, `InvalidRecipient`, `ContractPaused`, `InsufficientBalance`) surface as TIP-20 errors.

Storage layout (precompile slots in declaration order):

| Slot | Field | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | positions | `mapping(owner => mapping(token => {uint128 reserved; uint128 debt}))` |
| 1 | creditors | `mapping(owner => mapping(token => address))` |
| 2 | debtCaps | `mapping(owner => mapping(creditor => mapping(token => uint128)))` |
| 3 | authorizations | `mapping(owner => mapping(id => {address token; uint96 held; address payee; uint64 expiresAt; uint32 burnEpoch; uint96 captured; uint96 refunded; address keyId; uint64 limitPeriodEnd; uint96 counted}))` |
| 4 | shortfalls | `mapping(owner => mapping(token => {uint128 unbacked; uint32 epoch}))` |

The TIP-403 registry's per-account receive-policy configuration slot gains `uint32 reservationDebts` after its existing fields (see [TIP-20 debt-first credit](#tip-20-debt-first-credit-t15)).

# Compatibility

Before T15 nothing changes. From T15, accounts that never call the precompile behave as before except for about 100 gas per TIP-20 credit and one more read in `balanceOf`. Wallets and indexers should show `available` rather than `balanceOf` as spendable for accounts with holds; `balanceOf` still includes reserved funds, and holds emit no `Transfer`. Indexers tracking balances from `Transfer` events stay correct: repayments emit a `Transfer` to the creditor. A compliance burn can lower `reserved` without a reservations event; indexers read `reserved` after `BurnBlocked` and `BurnAt`.

From T15 every fee affordability check uses the available balance, which is the balance slot, matching fee collection: the fallback fee-token choice, the transaction validity check against the maximum fee, the RPC gas allowance, and transaction pool admission and maintenance. The pool re-checks a fee payer's pending transactions when `HoldPlaced` or `HoldAdjusted` names it and the fee token; other operations that lower the available balance emit a TIP-20 `Transfer` from the owner.

# Observability

Every state change emits one of the events above: holds placed, adjusted, released, captured (with the debt created), charges (with debt), refunds, and each debt repayment with its creditor. Access key spending under a card mandate is visible through the existing `AccessKeySpend` event.

# Threat Model

| Actor | Controls | Enforcing check | On compromise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Issuer access key | hold, adjust, capture, charge, release on the cardholder account | TIP-1011 call scope, per-token periodic spending limit counting open holds, capture excess and charges, debt cap | Commits (holds plus payments) up to the limit per period, into debt only up to the cap set by the root key; cannot act on another key's holds |
| Cardholder root key | debt caps, all funds | none beyond key ownership | Already controls all funds |
| Payee | refunds | caller must be the record's payee; refunds capped by captured | Can only return funds |
| Anyone | releaseExpired | expiry | Can only release expired holds |

The most consequential attack is a compromised issuer key draining the cardholder: captures and charges are bounded by the key's spending limit and by the root-key debt cap, and the key cannot change either. Holds consume the limit, so a compromised key can lock at most its remaining limit per period, until holds expire or the root key releases them; holds cannot move funds by themselves. Releases and refunds return headroom only to the key that placed the authorization and only within the period it was counted in, so a key cannot recycle the same allowance across periods. Holds cannot shield funds from token administrators: compliance burns (`burnBlocked`, `burnAt`) take reserved funds, and captures and charges re-check TIP-403 at settlement, so a payee or owner blocked after a hold was placed cannot be paid. Debt repayments obey pause, TIP-403 and the creditor's receive policy, so debt cannot move funds the token's policy forbids. Holds last at most 31 days.

# Invariants

- For every owner and token, `reserved` never exceeds the sum of the amounts of the owner's live holds in that token, and equals it less the owner's `unbacked` amount, which only compliance burns raise.
- `debt <= debtCap(owner, creditor, token)` at the time each debt increase is made, and debt has at most one creditor per owner and token; `creditor == 0` exactly when `debt == 0`.
- For every record, `refunded <= captured`.
- Within one limit period, an access key's remaining limit never exceeds its maximum, and amounts given back to it never exceed what the same authorization counted against it in that period.
- For every token, the sum over accounts of the balance slot plus `reserved` equals `totalSupply`: holds move funds between the two without minting or burning.
- An account's `reservationDebts` equals the number of tokens in which its `debt` is non-zero.
- Every TIP-20 credit to an account with debt pays `min(debt, amount)` to the creditor before the account can spend it, unless the token is paused or the token's or creditor's policy forbids the repayment, in which case the debt is unchanged.
- No live hold expires more than `MAX_HOLD_DURATION` after it was placed.
- Captures and charges never transfer more than the available balance at the time of payment; the rest is debt.
