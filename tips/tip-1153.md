# TIP-1153: Reservation Core

## Abstract

A precompile at `0x5245535600000000000000000000000000000000` keeps the parts of a payment that must be atomic with TIP-20 balances: holds, captures, charges, refunds, pledges and liens. A hold moves part of an owner's balance out of its TIP-20 balance slot and reserves it for a payee until a hard expiry; a capture pays the payee from it, up to a tolerance the owner set, and a release returns the rest. Records are placed by the owner or by a **holder**, a contract the owner gives a spend budget for a token. Every reservation is a two-slot record keyed by `(owner, placer, id)` and deleted when it settles; an owner's first live reservation in a token also writes a position slot, deleted with the last one. The core never pays more than a record backs or the owner holds: there is no credit, overdraft or debt in the protocol. Program logic (who may spend, limits, multi-party splits, refund bookkeeping, credit) lives in holder contracts.

## Motivation

Many payments need funds set aside before the final amount is known, without moving them out of the payer's account:

- **Authorization and capture.** A card network, a fuel pump or an EV charger authorizes an amount, then captures the final one, which may be a little higher (a tip) or lower.
- **Deposits.** A rental or hotel holds a deposit for the length of a stay and releases it, or captures part of it, at the end.
- **Capped recurring payments.** A subscription or usage-billing contract charges within a budget per period that the payer set.
- **Escrow and collateral in place.** A marketplace keeps a buyer's payment set aside until delivery; a lender or merchant acquirer keeps a reserve pledged without taking custody of it.

Holding funds in place keeps custody with the owner, leaves everything not committed spendable, and needs no float in an intermediary. Everything else a payment program does changes often and differs per program, so it belongs in contracts; the protocol bounds what such a contract, or a key, can take from an owner's balance, and makes every movement follow the token's policy.

## Assumptions

- A holder is trusted up to its budget: what it may spend per period plus what it may hold at once. A buggy or compromised holder can take at most that, and holds and pledges it placed end at their expiry (31 days for a hold, 400 days for a pledge).
- Budgets, capture tolerances and owner pledges need the owner's root key; an access key cannot change them.
- Each placer's ids are its own namespace. A record's id is free again once it settles, so a placer that needs ids to stay unique (one per purchase or order) keeps that rule in its own storage.
- The core keeps no state for a settled record, so it neither bounds refunds by what was captured nor stops an id being reused. Placers carry both duties; see Placer obligations.

# Specification

## Records

A record is a hold, a pledge or a lien, stored in two slots keyed by `(owner, placer, id)`:

| Slot | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| head | `token` (20 bytes), `held` (u64, still reserved), `expiresAt` (u32; 0 only for a lien without expiry) |
| terms | `payee` (20 bytes), `captureLimit` (u64, what captures may still pay), `toleranceBps` (u16), `kind` (u8) |

The record exists while `token` is non-zero. A final capture, a release, an expired release, and a pledge or lien seized or reduced to nothing delete both slots. Amounts are u128 in the ABI and must fit 64 bits (`InvalidAmount`).

| Kind | Placed by | Payee | Expiry | Adjust | Capture | Release |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| hold (1) | owner (`hold`) or holder (`holdFrom`) | the payee named | `now < expiresAt <= now + 31 days` | placer | placer or payee | placer or payee |
| pledge (2) | owner (`pledge`, root key) or the authority as holder (`pledgeFrom`) | authority | `now < expiresAt <= now + 400 days` | payee, down only | payee (seizure) | payee |
| lien (3) | holder of the token's `LIEN_ROLE`, root key | authority (the placer) | 0 (none) or in the future | payee, down only | payee, root key, with the role (seizure) | payee |

From `expiresAt`, `adjust` and `capture` revert `HoldExpired`; anyone may call `releaseExpired` (`HoldNotExpired` before). Calls name the record by owner, placer and id; a missing record reverts `HoldNotFound`, an occupied id `HoldExists`, any other caller `Unauthorized`.

## Budgets

`approveHolder(holder, token, limit, period)` (owner's root key) sets one slot per (owner, holder, token): `{limit u64, spent u64, outstanding u64, period u32, periodStart u32}`.

- `outstanding` is what the holder's live holds and pledges reserve. Placing or raising one draws on it; releasing, reducing or expiring returns to it.
- `spent` is what the holder's captures, charges and seizures paid in the current period. It is never returned. A capture moves what it pays from the hold out of `outstanding` into `spent`; a capture beyond the hold and a charge add to `spent` directly.
- Every draw needs `spent + outstanding + draw <= limit` (`BudgetExceeded`). A capture's excess beyond its hold needs the same room, even from a revoked holder.
- With `period > 0`, `spent` resets to 0 once `now >= periodStart + period`, and `periodStart` advances by whole periods. With `period = 0` the budget is a lifetime one. Changing `period` restarts it at `now`.
- `limit = 0` revokes: no new holds, raises, charges or pledges (`Unauthorized`). Existing holds still capture within what they hold, release and expire, and pledges still seize and release.
- `holdFrom`, `pledgeFrom` and a holder's `charge` check the caller's budget (`Unauthorized`, then `BudgetExceeded`) before the owner's payment policy and available balance, so a caller the owner never approved learns nothing about either.

## Holds

- `hold(request)` places on the caller's own balance; `holdFrom(owner, request)` places on `owner`'s balance within the caller's budget.
- The payee resolves through the address registry (TIP-1022); the payment check below must pass for owner to payee.
- A hold reserves `amount`, which must be available (`InsufficientAvailable` otherwise).
- `captureLimit = amount * (10000 + toleranceBps) / 10000`, with the tolerance the owner set for that payee and token at placement (default 0). Changing the tolerance later does not change live holds.
- An owner-placed hold charges the signing access key (TIP-1011) its full capture limit, tolerance included, since captures need no further key check. A holder's hold draws `amount` on its budget.
- `adjust(owner, placer, id, amount)` sets what a hold reserves. Raising it needs the increase available, rechecks the payment policy, draws the increase on the budget, and raises `captureLimit` to `amount * (1 + tolerance)` if that is higher, charging an owner's key for the raise. Lowering it returns the difference.

## Capture tolerance

`setCaptureTolerance(payee, token, toleranceBps)` (owner's root key) sets, in one slot per (owner, payee, token), how far captures of the owner's new holds to that payee may exceed the held amount. The payee resolves as a hold's does, and may not be the owner (`Unauthorized`). The tolerance is copied into each hold when it is placed. It is what lets a payment authorized at X settle at a final amount up to X plus a margin, such as a tip, a fuel top-up or a currency move, without a new authorization. The part beyond the hold is paid from the owner's free balance at capture.

## Capture

`capture(owner, placer, id, amount, isFinal)` pays the payee:

1. `amount <= captureLimit` (`CaptureLimitExceeded`); `amount = 0` only with `isFinal`.
2. The part up to `held` comes from the hold; the rest from the owner's available balance. If the available balance does not cover the rest, the capture reverts `InsufficientAvailable` and nothing moves.
3. `captureLimit` drops by `amount`. A final capture releases what the hold still reserves and deletes the record.

A pledge or lien seizure pays only what the record still backs; seizing all of it deletes the record.

## Payment policy

Every movement out of an owner's balance (capture, charge, seizure) and every refund goes through one TIP-20 internal check: the token is not paused, the recipient is a valid direct recipient other than the sender, the TIP-403 policy authorizes the sender and the recipient, and the recipient's TIP-1028 receive policy accepts the sender. Placing a hold, raising it and placing a pledge run the same check. A failure reverts `PolicyForbids`; releases never check policy. Two exemptions waive only the sender's TIP-403 authorization:

- **A hold's held part.** A capture paid entirely from what a hold still backs pays its own payee, fixed when the hold was placed under the policy then in force. After a compliance burn took part of the hold, the part it took no longer counts: a capture that would draw on the owner's free balance in its place is a new payment and is checked. A payee that delivered against an authorization is paid after the owner is blocked; anything beyond the hold, and every charge, pledge seizure, new hold or raise from a blocked owner, reverts. A hold lasts at most 31 days, which bounds this window; to stop it sooner, the placer or payee releases the hold, or the policy blocks the payee.
- **A lien seizure.** A lien is an issuer freeze; its authority seizes whatever the owner's status.

Compliance burns may take what holds reserve, never what pledges and liens hold. `burnBlocked` is one; `burnAt` is one only when the policy blocks the owner as a sender, and otherwise burns only the free balance (`InsufficientBalance` beyond it), so a bridge never burns reserved funds of an owner in good standing. The part taken is recorded as a shortfall on the owner's holds; ending holds consumes it first, so what remains still backs the others, while pledges and liens return or pay exactly what they hold. While a shortfall stands the owner places and raises no holds (`InsufficientAvailable`), so funds that arrive later never back the burned holds; pledges and liens, which the shortfall never reaches, still may. A capture from a hold returns its part to the balance first, shortfall included, and is exempt only when what came back covers the amount it pays.

## Charge and refund

- `charge(owner, token, id, amount, payee)`: the owner (charging its access key `amount`) or a holder (adding `amount` to `spent`). Pays only from the owner's available balance (`InsufficientAvailable` beyond it), after the budget and policy checks. No record; `id` is the placer's reference in `Charged`.
- `refund(owner, token, placer, id, amount)`: the caller pays `amount` of its own available balance back to `owner` (`InsufficientAvailable` beyond it) under the payment check from caller to owner, charging the caller's access key. `placer` and `id` name the hold it refunds, in `Refunded`.

## TIP-20 integration

- Reserving and unreserving go through TIP-20 internals that settle the owner's rewards first; reserved funds leave the opted-in supply and earn no rewards.
- Every debit reads only the balance slot, which excludes reserved funds; `balanceOf` adds `reserved`. Fee affordability checks use the balance slot. Transfers and mints are unchanged: a credit never reaches the core.

## Activation

The precompile and the TIP-20 hooks (`balanceOf` and compliance burns) activate at T16, on Tempo L1 and on zones in the same fork. Before T16 nothing changes. At the T16 boundary the core's account gets its native-precompile marker; both block executors apply this through one shared T16 step, so L1 and zones make it at the same boundary and never earlier.

## Zones

A zone runs the same core over its private balances:

- **Account.** From the T16 boundary, as on L1, the core's account gets its native-precompile marker, so its storage is never cleared as an empty account's.
- **Writes.** Every state-changing call needs T16 and the portal admin's transfer opt-in (`transfersEnabled`), as a zone-internal TIP-20 transfer does (`Unauthorized` otherwise).
- **Reads.** A view about an owner answers only the owner and the counterparties it chose: `getHold` the owner, placer and payee (anyone else reads an empty record); `reserved` and `available` the owner and a holder with a budget or outstanding holds (strangers read zero); `budget` its two parties; `captureTolerance` the owner and payee. Every other view reverts `Unauthorized`. `releaseExpired`, which anyone may call, refuses a caller other than the owner, placer or payee with `HoldNotFound` until the hold expires, as for a missing hold, so it reveals no live hold.
- **Settlement.** A capture pays the payee inside the zone; payees withdraw to L1 through the zone outbox.

## Placer obligations

Deleting a settled record is what keeps a hold at two slots (three when it is the owner's only live record in the token, counting the position slot) and lets later records reuse the storage credits it leaves (about 61,000 gas for a steady-state hold against about 551,000 with permanent slots). The core therefore keeps nothing about a record once it settles, and every placer that needs more must keep it in its own storage:

- **Replay protection.** An id is free again once its record settles, and `hold` with that id then places a new record. A placer that must never act twice on one payment (an authorization id, a subscription period) keeps its own used-id record, or derives each id from state it never repeats, such as a nonce, and rejects reuse before calling the core.
- **Link to the original payment.** The core forgets the token, payee and amounts of a settled record. A placer that refunds, disputes or reports against a payment stores that link itself, from its own calls or from `HoldCaptured` and `HoldReleased`, which carry owner, placer, id, token, payee and amounts.
- **Cumulative refund caps.** The core's `refund` is not bounded by what was captured, and cannot be after deletion. It moves only the caller's own balance to the owner, under the payment check. A refund over the captured amount takes nothing from the owner or anyone else; it is a payment the caller chose to make. A placer that pays refunds on behalf of others (a program refunding from its settlement balance) must enforce `refunded <= captured` per payment in its own storage before paying.

To keep any of this in the core instead, a record would keep a third slot (cumulative captured and refunded, and an occupied marker) after it settles. That costs one permanent new slot, 245,000 state gas, for every payment, and would be a new hardfork.

## Product constraints

These are deliberate limits of the core. Each is consensus code, so changing one means a hardfork.

- **No credit in the protocol.** Every payment is backed by a record's held part or the owner's free balance at the time it runs; a capture beyond its hold or a charge the free balance does not cover reverts. A program that needs to pay before the owner has funds (an overdraft, a credit line, a payment approved while the payer was offline) carries that risk itself: it pays from its own balance, or a lender's, and recovers the amount through its own contract, for example by charging the owner later within its budget or by seizing a pledge.
- **No default or liquidation.** The core has no notion of default. Collateral is taken only by its own authority: a pledge or lien is seized by calling `capture` on it, paying only what it still backs. Any default process (notices, collections, liquidating a pledge on a missed payment) belongs to the holder contract or the creditor, using these primitives.
- **Compliance exemptions.** Two payments skip only the sender's TIP-403 authorization, and nothing else is exempt. (a) A capture paid entirely from what a hold still backs pays its own payee after the owner is blocked, because the funds were reserved for that payee while the owner was authorized; the window is at most the hold's 31 days, and the placer, the payee or a recipient block ends it sooner. (b) A lien seizure pays its `LIEN_ROLE` authority whatever the owner's status, because a lien is an issuer freeze. Pause, recipient authorization and receive policies apply to both. Compliance burns of a blocked owner may take what holds reserve, recorded as a shortfall that ending holds absorb first; no new hold reserves funds until it is absorbed, so the exemption pays only funds reserved before the burn, and pledges and liens stay fully backed. `burnAt` of an owner in good standing never touches reserved funds. Removing exemption (a) would mean a blocked owner's authorized payments fail to pay payees who already delivered.

## Gas and storage

Storage per (owner, token): a position slot (`reserved` u128, shortfall flag, the part pledges and liens hold as u64) and a shortfall slot written only after compliance burns. Per (owner, holder, token): one budget slot. Per (owner, payee, token): one tolerance slot, only while non-zero. Per record: two slots, deleted on settlement.

New core slots per operation, enforced by `test_slots_per_operation` (`tempo-precompiles`):

| Operation | New core slots |
| --- | --- |
| `hold`, `holdFrom`, `pledge`, `pledgeFrom`, `lien` | 2, plus 1 for the position slot when the owner had nothing reserved in the token |
| `capture` part, `adjust` | 0 |
| `capture` final, `release`, `releaseExpired`, seizing a pledge or lien to zero | −2 (and −1 more when the owner's position empties) |
| `charge`, `refund` | 0 |
| `approveHolder`, `setCaptureTolerance` | 1 the first time (a zero tolerance deletes its slot) |

Transaction gas, enforced against per-operation budgets and snapshotted by `test_reservation_core_gas` (`tempo-evm`, T16, legacy transactions, intrinsic gas included). A new slot costs 245,000 state gas (TIP-1016); each slot a settlement deletes mints a TIP-1060 storage credit on the core's account, which a later record consumes instead, so in steady state, with records settling as fast as they are placed, a hold costs about 61,000 gas. An owner who keeps one hold at a time writes and deletes the position slot with every hold, so each of its holds takes three slots, all three from storage credits in steady state (`test_slots_single_hold_cycle`):

| --- | ---: | --- |

## Interface

```solidity
interface IReservationCore {
    struct HoldRequest {
        address token;
        bytes32 id;
        uint128 amount;
        uint64 expiresAt;
        address payee;
    }

    struct HoldRecord {
        address token;
        address payee;
        uint128 held;
        uint128 captureLimit;
        uint64 expiresAt;
        uint8 kind; // 0 none, 1 hold, 2 pledge, 3 lien
    }

    struct Budget {
        uint128 limit;
        uint128 spent;
        uint128 outstanding;
        uint32 period;
        uint64 periodStart;
    }

    function approveHolder(address holder, address token, uint128 limit, uint32 period) external;
    function setCaptureTolerance(address payee, address token, uint16 toleranceBps) external;

    function hold(HoldRequest calldata request) external;
    function holdFrom(address owner, HoldRequest calldata request) external;
    function pledge(address authority, address token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, uint64 expiresAt) external;
    function pledgeFrom(address owner, address token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, uint64 expiresAt) external;
    function lien(address owner, address token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, uint64 expiresAt) external;

    function adjust(address owner, address placer, bytes32 id, uint128 amount) external;
    function capture(address owner, address placer, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, bool isFinal) external;
    function release(address owner, address placer, bytes32 id) external;
    function releaseExpired(address owner, address placer, bytes32 id) external;
    function charge(address owner, address token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, address payee) external;
    function refund(address owner, address token, address placer, bytes32 id, uint128 amount) external;

    function getHold(address owner, address placer, bytes32 id) external view returns (HoldRecord memory);
    function reserved(address owner, address token) external view returns (uint128);
    function available(address owner, address token) external view returns (uint128);
    function budget(address owner, address holder, address token) external view returns (Budget memory);
    function captureTolerance(address owner, address payee, address token) external view returns (uint16);

    event HolderApproved(address indexed owner, address indexed holder, address indexed token, uint128 limit, uint32 period);
    event CaptureToleranceSet(address indexed owner, address indexed payee, address indexed token, uint16 toleranceBps);
    event HoldPlaced(address indexed owner, address indexed placer, address indexed token, bytes32 id, uint8 kind, address payee, uint128 amount, uint128 captureLimit, uint64 expiresAt);
    event HoldAdjusted(address indexed owner, address indexed placer, address indexed token, bytes32 id, uint128 held, uint128 captureLimit);
    event HoldCaptured(address indexed owner, address indexed placer, address indexed token, bytes32 id, address payee, uint128 amount, bool isFinal);
    event HoldReleased(address indexed owner, address indexed placer, address indexed token, bytes32 id, uint128 amount, bool expired);
    event Charged(address indexed owner, address indexed charger, address indexed token, bytes32 id, address payee, uint128 amount);
    event Refunded(address indexed owner, address indexed payee, address indexed token, address placer, bytes32 id, uint128 amount);

    error HoldExists();
    error HoldNotFound();
    error HoldExpired();
    error HoldNotExpired();
    error InsufficientAvailable();
    error BudgetExceeded();
    error CaptureLimitExceeded();
    error PolicyForbids();
    error Unauthorized();
    error InvalidAmount();
    error InvalidExpiry();
}
```

# Compatibility

Before T16 nothing changes. From T16, accounts that never use the core behave as before except for one more read in `balanceOf` and in compliance burns. Wallets should show `available` as spendable; holds emit no `Transfer`, and payments do. The transaction pool re-checks a fee payer's pending transactions when `HoldPlaced` or `HoldAdjusted` names it as owner (topic 1) with the token (topic 3), since only those lower a balance slot without a `Transfer`.

# Observability

Every state change emits one event. Every record event indexes owner, placer and token, so indexers rebuild live records from events.

# Threat Model

| Actor | Controls | Bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holder | `holdFrom`, `adjust`, `capture`, `release`, `charge`, `pledgeFrom` for owners that budgeted it | `spent + outstanding <= limit`, with `spent` never returned within a period; captures within each hold's limit and the owner's available balance; holds end within 31 days and pledges within 400 |
| Owner access key | `hold`, `adjust`, `capture`, `release`, `charge` on its own account | TIP-1011 limit, charged each hold's full capture limit and each charge; cannot change budgets, tolerances or pledge |
| Owner root key | budgets, capture tolerances, pledges | Already controls all funds |
| Payee | `capture`, `release` of holds to it; `refund` | Captures within each hold's limit and the owner's available balance |
| Pledge authority | reduce, release, seize of its pledges | What was pledged, under the payment check, until expiry |
| Lien authority | `lien`, reduce, release, seize | `LIEN_ROLE` at placement and seizure, root key; seizure pays only itself |
| Other placers and strangers | nothing on records they did not place or receive | Ids are per placer; every write checks its caller |
| Anyone | `releaseExpired` | Expiry |

# Invariants

Checked after every step of random sequences across two owners, two holders (lifetime and periodic budgets), two payees with different capture tolerances that change during the run, two tokens, a lien authority and a stranger, covering every write, transfers, policy changes and time (`proptest_random_sequences_match_the_model`). Each call's result is compared with a plain model, including the exact error:

1. For every (account, token): balance slot + `reserved` = `balanceOf`, and the token's supply is unchanged by the core; only compliance burns reduce it.
2. `reserved(owner, token)` = Σ live records' `held`, less the shortfall after a compliance burn, which never exceeds Σ live holds' `held`. Burns run as `burnBlocked` and as a bridge's `burnAt`, of blocked and unblocked owners.
3. Every record matches the model; a settled record leaves no storage, and the core's non-zero slots are exactly 2 per live record plus live positions, budgets, non-zero tolerances and shortfalls.
4. A budget's `outstanding` = Σ its holder's live holds' and pledges' `held`, and `spent + outstanding <= limit` at every draw.
5. No balance slot goes negative: every payment is covered by a record's held part or the payer's free balance, and every tolerance read matches the model.
6. From `expiresAt` a record can only be released.
