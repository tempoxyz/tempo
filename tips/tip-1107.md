# TIP-1107: Configurable Accounts (Meta)

## Abstract

Configurable accounts let users change an account's owners and approval threshold without changing its address. Weighted owner signatures authorise transactions, key rotation and recovery. Access keys provide delegated spending with separate permissions. Users can create a new account or migrate an existing externally owned account (EOA) in place.

## Motivation

Users and treasuries need key rotation, recovery and shared control without moving funds or changing deposit addresses and application permissions. Separately granted access keys let devices or employees spend within their permissions without collecting owner approvals for every payment.

## Assumptions

- The components marked T14 activate together.

# Specification

## Account model

The design separates three things:

1. **Account identity:** the address, derived from the initial configuration or retained through EOA migration. For direct owner-authorised transactions, it is the sender and the caller seen by directly called contracts.
2. **Current ownership:** weighted owners, threshold, salt and version. Owners supply the full configuration and [signatures meeting the threshold](./tip-1114.md#authorization) to approve the entire transaction, including every call in a batch. They sign with secp256k1, P256 or WebAuthn keys.
3. **Delegated permission:** AccountKeychain grants with their own limits, scopes, expiry and counters. [TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md) lets configurable accounts authorise access keys and act as access keys for other accounts. Only the parent's direct authority manages its grants; delegates cannot replace its owners. Changing owners does not revoke these grants.

## Lifecycle

**Derive.** The user chooses the owners and approval threshold. The wallet builds the initial configuration and [derives the account address](./tip-1110.md#address-derivation). The account can receive funds before registration.

**Register.** [Registration](./tip-1114.md#onboarding) happens when the account first authorises a valid transaction included onchain, even if execution fails. [TIP-1108](./tip-1108.md#overview) stores the configuration hash in the account leaf before calls execute; later owner witnesses must match the current hash. Registration also activates [root-key disablement](./tip-1112.md#authority-check).

**Rotate.** Current owners meeting the approval threshold call [`updateConfig`](./tip-1109.md) to replace owners or the threshold. The hash changes and the version increases, invalidating old-version approvals. The address remains unchanged.

**Recovery example.** Alice's passkey has weight 2; her wallet provider and an independent recovery provider each have weight 1, with threshold 2. Alice can transact independently; neither provider can act alone.

If Alice loses access:

1. Alice creates a new passkey.
2. The providers complete their identity checks offchain.
3. Both providers sign an `updateConfig` transaction replacing her old passkey.
4. Once the update succeeds, Alice uses her new passkey with the same account address and funds.

Together, the providers can also approve spending or replace owners at any time.

**Migrate.** An existing account's original key calls [`upgradeAccount`](./tip-1113.md) to install a version-one configuration at the same address. Successful migration disables the key's independent protocol authority, except for [existing channel settlement](./tip-1112.md#protocol-precompiles).

## T14 scope boundaries

- Configurable accounts cannot have deployed EVM code or EIP-7702 delegation. Registration installs a protocol [code marker](./tip-1114.md#account-code) that answers only ERC-1271 `isValidSignature` and `onERC721Received`.
- Accounts have up to 48 owners. The threshold is 1–255 and must be reachable with at most eight owner signatures.
- One threshold governs direct owner-authorised spending and ownership changes. Recovery providers have no recovery-only permission.
- Owner approvals cannot use configurable-account or access-key signatures. A [configurable delegate's owners sign directly](./tip-1111.md#delegate-eligibility); its access keys cannot exercise the parent's grant.
- Timelocks and arbitrary programmable authorisation policies are outside this component set.
- [Configurable-account signatures](./tip-1114.md#supported-signature-contexts) are unsupported in subblocks, authorisation lists and separate fee-payer signatures.
- There is no [quorum permit or channel-voucher format](./tip-1112.md#overview). Stateless TIP-1020 `recover`/`verify` and [keychain-verification helpers](./tip-1114.md#on-chain-signature-verification) do not accept configurable witnesses.
- [Zones](./tip-1114.md#zones) do not support configurable accounts: they reject configurable-account signatures, owner updates and migration, and do not sync commitments from Tempo. Zones keep separate account state, so accepting these signatures without sync could admit removed owners. [TIP-1154](./tip-1154.md) proposes zone support by reading commitments from Tempo.
- Ownership changes do not propagate to other chains. This design provides no [cross-chain recovery for derived addresses](./tip-1110.md#cross-chain-recovery).

# Compatibility

These are the behaviors specified for T14 by the component TIPs. They do not change third-party application verification or imply that integrations already support configurable accounts.

| Integration | T14 specification |
|---|---|
| Wallets and SDKs | Configurable-account witnesses contain the full configuration and owner approvals. Wallets collect approvals for its version and check the current commitment before submission. [Witness construction](./tip-1114.md#compatibility). |
| Fee sponsorship | Separate fee payers use the existing secp256k1 signature. An account with a nonzero commitment cannot sponsor with its original key; configurable accounts can self-pay or receive sponsorship. [Fee-payer rules](./tip-1112.md#compatibility). |
| TIP-20 approvals | TIP-20 `permit` rejects an original-key signature when the owner's commitment is nonzero. T14 adds no quorum permit format; an account can instead call `approve` through a quorum-authorised transaction. Permit2 and other application contracts are unchanged; Permit2 signature transfers verify registered accounts through ERC-1271. [Permit rules](./tip-1112.md#compatibility). |
| Message signing and sign-in | TIP-1114 specifies the stateful `verifyConfigurableAccount` precompile. `ecrecover`, TIP-1020's `recover`/`verify`, and application sign-in checks are unchanged. Registered configurable accounts answer ERC-1271 `isValidSignature` through their [code marker](./tip-1114.md#account-code); unregistered derived accounts have no code and no ERC-1271 entry point until they register. [Verification rules](./tip-1114.md#on-chain-signature-verification). |
| Access-key records | TIP-1111 adds key type `3`, which `getKey` can return. Solidity callers compiled with enums limited to 0–2 revert on type `3`. A migrated delegate can retain its earlier stored type but must sign with its current quorum. [Grant rules](./tip-1111.md#compatibility). |
| Onchain keychain verification | `verifyKeychain` and `verifyKeychainAdmin` accept only primitive inner signatures; an inner `0x05` signature reverts with `InvalidFormat`. Native transactions can use configurable delegates under TIP-1111. [Helper rules](./tip-1114.md#on-chain-signature-verification). |
| Payment channels | Channel `open` and nonzero `topUp` reject retired voucher signers. Retirement does not invalidate vouchers against existing deposits to the channel's fixed payee. [Channel rules](./tip-1112.md#protocol-precompiles). |
| Zones | Until [TIP-1154](./tip-1154.md) activates, a derived account cannot move funds sent to its address on a zone, and a migrated account's original key keeps full authority over its zone funds. This is a known limitation. Tooling must not send funds to zones for configurable accounts and must keep a migrated account's original key until its zone funds are withdrawn. |

# Observability

Check the commitment at the selected state; an old event does not prove current ownership. [TIP-1108](./tip-1108.md#observability) defines registration evidence and the `AccountUpgraded` and `ConfigurableAccountConfigUpdated` events; [TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md#observability) defines grant evidence.

# Threat Model

| Actor | Trust boundary |
|---|---|
| Current owners / recovery providers | A [current quorum](./tip-1114.md#authorization) can spend, replace owners and manage grants; recovery providers have no separate recovery-only authority. |
| Original account key / application verifiers | [TIP-1112](./tip-1112.md#threat-model) defines where retiring the original root removes authority and where application verification remains independent. |
| Delegates / their future owners | Parent owner rotation does not revoke an existing grant, so a compromised delegate remains authorised. Rotating a configurable delegate transfers that grant to its new quorum. [Grant rules](./tip-1111.md#owner-rotation-and-revocation). |

# Invariants

- Registration and owner rotation preserve a derived address; migration preserves an existing EOA's address.
- Direct transactions and owner changes follow the current owner configuration. Delegated access cannot authorize owner rotation.
- Owner rotation and migration preserve existing access-key grants, their permissions, and spending state until separately revoked.
- A committed configuration replaces independent original-root authority on Tempo, except for funded-channel voucher settlement under TIP-1112.
