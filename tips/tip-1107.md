# TIP-1107: Configurable Accounts (Meta)

## Abstract

Configurable accounts let users change an account's owners and approval threshold without changing its address. They support key rotation and account recovery, weighted multisig ownership, and delegated spending through access keys.

This meta TIP describes the feature and its components for the initial phase of shipping Configurable accounts. Component TIPs define interfaces, encodings, validation rules, and gas charges.

## Motivation

Users and treasuries need multisig control, key recovery, and key rotation without changing their account address. Configurable accounts let authorised owners approve transactions and replace signing keys while preserving balances, deposit addresses, and application permissions.

Access keys let users manage access across devices and regain access through a backup admin key. Configurable accounts add the ability to replace owner keys, supporting key rotation and recovery without changing the account address.

## Assumptions

- Phase 1 introduces new configurable accounts with weighted ownership, key rotation, and access-key support. Its component TIPs activate together.
- Later iterations will add further cost optimisations, existing-account migration, and root-key disablement.

# Specification

## Ownership and authorisation

Each account has a set of owners with assigned weights and an approval threshold. Owners sign with secp256k1, P256, or WebAuthn keys.

Owners authorise a transaction by supplying signatures and the full account configuration: owners and weights, approval threshold, salt, and version. Tempo verifies the signatures and checks that the signing owners' combined weight meets the threshold.

The owners' signatures approve the entire transaction, including every call in a batch. The configurable account is the transaction sender, origin, and top-level caller.

[TIP-1114](./tip-1114.md) defines authorisation and its limits.

## Account state

[TIP-1108](./tip-1108.md) stores a 32-byte hash of the account configuration in the account leaf. Keeping the hash in the account leaf avoids a separate storage lookup.

Before registration, Tempo checks that the supplied initial configuration derives the account address. After registration, it checks that the supplied configuration hashes to the stored value.

## Account creation and onboarding

During onboarding, the user chooses the owners and approval threshold. The wallet builds the initial configuration and [derives the account address](./tip-1110.md#address-derivation).

The account can receive funds before its configuration is registered. [Registration](./tip-1114.md#initial-use) happens when the account first authorises a valid transaction that is included onchain, even if its execution fails.

## Key rotation and recovery

Current owners whose combined weight meets the approval threshold can call [`updateConfig`](./tip-1109.md) to replace the owners and approval threshold. The update increments the configuration version, invalidating signatures for earlier versions. The account address and existing access-key permissions remain unchanged.

Alice can configure her account so she can act alone, while two recovery providers must act together.

| Owner | Weight |
|---|---|
| Alice's passkey | 2 |
| Wallet provider | 1 |
| Independent recovery provider | 1 |

With an approval threshold of **2**, Alice can transact independently. If she loses access, the two providers can jointly approve replacing her passkey. Neither provider can act alone.

1. Alice creates a new passkey.
2. The providers complete their identity checks offchain.
3. Both providers sign an `updateConfig` transaction replacing her old passkey.
4. Once the update succeeds, Alice uses her new passkey with the same account address and funds.

The providers must already be authorised owners before Alice loses access. Identity checks determine whether they sign; Tempo verifies their signatures.

## Access keys

[TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md) lets configurable accounts authorise access keys and act as access keys for other accounts. For example, three owners of a three-of-five treasury jointly authorise an employee's access key with a $500 spending limit. The employee can then make payments within that limit without further owner approvals.

## Cross-chain transfer recovery

[TIP-1110](./tip-1110.md) derives addresses using CREATE2. On supported destination chains, eligible initial owners can deploy the canonical recovery wallet at the same address to retrieve mistaken transfers. On Tempo, the address is a native configurable account.

## Components and phases

Phases group features; they do not commit to upgrade dates.

- **Phase 1:** new configurable accounts, multisig ownership, key rotation and recovery, and access-key support.
- **Phase 2:** account-root and access-key cost optimisations.
- **Phase 3:** existing-account migration and root-key disablement.

## Scope boundaries

- Configurable accounts cannot have deployed EVM code or EIP-7702 delegation.
- The approval threshold is limited to 1–8, and each set of owner approvals contains at most eight signatures. An account can have up to 48 owners.
- Owner weights determine whether approvals meet the threshold; they do not restrict owners to specific functions. Owners meeting the approval threshold can authorise both spending and ownership changes. There is no recovery-only role.
- Nested multisig owners are excluded to bound signature-verification work and prevent denial-of-service attacks through recursive validation.
- Access-key signatures cannot count towards the owner threshold. Configurable delegates under TIP-1111 use a separate, bounded validation path.
- Timelocks and arbitrary programmable authorisation policies are outside the current component set.

# Tooling

Wallets and SDKs must support account creation, independent collection of owner signatures, key rotation, and recovery. The component TIPs define the detailed tooling requirements.

# Observability

Component TIPs define the events and transaction data used to track account creation, ownership changes, and migration.

# Invariants

- Ownership changes preserve the account address and existing access-key permissions.
- Signatures for an earlier configuration version become invalid after rotation.
- Access keys cannot change the parent account's owners.
