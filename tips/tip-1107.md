# TIP-1107: Configurable Accounts (Meta)

## Abstract

Configurable accounts let users change an account's owners and approval threshold without changing its address. They support key rotation and account recovery, weighted multisig ownership, and delegated spending through access keys.

This meta TIP describes the account model, user journeys and proposed implementation phases. Component TIPs define the detailed protocol rules.

## Motivation

Users and treasuries need multisig control, key recovery, and key rotation without changing their account address. Configurable accounts let authorised owners approve transactions and replace signing keys while preserving balances, deposit addresses, and application permissions.

Access keys let users manage access across devices and regain access through a backup admin key. Configurable accounts add the ability to replace owner keys, supporting key rotation and recovery without changing the account address.

## Assumptions

- Proposed Phase 1 introduces new configurable accounts with weighted ownership, key rotation, and access-key support. Its component TIPs activate together.
- Later iterations will add further cost optimisations, existing-account migration, and root-key disablement.

# Specification

## Ownership and authorisation

A configurable account can have one owner or several. A single-owner account can replace its signing key without changing its address. Each owner has an assigned weight, and the account has an approval threshold. Owners sign with secp256k1, P256, or WebAuthn keys.

Owners authorise a transaction by supplying signatures and the full account configuration: owners and weights, approval threshold, salt, and version. During transaction validation, the Tempo node verifies the owner signatures and checks that the signing owners' combined weight meets the approval threshold, before executing any calls.

The owners' signatures approve the entire transaction, including every call in a batch. The configurable account is the transaction sender, origin, and top-level caller.

[TIP-1114](./tip-1114.md) defines authorisation and its limits. Applications can also [verify messages signed by the account's owner quorum](./tip-1114.md#on-chain-signature-verification) against its current configuration. Rotation can invalidate outstanding approvals signed under an earlier configuration.

## Account state

[TIP-1108](./tip-1108.md) stores a 32-byte hash of the account configuration in the account leaf. Keeping the hash in the account leaf avoids a separate storage lookup.

For a new account, no configuration hash is stored yet, so the node checks that the supplied initial configuration derives the account address. Once the account is registered, the node hashes the supplied configuration and compares it with the stored hash. Owner rotation replaces this hash, allowing the owners and approval threshold to change while the account address stays the same.

## Account creation and onboarding

During onboarding, the user chooses the owners and approval threshold. The wallet builds the initial configuration and [derives the account address](./tip-1110.md#address-derivation).

The account can receive funds before its configuration is registered. [Registration](./tip-1114.md#initial-use) happens when the account first authorises a valid transaction that is included onchain, even if its execution fails.

## Key rotation and recovery

Current owners whose combined weight meets the approval threshold can call [`updateConfig`](./tip-1109.md) to replace the owners and approval threshold. The update increments the configuration version, invalidating signatures for earlier versions. The account address remains unchanged. In Phase 1, existing AccountKeychain access-key grants, permissions, and spending counters also remain unchanged. Grants whose signing authority is compromised or no longer trusted must be revoked explicitly.

Alice can configure her account so she can act alone, while two recovery providers must act together.

| Owner | Weight |
|---|---|
| Alice's passkey | 2 |
| Wallet provider | 1 |
| Independent recovery provider | 1 |

With an approval threshold of **2**, Alice can transact independently. If she loses access, the two providers can jointly approve replacing her passkey. Neither provider can act alone. Together, the two providers can also approve spending or replace owners at any time.

1. Alice creates a new passkey.
2. The providers complete their identity checks offchain.
3. Both providers sign an `updateConfig` transaction replacing her old passkey.
4. Once the update succeeds, Alice uses her new passkey with the same account address and funds.

The providers must already be authorised owners before Alice loses access. Identity checks determine whether they sign; Tempo verifies their signatures.

## Access keys

[TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md) lets configurable accounts authorise access keys and act as access keys for other accounts. For example, three owners of a three-of-five treasury jointly authorise an employee's access key with a limit of 500 pathUSD per 24-hour period. The employee can then make payments within that limit without further owner approvals.

Ordinary access keys act within their granted permissions. Admin access keys have unrestricted spending and key-management authority, including authorising and revoking other keys. Neither can change the parent account's owners.

The parent account and the admin key may each be primitive or configurable; a configurable signer uses its own direct owner quorum. [TIP-1049](./tip-1049.md) and [TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md) define the signing requirements.

## Cross-chain transfer recovery

[TIP-1110](./tip-1110.md) derives addresses using CREATE2. On supported destination chains, the canonical recovery wallet at the same address can retrieve transfers when the initial secp256k1 owners can meet the original approval threshold. P256 and WebAuthn owners do not contribute to that recovery threshold. Owner rotation on Tempo does not change this recovery authority. On Tempo, the address is a native configurable account.

- **Proposed Phase 1:** new configurable accounts, multisig ownership, key rotation and recovery, and access-key support.
- **Proposed Phase 2:** account-root and access-key cost optimisations.
- **Proposed Phase 3:** existing-account migration and root-key disablement.

## Scope boundaries

- Configurable accounts cannot have deployed EVM code or EIP-7702 delegation.
- The approval threshold is 1–255 and must be reachable with at most eight owner signatures. An account can have up to 48 owners.
- The same approval threshold applies to spending and ownership changes. Recovery providers configured as owners can therefore also approve spending when their combined weight meets the threshold; their authority cannot be limited to recovery.
- Nested multisig owners are excluded to bound signature-verification work and prevent denial-of-service attacks through recursive validation.
- Access-key signatures cannot count towards the owner threshold. Configurable delegates under TIP-1111 use a separate, bounded validation path.
- Timelocks and arbitrary programmable authorisation policies are outside the current component set.

Wallets and SDKs must support account creation, independent collection of owner signatures, key rotation, and recovery. The component TIPs define the detailed tooling requirements.

# Observability

Component TIPs define the events and transaction data used to track account creation, ownership changes, and access-key activity.

# Invariants

- Ownership changes preserve the account address.
- Phase 1 owner rotation preserves AccountKeychain access-key grants, permissions, and spending counters.
- Owner approvals must match the configuration and version at the transaction's position in the block.
- Access keys, including admin keys, cannot change the parent account's owners.
- Every configurable signer of an included transaction retains a nonzero configuration hash, even if execution fails.
- Owner updates follow execution rollback rules without undoing initial registration.
- Owner rotation on Tempo preserves cross-chain recovery authority for eligible initial owners.
