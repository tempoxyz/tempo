# TIP-1107: Configurable Accounts (Meta)

## Abstract

Configurable accounts let users replace owners and approval thresholds without changing their address. Weighted owners authorise transactions and recovery; access keys provide delegated spending.

**The central attack class is treating a recovered or claimed address as proof of current account authority.** Transaction authority requires a current quorum, or an active grant used by an authorised delegate. Primitive authority must pass the applicable root-retirement check; applications that omit it can leave standing allowances spendable by the old key.

## Motivation

Users and treasuries need key rotation, recovery and multisig control without moving funds or changing deposit addresses and application permissions. Separately granted access keys let devices or employees spend within their permissions without collecting owner approvals for every payment.

## Assumptions

- The Phase 1 components below activate together at T14. Migration depends on root-key disablement; delegated administration is removed by TIP-1137.
- Phase 2 adds post-quantum signatures and reduces access-key costs.

# Specification

## Account model

The design separates three things:

1. **Account identity:** the address, derived from the initial configuration or retained through EOA migration.
2. **Current ownership:** weighted owners, threshold, salt and version. The account leaf stores their configuration hash; each owner witness supplies the full configuration and approvals.
3. **Delegated permission:** AccountKeychain grants with their own limits, scopes, expiry and counters. Changing owners does not revoke these grants.

## Operations

**Create and register.** A wallet chooses owners and a threshold, then [derives the address](./tip-1110.md#address-derivation). It can receive funds before registration. The first valid included transaction the account authorises [registers its initial commitment](./tip-1114.md#onboarding), even if execution fails. Later witnesses must match the current stored commitment.

**Transact.** Owners use secp256k1, P256 or WebAuthn signatures. A [current quorum](./tip-1114.md#authorization) approves the whole transaction, including every call in a batch. The configurable address is the transaction sender and the caller seen by directly called contracts. Applications can use [stateful message verification](./tip-1114.md#on-chain-signature-verification).

**Rotate or recover.** The current quorum calls [updateConfig](./tip-1109.md) to replace owners or the threshold. The version increases, old-version approvals become invalid, and the address stays fixed. Removing a delegate's access requires explicit revocation. For example, Alice's passkey has weight 2 and two recovery providers each have weight 1, with threshold 2. Alice acts alone; the providers can jointly replace her lost key after offchain identity checks. Their quorum can also spend or replace owners without those checks.

**Migrate an existing account.** Its live primitive root calls [upgradeAccount](./tip-1113.md) to install a configuration at the same address. [TIP-1112](./tip-1112.md) then rejects independent root authority in protocol checks, subject to its funded-channel exception. Ordinary access-key grants survive; [TIP-1137](https://github.com/tempoxyz/tempo/pull/8204) treats pre-T14 admin keys as revoked.

**Delegate spending.** A configurable account can grant access or act as another account's delegate under [TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md). A three-of-five treasury can authorise an employee to spend 500 pathUSD per 24-hour period without further owner approvals. Only the parent's direct authority manages grants; delegates cannot replace its owners.

## Scope boundaries

- Configurable accounts cannot have deployed EVM code or EIP-7702 delegation.
- Accounts have up to 48 owners. The threshold is 1–255 and must be reachable with at most eight owner signatures.
- One threshold governs direct owner-authorised spending and ownership changes. Recovery providers have no recovery-only permission.
- Nested multisig owners and access-key owner approvals are excluded. Configurable delegates use TIP-1111's separate, bounded validation path.
- Timelocks and arbitrary programmable authorisation policies are outside this component set.

| Payment channels | A separate primitive key signs vouchers and survives owner rotation; retiring it preserves claims against existing deposits to the fixed payee, but blocks new channels and further funding. [Channel rules](./tip-1112.md#protocol-precompiles). |

| Other chains | Tempo owner changes do not affect foreign-chain authority; derived addresses have no recovery path in this design, while migrated EOAs retain their original key there. [Cross-chain recovery](./tip-1110.md#cross-chain-recovery). |
| Nodes, indexers and account-proof libraries | Account decoding gains an optional commitment field that must survive storage, proofs and historical-state restoration. [Account encoding](./tip-1108.md#compatibility). |

# Observability

Initial registration is visible in the included witness and account commitment, including after failed execution. Migration emits `AccountUpgraded`; rotation emits `ConfigurableAccountConfigUpdated`. Their writes and events roll back together on failure. Check the commitment at the selected state; an old event does not prove current ownership. [TIP-1108](./tip-1108.md#observability) and [TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md#observability) define configuration and grant evidence.

# Threat Model

Phase 1 assumes correct consensus, secure signatures and hashes, and no known primitive private key for an unregistered derived address. Its main boundary is claimed signing identity versus current authority, as stated above.

| Actor | Capability and trust boundary |
|---|---|
| Owners / recovery providers | A current quorum can spend, replace all owners and manage grants. Signature weight enforces the threshold; the protocol cannot distinguish legitimate approval from quorum compromise or provider collusion. |
| Original root before migration | Can spend, create ordinary grants or install different owners until migration succeeds. Ordering can let a compromised root win that race; submitting migration does not revoke its authority. |
| Retired-root holder | Can still produce valid primitive signatures. Native consumers reject their independent root authority using the current commitment, except funded-channel settlement to the fixed payee. Applications, Zones and other chains enforce their own authority rules as described in Compatibility. |
| Application / authentication verifier | Decides whether a signature authorises an action. It must verify the current quorum or reject retired bare roots; accepting `ecrecover` or a witness's claimed address alone can bypass the configured owners. |
| Wallet / key generator | Chooses configurations, presents calls and may handle the EOA recovery key. The protocol checks signed bytes, not the accuracy of the display, key custody or possession of replacement keys. A compromised wallet can solicit harmful approvals. |
| Delegates / their future owners | Can spend under the parent's stored grant. Expiry, call scopes and supported spending checks bound that grant; rotating the delegate changes who can use it, not its permissions. Parent rotation does not revoke it. |
| Protocol clients / precompiles | Trusted to check current authority in every signing role and preserve the commitment through execution, cleanup and state restoration. A missed check can admit a retired root; losing the field can restore it. |
| RPC / proof providers | Can withhold configurations or return stale state. A checked proof binds data to a chosen root, not to the latest canonical state. Wallets must select the state and recover a configuration matching its commitment. |
| Signature collectors / relayers / fee payers | Can withhold approvals, delay submission or decline sponsorship. They cannot change the signed operation, account or version without fresh approvals; submission and fee payment grant no owner authority. |
| Public submitters | Can send invalid witnesses without paying fees. Owner/signature limits and rejection of recursion bound work per attempt, but do not prevent repeated invalid submissions. |

Losing enough owner keys or the full configuration can prevent authorisation. Migrating or rotating owners does not clean up ordinary access grants or application allowances. TIP-1137 revokes pre-T14 admin keys independently of those operations.

# Invariants

- Ownership changes preserve the account address.
- Owner rotation preserves ordinary access-key grants, permissions and spending counters; TIP-1137's admin-key revocation still applies.
- Owner approvals match the configuration and version at the transaction's position in the block.
- Access keys cannot manage the parent's keys or change its owners.
- Successful migration retires independent protocol root authority, except funded-channel settlement under TIP-1112.
- Every configurable signer of an included transaction retains a nonzero commitment, even if execution fails.
- Owner updates follow execution rollback without undoing initial registration.
- Tempo ownership changes do not change authority on other chains or Zones, or make applications enforce retirement automatically.
