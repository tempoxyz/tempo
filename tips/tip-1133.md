# TIP-1133: OIDC RS256 Proof Circuit v1

## Abstract

This TIP defines scheme `0x01` of [TIP-1131](./tip-1131.md): a Groth16 circuit over BN254 proving that an RS256-signed OpenID Connect ID token, signed by a key with a given hash, identifies a user and commits to an access key or a message, without revealing the token. It specifies the public input, every constraint, the hashing rules shared with nodes and publishers, size limits, and the trusted setup.

## Motivation

Signing in with an auth provider, such as Google or Apple, ends with an ID token: a statement, signed by the provider, naming the user who signed in. To let that sign-in sign for a Tempo account, nodes need to check the token without it appearing onchain, since it identifies the user.

This circuit proves what nodes need to know about a token: that a provider key published onchain signed it, which account its user controls, and which access key or message its nonce commits to. Nothing else about the token is revealed.

### Success Criteria

[TIP-1130](./tip-1130.md#success-criteria) defines the feature's success criteria. For this circuit:

- at least two independent audits are complete, with no open critical or high findings;
- an automated check for under-constrained signals reports no findings;
- at least two parties other than the ceremony coordinator verify the setup transcript;
- the circuit, the node's public-input computation, and the publisher's hashes agree on every test vector.

## Assumptions

- Providers sign with RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5 and SHA-256 (RS256), 2048-bit moduli, and exponent 65537.
- ID tokens are compact JWS tokens whose payload is compact JSON, with no whitespace outside strings. `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, and `nonce` are top-level string members, and `iat` and `exp` are top-level integer members.
- Providers echo the client's `nonce` exactly, and users cannot choose member names in tokens.
- Poseidon with circomlib parameters is collision resistant, and Groth16 over BN254 is sound.
- At least one contributor to each phase of the trusted setup was honest.

# Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHOULD", and "MAY" are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119.

## Overview

The circuit proves that, for the public input:

1. an RSA key whose hash is `key_hash` signed a token;
2. the token's top-level `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, `nonce`, `iat`, and `exp` members are read unambiguously;
3. `issuer` and `address_seed` are derived from those members and a private salt;
4. the nonce commits to two values, `commit_a` and `commit_b`, and a private blinding value: an access key and expiry for ZK signatures, or a message for message signatures;
5. for ZK signatures, the expiry is no later than the token's `exp`.

TIP-1131 checks everything else: times against the block, the publisher's key list, and the access key's signature.

## Notation

- `F` is the BN254 scalar field, of size `BN254_SCALAR_FIELD` from TIP-1131.
- `Poseidon(x_1, ..., x_n)`, for `1 <= n <= 16`, is the circomlib Poseidon hash over `F`, with width `n + 1` and circomlib's round counts and constants.
- `be32(x)` is the 32-byte big-endian encoding of `x`.
- `base64url` is RFC 4648 section 5 encoding without padding.
- Strings are byte strings, and character literals are ASCII.

## Hashing

Nodes (TIP-1131) and publishers ([TIP-1132](./tip-1132.md)) use these definitions too:

```text
hash_bytes(b, max_len) = Poseidon(len(b), c_0, ..., c_{k-1})
    where k = ceil(max_len / 31),
          b is right-padded with zero bytes to 31k bytes,
          c_i is the big-endian integer of bytes [31i, 31i + 31)

normalize_iss(iss) = iss without one leading "https://", if present

issuer       = hash_bytes(normalize_iss(iss), 128)
key_hash     = hash_bytes(n as 256 big-endian bytes, 256)
address_seed = Poseidon(1, hash_bytes(sub, 64), hash_bytes(aud, 128), salt)
nonce        = base64url(be32(Poseidon(commit_a, commit_b, blinding)))
public_input = Poseidon(1, issuer, key_hash, address_seed, commit_a, commit_b, issued_at)
```

- `hash_bytes` requires `len(b) <= max_len`. Hashing the length keeps zero-padded strings distinct.
- `normalize_iss` gives Google's two issuer forms, `accounts.google.com` and `https://accounts.google.com`, the same `issuer`.
- The `1` in `address_seed` is a domain tag shared by every scheme in the OIDC namespace. The `1` in `public_input` is the scheme.
- `nonce` is always 43 characters, and `issued_at` is the token's `iat` value.

The nonce commits to one of two forms:

| Form | `commit_a` | `commit_b` | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signature | `access_key_id` | `valid_until` | ZK signatures ([TIP-1131](./tip-1131.md#verification)) |
| Message | `uint256(d) mod BN254_SCALAR_FIELD` | `MESSAGE_TAG` | Message signatures ([TIP-1131](./tip-1131.md#message-signatures)) |

`MESSAGE_TAG` is `2^64`, larger than any `valid_until`, so the two forms never produce the same public input. Both forms share one circuit and one trusted setup.

## Inputs

`public_input` is the only public input.

| Private input | Description |
|---|---|
| `signed_input` | `base64url(header) || "." || base64url(payload)`, the bytes the provider signed, zero-padded to `MAX_SIGNED_INPUT_LEN` |
| `signed_input_len` | The unpadded length, `L` |
| `signature` | The RSA signature `s` |
| `modulus` | The RSA modulus `n` |
| Member offsets | Where each required member starts in the decoded payload |
| `salt` | A field element from the wallet operator's salt service |
| `blinding` | A random field element chosen by the wallet |
| `commit_a`, `commit_b` | The values the nonce commits to, in either form |
| `issued_at` | As in TIP-1131 |
| `issuer`, `key_hash`, `address_seed` | The values hashed into `public_input` |

Limb sizes and the encoding of hints are implementation choices, provided every constraint below holds.

## Constraints

A proof MUST be valid only if all of the following hold.

**Signed input**

1. `L <= MAX_SIGNED_INPUT_LEN`, and every byte of `signed_input` at index `L` or above is zero.
2. Exactly one byte in `[0, L)` is `.`, at index `j`. The bytes in `[0, j)` and `(j, L)` are in the base64url alphabet, and `L - j - 1` is not `1 mod 4`.

**Signature**

3. `n` has exactly 2048 bits, `0 < s < n`, and every limb of `n` and `s` is range-checked.
4. `s^65537 mod n` equals the EMSA-PKCS1-v1_5 encoding (RFC 8017 section 9.2) of `SHA-256(signed_input[0, L))`: `0x00 0x01`, 202 bytes of `0xff`, `0x00`, the DigestInfo prefix `3031300d060960864801650304020105000420`, and the 32-byte hash.
5. SHA-256 covers exactly `L` bytes, with padding computed in the circuit from `L`.
6. `key_hash = hash_bytes(n as 256 big-endian bytes, 256)`.

The header is hashed but not parsed: the RSA check fixes the algorithm, and `key_hash` fixes the key.

**Payload**

7. `P = base64url_decode(signed_input(j, L))`, with `len(P) <= MAX_PAYLOAD_LEN`.
8. The circuit lexes `P` one byte at a time, tracking whether each byte is inside a string, whether it is escaped, and the nesting depth of objects and arrays outside strings:
   - `P[0]` is `{`, and the depth first returns to zero at the last byte of `P`, which is `}`;
   - outside strings, every byte is one of `{`, `}`, `[`, `]`, `,`, `:`, `"`, a digit, a letter, `-`, `+`, or `.`, so there is no whitespace;
   - inside strings, every byte is at least `0x20`, and `\` escapes the byte after it.
9. A top-level member name is a string at depth 1 that immediately follows `{` or `,` and is immediately followed by `:`. Top-level member names MUST NOT contain `\`.
10. For each of `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, `nonce`, `iat`, and `exp`, exactly one top-level member has that name, and it starts at the hinted offset.
11. The values of `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, and `nonce` are strings that contain no `\` and are immediately followed by `,` or `}`. `normalize_iss(iss)` and `aud` are at most 128 bytes, `sub` is at most 64 bytes, and `nonce` is exactly 43 bytes.
12. The values of `iat` and `exp` are 1 to 10 decimal digits, immediately followed by `,` or `}`.

**Bindings**

13. `issuer = hash_bytes(normalize_iss(iss), 128)`.
14. The `nonce` value equals `base64url(be32(Poseidon(commit_a, commit_b, blinding)))`.
15. The `iat` value equals `issued_at`.
16. `address_seed = Poseidon(1, hash_bytes(sub, 64), hash_bytes(aud, 128), salt)`.
17. `issued_at < 2^64`. Unless `commit_b` equals `MESSAGE_TAG`, the signature form applies: `commit_a < 2^160`, `commit_b < 2^64`, and `commit_b` is at most the `exp` value.
18. `public_input` is computed as in [Hashing](#hashing).

## Limits

| Name | Value |
|---|---|
| `MAX_SIGNED_INPUT_LEN` | 1,024 bytes |
| `MAX_PAYLOAD_LEN` | 768 bytes |
| `normalize_iss(iss)`, `aud` | 128 bytes each |
| `sub` | 64 bytes |
| `nonce` | 43 bytes |
| `iat`, `exp` | 10 digits |

The circuit is expected to have about 0.9 to 1.0 million constraints, most of them for SHA-256 over up to 17 blocks.

## Verifying key

`VK_OIDC_RS256_V1` is fixed when the ceremony completes and included as a constant in the activating hardfork: `alpha` in G1, `beta`, `gamma`, and `delta` in G2, and `IC[0]` and `IC[1]` in G1, using TIP-1131's point encoding. Its hash is recorded in this TIP before review.

Nodes SHOULD precompute the pairing of `alpha` and `beta` and the prepared forms of `gamma` and `delta`.

## Trusted setup

1. **Phase 1:** reuse the Perpetual Powers of Tau transcript at the smallest power of two that fits the circuit, expected to be `2^20` or `2^21` constraints.
2. **Phase 2:** a public, circuit-specific ceremony open to independent contributors, including at least one from outside Tempo.
3. **Beacon:** a final contribution from a public randomness beacon announced in advance, such as a drand round.
4. **Publication:** the circuit source, the hash of the compiled constraint system, every contribution with its hash and attestation, and the final key. Anyone can check them with `snarkjs zkey verify`.

The setup is secure if at least one contributor in each phase was honest.

## Offchain values (non-normative)

- **Salt:** `HMAC-SHA256(salt_key, normalize_iss(iss) || 0x00 || aud || 0x00 || sub) mod BN254_SCALAR_FIELD`, computed by the wallet operator after checking the token. Using the normalized issuer gives both of Google's issuer forms the same salt, and so the same owner. The salt must be the same on every sign-in, so `salt_key` must be durable. The salt hides the identity; it does not protect the account.
- **Blinding:** a new random field element for each sign-in, so the nonce reveals nothing about the access key.
- **Expiry:** wallets set `valid_until` 9 minutes (540 seconds) after creating the nonce, measured with a clock synchronized to the wallet backend. Because the provider issues the token afterward, this keeps `valid_until` within `issued_at` plus the scheme's 600-second maximum window, even if the provider's clock runs up to a minute behind.

## Performance (non-normative)

- **Servers:** a few seconds per proof with rapidsnark on a multi-core machine, and less with GPU provers.
- **Native apps:** about 3 to 4 s on a 2024 flagship phone, based on published Groth16 results for circuits of about a million constraints.
- **Browsers:** tens of seconds with WebAssembly, plus a proving key of several hundred megabytes. This is not the default path.

## Draft review points

- Confirm from real tokens that 1,024 signed bytes covers the target providers with the `openid` scope.
- Confirm that requiring `exp`, and `valid_until <= exp`, excludes no target provider.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Other proof systems.** UltraHonk and hash-based proofs need no circuit-specific setup, but have larger proofs and slower verification ([TIP-1130](./tip-1130.md#alternatives-considered)). Groth16 over BN254 gives 256-byte proofs that verify in about 1 ms, fit [TIP-1045](./tip-1045.md)'s payment-lane bound, and use curve arithmetic nodes already link. Its costs are a circuit-specific trusted setup and slower proving, which a wallet operator's server absorbs.
- **A circuit per provider.** Sizing a circuit to one provider's tokens proves faster, but every provider would need its own setup and audit. One circuit accepting up to 1,024 signed bytes covers Google, Apple, and Microsoft when wallets request only the `openid` scope.
- **Hashing the start of the token outside the circuit.** This saves about a third of proving time, but the circuit could not check the members in that part for duplicates. It is left for a later version.

# Tooling

- **Reference circuit** in circom, a witness generator, and the [Hashing](#hashing) functions packaged for TypeScript and Rust.
- **Prover:** a reference rapidsnark integration and an optional WebAssembly build.
- **Test vectors**, signed with test RSA keys, each with inputs, the expected `public_input`, and a proof for valid cases:
  - token layouts like those of Google, Apple, and Microsoft;
  - `iss` with and without `https://`;
  - both forms, including a message-form proof checked in the signature form, and the reverse;
  - every length at its maximum;
  - rejections for: a duplicate top-level member; a required member only inside a nested object; an escaped value; an escaped member name; whitespace outside strings; an array `aud`; a missing `exp`; `valid_until` after `exp`; a different `iat`; a nonce for another access key; a modulus that does not match `key_hash`; `s >= n`; a 2047-bit modulus; nonzero bytes after `L`; two dots; a byte outside the base64url alphabet; and a payload length of `1 mod 4`.
- **Differential tests** against a standard JSON parser, checking that the circuit accepts only payloads the parser reads with the same member values.

# Observability

N/A. The circuit has no onchain state. TIP-1131 defines verification metrics, and TIP-1132 defines key events.

# Threat Model

The relation must reject every token the provider did not sign for the claimed identity. Audits of similar circuits have found the following classes of bug, each addressed by the constraints above:

- **Unbound lengths or offsets**, letting members be read from padding or outside the payload: constraints 1, 2, 5, 7, and 10.
- **JSON ambiguity**, where the circuit and a standard parser read different values from one payload through duplicate, nested, or escaped member names, escaped values, or whitespace: constraints 8 to 12, plus differential tests.
- **Unconstrained RSA values**, such as unchecked limbs, `s >= n`, or a short modulus: constraint 3.
- **Missing nonce binding**, letting a leaked token sign for another key or message: constraint 14.
- **Encoding mismatches** between the circuit, nodes, and publishers, such as Poseidon width, padding, or byte order: [Hashing](#hashing) defines one encoding for all three, and shared test vectors cover it.
- **Non-canonical field elements:** TIP-1131 requires canonical public values.
- **Trusted-setup compromise**, which allows forging any proof: a public ceremony with independent contributors and a verifiable transcript.

Before mainnet, at least two independent audits and an automated check for under-constrained signals MUST be complete.

Anyone who knows a user's salt and identity can compute their address, but cannot sign without a new token that the provider signed for a nonce they control.

# Invariants

- For every accepted proof, the key hashing to `key_hash` signed a token whose top-level `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, `nonce`, `iat`, and `exp` members produce `issuer`, `address_seed`, the committed values, and `issued_at` as defined here.
- A token whose nonce commits to one access key and expiry, or to one message, cannot prove any other, in either form.
- `issuer` is the same with or without a leading `https://`.
- The same `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, and salt always produce the same `address_seed`.
- The circuit, nodes' public-input computation, and publishers' hashes agree on every test vector.
