# TIP-1133: OIDC RS256 Proof Circuit v1

## Abstract

This TIP defines scheme `0x01` of [TIP-1131](./tip-1131.md): a Groth16 circuit over BN254 proving that an RS256-signed OpenID Connect ID token, signed by a key with a given hash, identifies a user and commits to an access key or a message, without revealing the token. It specifies the public input, constraints, shared hashing rules, limits, and trusted setup.

## Motivation

Signing in with an auth provider, such as Google or Apple, ends with an ID token: a provider-signed statement naming the user. For that sign-in to sign for a Tempo address, nodes must check the token without it appearing onchain. This circuit proves only what nodes need: that a published provider key signed the token, which address its user controls, and which access key or message its nonce commits to.

## Assumptions

- Providers sign with RS256: RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5 with SHA-256, 2048-bit moduli, and exponent 65537.
- ID tokens are compact JWS tokens with compact JSON payloads. `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, and `nonce` are top-level strings, and `iat` and `exp` are top-level integers.
- Providers echo the client's `nonce` exactly, and users cannot choose member names in tokens.
- Poseidon with circomlib parameters is collision resistant, Groth16 over BN254 is sound, and the universal and circuit-specific setups each had at least one honest contributor.

# Specification

## Overview

For the public input, the circuit proves that:

1. an RSA key hashing to `key_hash` signed a token;
2. the token's top-level `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, `nonce`, `iat`, and `exp` members are read unambiguously;
3. `issuer` and `address_seed` derive from those members and a private salt;
4. the nonce commits to `commit_a`, `commit_b`, and a private blinding value;
5. in the signature form, the committed expiry is no later than `exp`.

TIP-1131 checks the rest: times against the block, the publisher's list, and the access-key signature.

## Notation

- `F` is the BN254 scalar field, of size `BN254_SCALAR_FIELD` ([TIP-1131](./tip-1131.md#constants)).
- `Poseidon(x_1, ..., x_n)`, for `1 <= n <= 16`, is circomlib Poseidon over `F`, with width `n + 1` and circomlib's round counts and constants.
- `be32(x)` is the 32-byte big-endian encoding of `x`, and `base64url` is RFC 4648 section 5 without padding.
- Strings are byte strings, and character literals are ASCII.

## Hashing

Nodes and publishers ([TIP-1132](./tip-1132.md)) use the same definitions:

```text
hash_bytes(b, max_len) = Poseidon(len(b), c_0, ..., c_{k-1})
    where k = ceil(max_len / 31), b is zero-padded to 31k bytes,
          and c_i is the big-endian integer of bytes [31i, 31i + 31)

normalize_iss(iss) = iss without one leading "https://", if present

issuer       = hash_bytes(normalize_iss(iss), 128)
key_hash     = hash_bytes(n as 256 big-endian bytes, 256)
address_seed = Poseidon(1, hash_bytes(sub, 64), hash_bytes(aud, 128), salt)
nonce        = base64url(be32(Poseidon(commit_a, commit_b, blinding)))
public_input = Poseidon(1, issuer, key_hash, address_seed, commit_a, commit_b, issued_at)
```

- `hash_bytes` requires `len(b) <= max_len`. Hashing the length keeps zero-padded strings distinct.
- `normalize_iss` gives Google's `accounts.google.com` and `https://accounts.google.com` the same `issuer`.
- The `1` in `address_seed` is the OIDC namespace tag. The `1` in `public_input` is the scheme.
- `nonce` is 43 characters, and `issued_at` is the token's `iat`.

| Form | `commit_a` | `commit_b` | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signature | `access_key_id` | `valid_until` | [ZK signatures](./tip-1131.md#verification) |
| Message | `uint256(d) mod BN254_SCALAR_FIELD` | `MESSAGE_TAG` | [Message signatures](./tip-1131.md#message-signatures) |

`MESSAGE_TAG` (`2^64`) exceeds any `valid_until`, so the forms never share a public input. Both use one circuit and one trusted setup.

## Inputs

`public_input` is the only public input. The private inputs are:

| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| `signed_input` | `base64url(header) || "." || base64url(payload)`, zero-padded to `MAX_SIGNED_INPUT_LEN` |
| `signed_input_len` | The unpadded length, `L` |
| `signature`, `modulus` | The RSA signature `s` and modulus `n` |
| `salt` | A field element from the salt service |
| `blinding` | A random field element chosen by the wallet |
| `commit_a`, `commit_b` | The committed values of the [form](#hashing) |

The circuit computes `issuer`, `key_hash`, `address_seed`, and `issued_at`, and finds each required member by its name. Limb sizes and hints, such as RSA quotients and value lengths, are implementation choices.

## Constraints

A proof MUST be valid only if all of the following hold.

**Signed input**

1. `L <= MAX_SIGNED_INPUT_LEN`, and every byte at index `L` or above is zero.
2. Exactly one byte in `[0, L)` is `.`, at index `j`. The bytes before and after it are in the base64url alphabet, and `L - j - 1` is not `1 mod 4`.

**Signature**

3. `n` has exactly 2048 bits, `0 < s < n`, and every limb of `n` and `s` is range-checked.
4. `s^65537 mod n` equals the EMSA-PKCS1-v1_5 encoding (RFC 8017 section 9.2) of `SHA-256(signed_input[0, L))`: `0x00 0x01`, 202 bytes of `0xff`, `0x00`, the DigestInfo prefix `3031300d060960864801650304020105000420`, and the 32-byte hash.
5. SHA-256 covers exactly `L` bytes, with padding computed in the circuit from `L`.
6. `key_hash = hash_bytes(n as 256 big-endian bytes, 256)`.

The header is hashed but not parsed: the RSA check fixes the algorithm, and `key_hash` fixes the key.

**Payload**

7. `P = base64url_decode(signed_input(j, L))`, with `len(P) <= MAX_PAYLOAD_LEN`.
8. The circuit lexes `P` one byte at a time, tracking whether each byte is inside a string, whether it is escaped, and the nesting depth outside strings:
   - `P[0]` is `{`, and the depth first returns to zero at the last byte of `P`, which is `}`;
   - outside strings, every byte is one of `{`, `}`, `[`, `]`, `,`, `:`, `"`, a digit, a letter, `-`, `+`, or `.`;
   - inside strings, every byte is at least `0x20`, and `\` escapes the byte after it.
9. A top-level member name is a string at depth 1 immediately after `{` or `,` and immediately before `:`. It MUST NOT contain `\`.
10. Each of `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, `nonce`, `iat`, and `exp` names exactly one top-level member.
11. The values of `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, and `nonce` are strings without `\`, followed by `,` or `}`. `normalize_iss(iss)` and `aud` are at most 128 bytes, `sub` is at most 64 bytes, and `nonce` is exactly 43 bytes.
12. The values of `iat` and `exp` are 1 to 10 decimal digits, followed by `,` or `}`.

**Bindings**

13. `issuer = hash_bytes(normalize_iss(iss), 128)`.
14. The `nonce` value equals `base64url(be32(Poseidon(commit_a, commit_b, blinding)))`.
15. `issued_at` is the `iat` value.
16. `address_seed = Poseidon(1, hash_bytes(sub, 64), hash_bytes(aud, 128), salt)`.
17. Unless `commit_b` equals `MESSAGE_TAG`, `commit_a < 2^160`, `commit_b < 2^64`, and `commit_b` is at most the `exp` value.
18. `public_input` is computed as in [Hashing](#hashing).

## Limits

| Name | Value |
|---|---|
| `MAX_SIGNED_INPUT_LEN` | 1,024 bytes |
| `MAX_PAYLOAD_LEN` | 768 bytes |
| `normalize_iss(iss)`, `aud` | 128 bytes each |
| `sub` | 64 bytes |
| `nonce` | 43 bytes |
| `iat`, `exp` | 10 digits |

The reference circuit has 801,008 constraints, 515,576 of them for the 17 SHA-256 blocks.

## Verifying key

`VK_OIDC_RS256_V1` is fixed when the ceremony completes and included in the activating hardfork: `alpha` in G1, `beta`, `gamma`, and `delta` in G2, and `IC[0]` and `IC[1]` in G1, in TIP-1131's point encoding. Its hash is recorded in this TIP before review.

## Trusted setup

1. **Universal setup:** the Perpetual Powers of Tau transcript at `2^20`, the smallest power of two that fits the circuit.
2. **Circuit-specific setup:** a public ceremony with independent contributors, at least one from outside Tempo.
3. **Beacon:** a final contribution from a public randomness beacon announced in advance, such as a drand round.
4. **Publication:** the circuit source, the hash of the compiled constraint system, every contribution with its attestation, and the final key, all checkable with `snarkjs zkey verify`.

## Offchain components (non-normative)

- **Salt:** `HMAC-SHA256(salt_key, normalize_iss(iss) || 0x00 || aud || 0x00 || sub) mod BN254_SCALAR_FIELD`, computed by the Relay API after checking the token. The normalized issuer gives both of Google's issuer forms the same salt, and so the same address. The salt hides the identity but does not protect the address, and must never change, so `salt_key` must be durable.
- **Blinding:** a new random value for each sign-in, so the nonce reveals nothing about the access key.
- **Expiry:** wallets set `valid_until` 540 seconds after creating the nonce, using a clock synchronized to the Relay API. This keeps it within scheme `0x01`'s 600-second window even if the provider's clock runs a minute behind.
- **Proving:** a few seconds per proof with rapidsnark on a multi-core server, about 3 to 4 s natively on a 2024 flagship phone, and tens of seconds in a browser.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Other proof systems:** UltraHonk and hash-based proofs need no circuit-specific setup, but are larger and slower to verify ([TIP-1130](./tip-1130.md#alternatives-considered)). Groth16 gives 256-byte proofs that verify in about 1 ms with curve arithmetic nodes already link, at the cost of a trusted setup and slower proving.
- **A circuit per provider:** proves faster, but each provider would need its own setup and audit.
- **Hashing the start of the token outside the circuit:** saves about a third of proving time, but the circuit could not check that part for duplicate members.

# Tooling

- A reference circom circuit, a witness generator, and the [Hashing](#hashing) functions for TypeScript and Rust.
- A reference rapidsnark prover and an optional WebAssembly build.
- Test vectors signed with test RSA keys, each with inputs, the expected `public_input`, and a proof for valid cases, shared with TIP-1131.
- Differential tests checking that the circuit accepts only payloads that a standard JSON parser reads with the same member values.

# Observability

N/A. The circuit has no onchain state. TIP-1131 defines verification metrics, and TIP-1132 defines key events.

# Threat Model

The relation must reject every token the provider did not sign for the claimed identity. Audits of similar circuits found the following bug classes, each addressed above:

- **Unbound lengths or offsets:** constraints 1, 2, 5, 7, and 10.
- **JSON ambiguity** from duplicate, nested, or escaped member names, escaped values, or whitespace: constraints 8 to 12, plus differential tests.
- **Unconstrained RSA values**, such as unchecked limbs, `s >= n`, or a short modulus: constraint 3.
- **Missing nonce binding**, letting a leaked token sign for another key or message: constraint 14.
- **Encoding mismatches** between the circuit, nodes, and publishers: one [Hashing](#hashing) definition and shared test vectors.
- **Trusted-setup compromise:** a public ceremony with a verifiable transcript.

Before mainnet, at least two independent audits and an automated check for under-constrained signals MUST be complete. Knowing a user's salt and identity reveals their address, but signing still needs a new provider-signed token for a nonce the signer controls.

# Invariants

- For every accepted proof, the key hashing to `key_hash` signed a token whose top-level members produce `issuer`, `address_seed`, the committed values, and `issued_at` as defined here.
- A token whose nonce commits to one access key and expiry, or to one message, cannot prove any other, in either form.
- `issuer` is the same with or without a leading `https://`.
- The same `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, and salt always produce the same `address_seed`.
- The circuit, nodes, and publishers agree on every test vector.

## Test Cases

- Token layouts like those of Google, Apple, and Microsoft, and `iss` with and without `https://`.
- Both forms, including each form's proof checked as the other, and every length at its maximum.
- Rejections for: a duplicate top-level member; a required member only inside a nested object; an escaped value or member name; whitespace outside strings; an array `aud`; a missing `exp`; `valid_until` after `exp`; a different `iat`; a nonce for another access key; a modulus that does not match `key_hash`; `s >= n`; a 2047-bit modulus; nonzero bytes after `L`; two dots; a byte outside the base64url alphabet; and a payload length of `1 mod 4`.

# Open Questions

- Confirm from real tokens that 1,024 signed bytes covers the target providers with the `openid` scope.
- Confirm that requiring `exp`, and `valid_until <= exp`, excludes no target provider.
