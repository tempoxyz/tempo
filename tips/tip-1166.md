# TIP-1166: Configurable Account Voucher Signers for the Channel Reserve

## Abstract

This TIP lets configurable accounts ([TIP-1107](./tip-1107.md)) sign channel vouchers ([TIP-1034](./tip-1034.md)) with their owners. A channel whose signer has a configuration commitment (the hash of its owner set) must use that commitment as its salt, so the descriptor fixes it for the channel's lifetime. Vouchers are checked against it, so rotating owners later does not invalidate vouchers the payee already holds.

## Motivation

Today a configurable account cannot sign vouchers. Since T14, `open` rejects a signer that has a configuration commitment, so these accounts need a separate session key as `authorizedSigner`.

Checking `0x05` vouchers against the account's *current* commitment would let a payer cheat. Any `updateConfig` changes the commitment ([TIP-1109](./tip-1109.md)):

1. Alice opens a channel to Bob with 100 USDC.
2. Alice's owners sign a voucher for 60 USDC, and Bob serves her.
3. Alice rotates her owners, then calls `requestClose`.
4. Bob's voucher no longer matches Alice's config, so `close` fails.
5. After the 15-minute grace period, Alice withdraws all 100 USDC.

Bob can't defend against this, and owner rotation is a normal thing to do (for example, after losing a device). Fixing the commitment at open fixes the signer for the channel's lifetime, so Bob's voucher still works.

The descriptor's `salt` is payer-chosen entropy that already feeds `channelId`. Requiring it to equal the commitment puts the snapshot in calldata that every channel method already takes, so the ABI, the `channelId` formula and the channel's single storage slot are unchanged.

Alternatives considered:

- **Store the commitment in a second slot per channel.** No change for integrations, but each configurable-account channel pays a second state-creation charge (250,000 gas, [TIP-1000](./tip-1000.md)) at open and a cold read on every voucher.
- **Add the commitment to `ChannelDescriptor`.** No extra storage, but it changes `channelId` and every channel method, breaking existing integrations.
- **Accept any past configuration.** Tempo doesn't store config history.
- **Block `updateConfig` while channels are open.** A user who loses a device couldn't remove it.

## Assumptions

- A commitment covers the whole configuration, including its version. Otherwise two owner sets could share one.
- A version-0 configuration can't be forged for an address it doesn't derive (CREATE2 preimage resistance).
- Payees check a `0x05` voucher's config against the snapshot before accepting it.
- Payers rarely open several channels to the same payee, operator and token with the same configurable signer in one transaction (see [Open](#open)).

# Specification

## Overview

These rules apply from T15. The *signer* is `authorizedSigner` if set, otherwise `payer`. The *snapshot* is the signer's commitment when the channel opened (zero if it had none). A channel with a nonzero snapshot carries it as the descriptor's `salt`.

1. `open` requires the salt to equal a nonzero snapshot.
2. `settle` and `close` check vouchers against the snapshot, never the current config.
3. A nonzero `topUp` requires the signer's config to be unchanged.

Nothing the payer does after `open` changes who can sign vouchers for that channel. The one exception is new deposits, so old owners never gain control of new funds.

## Open

- The T14 `RootKeyRetired` check on the signer is removed.
- After the duplicate check, `open` reads the signer's current commitment. If it is nonzero and `salt` differs from it, `open` reverts with `SaltNotSignerCommitment(signer, commitment)`. Otherwise it records in the channel's packed state, in previously unused bytes, that the salt is the snapshot, and emits `SignerSnapshotCached`. A zero commitment leaves the salt free, as before.
- No storage is added: the channel keeps one state slot, paid for as in TIP-1034.
- An `updateConfig` earlier in the same transaction counts.
- Because the salt can't vary, a payer can open only one channel per payee, operator, token and configurable signer in a transaction; a second reverts with `ChannelAlreadyExists`. Channels opened in different transactions differ by `expiringNonceHash`.

## Voucher checks

| Snapshot | Signature | Accepted when |
|---|---|---|
| Any | Keychain (`0x03`, `0x04`) | Never (unchanged) |
| Nonzero | `0x05` | Its account is the signer, its config hashes to the snapshot, and its owner signatures reach the threshold |
| Nonzero | Primitive | Never: the signer's root key was retired at open ([TIP-1112](./tip-1112.md)) |
| Zero | `0x05` | Its account is the signer, its config is version 0 and derives the signer's address, and its owner signatures reach the threshold |
| Zero | Primitive | It recovers to the signer (unchanged) |

Owners sign the TIP-1034 voucher digest the same way they sign a `0x05` transaction. Gas follows the signature verifier's `verifyConfigurableAccount` schedule: the `0x05` signature's verification gas, plus the version-0 address-derivation gas when the snapshot is zero. The snapshot, not the signer's current commitment, decides whether the derivation charge applies. All gas is charged before verification. A failed check reverts with `InvalidSignature`.

## Top-up

A nonzero `topUp` reverts:

- with `SignerConfigChanged(signer)` if the snapshot is nonzero and differs from the signer's current commitment;
- with `RootKeyRetired(signer)` if the snapshot is zero and the signer now has a commitment (as at T14).

A zero `topUp` still works and cancels a close request. To add funds after a rotation, the payer closes the channel and opens a new one.

The second rule also blocks top-ups when an unregistered signer registers, even with the same version-0 owners. The precompile sees only the new commitment and can't tell registration from a rotation. The payer closes and reopens the channel in that case too.

## Payment lane

`settle` and `close` with a well-formed `0x05` signature count as payment-lane transactions. The classifier checks encoding only and reads no state. The TIP-1045 limit of 2,048 bytes of calldata per payment call still applies. Larger vouchers, such as large owner sets or most WebAuthn approvals, are valid but go to the general lane.

## Interfaces

Additions to `ITIP20ChannelReserve`. No existing method, event or the `channelId` formula changes.

```solidity
/// @notice Emitted by open when the signer has a nonzero configuration commitment.
/// @param channelId The channel that was opened.
/// @param signer The voucher signer: authorizedSigner if set, otherwise payer.
/// @param commitment The signer's configuration commitment when open ran.
event SignerSnapshotCached(bytes32 indexed channelId, address indexed signer, bytes32 commitment);

/// @notice Thrown by a nonzero topUp when the signer's configuration changed since open.
/// @param signer The voucher signer.
error SignerConfigChanged(address signer);

/// @notice Thrown by open when the signer has a configuration commitment and salt differs from it.
/// @param signer The voucher signer.
/// @param commitment The signer's current configuration commitment.
error SaltNotSignerCommitment(address signer, bytes32 commitment);
```

# Compatibility

Existing channels have no snapshot and behave as before. The ABI change is additive.

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Configurable payers | Open with the signer's commitment as `salt`; sign vouchers with `0x05`, using the config from when the channel opened |
| Payees | When the signer had a commitment at open (`SignerSnapshotCached`), check that the voucher's config hashes to `descriptor.salt`; this needs no state read |
| SDKs and wallets | Pick the salt as above instead of at random; keep each channel's opening config until it closes; on `SignerConfigChanged`, close and reopen |
| Indexers | Index `SignerSnapshotCached` |

# Observability

`SignerSnapshotCached` shows which owner set signs for a channel. Comparing it with the account's latest `ConfigurableAccountConfigUpdated` or `AccountUpgraded` event finds channels opened under an old config.

# Threat Model

- **Payer voids vouchers by rotating owners.** Stopped: vouchers are checked only against the snapshot.
- **Removed owner keeps signing.** Old owners who together meet the old threshold can still sign vouchers on channels opened before the rotation. The amount is capped at the deposit and can only go to the payee. To cut this off, the payer closes those channels (`requestClose`, then `withdraw` after 15 minutes).
- **Old owners gain new funds.** Stopped: nonzero `topUp` reverts after a config change.
- **Payee forges a config.** Stopped: it must hash to the snapshot, or derive the signer's address at version 0.

# Invariants

1. A channel's snapshot never changes while the channel exists.
2. While a channel exists, nothing the payer or signer does changes whether a voucher is valid.
3. A channel with a nonzero snapshot has it as its salt.
4. A nonzero `topUp` succeeds only if the signer's current commitment equals the snapshot (or both are zero).
5. A channel with a nonzero snapshot accepts only `0x05` vouchers.
6. A channel uses one state slot whatever its snapshot (TIP-1034 invariant 7 is unchanged).

Tests should cover: configurable payer and configurable `authorizedSigner`; the Alice/Bob rotation case; a voucher from the new config rejected on an old channel; `updateConfig` and `open` in one transaction, in both orders; an unregistered signer that registers later; an EOA upgraded via TIP-1113 after open; `open` with a salt other than the commitment; `topUp` after rotation; `withdraw` after rotation; payment-lane limits; and unchanged behavior at T14.

## Open questions

2. Should the precompile add a `verifyVoucher` view for payees?
3. Is T15 the right fork?
