# TIP-1166: Configurable Account Voucher Signers for the Channel Reserve

## Abstract

This TIP lets a configurable account ([TIP-1107](./tip-1107.md)) sign vouchers for the TIP-20 channel reserve precompile ([TIP-1034](./tip-1034.md)) with its `0x05` multisig signature. Tempo stores only a hash of each configurable account's owners, its configuration commitment, and a `0x05` signature is valid only for the configuration matching it. When a channel opens, the precompile caches the voucher signer's configuration commitment. Vouchers verify against that cached commitment, so rotating owners with `updateConfig` never invalidates vouchers the payee already holds. The cost is one extra storage slot per channel whose signer is a configurable account. The main limit: owners removed after the open keep voucher authority over that channel's existing deposit until it closes.

## Motivation

In a TIP-1034 channel, a payer deposits TIP-20 tokens for one payee. A voucher signer, either the payer or a delegated `authorizedSigner`, signs vouchers for a growing cumulative amount. The payee, or its operator, redeems the latest voucher with `settle` or `close`. The payer can call `requestClose` and, after a 15-minute grace period, `withdraw` whatever was not captured.

A configurable account is one address controlled by weighted owner keys and a threshold. Its configuration commitment is a hash of its owners, threshold, salt and version. A `0x05` signature carries the full configuration and the owner signatures. It is valid only while that configuration hashes to the account's stored commitment. `updateConfig` replaces the owners and increments the version. An account that has never transacted is unregistered: its stored commitment is zero, and its address is derived from its version-0 configuration.

Since T14, `open` reverts with `RootKeyRetired` when the voucher signer has a configuration commitment. Voucher verification also accepts only primitive signatures through TIP-1020 `recover`. A configurable account can therefore pay through a channel only by delegating to a primitive `authorizedSigner`, such as a session key. It cannot sign vouchers with its own owners.

Allowing `0x05` vouchers checked against the signer's current commitment would hand the payer a way to void payments, because `updateConfig` changes that commitment ([TIP-1109](./tip-1109.md)). Example:

1. Alice is a 2-of-3 configurable account at version 0. She opens a channel to Bob with a 100 USDC deposit.
2. Alice's owners sign a voucher for a cumulative 60 USDC. Bob checks it and serves Alice.
3. Alice calls `updateConfig`, which moves her account to version 1, then calls `requestClose`.
4. Bob calls `close` with the version-0 voucher. Its configuration no longer matches Alice's stored commitment, so the call reverts with `InvalidSignature`.
5. After the 15-minute grace period, Alice calls `withdraw` and recovers all 100 USDC.

Bob cannot prevent this. Owner rotation is a normal operation: users replace lost devices with it. So Bob cannot refuse configurable payers that might rotate.

Caching the commitment at open fixes the voucher authority for the channel's lifetime. In the example, Bob's version-0 voucher still verifies after step 3, and he captures 60 USDC.

Alternatives considered:

- **Add the commitment to `ChannelDescriptor`.** It needs no new storage, because the descriptor is hashed into `channelId`. It changes the descriptor struct, the `channelId` formula and every post-open selector, which breaks all existing integrations and channels.
- **Accept vouchers signed under any past configuration.** The precompile would need the account's configuration history, which Tempo does not store. Every former owner set would also keep authority over every channel.
- **Block `updateConfig` while the account has open channels.** A user who loses a device could not rotate it out until every channel closes.
- **Keep requiring a primitive `authorizedSigner`.** This is today's behavior. Configurable accounts then need a separate session key, which their owners and threshold do not govern.

## Assumptions

| Assumption | If violated |
|---|---|
| A configuration commitment covers the full configuration, including its version ([TIP-1108](./tip-1108.md)). | Two different owner sets could share a cached commitment, and either could sign vouchers. |
| Each `0x05` owner signature binds the account address and configuration version ([TIP-1107](./tip-1107.md)). | A signature for one configuration could be replayed under another one. |
| A version-0 configuration derives its account address through CREATE2, and finding a second preimage is infeasible. | Anyone could forge a version-0 configuration for an unregistered signer. |
| Payees check the cached commitment before they accept a `0x05` voucher. | A payee could accept a voucher that matches the payer's current configuration but not the cached one, and fail to capture it. |

# Specification

## Overview

All rules apply from T15. Before T15, TIP-1034 as amended at T14 applies unchanged.

**Terms.** The *signer* is `authorizedSigner` when nonzero, otherwise `payer`, as in TIP-1034. The *signer snapshot* is the signer's configuration commitment read when `open` runs. A zero snapshot means the signer had no stored commitment at that moment: it was a primitive account or an unregistered configurable account.

**Lifecycle.**

1. `open` reads the signer's current configuration commitment. When it is nonzero, the precompile stores it as the snapshot in `signerSnapshots[channelId]`, a second storage slot beside the channel state.
2. `settle` and `close` verify each voucher against the snapshot, never against the signer's current commitment.
3. A nonzero `topUp` requires the signer's current commitment to equal the snapshot.
4. `close` and `withdraw`, which end the channel, delete the snapshot with the channel state.

**Authority boundary.** The snapshot fixes who may sign vouchers for a channel. Nothing the payer or signer does after `open` changes it: `updateConfig`, a TIP-1113 upgrade or access-key changes. New deposit is the exception: a nonzero `topUp` checks that the signer's configuration has not changed, so stale authority never extends to new funds.

## Open

`open` runs the TIP-1034 checks in their existing order, with these changes:

1. The T14 `RootKeyRetired` check on the signer is removed from `open`.
2. After deriving `channelId` and rejecting duplicates, `open` reads the signer's configuration commitment at that point of execution. An `updateConfig` earlier in the same transaction is therefore included.
3. If the commitment is nonzero, `open` writes it to `signerSnapshots[channelId]` and emits `SignerSnapshotCached`. If it is zero, `open` writes nothing.
4. The deposit transfer and `ChannelOpened` follow, unchanged.

Writing the snapshot creates one storage slot. It is charged at the normal new-slot price ([TIP-1000](./tip-1000.md)). A failed `open` reverts the write.

## Voucher verification

`settle` and `close` accept a voucher signature only as the matching row allows. The *voucher digest* is the TIP-1034 EIP-712 digest of `Voucher(channelId, cumulativeAmount)`.

| Snapshot | Signature type | Valid when |
|---|---|---|
| Any | Keychain (`0x03`, `0x04`) | Never, as in TIP-1034. |
| Nonzero | `0x05` | The signature's `account` equals the signer. Its configuration hashes to the snapshot. Its owner approvals over the voucher digest reach the threshold. |
| Nonzero | Primitive | Never. The signer's root key was retired at open ([TIP-1112](./tip-1112.md)). |
| Zero | `0x05` | The signature's `account` equals the signer. Its configuration has version 0 and derives the signer's address. Its owner approvals over the voucher digest reach the threshold. |
| Zero | Primitive | TIP-1020 `recover` returns the signer, as in TIP-1034. |

Owner approvals are checked as for a `0x05` transaction signature. Each owner signs `keccak256("tempo:multisig:signature" || voucherDigest || account || uint64(version))`, and access keys never count toward the threshold.

Verification never reads the signer's current commitment. A voucher that verifies at one block keeps verifying at every later block while the channel exists.

Verification gas is charged before any signature check, in the amounts TIP-1020 charges for `verifyMultisig`. This includes address derivation gas for a zero snapshot. A failed check reverts with `InvalidSignature`. The caller's transaction fee and nonce are spent, and channel state is unchanged.

## Top-up

A nonzero `topUp` runs one new check after the payer check:

1. If the snapshot is nonzero and differs from the signer's current commitment, `topUp` reverts with `SignerConfigChanged(signer)`.
2. If the snapshot is zero and the signer now has a nonzero commitment, `topUp` reverts with `RootKeyRetired(signer)`, as at T14.

A zero `topUp` skips this check. It still cancels a pending close request.

After a revert, the payer can request close, withdraw after the grace period, and open a new channel. The new channel caches the current commitment.

## Close and withdraw

`close` and `withdraw` end the channel. They delete `signerSnapshots[channelId]` in the same step that deletes the channel state. The TIP-1034 same-transaction reopen guard is unchanged. A reopen in a later transaction derives a new `channelId` and reads a fresh snapshot.

## Payment-lane classification

TIP-1034 classifies channel calls as payment-lane traffic, so they use block space reserved for payments under congestion. The classifier admits `settle` and `close` only when their `signature` argument has a valid signature encoding. From T15, a well-formed `0x05` encoding also passes. The classifier checks only the encoding and the format limits: 1 to 48 owners and 1 to 8 owner signatures. It reads no state.

## Interfaces

These additions go to `ITIP20ChannelReserve` and its reference file [`tips/verify/src/interfaces/ITIP20ChannelReserve.sol`](verify/src/interfaces/ITIP20ChannelReserve.sol). No existing struct, function, event or error changes.

```solidity
/// @notice Emitted by `open` when the voucher signer has a nonzero configuration commitment.
/// @param channelId The channel opened.
/// @param signer The voucher signer: `authorizedSigner` when nonzero, otherwise `payer`.
/// @param commitment The signer's configuration commitment when `open` ran.
event SignerSnapshotCached(bytes32 indexed channelId, address indexed signer, bytes32 commitment);

/// @notice Thrown by a nonzero `topUp` when the signer's configuration changed since `open`.
/// @param signer The voucher signer.
error SignerConfigChanged(address signer);

/// @notice Returns the signer snapshot of a channel.
/// @param channelId The channel.
/// @return commitment The cached configuration commitment, or zero when the channel does not
///         exist or its signer had no commitment at `open`.
function getSignerSnapshot(bytes32 channelId) external view returns (bytes32 commitment);
```

Storage adds one mapping beside the TIP-1034 channel states:

```text
signerSnapshots: mapping(bytes32 channelId => bytes32 commitment)
```

The slot exists only while the channel exists and its snapshot is nonzero.

# Compatibility

Existing channels have no snapshot. They were opened before T14, or under the T14 rule that rejected signers with a commitment. Primitive vouchers on them verify exactly as before.

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Payers with configurable accounts | Can open a channel with themselves, or another configurable account, as signer and sign vouchers with `0x05`. Vouchers must use the configuration cached at open, even after an update. |
| Payees and operators | Before accepting a `0x05` voucher, check that its configuration hashes to `getSignerSnapshot(channelId)`, and verify the owner approvals off-chain. Payees should still check that the snapshot names owners they trust. |
| SDKs (mppx, mpp-rs, pympp, viem) | Sign vouchers with `0x05` for configurable signers, using the configuration kept at open. Map `SignerConfigChanged` to "close and reopen the channel". |
| Wallets | Keep the configuration that was current at each channel's open until that channel closes. Configurable accounts no longer need a separate session key for channel payments. |
| Indexers | Index `SignerSnapshotCached` beside `ChannelOpened`. |

# Observability

`SignerSnapshotCached` answers which configuration authorizes a channel's vouchers. Payees and wallets read it to sign and verify vouchers. Comparing it with the account's latest `MultisigConfigUpdated` event shows channels whose snapshot is stale. A nonzero `topUp` on those channels reverts with `SignerConfigChanged`.

# Threat Model

| Actor | Controls | Check that stops misuse | If the check fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payer voiding vouchers | `updateConfig`, TIP-1113 upgrade, `requestClose` | Verification reads only the snapshot | Payer recovers funds the payee earned. This TIP removes that attack. |
| Former owner removed after open | Its old key | None until the channel closes | With old owners whose weights reach the old threshold, it can sign vouchers up to the deposit, payable only to the channel's payee. |
| Payer adding funds under stale authority | Nonzero `topUp` | `SignerConfigChanged` | Removed owners would gain voucher authority over new deposit. |
| Payee forging a configuration | Voucher bytes | Configuration hash must equal the snapshot, or derive the signer at version 0 | Payee captures funds without the payer's approval. |
| Sender of large `0x05` vouchers in the payment lane | Calldata | Format limits in the classifier; gas charged before verification | Payment-lane capacity spent on verification work. |

Residual risk: a payer who removes a compromised owner should also close channels opened before the removal. Until they close, the compromised key and old owners reaching the old threshold can pay the payee up to the deposit. The payer cuts this off with `requestClose` and `withdraw` after the 15-minute grace period. The payee can still settle valid vouchers during that window.

# Invariants

1. For every open channel, `getSignerSnapshot(channelId)` equals the signer's configuration commitment when `open` ran, and never changes while the channel exists.
2. While a channel exists, no call by the payer or signer changes whether a given voucher signature verifies for it.
3. A channel with no stored state has no stored snapshot.
4. A nonzero `topUp` succeeds only when the signer's current commitment equals the snapshot, or both are zero.
5. A channel with a nonzero snapshot never accepts a non-`0x05` voucher signature.
6. A primitive voucher accepted under TIP-1034 for a channel with a zero snapshot is accepted identically.
7. A channel uses one slot of mutable state, plus one snapshot slot only when its snapshot is nonzero. This amends TIP-1034 invariant 7.
8. All other TIP-1034 invariants hold unchanged.

Tests must cover each case at T15, and check that T14 behavior is unchanged:

- A configurable payer and a configurable `authorizedSigner`, each opening, settling and closing with `0x05` vouchers.
- The motivation example: `updateConfig` after a voucher is signed, then `close` succeeding with the old voucher.
- A voucher signed under the updated configuration rejected for a channel opened before the update.
- An `updateConfig` and an `open` in the same transaction, in both orders.
- An unregistered configurable signer at open, then registered and updated, still verifying version-0 vouchers.
- An EOA signer upgraded with TIP-1113 after open, still verifying primitive vouchers, with nonzero `topUp` reverting.
- Nonzero and zero `topUp` after a configuration change.
- Snapshot deletion on `close` and `withdraw`, and same-transaction reopen rejection.
- Payment-lane classification of `settle` and `close` with `0x05` signatures, at the format limits and one past them.

## Open questions

1. Should verification also accept the signer's current configuration, so new owners can sign vouchers on old channels? It adds no griefing path, but payees would have two configurations to check.
2. Should the precompile expose `verifyVoucher(descriptor, cumulativeAmount, signature)` so payees can verify `0x05` vouchers with `eth_call` instead of off-chain code?
3. Is T15 the right activation fork, given T14 ships configurable accounts?
