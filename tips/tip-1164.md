# TIP-1164: Organization Accounts

## Abstract

An organization applies one policy to many accounts. The organization lives in a zone, so its member list, its policy and the member balances are private. Each member account is a self-custodied configurable account. The organization can narrow what a member does, but it cannot move member funds, change member owners, or stop a member from leaving.

This TIP adds three things and nothing else:

1. An organization record in the zone, with a policy and an admin.
2. A binding on Tempo that ties each member account to its zone and to a recovery address.
3. An eject, by which the recovery address ends membership.

Everything else comes from existing specifications: configurable accounts for ownership, TIP-20 allowances for integrations, the zone for privacy, and zone withdrawals and TIP-1094 deposits for flows to Tempo.

## Motivation

Some applications create accounts for many users and must apply the same rules to all of them. Today they have three options:

- An allowlist in each contract that the users touch. Each list changes one account at a time, and each list is public.
- A TIP-403 policy on a dedicated token. It limits who holds the token, not what an account does, and it is public on Tempo.
- A key that controls every user account. The application is then custodial.

The problem has three parts, and a solution must solve all three:

1. **One policy for many accounts.** An integration authorizes the set once. A rule change applies to every member in one transaction.
2. **A private set.** Observers cannot list the members or link them to each other.
3. **Self-custody.** Only the user's keys move the user's funds, and the user can always leave.

A zone already solves part 2 for balances. Configurable accounts already solve part 3 for keys. This TIP adds the smallest layer that solves part 1 without breaking parts 2 and 3.

## Assumptions

- Configurable accounts ([TIP-1107](./tip-1107.md), [TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md)) are active. Zones read their commitments at the zone anchor, as [TIP-1154](./tip-1154.md) specifies. Owners change only on Tempo.
- Zones keep the [privacy modifications](../zones/specs/spec.md#privacy-modifications) of the Zones specification: account-indexed access control on getters, fixed gas for transfers, and no contract creation.
- The zone sequencer is trusted for liveness, as the Zones specification states. If it stops, no zone account can move funds. This TIP does not change that.
- The zone prover is sound. The zone state transition function enforces every rule in this TIP.
- The member controls its recovery address, alone or with a recovery service. If the organization also controls it, the organization controls the account. The protocol cannot detect this.

# Specification

## Overview

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organization | A record in the organization registry of one zone. It holds an admin, a base policy and groups. It holds no funds. |
| Member | A configurable account that an organization created. It is a member of one organization until it ejects. |
| Admin | The address that changes the policy and creates members. It is usually a configurable account with several owners. |
| Policy | The rules that limit what a member can send, receive and call. |
| Group | A subset of members whose policy is narrower than the base policy. |
| Binding | A record on Tempo that ties a member to its zone and to its recovery commitment. It does not name the organization. |
| Recovery address | The address that can eject a member. |
| Recovery commitment | `keccak256("tempo:org:recovery" \|\| recoveryAddress \|\| recoverySalt)`, with a random 32-byte `recoverySalt`. |
| Eject | The end of membership. The account keeps its address and balances, and becomes a regular configurable account. |

The design follows four rules. Each rule is checked in one place.

| Rule | Where it is enforced |
|---|---|
| The organization limits a member, but never acts for it. | Every TIP-20 debit from a member in the zone passes the policy. No organization call moves funds. |
| A wider policy waits, a narrower policy applies at once. | `setPolicy` ([Policy changes](#policy-changes)). |
| The recovery address can always end membership. | `eject` on Tempo, which no zone state can block ([Eject](#eject)). |
| Membership is private. | Registry state and events are zone state, readable only by the member and the admin ([Privacy](#privacy)). |

### Lifecycle

1. A zone account creates an organization and names its admin.
2. The admin creates a member for a user. The user's wallet supplies the owners, the recovery commitment and a random salt.
3. A later zone batch writes the member's binding to Tempo.
4. The member sends, receives and calls in the zone as the policy permits. A new member with an empty policy can do nothing.
5. Optionally, the recovery address ejects the member on Tempo.

An existing account cannot become a member. An ejected account cannot become a member again.

## Mechanics

### Organization registry

The organization registry is a zone predeploy. An organization has:

- An `orgId` equal to `keccak256("tempo:org:id" || zoneId || creator || salt)`.
- An admin, and an owner that can replace the admin. Both transfer in two steps: propose, then accept.
- A base policy, and up to `MAX_GROUPS` group policies.
- A queue of pending policy changes.

The registry also keeps a permanent set of every address that was ever a member. It never removes an address from this set.

### Creating a member

The admin calls `createMember(orgId, groupId, config, recoveryCommitment, userSalt)`. `config` is a TIP-1107 version-0 configuration. The user's wallet chooses the owners, the recovery commitment and `userSalt`.

The registry derives the member address with the TIP-1107 formula and this salt:

```text
salt = keccak256("tempo:org:member" || zoneId || orgId || recoveryCommitment || userSalt)
```

The registry requires all of these conditions:

- The caller is the admin of `orgId`, and the group exists.
- The address was never a member.
- The address has a zero commitment at the zone anchor, a zero nonce, and no code.

The salt binds the zone, the organization and the recovery commitment into the address. As a result, an existing account cannot have this address, no other organization can derive it, and the admin cannot choose it without the user's salt.

The member is under the policy from its first state write.

### Binding on Tempo

Each zone batch output carries the members created since the last batch, sorted by address, as `(account, recoveryCommitment, configCommitment)`. The batch proof covers this list. When the portal accepts the batch, the binding precompile does these steps for each entry:

1. It stores `binding[account] = (zoneId, recoveryCommitment)`.
2. If the account has a zero commitment on Tempo, it stores `configCommitment` in the account leaf. This registers the account under TIP-1107 and retires any colliding key. It emits no configuration event, so the owners stay private.

The precompile keeps a permanent set of every address that was ever bound. The member salt includes the zone, so a valid batch never binds such an address again. A batch that tries to fails proof verification.

A bound account on Tempo can only put funds into its zone:

- Every TIP-20 debit from the account MUST credit the portal of its zone, except the fee of its own transaction. Other debits revert with `ZoneBoundAccount`.
- The account can `approve` only the portal of its zone.
- Its transactions can only deposit into that portal, approve that portal, call `updateConfig`, or call the binding precompile.

The zone credits every deposit from a bound account to the account itself, whatever recipient the deposit names. As a result, a deposit cannot skip the policy.

In closed access mode, the portal treats every address that is or was bound to its zone as an allowed account. As a result, the public portal role list never lists members.

Integrations pay members through [TIP-1094](./tip-1094.md) zone deposit addresses, never at the member's Tempo address.

### Policy

A policy has four lists. Each list has a fixed maximum size, so evaluation fits within the fixed transfer gas of the zone.

| List | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `tokens` | The TIP-20 tokens that a member can hold. |
| `counterparties` | The addresses that a member can pay and receive from, in addition to deposits from Tempo. A zone withdrawal recipient on Tempo is a counterparty too. |
| `intraOrg` | Whether members of the organization can pay each other. Off by default. |
| `calls` | The predeploys and selectors that a member can call, in the format of TIP-1011 call scopes. |
| `limits` | Spending limits for each token and period, in the format of TIP-1011 periodic limits. A token with no limit entry cannot be sent. |

The zone applies the policy to the member account, whatever key signs:

- Every TIP-20 debit from a member, through `transfer`, `transferFrom` by any spender, or a withdrawal, MUST credit a counterparty, or another member if `intraOrg` is on, and MUST fit the limits.
- A member can `approve` only a counterparty.
- Every TIP-20 credit to a member MUST come from a counterparty, a deposit from Tempo, or another member if `intraOrg` is on.
- The fee of the member's own zone transaction is exempt from these checks in every state, including an empty policy, a freeze and a pending eject. Without this, a member could not pay for `exit` or any other call.
- Every top-level call from a member MUST match `calls`.

A blocked transfer reverts with the zone error `PolicyForbids`. A blocked deposit from Tempo bounces back, as the Zones specification defines.

The effective policy of a member is the intersection of the base policy and its group policy: a member can do something only if both permit it, and both sets of limits apply. A group can only narrow the base policy, so the base policy bounds every member.

### Integrations

An integration, for example a vault, a card program or a rewards distributor, is a zone predeploy with its own TIP. To work with an organization, it needs nothing new:

- The admin adds it to `counterparties` and its functions to `calls`.
- A member deposits by calling it, or lets it pull with a TIP-20 allowance.
- The policy limits bound every pull.
- If the admin removes the integration, its allowances stop working at once, because a debit to it no longer passes the policy.

An integration that holds member funds MUST implement `exit(member)`. It returns the member's whole position to the member. A member can always call `exit` on any predeploy that was ever a counterparty of its organization, and the transfer back always passes the policy. This holds while the member is frozen, after the integration is removed, and after the member ejects. As a result, narrowing a policy never traps funds.

An integration checks membership with `memberOf(account)` while it is a counterparty of the account's organization.

### Policy changes

The admin calls `setPolicy(orgId, groupId, policy)`.

- If the new policy is narrower than or equal to the current one in every list, it applies at once.
- Otherwise it applies after `POLICY_DELAY`. The admin can cancel it during the delay.

A policy is wider if it adds a token, a counterparty or a call, turns on `intraOrg`, raises a limit amount, shortens a limit period, or adds a limit for a token that had none.

This one rule replaces separate plugin queues and upgrade roles. A compromised admin can stop members at once, but it cannot open a new path for funds without warning. During the delay, a member whose owner keys are held by someone else can eject.

### Freeze

The admin can `freeze` and `unfreeze` a member. Freeze is the narrowest policy: a frozen member cannot send, and no spender can debit it, except through `exit` calls and its own fees. It can still receive and eject. A freeze never moves funds.

### Eject

The recovery address calls `eject(account, recoveryAddress, recoverySalt, newConfig)` on the binding precompile on Tempo, with `msg.sender == recoveryAddress`, at any call depth. The precompile checks the recovery commitment against the binding. Any account can pay the fee. No zone state, freeze or policy can stop an eject.

**Without new owners.** If `newConfig` is empty, the eject is pending for `RECOVERY_DELAY`, and the recovery address can cancel it. If the account itself also calls `approveEject`, which needs its owner quorum, the eject applies at once. After it applies, the precompile deletes the binding and emits `MemberEjected`. The delay stops a copied recovery key from lifting the policy at once.

**With new owners.** If `newConfig` is not empty, the eject is pending for `RECOVERY_DELAY`. Use this form if someone else holds the owner keys, for example an embedded wallet provider.

- While it is pending, the account cannot send on Tempo, and no spender can debit it. When the zone anchor includes the start of the eject, the account cannot send in the zone, except through `exit` calls.
- Only the recovery address can cancel it. The owners cannot, so an owner-key holder cannot block an exit.
- After the delay, anyone calls `completeEject`. The precompile replaces the configuration with `newConfig`, increases its version, and revokes every access key on Tempo. It then deletes the binding and emits `MemberEjected`.

TIP-20 allowances and permit nonces of a member account record the TIP-1107 configuration version at which the account set them. An allowance or nonce from an older version reads as zero. Tempo checks the current version. The zone checks the version at its anchor, and revokes the account's zone access keys when the anchor includes a new version. As a result, `completeEject` voids every allowance, permit and access key of the old owners, on Tempo and in the zone, with no write to zone state.

`RECOVERY_DELAY` MUST be much longer than the maximum anchor delay of the zone.

Both forms count as a pending eject for the rules below.

**Recovery rotation.** The recovery address can call `rotateRecovery` with a fresh commitment. The rotation waits `RECOVERY_DELAY`, and the current recovery address can cancel it. A rotation and an eject cannot be pending at the same time. As a result, a copied recovery key cannot lock out its real holder.

**In the zone.** When the zone anchor includes `MemberEjected`, the zone marks the member as ejected and stops applying the policy. The address, balances, allowances and positions do not change. The account can then withdraw to any address, including its own address on Tempo.

### Withdrawals and forced exit

A member withdraws to Tempo through the zone outbox. The recipient must be a counterparty. If the recipient is a member, its binding must be visible at the zone anchor, so the funds stay bound on Tempo. The zone withdrawal already hides the sender behind `senderTag`.

The current forced exit design accepts only a primitive root-key signature. This TIP requires it to also accept a TIP-1107 owner quorum, checked against the commitment at the zone anchor. A current member can force-exit only to its own bound address. An ejected account can force-exit to any address. Both paths need a live sequencer.

### Privacy

Registry state and events are zone state. They follow the account-indexed access control of the Zones specification. The additional readers are:

- The admin and owner of the organization, for all of its state.
- A member, for its own record and for its organization's policy.
- A counterparty predeploy, for `memberOf` of the accounts that use it.

Observers on Tempo see that an address is bound to a zone, the portal flows, and a recovery address when it acts. They do not see the organization, the other members, the policy, or zone balances. The anonymity set of a binding is every member of every organization in the zone. A zone with one organization gives no organization-level privacy on Tempo.

If `intraOrg` is on, a member can learn whether a bound address is another member by testing a transfer. Turn it on only if members need to pay each other. A sender can learn whether a member refuses its transfer.

## Constants

| Name | Proposed value |
|---|---|
| `MAX_GROUPS` | 256 |
| `MAX_TOKENS`, `MAX_COUNTERPARTIES`, `MAX_CALLS`, `MAX_LIMITS` | 16, 64, 32, 8 |
| `POLICY_DELAY` | 7 days |
| `RECOVERY_DELAY` | 7 days |

The list sizes MUST let one transfer evaluate the policy within the fixed transfer gas.

## Interfaces

```solidity
interface IOrganizationRegistry {
    struct CallScope { address target; bytes4[] selectors; }
    struct Limit { address token; uint256 amount; uint64 period; }
    struct Policy {
        address[] tokens;
        address[] counterparties;
        CallScope[] calls;
        Limit[] limits;
        bool intraOrg;
    }

    function createOrganization(bytes32 salt, address owner, address admin) external returns (bytes32 orgId);
    function proposeAdmin(bytes32 orgId, address admin) external;   // owner only
    function acceptAdmin(bytes32 orgId) external;
    function proposeOwner(bytes32 orgId, address owner) external;   // owner only
    function acceptOwner(bytes32 orgId) external;

    function createMember(bytes32 orgId, uint32 groupId, bytes calldata config,
        bytes32 recoveryCommitment, bytes32 userSalt) external returns (address member);
    function setGroup(bytes32 orgId, address member, uint32 groupId) external; // into a narrower or equal group only, otherwise delayed
    function freeze(bytes32 orgId, address member) external;
    function unfreeze(bytes32 orgId, address member) external;

    function setPolicy(bytes32 orgId, uint32 groupId, Policy calldata policy) external returns (uint64 effectiveAt);
    function cancelPolicy(bytes32 orgId, uint32 groupId) external;

    function memberOf(address account) external view returns (bytes32 orgId, uint32 groupId, bool frozen);
    function policyOf(address account) external view returns (Policy memory);

    event OrganizationCreated(bytes32 indexed orgId, address owner, address admin);
    event MemberCreated(bytes32 indexed orgId, address indexed member, uint32 groupId);
    event PolicyQueued(bytes32 indexed orgId, uint32 groupId, uint64 effectiveAt);
    event PolicyApplied(bytes32 indexed orgId, uint32 groupId);
    event MemberFrozen(bytes32 indexed orgId, address indexed member, bool frozen);
    event MemberLeft(bytes32 indexed orgId, address indexed member);
}

interface IOrgExit {
    /// Returns the whole position of member to the member. Always callable by member.
    function exit(address member) external;
}

interface IZoneBinding {
    function bindingOf(address account) external view returns (uint64 zoneId, bytes32 recoveryCommitment, uint64 ejectUnlock, uint64 rotationUnlock);
    function eject(address account, address recoveryAddress, bytes32 recoverySalt, bytes calldata newConfig) external;
    function approveEject(address account) external; // called by the account itself
    function completeEject(address account) external;
    function cancelEject(address account, address recoveryAddress, bytes32 recoverySalt) external;
    function rotateRecovery(address account, address recoveryAddress, bytes32 recoverySalt, bytes32 newCommitment) external;
    function completeRotation(address account) external;
    function cancelRotation(address account, address recoveryAddress, bytes32 recoverySalt) external;

    event Bound(address indexed account, uint64 indexed zoneId);
    event EjectStarted(address indexed account, uint64 ejectUnlock);
    event EjectCancelled(address indexed account);
    event MemberEjected(address indexed account, uint64 indexed zoneId);
    event RotationStarted(address indexed account, uint64 rotationUnlock);
    event RotationCancelled(address indexed account);
    event RecoveryRotated(address indexed account);

    error ZoneBoundAccount(address account);
}
```

Precompile addresses, storage layouts, the batch output encoding and gas costs are still to be specified.

# Compatibility

Existing accounts do not change and cannot join. To move users into an organization, the admin creates new members, and each user moves its funds.

| Component | What changes |
|---|---|
| Zones | A new registry predeploy. The policy applies to member debits, credits and calls. Bindings appear in batch output. |
| Zone portal | Writes bindings. Treats bound and ejected accounts as allowed in closed access mode. Credits deposits from a bound account to that account. |
| Zone forced exit | Accepts a TIP-1107 owner quorum. |
| Configurable accounts | Two new write paths without an owner quorum: registration at binding, and `completeEject`. |
| Integrations | No new interface, except `exit` for integrations that hold member funds. |
| Wallets | MUST use a fresh random `userSalt`, a fresh recovery address and `recoverySalt` for each member, and MUST NOT reuse owner keys across members. MUST NOT control the recovery address alone. |
| Recovery services | Co-own recovery addresses, pay eject fees, and watch `EjectStarted` and `RotationStarted`. |

# Observability

| Event | Where | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| `Bound`, `MemberEjected` | Tempo | Which addresses are bound to a zone, and which left. |
| `EjectStarted`, `EjectCancelled`, `RotationStarted`, `RotationCancelled`, `RecoveryRotated` | Tempo | Whether someone is taking over a member. Member wallets and recovery services alert on these. |
| `MemberCreated`, `MemberLeft`, `MemberFrozen` | Zone | Membership and freeze state. |
| `PolicyQueued`, `PolicyApplied` | Zone | When a member's permissions widen. Members watch `PolicyQueued`. |

# Threat Model

The most consequential attack is a stolen recovery key. The attacker starts an eject with new owners. The real holder sees `EjectStarted` and cancels during `RECOVERY_DELAY`. The attacker cannot rotate the key first, because a rotation waits the same delay. The recovery address SHOULD have several owners, for example the member's passkey and a recovery service.

| Actor | Can | Cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Admin | Create members, freeze, narrow the policy at once, widen it after `POLICY_DELAY` | Move funds, change owners or recovery, block eject or `exit` |
| Owner | Replace the admin | Anything the admin cannot |
| Owner-key holder, for example a wallet provider | Spend within the policy | Spend outside it. The recovery address replaces it. |
| Integration | Pull within allowances and limits | Pull after removal, or block `exit` |
| Recovery address | Eject after `RECOVERY_DELAY`, at once with the owners' approval | Act faster than `RECOVERY_DELAY` alone, or if the real holder cancels |
| Sequencer | Stop or censor the zone | Break any rule, or forge or drop a binding |
| Observer on Tempo | See bindings and portal flows | Learn the organization, the other members or the policy |

Residual risks:

- **Liveness.** A member moves zone funds only while the sequencer is live, as for every zone account.
- **Collusion.** An admin that widens the policy and an owner-key holder that spends can act together after `POLICY_DELAY`. The member has the delay to eject.
- **Anchor delay.** Old owners can spend within the policy until the zone anchor includes `EjectStarted`.
- **Linking on Tempo.** Shared owner keys, deposits from member addresses and reused recovery addresses link members. The wallet rules prevent this. The protocol cannot enforce them.

# Invariants

- No organization call decreases a member's balance.
- No organization call changes a member's owners or recovery commitment.
- Every TIP-20 debit from a member in the zone passes the member's effective policy.
- Every TIP-20 debit from a bound account on Tempo, except its own fee, credits its zone portal.
- The effective policy never permits more than the base policy.
- A wider policy never applies before `POLICY_DELAY`.
- A valid eject succeeds under every policy and while frozen.
- A member can always pay the fee of its own zone transaction.
- A member can call `exit` on every predeploy that was ever a counterparty of its organization.
- An address is a member at most once, of one organization, in one zone.
- After `completeEject`, no access key, allowance or permit from an earlier version works.
- No registry getter or event reveals a member to a caller that [Privacy](#privacy) does not permit.

The test suite must cover:

- Eject while frozen and under an empty policy, delayed alone and immediate with `approveEject`.
- An `exit` call by a frozen member with an empty policy, paying its own fee.
- A policy that removes a limit, shortens a period, or turns on `intraOrg`, before and after `POLICY_DELAY`.
- An old allowance used after `completeEject`, on Tempo and in the zone.
- Eject with new owners: no sends on Tempo, no sends in the zone after anchor inclusion, owners cannot cancel, completion revokes old keys and allowances.
- Rotation and eject at the same time.
- A widening policy before and after `POLICY_DELAY`, and a narrowing policy at once.
- An integration pull after the integration is removed, and `exit` after removal and after eject.
- A bound account on Tempo that pays any address other than its portal, through `transfer`, `transferFrom` or `permit`.
- `createMember` at an address that exists, was a member, or was bound.
- A non-member that calls `memberOf` directly and through a predeploy.
