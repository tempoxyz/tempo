# TIP-1164: Organization Accounts

## Abstract

This TIP adds organizations to Tempo. An organization is a group of member accounts that share one set of policies. Its policy admin sets the policies once, and every member account follows them. A contract can authorize the organization once, and the authorization covers every member. The organization holds no funds and cannot move them. Each member can always leave the organization with the same address and balance. The main limit is that existing accounts cannot join an organization. Only new accounts that the membership admin creates as members can be in one.

## Motivation

Today, the protocol applies policies to one account at a time. A set of accounts that must follow the same rules needs one of these mechanisms:

- An allowlist in each contract that the accounts use. Each contract keeps its own list, and each list changes one account at a time.
- A TIP-403 policy on a dedicated TIP-20 token. This limits who can hold the token. It cannot limit what an account calls.
- A smart contract wallet with modules. This cannot use native account features such as access keys ([TIP-1011](./tip-1011.md)).

None of these lets a contract authorize a whole set of accounts with one check. None of them limits what every key of an account can call. A change to a set of accounts, such as a move between vaults, needs one transaction for each account, or a key that can move the funds of every account.

This TIP adds one onchain record, the organization, that holds the policies for a set of accounts. A contract authorizes the organization with one call to `isMember`. A policy change applies to every member in one transaction. No role in the organization can move member funds.

## Assumptions

- Configurable accounts ([TIP-1107](./tip-1107.md)) and configurable account access keys ([TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md)) are active. A member account is a configurable account.
- Zones read Tempo state as [TIP-1154](./tip-1154.md) specifies. A zone applies an organization change only after it imports the Tempo block that contains the change.
- Receive policies ([TIP-1028](./tip-1028.md)), access-key call scopes ([TIP-1011](./tip-1011.md)) and virtual addresses ([TIP-1022](./tip-1022.md)) work as specified.
- The member, or a recovery service that the member chose, holds the recovery key. If a role holder also holds the recovery key, the role holder controls the account. The protocol cannot detect this.
- Router contracts, for example a vault router or a spend router, are separate TIPs. This TIP specifies only how the organization registers and limits them.

# Specification

## Overview

### Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organization | An onchain record that groups member accounts and holds their shared policies. It holds no funds. |
| Member account | A configurable account that the organization created. It belongs to exactly one organization until it ejects. |
| Role | A permission that an address holds for one organization. Roles are listed in [Roles](#roles). |
| Group | A named subset of members inside one organization. A group adds its own policy to the base policy. |
| Base policy | The policy that applies to every member of the organization. |
| Inbound filter | The part of a policy that controls what a member account can receive. |
| Outbound filter | The part of a policy that controls what a member account can call and pay. |
| Plugin | A contract that the organization registers, for example a vault router or a spend router. |
| Member virtual address | A virtual address that resolves to one member account. |
| Passthrough address | A virtual address that the organization owns. It forwards payments to one named payee. |
| Recovery key | A key that the member holds. While the account is a member, this key can only eject the account. |
| Freeze | A state in which the member account cannot send. It can still receive and eject. |
| Eject | The action that removes a member account from its organization. The account becomes a regular configurable account. |

### Lifecycle

1. An account creates an organization and assigns its roles.
2. The membership admin creates a member account for each new user. The user holds the keys that own the account. The user, or a recovery service, holds the recovery key.
3. The member account sends and receives only what the organization policies permit. By default, a member account cannot send or receive anything.
4. The member uses plugins, for example to deposit into a vault or to pay with a card.
5. Optionally, the member ejects. The account keeps its address, balances and owners. The organization policies stop applying.

An account cannot join an organization after it exists. An ejected account cannot join again.

### Trust boundary

The organization controls what a member account can do. It never controls who can sign for the account. Only the member account owners can authorize a payment. No role can transfer member funds, change member owners, or stop an eject. In the worst case, a compromised role can freeze members or narrow their policies.

## Mechanics

### Organization registry

A new precompile, the organization registry, stores every organization at a reserved address. Each organization has:

- An `orgId` (`uint64`), assigned in creation order.
- The holder of each role.
- A base policy.
- A list of groups and the policy of each group.
- A list of plugins and the timelock for plugin changes.
- A table of member virtual addresses and passthrough addresses.

### Roles

Each organization has four roles. One address can hold more than one role. A role holder is usually a configurable account with several owners.

| Role | What it can do | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Assign and remove the other roles. Change the plugin timelock, subject to the timelock itself. | Act as another role without assigning that role. |
| Membership admin | Create member accounts. Move a member between groups. | Remove a member. Only the member can leave. |
| Policy admin | Change the base policy and group policies. Freeze and unfreeze a member. | Make a policy that blocks eject or blocks withdrawals from plugins back to the member. |
| Plugin admin | Queue plugin additions and removals. | Activate a plugin before the timelock ends. |

No role can transfer funds from a member account. No role can change the owners of a member account.

### Creating an organization

Any account can call `createOrganization(roles, timelock)`. The registry assigns the next `orgId`, stores the roles, and stores an empty base policy. An empty policy permits nothing. The registry emits `OrganizationCreated`.

### Creating a member account

The membership admin calls `createMember(orgId, groupId, config, recoveryCommitment)`.

- `config` is the version-0 configuration of a configurable account ([TIP-1107](./tip-1107.md#configuration)). The user holds the keys that it lists as owners.
- `recoveryCommitment` is the hash of the recovery key address.

The registry derives the member address with the TIP-1107 formula. The salt binds the organization and the recovery commitment:

```text
salt = keccak256("tempo:organization:member" || uint64(orgId) || recoveryCommitment || userSalt)
```

The registry requires all of these conditions:

- The caller holds the membership admin role for `orgId`.
- The group exists.
- The derived address has a zero nonce, a zero balance in every TIP-20 token, no code, and no configuration commitment.
- No role holder of the organization is an owner in `config`.

If all conditions are true, the registry records the membership, registers the configurable account, and emits `MemberCreated`. The account is a member from its first state write. No transaction can use the account before the organization policies apply to it.

The condition on the derived address is what makes joining impossible. An account that already exists has a different address or a nonzero state, so `createMember` reverts.

### Policies

A policy has two filters.

The inbound filter is a receive policy in the sense of [TIP-1028](./tip-1028.md). It lists the TIP-20 tokens that a member can receive and the senders that can send them. While an account is a member, the protocol uses the organization inbound filter as the receive policy of the account. A call by the member to change its own receive policy reverts.

The outbound filter controls every transaction from a member account, whatever key signs it. This includes the owners and every access key. It has three parts:

- Call targets. The contracts and selectors that the member can call, in the format of TIP-1011 call scopes.
- Payees. A member can send TIP-20 tokens only to another member, a registered plugin, or a passthrough address of the same organization.
- Limits. Optional spending limits per token and per period, in the format of TIP-1011 periodic limits.

The effective policy of a member combines the base policy and the policy of its group:

- Inbound: a transfer must pass both the base filter and the group filter.
- Outbound: the member can call a target if the base policy or the group policy permits it. Limits from both apply.

For example, a card program can be in the policy of one group only, so only members of that group can use it.

Two paths are always open, and no policy can close them:

- The eject call by the recovery key.
- A plugin withdrawal that returns funds to the member account.

### Freeze

The policy admin can call `freeze(orgId, member)`. A frozen member cannot send transactions, except an eject. The account can still receive funds, and plugins can still return funds to it. The policy admin can call `unfreeze` to end the freeze. A freeze never moves funds.

### Virtual addresses

The organization registers as a virtual master ([TIP-1022](./tip-1022.md)). Under TIP-1022 today, a virtual address credits the master wallet. For an organization, the registry resolves each virtual address through the organization table instead. Each entry points to one of two targets:

- A member account. A payment to the address credits that member account.
- A payee. This is a passthrough address. A payment to it is forwarded to the named payee in the same transaction.

The membership admin creates member virtual addresses. The policy admin creates passthrough addresses. A member can give a virtual address to a payer or a ramp, and does not have to share the account address.

In a public organization, the TIP-20 `Transfer` log still names the member account as the sender of an outbound payment. A passthrough address hides the sender only in a private organization.

### Plugins

A plugin is a contract that the organization registers, for example a vault router, a spend router or a rewards router. Each plugin type has its own TIP.

To add or remove a plugin, the plugin admin calls `queuePluginChange`. After the timelock ends, anyone can call `applyPluginChange`. Members and integrating contracts can see every change before it applies, and members can withdraw first.

A plugin acts on a member account only in two ways:

- The member calls the plugin in its own transaction, as the outbound filter permits.
- The plugin uses an access key that the member authorized under TIP-1011. The key is scoped to the plugin and to its limits.

When a plugin is removed, members can still withdraw their funds from it.

### Eject

The member signs `eject(member, newConfig)` with the recovery key. The registry requires these conditions:

- The account is a member.
- The signature is from the key that matches the `recoveryCommitment` of the member.

Then the registry does these steps:

1. It removes the membership record.
2. It clears the organization inbound filter and outbound filter from the account.
3. It revokes every access key on the account that is scoped to a plugin of the organization.
4. It removes the member virtual addresses that point to the account.
5. It emits `MemberEjected`.

The address, balances and plugin positions do not change. After step 5, the account is a regular configurable account, and its owners set its rules.

If `newConfig` is empty, the owners stay the same and the eject applies at once. If `newConfig` is not empty, the eject also replaces the owners. This is for a member that lost its owner keys, or whose owner keys are held by a wallet provider. An eject with `newConfig` waits for the recovery delay. During the delay, the current owners can cancel it. See [Threat Model](#threat-model).

The recovery key or another account can pay the fee for an eject. If no other account pays, the fee comes from the member account. A freeze does not stop an eject.

### Zones

The organization state lives on Tempo. Each zone reads it from the latest Tempo block that the zone imported, as TIP-1154 specifies for configurable accounts.

A shared zone admits only member accounts of the organizations that use it. Its sequencer allowlist is the union of those member lists, and it rejects every other account.

A member of a private organization can leave the zone with a forced withdrawal. The forced withdrawal moves the account to a public account on Tempo. It works even if the zone sequencer stops. The Zones specification defines this mechanism. This TIP adds one rule: an eject from a private organization starts a forced withdrawal.

This draft does not yet specify which parts of the organization state are public on Tempo and which parts stay private in the zone.

## Interfaces

```solidity
interface IOrganizationRegistry {
    struct Roles {
        address owner;
        address membershipAdmin;
        address policyAdmin;
        address pluginAdmin;
    }

    struct CallScope {
        address target;
        bytes4[] selectors; // empty means every selector
    }

    struct Limit {
        address token;
        uint256 amount;
        uint64 period; // seconds; 0 means one limit for the life of the account
    }

    struct Policy {
        uint64 receivePolicyId; // TIP-1028 receive policy for the inbound filter
        CallScope[] calls;      // outbound call targets
        Limit[] limits;         // outbound spending limits
    }

    // Organization
    function createOrganization(Roles calldata roles, uint64 timelock) external returns (uint64 orgId);
    function setRole(uint64 orgId, uint8 role, address holder) external;
    function roles(uint64 orgId) external view returns (Roles memory);

    // Members
    function createMember(
        uint64 orgId,
        uint32 groupId,
        bytes calldata config,
        bytes32 recoveryCommitment,
        bytes32 userSalt
    ) external returns (address member);
    function setGroup(uint64 orgId, address member, uint32 groupId) external;
    function organizationOf(address account) external view returns (uint64 orgId, uint32 groupId, bool frozen);
    function isMember(uint64 orgId, address account) external view returns (bool);

    // Policies
    function setBasePolicy(uint64 orgId, Policy calldata policy) external;
    function setGroupPolicy(uint64 orgId, uint32 groupId, Policy calldata policy) external;
    function canCall(address member, address target, bytes4 selector) external view returns (bool);
    function freeze(uint64 orgId, address member) external;
    function unfreeze(uint64 orgId, address member) external;

    // Virtual addresses
    function setMemberAddress(uint64 orgId, bytes6 userTag, address member) external;
    function setPassthrough(uint64 orgId, bytes6 userTag, address payee) external;

    // Plugins
    function queuePluginChange(uint64 orgId, address plugin, bool add) external returns (bytes32 changeId);
    function applyPluginChange(uint64 orgId, bytes32 changeId) external;
    function isPlugin(uint64 orgId, address plugin) external view returns (bool);

    // Eject
    function eject(address member, bytes calldata newConfig, bytes calldata recoverySignature) external;
    function cancelEject(address member) external;

    event OrganizationCreated(uint64 indexed orgId, Roles roles, uint64 timelock);
    event RoleUpdated(uint64 indexed orgId, uint8 role, address holder);
    event MemberCreated(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed member, uint32 groupId);
    event MemberGroupChanged(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed member, uint32 groupId);
    event PolicyUpdated(uint64 indexed orgId, uint32 groupId);
    event MemberFrozen(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed member);
    event MemberUnfrozen(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed member);
    event VirtualAddressSet(uint64 indexed orgId, bytes6 userTag, address target, bool passthrough);
    event PluginChangeQueued(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed plugin, bool add, uint64 readyAt);
    event PluginChangeApplied(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed plugin, bool add);
    event EjectStarted(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed member, uint64 readyAt);
    event EjectCancelled(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed member);
    event MemberEjected(uint64 indexed orgId, address indexed member);

    error Unauthorized();
    error AccountNotNew();
    error NotMember();
    error AlreadyMember();
    error GroupNotFound();
    error RoleHolderIsOwner();
    error MemberFrozen();
    error CallNotPermitted(address target, bytes4 selector);
    error PayeeNotPermitted(address payee);
    error LimitExceeded(address token);
    error TimelockActive(uint64 readyAt);
    error InvalidRecoverySignature();
}
```

The precompile address, the role encoding, the exact storage layout and gas costs are still to be specified.

# Compatibility

Existing accounts do not change. They cannot join an organization. To put the users of existing accounts in an organization, the membership admin creates new member accounts, and each user moves its funds. This TIP does not specify a conversion of existing accounts.

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Gated contracts, for example vaults | A contract calls `isMember(orgId, account)` or `isPlugin(orgId, caller)` instead of keeping its own allowlist. |
| Contracts that spend for a member | The contract is a registered plugin and uses an access key that the member authorized under TIP-1011, scoped to the plugin. |
| Deposits | A payer sends TIP-20 tokens to a member virtual address. The registry resolves it to the member account. |
| Withdrawals | A member pays out through a passthrough address of the organization. |
| Account creation | A wallet calls `createMember` and uses the keys that it manages for the user as owners. The recovery key stays with the user or a recovery service. |
| Zones | A zone that serves organizations uses the union of their member lists as its sequencer allowlist. Private virtual addresses can use [TIP-1094](./tip-1094.md). Organizations in several zones can share settlement under [TIP-1149](./tip-1149.md). |
| Access key policies | [TIP-1161](./tip-1161.md) policy contracts apply to one access key. The outbound filter of this TIP applies to every key of a member account. |

SDK support: viem actions for every registry function, and a helper that computes the member address from `orgId`, `config` and the recovery commitment.

# Observability

| Event | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| `OrganizationCreated`, `RoleUpdated` | Who controls each organization role. |
| `MemberCreated`, `MemberGroupChanged` | Which accounts are members, and in which group. |
| `PolicyUpdated` | When the rules for members changed. |
| `MemberFrozen`, `MemberUnfrozen` | Which members cannot send, and since when. |
| `PluginChangeQueued`, `PluginChangeApplied` | Which plugins will change, and when. Members and integrating contracts watch this event during the timelock. |
| `EjectStarted`, `EjectCancelled`, `MemberEjected` | Which members left, and whether an owner replacement is pending. |
| `VirtualAddressSet` | Where a virtual address resolves. In a private organization, this event is private. |

A rejected outbound transaction reverts with `CallNotPermitted`, `PayeeNotPermitted`, `LimitExceeded` or `MemberFrozen`, so the member wallet can show the reason.

# Threat Model

The most consequential attack is a stolen recovery key. With it, an attacker can eject the account and replace its owners. The recovery delay limits this attack: the current owners can cancel during the delay. If the attacker also controls the current owners, the account is lost, as it is for any account whose keys are stolen.

| Actor | What it controls | What enforces the limit | Result if compromised |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organization owner | Assigns roles | Roles cannot move funds or change member owners | Attacker assigns itself every role. Worst case is the same as all roles below. |
| Membership admin | Creates members, moves groups | `createMember` accepts only new addresses | Attacker creates unwanted members or moves members to a stricter group. |
| Policy admin | Policies and freeze | Eject and plugin withdrawals stay open | Attacker freezes every member. Members can still eject with their funds. |
| Plugin admin | Plugin list | Timelock | Attacker queues a malicious plugin. Members see it during the timelock and can withdraw. |
| Plugin | Funds that members deposit, and scoped access keys | TIP-1011 scopes and limits | Losses are limited to plugin deposits and access-key limits. This is the residual risk of each plugin TIP. |
| Recovery key holder | Eject | Recovery delay for owner replacement | Attacker ejects the member. With owner replacement, attacker takes over the account unless the owners cancel. |
| Wallet provider | Member owner keys | Normal account security | Same as any account today. |
| Zone sequencer | Ordering in a private organization | Forced withdrawal | Sequencer can censor members for a time, but cannot stop a forced withdrawal. |

Excluded threats: a role holder that also holds the recovery keys of members. The protocol cannot see who holds a key.

# Invariants

- No role holder can decrease the TIP-20 balance of a member account.
- No role holder can change the owners of a member account.
- An account becomes a member only through `createMember`, and only if it has no prior state.
- An account belongs to at most one organization.
- An ejected account is never a member again.
- A valid eject by the recovery key succeeds when the member is frozen, and under every policy.
- A member with an empty effective policy cannot send or receive TIP-20 tokens, except plugin withdrawals.
- A plugin change never applies before its timelock ends.
- After an eject, no access key scoped to an organization plugin is active on the account.

The test suite must cover these cases:

- Eject during a freeze.
- Eject under an empty policy.
- Owner replacement, and its cancel during the recovery delay.
- `createMember` on an address that has a balance.
- A group policy that adds a call target.
- A plugin removal while members hold positions.
