# TIP-1164: Organization Accounts

## Abstract

This TIP adds organizations. An organization is a set of member accounts that follow one set of policies. The organization and its members live in a zone, so the member list, the policies and the balances are private. On Tempo, each member account carries only a binding to its zone and a commitment to its recovery address. The binding does not name the organization.

No organization role can move member funds, change member owners or stop a member from leaving. The recovery address of a member can always eject the account on Tempo. The account keeps its address and its balances. Two limits remain. A member can move zone funds only while the zone sequencer is live, as for every zone today. Existing accounts cannot join an organization.

## Motivation

Today, the protocol applies policies to one account at a time. A set of accounts that must follow the same rules needs one of these mechanisms:

- An allowlist in each contract that the accounts use. Each list changes one account at a time, and each list is public.
- A TIP-403 policy on a dedicated TIP-20 token. This limits who can hold the token. It cannot limit what an account calls, and the policy is public on Tempo.
- A smart contract wallet with modules. This cannot use native account features such as access keys ([TIP-1011](./tip-1011.md)), and zones do not permit contract deployment.

None of these mechanisms lets an integration authorize a whole set of accounts with one check. None of them limits what every key of an account can do. A change to the set, such as a move between vaults, needs one transaction for each account, or a key that can move the funds of every account. Also, each mechanism publishes the set.

This TIP adds one record, the organization, that holds the policies for a set of accounts. The record lives in a zone. An integration authorizes the organization once. A policy change applies to every member in one transaction. The member list stays private, and no role can move member funds.

## Assumptions

- Configurable accounts ([TIP-1107](./tip-1107.md), [TIP-1111](./tip-1111.md)) are active. A member account is a configurable account.
- Zones read Tempo state at the zone anchor, as [TIP-1154](./tip-1154.md) specifies. Owner updates happen only on Tempo. A zone applies a Tempo change only after its anchor includes the Tempo block with that change.
- Zones keep the [privacy modifications](../zones/specs/spec.md#privacy-modifications) of the Zones specification: account-indexed access control on getters, fixed gas for transfers, and no contract creation.
- The zone sequencer is trusted for liveness and data availability, as the Zones specification states. Zones have no forced inclusion and no permissionless exit. If the sequencer stops or censors, members cannot move zone balances. This TIP does not change that. See [Threat Model](#threat-model).
- The zone prover is sound. The zone state transition function enforces the rules of this TIP, so a sequencer cannot settle a batch that breaks them.
- The member, or a recovery service that the member chose, controls the recovery address. If a role holder also controls the recovery address, the role holder controls the account. The protocol cannot detect this.
- Plugins are zone predeploys. Each plugin type is specified in its own TIP. This TIP specifies only how an organization enables plugins and limits them.

# Specification

## Overview

### Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organization | A record in the organization registry of one zone. It groups member accounts and holds their policies. It holds no funds. |
| Organization registry | A zone predeploy that stores every organization in that zone. |
| Member account | A configurable account that an organization created. It is a member of exactly one organization until it ejects. |
| Zone binding | A record on Tempo that ties an account to one zone and to a recovery commitment. It does not name the organization. |
| Recovery address | The address that can eject a member account. It is a configurable account that the member controls, with or without a recovery service. |
| Recovery commitment | `keccak256("tempo:org:recovery" \|\| recoveryAddress \|\| recoverySalt)`, where `recoverySalt` is 32 random bytes. The binding stores only this hash. |
| Role | A permission that an address holds for one organization. See [Roles](#roles). |
| Base policy | The policy that every member of the organization follows. |
| Group | A named subset of members. A group policy narrows the base policy for its members. |
| Inbound filter | The part of a policy that controls what a member can receive. |
| Outbound filter | The part of a policy that controls what a member can call, pay and withdraw. |
| Plugin | A zone predeploy that an organization enables, for example a vault router. |
| Plugin grant | A permission that a member gives to one plugin to pull up to a limit of one token. |
| Org address | An address in the TIP-1022 format that the registry resolves. It is either a member address or a passthrough address. |
| Member address | An org address that resolves to one member account. |
| Passthrough address | An org address that forwards each payment to one fixed payee. |
| Freeze | A state in which a member account cannot send in the zone. It can still receive, exit its plugins, and eject. |
| Eject | The action that ends membership. The account becomes a regular configurable account. |

### Where the state lives

| State | Location | Visible to |
|---|---|---|
| Zone binding, recovery commitment, configuration commitment | Tempo | Everyone |
| Pending eject or recovery rotation | Tempo | Everyone |
| Organization roles, policies, groups, plugin list and queue | Zone registry | Role holders. Members read the roles, the plugin list and the queue. |
| Member record: organization, group, freeze state | Zone registry | Role holders and the member |
| Org address table | Zone registry | Role holders. A member reads its own member addresses. |
| Balances, plugin grants, limit counters | Zone state | The member and role holders. A plugin reads the grants that name it. |

[Read access](#read-access) gives the exact rules.

### Lifecycle

1. A zone account creates an organization and assigns its roles.
2. The membership admin creates a member account for each user in the zone. The user wallet supplies the owner configuration, the recovery commitment and a random salt.
3. A later settled batch writes the zone binding of the member to Tempo, and registers its configuration commitment.
4. The member sends and receives in the zone only as the organization policies permit. By default, a member cannot send or receive anything.
5. Optionally, the recovery address ejects the account on Tempo. The zone applies the eject when its anchor includes that Tempo block.

An account cannot join an organization after the account exists. An ejected account cannot become a member again.

### Trust boundary

The organization controls what a member account can do. It never controls who signs for the account, and it never signs for the account. Only the owners of the member account authorize payments and calls. Only the recovery address can eject.

No role can transfer member funds, change member owners, change the recovery commitment, or stop an eject. In the worst case, a compromised role freezes members or narrows their policies.

A party that holds the owner keys of a member, for example an embedded wallet provider, can spend as those owners, but only within the policy. If that party is hostile, the recovery address replaces the owners with an [eject with new owners](#eject).

### Privacy

The design hides these facts from observers on Tempo:

- Which organization an account belongs to.
- Which bound accounts belong to the same organization.
- The policies, groups, plugins and org addresses of any organization.
- Balances and transfers inside the zone.

Observers on Tempo can still see these facts:

- That an address is bound to a zone, so it is a member of some organization in that zone. The anonymity set is the members of all organizations in that zone. A zone with one organization gives no organization-level privacy on Tempo.
- Deposits and withdrawals at the zone portal. A deposit from the member address itself names that address. [TIP-1094](./tip-1094.md) deposit addresses hide the member. [TIP-1149](./tip-1149.md) shared settlement makes flows harder to link.
- The owner configuration of a member, when the member signs a Tempo transaction. Owner keys that several members share link those members.
- The recovery address, when it calls `eject`, `cancelEject` or `rotateRecovery`.

Zone parties learn more:

- A sender learns whether a member refuses a transfer from it, because the transfer reverts.
- A plugin learns which members use it, and their amounts.
- Role holders see all members of their organization. Privacy of members from their own organization is not a goal of this TIP.

## Mechanics

### Organization registry

The organization registry is a predeploy in each zone that activates this TIP. Each organization has:

- An `orgId` (`bytes32`), equal to `keccak256("tempo:org:id" || creator || salt)`. The ID does not reveal how many organizations exist.
- The holder of each role.
- A base policy, and up to `MAX_GROUPS` groups with one policy each.
- Up to `MAX_PLUGINS` enabled plugins, a plugin timelock, and a queue of pending plugin changes.
- The org addresses of the organization.

The registry keeps a permanent record of every address that was ever a member, in any organization. It never deletes this record.

### Roles

Each organization has four roles. One address can hold more than one role.

| Role | It can | It cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Assign and remove the other roles. Transfer the owner role in two steps. Queue a change to the plugin timelock. | Make a timelock change apply before the current timelock ends. |
| Membership admin | Create member accounts. Move a member between groups. Create member addresses. | Remove a member. Only the recovery address ends membership. |
| Policy admin | Change the base policy and group policies. Freeze and unfreeze members. Create and delete passthrough addresses. | Change the payee of a passthrough address. Reuse an org address. Block an eject or a plugin exit. |
| Plugin admin | Queue plugin changes. Cancel a queued change. | Apply a plugin change before the timelock ends. |

The owner role transfers in two steps: the owner proposes a new owner, and the new owner accepts.

### Read access

Every registry getter follows the account-indexed access control of the Zones specification, in direct and in nested calls:

- `memberOf(account)` and `canCall(account, ...)` answer only if `msg.sender` is the account, a role holder of its organization, or an enabled plugin of its organization. Otherwise they revert with `Unauthorized()`.
- `isMember(orgId, account)` answers only if `msg.sender` is the account, a role holder of `orgId`, or an enabled plugin of `orgId`.
- `policy` and the org address table answer only for role holders. A member reads only its own entries.
- `roles(orgId)`, `plugins(orgId)` and the plugin queue answer for role holders, members and enabled plugins of `orgId`.

The redacted RPC shows a registry event only to the callers in this table:

| Event | Visible to |
|---|---|
| Events about one member: `MemberCreated`, `MemberGroupChanged`, `MemberFrozen`, `MemberUnfrozen`, `MemberLeft`, `OrgAddressCreated` for a member address | Role holders, and the member it names |
| `PluginGranted`, `PluginRevoked`, `PluginPulled` | Role holders, the member, and the plugin it names |
| Plugin queue events | Role holders, members and enabled plugins |
| Other organization events | Role holders |

The same rules apply to logs in transaction receipts.

### Creating an organization

Any zone account can call `createOrganization(salt, roles, pluginTimelock)`. The registry requires `pluginTimelock >= MIN_PLUGIN_TIMELOCK`. It stores the roles, an empty base policy and no plugins, and emits `OrganizationCreated`. An empty policy permits nothing.

### Creating a member account

The membership admin calls `createMember(orgId, groupId, config, recoveryCommitment, userSalt)`.

- `config` is the version-0 configuration of a configurable account ([TIP-1107](./tip-1107.md#configuration)). The user wallet chooses its owners.
- `recoveryCommitment` comes from the user wallet. The registry cannot see the recovery address.
- `userSalt` is 32 random bytes from the user wallet.

The registry sets the TIP-1107 salt of the account as follows:

```text
salt = keccak256("tempo:org:member" || zoneId || orgId || recoveryCommitment || userSalt)
```

The registry then derives the member address with the TIP-1107 formula. The registry requires all of these conditions:

- The caller holds the membership admin role of `orgId`.
- The group exists.
- The derived address was never a member.
- The derived address has a zero commitment at the zone anchor, a zero nonce and no code in the zone.

If all conditions are true, the registry stores the member record, adds the address to the pending binding set, and emits `MemberCreated`. The account is a member from its first state write, so no transaction can use the account before the policies apply.

The salt binds the zone, the organization and the recovery commitment into the address. As a result, an account that already exists cannot have the derived address, and no other organization, in this zone or another zone, can derive the same address. Because the user wallet chooses `userSalt`, the membership admin cannot choose the address alone.

### Zone binding on Tempo

The zone publishes pending bindings once every `BINDING_EPOCH` batches. The batch output for that epoch carries every pending binding of every organization in the zone, sorted by address:

```text
(account, recoveryCommitment, configCommitment)
```

`configCommitment` is the TIP-1107 commitment of the version-0 configuration. The batch proof covers the list, so the sequencer cannot add, drop or change a binding. When the portal accepts the batch, the binding precompile writes for each entry:

1. `binding[account] = (zoneId, recoveryCommitment, no pending eject, no pending rotation)`.
2. The configuration commitment in the Tempo account leaf of the account, if the commitment there is zero. This registers the account under TIP-1107 and retires its colliding key. This write emits no configuration event, so the owners stay private.

The binding precompile keeps a permanent record of every address that ever had a binding. The zone state transition function never emits a binding for such an address, because the zone ID in the salt makes member addresses unique across zones. A batch with such a binding fails proof verification. The precompile does not skip or revert one entry, so no member is left without a binding.

Sorting and epochs prevent an observer from linking the members that one organization created together. A membership admin SHOULD also spread member creation over time.

#### Rules for a bound account on Tempo

While an account has a binding, Tempo applies these rules to the account:

- Every TIP-20 debit from the account, through `transfer`, `transferFrom`, `permit` or any other path, MUST credit the portal of the bound zone. Any other debit reverts with `ZoneBoundAccount(account)`.
- `approve` and `permit` by the account revert unless the spender is the portal of the bound zone.
- Each top-level call that the account sends MUST be one of these:
  - A deposit into the portal of the bound zone.
  - An `approve` whose spender is that portal.
  - A TIP-1107 `updateConfig`.
  - A call to the binding precompile.
- The fee of a permitted transaction is the only other permitted debit. The fee manager collects it as for any Tempo transaction. A sponsor can also pay it.
- While an eject with new owners is pending, every call from the account reverts, and every debit from the account reverts, including fees. A sponsor pays the fee for calls to the binding precompile in this state.

A bound account can receive any TIP-20 transfer on Tempo. Funds that arrive on Tempo can only go into the bound zone.

The zone state transition function credits every deposit whose Tempo sender is a bound account to that account itself, whatever the encrypted recipient is. The refund recipient of such a deposit MUST be the account itself. As a result, a member cannot use a Tempo deposit to skip its outbound filter.

The portal treats an address that is bound to its zone, or that ejected from its zone, as allowed in closed access mode. This applies to the depositor, the deposit refund recipient, and the `to` of a plain withdrawal. As a result, the portal `Account` role list never lists members.

Before its binding settles, a member has no Tempo funds, because the organization never publishes its Tempo address. Integrations MUST NOT pay a member address on Tempo directly. They pay [TIP-1094](./tip-1094.md) zone deposit addresses.

### Policies

A policy has an inbound filter and an outbound filter. Each filter has fixed maximum sizes. The evaluation of both filters MUST fit within the fixed transfer gas of the zone, so the gas cost of a transfer does not reveal a policy.

#### Inbound filter

The inbound filter lists the TIP-20 tokens that a member can receive, and the classes of sender that can send them:

- Members of the same organization.
- Passthrough addresses of the organization.
- Enabled plugins of the organization.
- Deposits from Tempo through the zone inbox.
- An optional set of up to `MAX_SENDERS` other zone addresses, stored in the registry.

The filter never lists members in a TIP-403 policy, because TIP-403 policies are public on Tempo. Token issuer policies under TIP-403 still apply, as for every zone transfer.

A transfer that the inbound filter blocks reverts with the generic `PolicyForbids` error of the zone, the same error as a blocked TIP-1028 receive policy. A deposit from Tempo that the filter blocks fails and bounces back, as the Zones specification defines for failed deposits.

Two kinds of transfer always pass the inbound filter:

- A transfer from a plugin that was ever enabled for the organization, through its exit function.
- A deposit from Tempo whose sender is the bound account itself.

#### Outbound filter

The outbound filter controls every zone transaction from a member, whatever key signs it. This includes the owners and every access key. It also controls every TIP-20 debit from the member. It has four parts:

- Call targets: the predeploys and selectors that the member can call, in the format of TIP-1011 call scopes.
- Payees: a member can send TIP-20 tokens only to an org address of its organization, or to an enabled plugin through a plugin call. A raw member account address is not a payee. As a result, a member cannot test whether an address is a co-member.
- Withdrawal payees: the Tempo addresses that a member can name as `to` in a zone withdrawal. The member itself is a withdrawal payee only if the policy says so.
- Limits: optional spending limits for each token and period, in the format of TIP-1011 periodic limits. Pulls under plugin grants count toward the limits.

A member cannot `approve` or `permit` any spender in the zone, except the zone outbox for a permitted withdrawal. Plugins pull funds only under plugin grants.

A member transaction that the outbound filter blocks reverts with `PolicyForbids`. The member can read its own policy with `canCall` to learn why.

#### Groups

The effective policy of a member is the intersection of the base policy and the policy of its group:

- A transfer to the member must pass both inbound filters.
- A call, payment or withdrawal by the member must be permitted by both outbound filters.
- Both sets of limits apply.

A group can only narrow the base policy. As a result, the base policy is an upper bound for every member, and a review of the base policy covers the whole organization. For example, the base policy permits a card program, and only one group policy also permits it. Then only the members of that group can use the card program.

A policy change applies from the next transaction. It does not change transactions that are already in a block.

#### Paths that no policy can close

- The eject of the account by its recovery address on Tempo.
- The exit function of any plugin that was ever enabled for the organization. See [Plugins](#plugins).

### Freeze

The policy admin can call `freeze(orgId, member)`. A frozen member cannot send zone transactions, except calls to plugin exit functions. It can still receive funds, and its recovery address can still eject it on Tempo. The policy admin can call `unfreeze` to end the freeze. A freeze never moves funds.

A freeze applies only in the zone. Tempo does not see it. This does not open a gap, because a bound account can only send funds on Tempo to its own zone balance, where the freeze applies.

### Org addresses

Org addresses use the [TIP-1022](./tip-1022.md) format. The Zones specification registers no virtual masters. This TIP adds one: the registry itself is the only virtual master in the zone. Every org address of every organization uses the same master ID. The 6-byte tag is random, and the registry rejects a tag that was ever used. As a result, two org addresses do not reveal that they belong to the same organization.

A TIP-20 transfer in the zone to an org address resolves through the registry:

- A member address credits its member account. The membership admin creates it. Its target never changes. After the member ejects, the address still credits the former member account.
- A passthrough address forwards the payment to its payee in the same transfer. The policy admin creates it. The payee MUST NOT be an org address or a virtual address, so resolution runs exactly one level. Its payee never changes. The policy admin can delete it. A deleted tag is never used again.

A transfer to a deleted or unknown org address reverts with `PolicyForbids`. Funds never go to the registry. A deposit from Tempo to such an address fails and bounces back to the depositor.

The receipt of a transfer to an org address shows the resolution to the member account only to the member and to role holders. The sender sees only the org address.

For deposits from Tempo, a payer uses a [TIP-1094](./tip-1094.md) zone deposit address that encrypts an org address. A wallet SHOULD issue a separate deposit address for each payer.

### Plugins

A plugin is a zone predeploy, for example a vault router, a spend router or a rewards router. Each plugin type has its own TIP. The protocol keeps a fixed set of plugin predeploys, and each one implements `IOrgPlugin`. `queuePluginChange` reverts with `PluginNotEnabled` for any other address. Zones do not permit contract deployment, so the code of a plugin changes only in a network upgrade.

To enable or disable a plugin, the plugin admin calls `queuePluginChange`. After the timelock ends, anyone can call `applyPluginChange`. Members and enabled plugins can read the queue during the timelock, and members can exit before the change applies.

A plugin acts on a member account in only two ways:

1. The member calls the plugin, as the outbound filter permits.
2. The plugin pulls funds under a plugin grant.

To make a grant, a member calls `grantPlugin(plugin, token, limit, period, expiry)`. The registry uses the organization of `msg.sender`, and requires that `msg.sender` is a member and that the plugin is enabled for that organization. Each member can hold at most `MAX_GRANTS` grants.

The plugin then calls `pull(member, token, amount)`. The registry requires all of these conditions:

- The caller is a plugin that is enabled for the organization of `member`.
- The member is not frozen and has no pending eject.
- The grant exists, is not expired, matches the current plugin epoch and member generation, and has enough limit left in the current period.
- The amount also fits the outbound limits of the member.

Then the registry transfers `amount` from the member to the plugin, and emits `PluginPulled`. A pull never sends funds to any other address.

Revocation is lazy, so its cost does not depend on the number of members. Each plugin has an epoch for each organization, which increases when the plugin is disabled. Each member has a generation, which ends on eject. A grant is valid only while its stored epoch and generation are current. A member can also call `revokePlugin` at any time.

Every plugin MUST implement `exit(member)`. It returns the whole position of the member to the member account. The member can call `exit` on any plugin that was ever enabled for its organization:

- While the member is frozen.
- Whatever its call targets and limits are.
- After the plugin is disabled.
- After the member ejects.

The transfer from `exit` always passes the inbound filter. Each plugin TIP MUST specify `exit`, and MUST apply account-indexed access control to its getters for each member.

### Eject

The recovery address calls `eject(account, recoveryAddress, recoverySalt, newConfig)` on the binding precompile on Tempo, with `msg.sender == recoveryAddress`. The call can come from any call depth, so a recovery address can be a multisig. The precompile requires all of these conditions:

- The account has a binding.
- `keccak256("tempo:org:recovery" || recoveryAddress || recoverySalt)` equals the recovery commitment of the binding.
- No recovery rotation is pending.

The fee payer can be any account, for example a recovery service. A freeze does not stop an eject.

**Eject without new owners.** If `newConfig` is empty, the precompile deletes the binding at once and emits `MemberEjected`. The account keeps its owners. Use this form only if the member controls the owners.

**Eject with new owners.** If `newConfig` is not empty, the precompile records the pending eject with an unlock time of `now + RECOVERY_DELAY`, and emits `EjectStarted`. While the eject is pending:

- On Tempo, every call and debit from the account reverts.
- In the zone, when the anchor includes `EjectStarted`, the zone state transition function stops every send from the account, except plugin exits. It ends the stop when the anchor includes `EjectCancelled`.
- Only the recovery address can cancel, with `cancelEject`. The owners cannot cancel, because owners that a provider or role holder controls could then block every exit.

After the unlock time, anyone can call `completeEject(account)`. The precompile does these steps:

1. It replaces the TIP-1107 configuration of the account with `newConfig` and increases its version by one.
2. It revokes every access key of the account on Tempo. When the zone anchor includes this block, the zone revokes every access key of the account in the zone.
3. It voids every TIP-20 allowance and permit nonce that the account set before this version, on Tempo and in the zone.
4. It deletes the binding and emits `MemberEjected`.

As a result, the old owners keep no access key or allowance that works after the eject. This is a new configuration write path in TIP-1107. It does not need an owner quorum.

Between `EjectStarted` on Tempo and its inclusion in the zone anchor, the old owners can still spend within the policy. `RECOVERY_DELAY` MUST be much longer than the maximum anchor delay of the zone. A wallet SHOULD use an eject with new owners if a provider or a role holder holds the owner keys.

**In the zone.** When the zone anchor includes the block with `MemberEjected`, the zone state transition function does these steps for the account:

1. It ends the member generation, so every plugin grant of the account becomes invalid.
2. It marks the member record as ejected. The registry keeps the record, so the address can never be a member again.
3. It stops applying the organization policies and the freeze.
4. It emits `MemberLeft`.

These steps have a fixed cost. The address, the balances and the plugin positions of the account do not change, and its member addresses still credit it. The account is then a regular configurable account in the zone, and it can withdraw to any Tempo address, including its own.

**Recovery rotation.** The recovery address calls `rotateRecovery(account, recoveryAddress, recoverySalt, newCommitment)` with the same caller check as `eject`. `newCommitment` MUST differ from every earlier commitment of the account. The rotation applies after `RECOVERY_DELAY`, and the current recovery address can cancel it during the delay. An eject cannot start while a rotation is pending, and a rotation cannot start while an eject is pending. As a result, an attacker who copies the recovery key cannot lock out the real holder by rotating first.

### Withdrawals and forced exit

A member withdraws from the zone through the zone outbox. The zone requires the `to` of the withdrawal to be a withdrawal payee of the member policy. If `to` is the member itself or another member, the zone also requires that the binding of that account is visible at the zone anchor. Before then, the Tempo address of the account is not bound, and funds sent there are not under the policy.

The current [forced exit](../crates/zones/exithatch/README.md) design accepts only a primitive root-key signature, and it excludes counterfactual accounts. A member account is a configurable account, so it cannot use that forced exit. This TIP requires the forced exit to also accept a TIP-1107 owner quorum, checked against the commitment at the zone anchor. With that change, these rules apply:

- A current member can request a forced exit only to the member account itself on Tempo, and only after its binding is visible at the anchor. Those funds stay bound on Tempo, so they can only go back into the zone until the member ejects.
- An ejected account can request a forced exit to any recipient.

Without that change, the exit path is an eject on Tempo, then a normal withdrawal from the zone.

Both paths need a live sequencer.

### Zones

An organization lives in exactly one zone. Organizations in different zones can share settlement under [TIP-1149](./tip-1149.md). A member moves to another zone only after it ejects, through a withdrawal to Tempo and a deposit.

The zone state transition function enforces every rule in this TIP. A batch that breaks a rule cannot be proven, so the portal cannot accept it.

## Interfaces

### Zone predeploy

```solidity
interface IOrganizationRegistry {
    struct Roles {
        address owner;
        address membershipAdmin;
        address policyAdmin;
        address pluginAdmin;
    }

    struct CallScope {
        address target;
        bytes4[] selectors; // empty means every selector of target
    }

    struct Limit {
        address token;
        uint256 amount;
        uint64 period; // seconds; 0 means one limit for the life of the membership
    }

    // Bit flags for the sender classes of the inbound filter:
    // 1 = members, 2 = passthrough addresses, 4 = enabled plugins, 8 = deposits from Tempo
    struct Policy {
        address[] inboundTokens;
        uint8 inboundSenderClasses;
        address[] inboundSenders;
        CallScope[] calls;
        address[] withdrawalPayees;
        Limit[] limits;
    }

    enum Role { Owner, MembershipAdmin, PolicyAdmin, PluginAdmin }

    // Organizations
    function createOrganization(bytes32 salt, Roles calldata roles, uint64 pluginTimelock)
        external returns (bytes32 orgId);
```

# Compatibility

Existing accounts do not change. They cannot join an organization. To put the users of existing accounts in an organization, the membership admin creates new member accounts, and each user moves its funds.

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Plugins | Each plugin is a zone predeploy with its own TIP. It checks members with `isMember`, pulls funds with `pull` under a grant, and implements `exit`. It never holds member keys. |
| Contracts on Tempo | A contract on Tempo cannot read membership. It deals with a plugin of the organization, which acts for all members. |
| Deposits from Tempo | A payer sends to a TIP-1094 zone deposit address that encrypts an org address. |
| Withdrawals to Tempo | A member pays out through a passthrough address, or withdraws to a withdrawal payee of its policy. |
| Wallets | A wallet calls `createMember` with an owner configuration, a recovery commitment and a random `userSalt`. The wallet MUST NOT control the recovery address alone. It MUST use a fresh recovery account and a fresh `recoverySalt` for each membership, and MUST NOT reuse owner keys across member accounts. |
| Recovery services | A recovery service co-owns the recovery address of a member and can pay the fee for `eject`. It watches `EjectStarted` and `RotationStarted` for the accounts that it serves. |
| Zone forced exit | The forced exit accepts a TIP-1107 owner quorum, checked against the commitment at the zone anchor. |
| Zone portal | The portal writes bindings at batch settlement. In closed access mode, it treats bound and ejected accounts of its zone as allowed. It credits deposits from a bound account to that account. |
| Zone RPC | The read and event rules in [Read access](#read-access). |
| Configurable accounts | The binding registers the configuration commitment without an event. `completeEject` writes a new configuration without an owner quorum. Both are new write paths in TIP-1107. |
| Access key policies | [TIP-1161](./tip-1161.md) policy contracts apply to one access key. The outbound filter of this TIP applies to every key of a member account. |

SDK support: viem actions for the registry and the binding precompile, a helper that computes the member address and the recovery commitment, and a helper that builds an eject transaction for a recovery service.

# Threat Model

The most consequential attack is a stolen recovery key. With it, an attacker can start an eject with new owners and take the account after `RECOVERY_DELAY`. The real holder can cancel during the delay. If both parties hold the key, each can cancel the other, and the account stays locked until one of them stops. The attacker cannot rotate the key away from the real holder, because a rotation also waits `RECOVERY_DELAY` and the current holder can cancel it. To reduce this risk, the recovery address SHOULD be a configurable account with more than one owner, for example the passkey of the member and a recovery service.

| Actor | Controls | Enforced limit | If compromised |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organization owner | Role assignment | Roles cannot move funds, change owners or block an eject | Same as all roles below together. |
| Membership admin | Member creation, groups, member addresses | New addresses only. Tags are never reused. It cannot remove a member. | Creates unwanted members, or moves members to a stricter group. |
| Policy admin | Policies, freeze, passthrough addresses | Eject and plugin exits stay open. Payees and tags never change. | Freezes every member. Members can still exit plugins and eject. |
| Plugin admin | Enabled plugins | Timelock. Plugins are predeploys. | Enables a plugin that members do not want. Members see it during the timelock. |
| Plugin | Member deposits, plugin grants | Grant limits, outbound limits. A pull goes only to the plugin. `exit` is always open. | Bounded by its own TIP. Loses at most the deposits and the grant limits. |
| Holder of member owner keys | Signing for the member | The organization policy | Spends within the policy. The member recovers with an eject with new owners. |
| Recovery address | Eject and rotation | `RECOVERY_DELAY` for owner changes and rotations | Ejects the member. With new owners, takes the account after the delay, unless the real holder cancels. |
| Zone sequencer | Ordering, inclusion, liveness, anchor | Proof of the state transition | Cannot break the rules or forge bindings. Can stop or censor the zone, which stops all zone balances. Can delay the anchor, which delays the lock of an eject with new owners. |
| Observer on Tempo | Public chain data | Bindings do not name the organization. Epochs and sort order hide creation batches. | Learns which addresses are bound to the zone, portal flows, and owner configurations that members use on Tempo. |
| Zone user | Transfers to members | Raw member addresses are not payees. Getters and events are access-controlled. | Learns whether a member refuses a transfer from it. |

Residual risks:

- **Sequencer liveness.** An eject on Tempo always succeeds, but a member can move zone funds only while the sequencer is live. This is the same trust that every zone has today. A permissionless exit for zones would remove this risk. It is outside this TIP.
- **Anchor delay.** The old owners can spend within the policy between `EjectStarted` and its inclusion in the zone anchor.
- **Linking on Tempo.** Owner keys that members share, deposits from member addresses, and recovery addresses that members reuse can link members. The wallet rules in [Compatibility](#compatibility) prevent this. The protocol cannot enforce them.
- **Single-organization zones.** In a zone with one organization, a binding reveals membership in that organization.

Excluded threats: a party that controls both the owner keys and the recovery address of a member. That party controls the account. The protocol cannot see who controls an address.

# Invariants

- No role holder can decrease the balance of a member account, except through a plugin pull under a grant that the member made.
- No role holder can change the owners or the recovery commitment of a member account.
- An account becomes a member only through `createMember`, only at an address that was never a member and never bound, and only with a zero commitment, a zero nonce and no code.
- An account is a member of at most one organization, and never a member twice.
- A valid eject succeeds when the member is frozen, and under every policy.
- While an eject with new owners is pending, no key can send from the account on Tempo. After the zone anchor includes `EjectStarted`, no key can send from it in the zone, except plugin exits.
- The effective policy of a member never permits more than the base policy.
- Every TIP-20 debit from a member in the zone passes the outbound filter and the limits.
- Every TIP-20 debit from a bound account on Tempo credits its zone portal, and every deposit from it credits the account itself in the zone.
- A plugin pull sends funds only to the calling plugin, and never more than the grant and the outbound limits permit.
- A member can call `exit` on every plugin that was ever enabled for its organization, in every state.
- An org address tag is used at most once in a zone. The target of an org address never changes.
- No registry getter returns state about an account to a caller that the read rules do not permit, in direct or nested calls.
- The gas cost of a transfer does not depend on any policy.
- No address is a member in two zones.
- After `completeEject`, no access key or allowance from before the new configuration version works, on Tempo or in the zone.

The test suite must cover these cases:

- Eject during a freeze, and eject under an empty policy.
- An eject with new owners: no send on Tempo during the delay, no send in the zone after anchor inclusion, owners cannot cancel, the recovery address can cancel, and completion after the delay.
- A recovery rotation during a pending eject, and an eject during a pending rotation.
- `createMember` at an address that is registered, was a member, or was bound.
- A group policy that tries to add a call target that the base policy does not permit.
- A plugin pull above the grant, after the grant expires, after the plugin is disabled, and from a frozen member.
- `exit` while frozen, after the plugin is disabled, and after eject.
- A transfer to a deleted passthrough address, and a new passthrough that tries to reuse its tag.
- A bound account on Tempo that calls a target other than its zone portal, uses `transferFrom` or `permit` toward another address, or deposits to another zone recipient.
- A non-member that calls `memberOf`, `isMember` or `canCall`, directly and through a predeploy.
- A batch that omits or changes a binding, or binds an address that was bound before.
- An access key and an allowance of the old owners, used after `completeEject`.
- A passthrough whose payee is an org address.
- A withdrawal to a member before its binding is visible at the anchor.
