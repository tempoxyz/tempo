# TIP-1126: TIP-20 Asset Token Extensions

## Abstract

This TIP lets issuers of tokenized stocks, funds, and other securities issue them as ordinary TIP-20 tokens. It adds to every TIP-20 token a choice of 6 or 18 decimals, partial freezes and forced transfers for transfer agents (ERC-7943), a display multiplier for splits (ERC-8056), renaming, and issuer metadata such as an ISIN.

This TIP also defines how the most common corporate actions work, on Tempo and inside zones: renames and ticker changes, stock splits, dividends, and mergers. Actions that are the same for every holder, such as a rename or a split, change only the token's state on Tempo, and zones read that state directly. Actions that pay each holder in proportion to their holdings, such as a cash dividend or a merger, treat each zone as a single holder on Tempo. The issuer pays the zone's share in one call, and the zone passes it through to its holders.

A token that never uses these features behaves exactly as it does today.

## Motivation

Issuers of tokenized assets need controls that TIP-20 does not have:

- **Decimals.** TIP-20 is fixed at 6 decimals. Many asset tokens and bridged assets use 18, and 6 decimals causes rounding problems for bridges and trading.
- **Freezes, seizures, and lost keys.** Transfer agents must hold or move specific amounts under court orders and sanctions, and recover holdings when a holder loses a key. ERC-7943 standardizes these controls.
- **Corporate actions.** Issuers rename tokens, split shares, pay dividends, and retire shares when companies merge. TIP-20 cannot rename a token, and has no standard way to record a split.
- **Security identifiers.** Integrators need to map a token to the security it represents, for example by ISIN.

Zones are the reason many issuers want a native token at all: they let holders keep and settle positions privately. That only works if corporate actions reach holders in a zone. A zone runs no issuer contracts and its balances are private, so an issuer cannot see who to pay, and cannot run its usual process there. This TIP gives every action in scope a defined path into zones.

### Alternatives Considered

1. **A separate token type at its own address prefix.** An earlier draft of this TIP. It duplicates routing, recipient validation, keychain rules, and zone enablement for a second prefix, and existing issuers would lose their token address to adopt it. Every feature here fits TIP-20 directly.
2. **A separate prefix only for 18-decimal tokens.** Decimals do not affect any transfer path. The only components that assume 6 decimals are the fee manager and the stablecoin DEX, and both accept only `USD` tokens. Forbidding `USD` with 18 decimals protects them without a second prefix.
3. **Rebasing balances for splits.** Rebasing changes `balanceOf` whenever the share count changes, which breaks DeFi accounting. ERC-8056 keeps balances raw and changes only how they are displayed.
4. **A dividend accumulator on every Tempo token.** Each transfer would settle per-holder dividend state, as TIP-20 rewards did. TIP-1075 removed rewards for exactly that cost on the payment path. This TIP keeps Tempo transfers unchanged and puts per-holder settlement only inside zones, where it is needed.
5. **Wrapping assets before they enter a zone.** A wrapper contract would hold the asset on Tempo and pay dividends when holders leave the zone. Holders would wait until exit to receive cash, the wrapper would need its own compliance controls, and integrators would see two tokens per asset.
6. **A token-level hook that calls issuer code on every transfer.** Precompiles cannot call EVM code today, and zones cannot run arbitrary contracts. Issuer logic belongs in a future TIP-403 policy type (see [Extension Points](#extension-points-non-normative)).

## Assumptions

| Assumption | Consequence if violated |
|---|---|
| The TIP-20 implementation at activation includes TIP-1006 (`burnAt`), TIP-1028 (receive policies), and TIP-1075 (rewards deprecated). | The frozen-token rules for `burnAt` and receive-policy redirects refer to behavior that does not exist. |
| The fee manager and stablecoin DEX accept a token only if its `currency()` is `USD`, and these are the only components that assume 6 decimals. | An 18-decimal token could reach a component that misprices it. Such a component needs its own decimals check. |
| Zones read selected Tempo storage at their Tempo anchor and reject zone writes to it, as they do today for the TIP-403 registry and, from T15, the TIP-20 pause flag. | Token state could differ between Tempo and a zone. The [zone rules](#zones) would need a different delivery path. |
| A zone processes its portal's queued entries in queue order, before any zone transaction that follows them. | The record point of a distribution would not be well defined in the zone. |
| ERC-8056 is a Draft. This TIP follows the text and reference implementation at ERCs commit `554d3467b29742aae0f864cdd2fdb492510a91ba` (2026-09-01). | Later changes to ERC-8056 do not apply unless a TIP adopts them. |

# Specification

## Overview

### Features

Every TIP-20 token gains these features at activation:

| Feature | What the issuer can do | Standard | Gated by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decimals | Choose 6 or 18 at creation. Immutable. | — | Set at creation |
| Partial freeze | Hold a chosen amount of an account's tokens. The rest moves normally. | ERC-7943 | `FREEZE_ROLE` |
| Forced transfer | Move tokens without the holder's authorization, for seizures and lost-key recovery. | ERC-7943 | `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` |
| Display multiplier | Record splits and reinvested dividends without changing any balance. | ERC-8056 | `CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE` |
| Name and symbol | Update `name` and `symbol` after creation. | — | `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` |
| Metadata | Publish a contract URI and key-value entries such as an ISIN. | ERC-7572 (URI) | `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` |

Nobody holds a new role until the admin grants it. Existing tokens read as 6 decimals, a multiplier of `1e18`, nothing frozen, and empty metadata.

### Corporate Actions

This TIP covers five corporate actions. The table shows what the issuer does on Tempo and what happens for holders in a zone.

| Action | On Tempo | In a zone |
|---|---|---|
| Rename or ticker change | `setName`, `setSymbol` | The zone reads the new name and symbol from Tempo. Nothing to do. |
| Split or reverse split | `setUIMultiplier` | The zone reads the multiplier from Tempo. Nothing to do. |
| Dividend reinvested in shares | `setUIMultiplier` | Same as a split. |
| Cash dividend | The issuer snapshots holders and pays each one, as issuers do today | The issuer pays the zone's share with one `distribute` call. The zone credits each holder in proportion to their balance. |
| Merger, acquisition, or delisting | The issuer burns the old token with `burnAt` and pays holders the new token or cash | `distribute` with `cancelAsset`. The zone credits the consideration and cancels the old token. |

The first three are **token-level** actions: they change one value that applies to every holder. The last two are **holder-level** actions: each holder receives an amount proportional to what they hold at a record point. Elective actions, where each holder chooses what to receive, are out of scope (see [Open Questions](#open-questions)).

### Zones in Brief

A zone is a private chain attached to Tempo. Its **portal** is a contract on Tempo that holds every token deposited into the zone. On Tempo, the portal looks like a single large holder of each token.

- **Token-level state** (name, symbol, decimals, multiplier, metadata, pause, transfer policy) is read by the zone from Tempo. The issuer changes it once, on Tempo.
- **Holder-level actions** go through the portal. The issuer pays the portal's share of a dividend or merger with `distribute`, as if the portal were any other holder. The zone splits that payment among its holders.
- **Freezes and forced transfers** apply to Tempo balances. To act on one holder inside a zone, the issuer blocks the address in its transfer policy, which the zone enforces, or queues a `forceWithdraw` through the portal.

### Example

An issuer creates an 18-decimal stock token with currency `""` and an allowlist transfer policy bound at creation. It grants `FREEZE_ROLE` and `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` to its compliance contract and `CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE` to its corporate-actions desk. Alice holds 100 shares on Tempo. Bob holds 40 shares inside a zone, and the zone holds 1,000 shares in total.

1. **Split.** The company does a 4-for-1 split. The issuer schedules the multiplier to change from `1e18` to `4e18`. Once it takes effect, Alice's `balanceOf` is still 100 and her `balanceOfUI` is 400. The zone reads the same multiplier, so Bob's wallet shows 160.
2. **Cash dividend.** The company pays $0.25 per share. Each token now represents 4 shares, so the issuer pays $1.00 per token. It pauses the token, calls `distribute` on the zone's portal at $1.00 per token, snapshots Tempo holders, unpauses, and pays Alice $100 in a batched transfer. The portal pulls $1,000 for its 1,000 tokens. Inside the zone, Bob can claim $40.
3. **Freeze and seizure.** A court freezes 30 of Alice's 100 tokens. She can still transfer up to 70. The court then orders 80 tokens moved. `forcedTransfer` takes her 70 unfrozen tokens and 10 frozen ones, and leaves her a balance of 20, all frozen.
4. **Merger.** Another company acquires the stock at 0.5 acquirer shares per share, which is 2 acquirer tokens per token. The issuer calls `distribute` on the portal with the acquirer's token, a rate of 2, and `cancelAsset` set. Inside the zone, Bob's 40 tokens are cancelled and Bob can claim 80 acquirer tokens. On Tempo, the issuer burns Alice's remaining 20 tokens with `burnAt` and mints her 40 acquirer tokens.
5. **Rename.** The surviving company changes its ticker. The admin calls `setSymbol`. Wallets on Tempo and in the zone show the new symbol.

### Definitions

```
frozen(a)   = frozen amount of a
unfrozen(a) = balanceOf(a) - frozen(a)
```

`balanceOf(a)` is always the holder's total balance, frozen and unfrozen together.

**Protected addresses** are the addresses `burnAt` refuses: the TIP-20 `PROTECTED` system custody addresses active at the current hardfork (including `ReceivePolicyGuard`), the token's own address, and ZonePortal addresses. They can never be frozen, force-debited, or force-credited.

**Asset** is the token a corporate action applies to. **Payout token** is the token holders receive from a distribution: a stablecoin for a cash dividend, or the acquirer's token for a merger.

### Naming

Names follow TIP-20 conventions (`setX` setters, `XUpdated(address indexed updater, …)` events, one role per kind of privileged action), except where an ERC fixes the name. ERC-7943, ERC-8056, and ERC-7572 names are used exactly, because integrators detect and call them by selector.

## Activation

All behavior in this TIP applies from the protocol version assigned to it, to every TIP-20 token and every zone portal. Before activation, the new functions follow unknown-selector behavior.

## Token Creation

`TIP20Factory` adds a `createToken` overload with two more arguments, `decimals` and `transferPolicyId`. It validates everything before writing any state:

1. Revert `InvalidDecimals` unless `decimals` is 6 or 18.
2. Revert `InvalidCurrency` if `decimals` is 18 and `currency` is `USD`.
3. Revert `InvalidTransferPolicyId` unless `transferPolicyId` exists in the TIP-403 registry. The built-in allow-all (`1`) and reject-all (`0`) policies exist.
4. Apply every check of the existing `createToken` overload with `logoURI`.

It then creates the token as that overload does, stores `decimals`, and binds `transferPolicyId` to the token. Binding the policy at creation means a regulated token is never unrestricted, even before its first mint. It emits the same events as the existing overload, then `TokenCreatedWithDecimals(token, decimals, transferPolicyId)`.

Tokens created with the existing overloads have 6 decimals and the allow-all policy, as today.

An issuer keeps an asset token out of the fee manager and stablecoin DEX by choosing a `currency` other than `USD`, since both already reject non-`USD` tokens. Asset tokens SHOULD use a currency other than `USD`, and SHOULD record the underlying asset's denomination in [extra metadata](#extra-metadata).

## Decimals

`decimals()` returns 6 or 18. It never changes after creation. Amounts in every function and event stay in raw units.

## Name and Symbol

`setName(newName)` and `setSymbol(newSymbol)`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`.
2. Store the new value.
3. Emit `NameUpdated(msg.sender, newName)` or `SymbolUpdated(msg.sender, newSymbol)`.

They apply the same validation as `createToken` applies to `name` and `symbol`, and work while the token is paused.

The EIP-712 domain separator used by `permit` includes `name`, so a rename changes `DOMAIN_SEPARATOR()`. Permits signed before the rename fail after it. Nonces are unchanged.

## Frozen Tokens

An account with `FREEZE_ROLE` sets a frozen amount per account. The holder cannot move that many tokens, and the rest of the balance moves normally. The frozen amount can never exceed the balance.

### `setFrozenTokens(account, amount)`

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `FREEZE_ROLE`.
2. Revert `ProtectedAddress` if `account` is protected.
3. Revert `VirtualAddressNotAllowed` if `account` is a TIP-1022 virtual address. Virtual addresses hold no balance; their master does.
4. Revert `InsufficientBalance` if `amount > balanceOf(account)`.
5. Set `frozen(account) = amount`.
6. Emit `Frozen(account, amount)`. Return `true`.

`setFrozenTokens` works while the token is paused. To hold tokens that have not arrived yet, the issuer freezes them after they arrive, or uses the transfer policy to block the account.

### Effect on Each Operation

| Operation | Debited account | Limited to `unfrozen`? | Changes `frozen`? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `transfer`, `transferWithMemo` | caller | Yes | No |
| `transferFrom`, `transferFromWithMemo` | `from` | Yes | No |
| `burn`, `burnWithMemo` | caller | Yes | No |
| Deposit into a zone | depositor | Yes | No |
| Receive-policy redirect of any of the above (TIP-1028) | sender | Yes, same check as the transfer | No |
| Fee payment through the fee manager | payer | Yes | No |
| `burnAt`, `burnBlocked` | burned account | No | Yes, see below |
| `forcedTransfer` | `from` | No | Yes, see below |
| `mint`, incoming transfers | none | n/a | No |
| `approve`, `permit` | none | n/a | No |

**Limited operations.** A limited debit of `amount` from `a` reverts if `amount > unfrozen(a)`. If `amount <= balanceOf(a)`, the error is `ERC7943InsufficientUnfrozenBalance(a, amount, unfrozen(a))`; otherwise it is `InsufficientBalance`. The check runs before the TIP-1028 receive-policy check, so it also limits a transfer that would be redirected to `ReceivePolicyGuard`.

**Privileged debits.** Let `u = unfrozen(a)` before the debit. If `amount > u`, then before the balance changes `frozen(a) = frozen(a) - (amount - u)`, and the token emits `Frozen(a, frozen(a))` before the `Transfer` event.

### Storage of Frozen Amounts

`balances[a]` holds the unfrozen amount, and a separate `frozen[a]` mapping holds the frozen amount. `balanceOf(a)` returns their sum, so wallets show holders their full position. `setFrozenTokens` moves tokens between the two slots and leaves `balanceOf` and `totalSupply` unchanged.

Transfers, fee payment, and the transaction pool's fee-balance check read only `balances[a]`, as today. The frozen check costs nothing on the payment path, and no component outside the token needs to know about freezes. `frozen[a]` is read only by `balanceOf`, the failure path of a limited debit (to choose the error), and privileged debits.

Storing the frozen amount next to an unchanged total balance would add a second storage read to every transfer and fee payment, and would need frozen checks in the fee manager and transaction pool.

## Forced Transfer

`forcedTransfer(from, to, amount)`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE`.
2. Revert `ProtectedAddress` if `from` is protected.
3. Resolve `to` as TIP-20 transfers do (TIP-1022 virtual addresses resolve to their master). Revert `InvalidRecipient` if the resolved target is zero or has the TIP-20 prefix. Revert `ProtectedAddress` if it is protected.
4. Revert `ERC7943CannotReceive(to)` if `canReceive(to)` is false.
5. Revert `PolicyForbids` if the resolved target's TIP-1028 receive policy rejects this token from `from`. A forced transfer is never redirected to `ReceivePolicyGuard`, because the guard's recovery path could return seized tokens to `from`.
6. Revert `InsufficientBalance` if `amount > balanceOf(from)`.
7. Apply the [privileged-debit](#effect-on-each-operation) rule to `from`.
8. Debit `from` and credit the target. Emit `Transfer(from, to, amount)` (plus the TIP-1022 forwarding `Transfer` for a virtual `to`), then `ForcedTransfer(from, to, amount)`. Return `true`.

A forced transfer does not check the pause state, the sender leg of the transfer policy, allowances, or access-key spending limits. A lost-wallet recovery is a `forcedTransfer` of the full balance to the holder's new address.

## Permission Views

The three views revert only if they run out of gas. On any other internal error they return `false`.

- `canSend(account)`: the token's TIP-403 transfer policy authorizes `account` as sender.
- `canReceive(account)`: `account` resolves as a transfer recipient (TIP-1022), and the policy authorizes the resolved address as recipient.
- `canTransfer(from, to, amount)`: the token is not paused, `canSend(from)` and `canReceive(to)` are true, and `amount <= unfrozen(from)`.

They do not evaluate TIP-1028 receive policies, allowances, or access-key spending limits.

## UI Multiplier

The multiplier tells wallets how to display balances. It never changes balances, transfers, allowances, total supply, the supply cap, or any event amount. Issuers use it for splits, reverse splits, and dividends reinvested in shares.

The token stores the multiplier in effect now (`uiMultiplier`), a scheduled multiplier (`newUIMultiplier`), and when the scheduled one takes effect (`effectiveAt`). A stored multiplier of 0 reads as `1e18`, so existing tokens need no migration.

```
UI_MULTIPLIER_ONE  = 1e18
MAX_UI_MULTIPLIER  = 1e36
```

### Views

These follow the ERC-8056 reference implementation:

- `uiMultiplier()`: `newUIMultiplier` if `block.timestamp >= effectiveAt`, else `uiMultiplier`.
- `newUIMultiplier()`, `effectiveAt()`: the stored values.
- `toUIAmount(raw) = raw * uiMultiplier() / 1e18`, rounded down.
- `fromUIAmount(ui) = ui * 1e18 / uiMultiplier()`, rounded down.
- `balanceOfUI(a) = toUIAmount(balanceOf(a))`. `totalSupplyUI() = toUIAmount(totalSupply())`.

`toUIAmount` and `fromUIAmount` revert on overflow.

### Scheduling an Update

`setUIMultiplier(newMultiplier, effectiveAtTimestamp)`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE`.
2. Revert `InvalidUIMultiplier` unless `0 < newMultiplier <= MAX_UI_MULTIPLIER`.
3. Revert `InvalidEffectiveAt` unless `block.timestamp < effectiveAtTimestamp < 2^64`.
4. If no update is pending (`block.timestamp >= effectiveAt`): let `old = newUIMultiplier`. Set `uiMultiplier = old`, `newUIMultiplier = newMultiplier`, `effectiveAt = effectiveAtTimestamp`. Emit `UIMultiplierUpdated(old, newMultiplier, effectiveAtTimestamp)`.
5. If an update is pending: emit `UIMultiplierUpdateCancelled(newUIMultiplier, effectiveAt)`. Set `newUIMultiplier = newMultiplier` and `effectiveAt = effectiveAtTimestamp`. Emit `UIMultiplierUpdated(uiMultiplier, newMultiplier, effectiveAtTimestamp)`.

`cancelUIMultiplierUpdate()`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE`.
2. Revert `NoPendingUIMultiplierUpdate` unless `block.timestamp < effectiveAt`.
3. Emit `UIMultiplierUpdateCancelled(newUIMultiplier, effectiveAt)`. Set `newUIMultiplier = uiMultiplier` and `effectiveAt = 0`.

Both work while the token is paused, so an issuer can pause trading, apply a corporate action, and unpause. No event is emitted when a scheduled multiplier takes effect; indexers derive that moment from `effectiveAt`.

## Metadata

### Contract URI

`contractURI()` returns the stored string. `setContractURI(newContractURI)` requires `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`. It reverts `ContractURITooLong` if the value is longer than 1024 bytes, and `InvalidContractURI` if the value is non-empty and its scheme fails the TIP-1026 `logoURI` scheme rules. It then stores the value and emits `ContractURIUpdated()`, which has no parameters because ERC-7572 defines it that way.

### Extra Metadata

`extraMetadata(key)` returns the value stored under `key`, or the empty string.

`setExtraMetadata(key, newValue)`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`.
2. Revert `InvalidExtraMetadataKey` if `key` is empty or longer than 32 bytes.
3. Revert `ExtraMetadataValueTooLong` if `newValue` is longer than 256 bytes.
4. Store `newValue` under `key`, or remove the entry if `newValue` is empty.
5. Emit `ExtraMetadataUpdated(msg.sender, key, newValue)`.

Keys are case-sensitive. Entries cannot be listed on-chain; indexers rebuild them from events. Issuers SHOULD use lowercase keys, SHOULD store a 12-character ISIN under `isin`, and SHOULD store the underlying asset's denomination under `denomination`.

## Roles

| Role | Gates |
|---|---|
| `FREEZE_ROLE` | `setFrozenTokens` |
| `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` | `forcedTransfer`; `forceWithdraw` on a portal |
| `CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE` | `setUIMultiplier`, `cancelUIMultiplierUpdate`; `distribute` on a portal |
| `BURN_AT_ROLE` (TIP-1006) | `burnAt`; additionally required for `distribute` with `cancelAsset` |

`DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` administers all of them and gates `setName`, `setSymbol`, `setContractURI`, and `setExtraMetadata`, as it gates `setLogoURI`.

Freezing and forced transfer are separate roles, so an issuer can grant the ability to hold funds without the ability to move them. A cancelling distribution destroys holders' tokens inside a zone, so it requires the same `BURN_AT_ROLE` that authorizes destroying them on Tempo.

## Zones

A zone keeps its own balances, but its copy of each TIP-20 token takes token-level state from Tempo. This section defines what a zone reads from Tempo, which controls stop at the portal, and how holder-level corporate actions reach zone holders.

### Token State Read from Tempo

The zone reads these fields of each enabled token from Tempo state at its Tempo anchor, in the same way it reads the TIP-403 registry and the pause flag today:

- `name`, `symbol`, and `decimals`
- `uiMultiplier`, `newUIMultiplier`, and `effectiveAt`
- `logoURI`, `contractURI`, and every `extraMetadata` entry
- the pause flag and the transfer policy binding (unchanged from today)

Zone transactions MUST NOT write these fields. The zone rejects every setter for them (`setName`, `setSymbol`, `setUIMultiplier`, `cancelUIMultiplierUpdate`, `setContractURI`, `setExtraMetadata`, `setLogoURI`) for every caller.

A rename, split, or reinvested dividend therefore reaches the zone as soon as the zone imports the Tempo block that contains it. A scheduled multiplier takes effect in the zone when the zone's block timestamp reaches `effectiveAt`.

### Controls That Stop at the Portal

Frozen amounts belong to Tempo accounts. The zone does not read `frozen[a]`, and zone balances are never frozen. A frozen amount cannot enter a zone, because a deposit is a limited debit.

`forcedTransfer`, `burnAt`, and `burnBlocked` cannot reach zone balances, because the portal is a protected address. The issuer has two ways to act on one holder inside a zone:

1. **Block the address.** The issuer adds the address to the token's TIP-403 policy. The zone enforces the policy from Tempo, so the address can no longer send, receive, or withdraw the token in the zone.
2. **Queue a forced withdrawal.** The issuer calls `forceWithdraw` on the portal, which moves the holder's zone balance to a Tempo address the issuer names. See below.

`forceWithdraw(asset, account, amount, to)` on a portal:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` on `asset`.
2. Revert `TokenNotEnabled` unless `asset` is enabled in the zone.
3. Revert `InvalidRecipient` if `to` is zero, has the TIP-20 prefix, or is protected.
4. Append a forced-withdrawal entry to the portal's deposit queue and emit `ForcedWithdrawalQueued(id, asset, account, amount, to)`.

When the zone processes the entry, it debits `min(amount, balance of account)` from `account`, ignoring the zone's pause, transfer policy, and transfer opt-in, and queues a withdrawal of that amount to `to`. It emits `ForcedWithdrawalApplied(id, asset, account, debited)` on the zone. The amount released on Tempo shows the issuer whether the holder had at least `amount`; the issuer names the account, so this reveals nothing about anyone else.

### Distributions

A distribution pays every zone holder of an asset a fixed amount of a payout token per unit of the asset they hold at a record point. On Tempo, the issuer treats the portal as one holder: it pays the portal's share with `distribute` instead of a transfer. The zone then splits that share among its holders.

`distribute(asset, payoutToken, rate, cancelAsset, refundRecipient)` on a portal:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE` on `asset`, and also `BURN_AT_ROLE` if `cancelAsset` is true.
2. Revert `TokenNotEnabled` unless `asset` is enabled in the zone, and `payoutToken` too if `rate > 0`.
3. Revert `InvalidDistribution` if `payoutToken == asset`, if `rate == 0` and `cancelAsset` is false, or if `asset` is already cancelled in this portal.
4. Revert `TooManyPayoutTokens` if `payoutToken` would be the fifth distinct payout token distributed for `asset` in this portal.
5. Let `held` be the portal's balance of `asset`. Pull `funding = ceil(rate * held / 1e18)` of `payoutToken` from the caller.
6. If `cancelAsset` is true, mark `asset` cancelled in this portal. Deposits of a cancelled asset revert from this point on.
7. Append a distribution entry to the portal's deposit queue and emit `DistributionQueued(id, asset, payoutToken, msg.sender, rate, funding, cancelAsset, refundRecipient)`.

`rate` is in payout-token base units per asset base unit, scaled by `1e18`. It is the same per-token amount the issuer pays holders on Tempo, so Tempo and zone holders are paid at the same rate. `rate` is per raw token, not per displayed share: an issuer converts a per-share amount by multiplying it by `uiMultiplier() / 1e18`. A delisting with no consideration uses `rate == 0` with `cancelAsset`.

`distribute` is authorized by the asset's roles, not by the portal's depositor allowlist, and works while the asset is paused.

#### Applying a Distribution in the Zone

The zone applies the entry when it reaches it in the queue. That moment is the **record point** in the zone: every deposit queued before the entry has been credited, and none queued after it.

At the record point the zone:

1. Reads `S`, the zone's total supply of `asset`.
2. Records the distribution. Each holder `h` is entitled to `floor(rate * balance(h) / 1e18)` of `payoutToken`, where `balance(h)` is `h`'s balance of `asset` at the record point.
3. Queues a withdrawal of the **refund**, `funding - ceil(rate * S / 1e18)`, to `refundRecipient` on Tempo, if it is not zero.
4. If `cancelAsset` is true, sets the zone's supply of `asset` to zero, treats every holder's balance of `asset` as zero from now on, and rejects every later transfer, withdrawal, and deposit of `asset`. It queues a withdrawal of `S` of `asset` to `refundRecipient`, so the issuer can burn it on Tempo.
5. Emits `DistributionApplied(id, asset, payoutToken, rate, S, refund)` on the zone.

The refund is the difference between the portal's balance on Tempo and the zone's supply. That difference is made up of withdrawals the zone had already processed before the record point but Tempo had not yet released. Their owners receive the asset on Tempo after the issuer's snapshot, so the issuer pays them from the refund.

#### Settlement

The zone does not visit every holder at the record point. It settles each holder lazily:

- Before any change to `h`'s balance of `asset` in the zone (a transfer, a withdrawal, or a deposit credit), the zone adds `h`'s unpaid entitlements to `h`'s claimable amount of each payout token.
- `claimDistributions(asset)` settles the caller and credits the caller's claimable amounts of every payout token for `asset`. For a cancelled asset, it also clears the caller's asset balance. It emits `DistributionClaimed(account, asset, payoutToken, amount)` for each payout token with a non-zero amount.
- `pendingDistribution(asset, payoutToken, account)` returns `account`'s unclaimed amount, including entitlements not yet settled. Like `balanceOf` in a zone, only `account` may read it.

Settlement uses one cumulative per-unit index for each pair of asset and payout token, so the work per settlement is bounded by the four-payout-token limit. It fits inside the zone's fixed gas charge for transfers, so the cost of a transfer reveals nothing about pending distributions.

Credits from a distribution are backed by the funding the portal pulled: the zone never credits more than `ceil(rate * S / 1e18)` in total, and the portal keeps exactly that after the refund.

### Doing a Corporate Action on Tempo and in Zones (Non-Normative)

The issuer runs its usual process for Tempo holders and calls `distribute` once for each zone portal that holds the asset.

**Cash dividend.**

1. Pause the asset. The pause also stops transfers in every zone, and stops deposits, so no holding moves between Tempo and a zone.
2. Call `distribute(asset, stablecoin, rate, false, issuer)` on every portal that holds the asset, in one block.
3. Snapshot Tempo holders as of that block. Skip portals and other protected addresses.
4. Unpause the asset.
5. Pay each Tempo holder `rate × balance` with batched transfers, using a memo that identifies the dividend.
6. When each portal's refund arrives, pay the holders of the in-flight withdrawals it covers.

Taking the snapshot in the same block as the `distribute` calls means each holding is counted exactly once: either in the snapshot, in a zone's supply, or in a refund.

**Merger.**

1. Change the asset's transfer policy so that only the zone portals and the issuer can send the asset. Holders can no longer trade it on Tempo, and portals can still release it.
2. Call `distribute(asset, acquirerToken, rate, true, issuer)` on each portal that holds the asset.
3. Snapshot Tempo holders.
4. For each Tempo holder, `burnAt` their balance and mint `rate × balance` of the acquirer's token.
5. Burn the asset each portal returns, and convert in-flight withdrawals as they arrive.

**Rename, split, or reinvested dividend.** Call `setName`, `setSymbol`, or `setUIMultiplier` on Tempo. Nothing else is needed for zone holders. An issuer that halts trading around a split pauses the asset, schedules the multiplier a short time ahead, and unpauses after `effectiveAt`. Zones read both the pause and the multiplier, so they halt and resume with Tempo.

A reinvested dividend raises the multiplier by the dividend, net of any withholding, divided by the share price. The token's value then tracks the stock's total return rather than its price.

## Changes to Other Components

- **TIP-20 Factory.** The new `createToken` overload and the `USD`/18-decimal rule.
- **TIP-20 precompile.** The functions, roles, and storage in this TIP.
- **ZonePortal.** `distribute`, `forceWithdraw`, `isAssetCancelled`, and the two new entry types in the deposit queue. Deposits of a cancelled asset revert.
- **Zone execution.** The zone reads the fields in [Token State Read from Tempo](#token-state-read-from-tempo), rejects their setters, applies the two new queue entries, and adds `claimDistributions` and `pendingDistribution` to its token precompile. New views (`balanceOfUI`, `getFrozenTokens`, `canTransfer`, `pendingDistribution`) follow the zone's existing privacy rule for per-account reads.
- **Fee manager and transaction pool.** No change. Both read `balances[a]`, which holds only the unfrozen amount.
- **No change.** Precompile routing, recipient validation, the TIP-403 registry, the account keychain, the stablecoin DEX, the channel reserve, and payment classification, since asset tokens are TIP-20 tokens.

## Extension Points (Non-Normative)

Issuer-defined transfer logic belongs in a future TIP-403 policy type whose authorization is answered by a contract. A token adopts it with `changeTransferPolicyId`, and `canSend`, `canReceive`, and `canTransfer` reflect it because they delegate to TIP-403. A zone cannot run that contract, so a token using such a policy needs a simple allowlist or blocklist policy for zones to enforce alongside it.

New holder-level corporate actions that pay a fixed amount per share, such as spin-offs and special dividends, need no protocol change: they are distributions with a different payout token.

Issuers that want limits on multiplier updates, such as a maximum change per update or a minimum time between updates, grant `CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE` to a contract that enforces them. The same applies to approval flows for `distribute`.

## Open Questions

1. **Elective actions.** Some actions let each holder choose: cash or stock, tender offers, and rights offerings. Distributions pay every holder the same way, so this TIP does not support them. Zone holders would withdraw to Tempo to elect.
2. **Withdrawals across a merger.** A withdrawal processed in the zone before a cancelling distribution, and released on Tempo after it, delivers the old asset to its owner on Tempo. The issuer converts it there. An alternative is to have the portal release the payout token instead.
3. **Payment lane.** Asset-token transfers are classified as payments under TIP-1045, because classification is by prefix. Excluding them would need a stateful check at classification time.

# Tooling

- **Contract bindings:** add the new token functions to `ITIP20`, the factory overload, and the portal and zone additions to `tempo-contracts` and the SDKs.
- **Explorers and wallets:** read `decimals()` instead of assuming 6. Show `balanceOfUI` next to `balanceOf` when `supportsInterface(0xa60bf13d)` is true and `uiMultiplier() != 1e18`, and show pending updates from `newUIMultiplier()` and `effectiveAt()`. Refresh `name` and `symbol` on `NameUpdated` and `SymbolUpdated`.
- **Zone wallets:** show `pendingDistribution` for each held asset, and offer `claimDistributions`.
- **Issuer tooling:** a snapshot helper that lists Tempo holders at a block, excludes portals and protected addresses, and lists the portals that hold the asset.
- **`tips/verify`:** extend the TIP-20 reference implementation and invariant suite, and add a zone distribution model.

# Threat Model

- **`DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` holder:** fully trusted by holders. Can grant every role, change the transfer policy, rename the token, and change its metadata, including the ISIN. A rename can make a token resemble another; integrators SHOULD identify tokens by address.
- **`FREEZE_ROLE` holder:** trusted to hold funds only when legally required. Can freeze any unprotected Tempo account, including while paused. Cannot move funds.
- **`FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` holder:** trusted to move funds only when legally required. Can move any unprotected Tempo balance, and any zone holder's balance through `forceWithdraw`, including while paused. Cannot touch protected addresses or bypass the recipient's transfer or receive policy on Tempo.
- **`CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE` holder:** trusted to record corporate actions correctly. A wrong multiplier misleads displays and pricing, but cannot change any balance. A distribution can only add to holders' balances, and is funded up front.
- **`BURN_AT_ROLE` holder:** trusted to destroy holdings only when legally required. With `CORPORATE_ACTION_ROLE`, can cancel every zone holder's balance of the asset through a cancelling distribution.
- **Zone operator:** sees every zone balance, as today. Cannot change token-level state, which the zone reads from Tempo, and cannot credit more of a payout token than the portal holds.
- **Holders:** untrusted. Cannot move frozen tokens, including through a receive-policy redirect, a zone deposit, or fee payment. Cannot claim a distribution for balance acquired after its record point.
- **Integrators and DeFi contracts:** must treat `balanceOf` as raw units, read `decimals()`, and apply `uiMultiplier()` only for display or pricing.

# Invariants

1. The sum of all balances equals `totalSupply()`.
2. `setUIMultiplier`, `cancelUIMultiplierUpdate`, `setFrozenTokens`, `setName`, `setSymbol`, `setContractURI`, and `setExtraMetadata` never change any `balanceOf` or `totalSupply()`.
3. A holder-initiated outflow of `amount` from `a`, including a zone deposit and fee payment, succeeds only if `amount <= unfrozen(a)`.
4. `frozen(a)` changes only through `setFrozenTokens`, `forcedTransfer`, `burnAt`, and `burnBlocked`. The last three only decrease it, by exactly the part of the debit that exceeded `unfrozen(a)`.
5. Protected addresses always have `frozen = 0`, and `forcedTransfer` never debits or credits them.
6. `0 < uiMultiplier() <= MAX_UI_MULTIPLIER` at all times.
7. `decimals()` is 6 or 18 and never changes. No token with 18 decimals has currency `USD`.
8. If `canTransfer(from, to, amount)` is `true`, a `transfer` or `transferFrom` of `amount` does not revert for pause, transfer-policy, or frozen-balance reasons.
9. A zone's name, symbol, decimals, multiplier, and metadata for a token equal Tempo's at the zone's anchor.
10. For each distribution, the payout credited in the zone never exceeds `ceil(rate * S / 1e18)`, and that amount plus the refund equals the funding.
11. A holder's entitlement from a distribution depends only on their balance at the record point.
12. After a cancelling distribution, the zone's supply of the asset is zero and stays zero.
13. A token that never uses this TIP's functions behaves exactly as it did before activation.
