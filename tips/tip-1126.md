# TIP-1126: TIP-20A Asset Tokens

## Abstract

This TIP adds TIP-20A, a second native token type for tokenized securities, funds, and other non-payment assets. A TIP-20A token keeps the full TIP-20 interface and behavior and adds four features that issuers need: a choice of 6 or 18 decimals at creation, a display multiplier for splits and reinvested dividends (ERC-8056), partial freezes and forced transfers for transfer agents (ERC-7943), and issuer metadata, made up of an ERC-7572 contract URI and key-value entries such as an ISIN.

This TIP places TIP-20A tokens at their own `0x20A0` address prefix. The fee manager, stablecoin DEX, channel reserve, and payment classification already accept only the TIP-20 `0x20C0` prefix, so they reject TIP-20A tokens without any change. Precompiles evaluate every rule in this TIP natively over token state and TIP-403 policy state, and nothing calls into EVM code. This TIP leaves issuer-specific transfer logic to a future TIP-403 policy type.

## Motivation

Issuers of tokenized assets need controls that TIP-20 does not have. TIP-20 also has payment-lane properties that asset tokens should not have.

- **Decimals.** TIP-20 is fixed at 6 decimals. Most asset tokens in use today use 6 or 18. Any other value adds conversion and display risk for integrators without a matching need.
- **Stock splits and reinvested dividends.** Equity issuers record these by changing a per-token multiplier instead of rewriting every balance. ERC-8056 standardizes this and leaves balances in raw units.
- **Seizure, partial freezes, and lost-key recovery.** Transfer agents must move or hold specific amounts under court orders and sanctions. ERC-7943 (Final) standardizes this, building on ERC-3643.
- **Security identifiers.** Integrators need to map a token to the security it represents, for example by ISIN.
- **Separation from payments.** Asset tokens should not be fee tokens, DEX quote tokens, or payment-classified transactions.

### Alternatives Considered

1. **A flag on TIP-20 tokens.** Every component that accepts TIP-20 would need a new storage read to exclude asset tokens. A prefix makes the exclusion free and visible from the address.
2. **A token-level hook that calls an issuer contract on every transfer.** Precompiles cannot call into EVM code today, and zones cannot evaluate arbitrary contracts. The extension point for issuer logic stays in TIP-403, where a future TIP can add it without changing TIP-20A tokens (see [Extension Points](#extension-points-non-normative)).
3. **Rebasing balances.** Rebasing changes `balanceOf` whenever the multiplier changes, which breaks DeFi accounting. ERC-8056 keeps balances raw. A future TIP could add a rebasing variant as a separate token type with its own factory function.

## Assumptions

| Assumption | Consequence if violated |
|---|---|
| The T9 TIP-403 registry binding is active: a token's transfer policy id is stored in the TIP-403 registry, not in token storage. | `createAssetToken` has nowhere to bind the policy, and this TIP cannot activate. |
| The TIP-20 implementation at activation includes TIP-1006 (`burnAt`), TIP-1028 (receive policies), and TIP-1075 (rewards deprecated). | The frozen-token rules for `burnAt`, receive-policy redirects, and rewards refer to behavior that does not exist. |
| ERC-8056 is a Draft. This TIP follows the text and reference implementation at ERCs commit `554d3467b29742aae0f864cdd2fdb492510a91ba` (2026-09-01). | Later changes to ERC-8056 do not apply unless a TIP adopts them. Integrators that follow a newer draft may see differences. |

# Specification

## Overview

A TIP-20A token is a TIP-20 token with four additions, deployed at a different address prefix.

| Feature | What the issuer can do | Standard | Gated by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decimals | Choose 6 or 18 at creation. Immutable. | — | Set at creation |
| Display multiplier | Record splits and reinvested dividends without changing any balance. | ERC-8056 | `UI_MULTIPLIER_ROLE` |
| Partial freeze | Hold a chosen amount of an account's tokens. The rest moves normally. | ERC-7943 | `FREEZE_ROLE` |
| Forced transfer | Move tokens without the holder's authorization, for seizures and lost-key recovery. | ERC-7943 | `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` |
| Metadata | Publish a contract URI and key-value entries such as an ISIN. | ERC-7572 (URI) | `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` |

Everything else (roles, mint and burn, memos, supply cap, pause, TIP-403 transfer policies, TIP-1028 receive policies, TIP-1022 virtual recipients, `permit`, access-key spending limits) works as on TIP-20. [Differences from TIP-20](#differences-from-tip-20) lists every exception.

### Separation from Payments

The fee manager, stablecoin DEX, TIP-20 channel reserve, and payment classification validate tokens by the TIP-20 `0x20C0` prefix. TIP-20A tokens live at `0x20A0`, so those components reject them with no change. A small set of other components change so they accept TIP-20A tokens where they should: precompile routing, the code-creation guard, recipient validation, the TIP-403 registry, and the account keychain. See [Changes to Other Components](#changes-to-other-components).

### Out of Scope

- **Zones.** Zones route TIP-20 tokens by the `0x20C0` prefix, and ZonePortal token enablement requires `isTIP20`. A TIP-20A token cannot be enabled in a zone until a zone TIP adds support.
- **Issuer transfer hooks.** Issuer-defined transfer logic belongs in a future TIP-403 policy type (see [Extension Points](#extension-points-non-normative)).
- **Rebasing balances.** See [Alternatives Considered](#alternatives-considered).

### Example

An issuer creates an 18-decimal fund token with an allowlist transfer policy bound at creation. It grants `FREEZE_ROLE` and `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` to its compliance contract and `UI_MULTIPLIER_ROLE` to its corporate-actions contract.

1. **Freeze.** A holder has 100 tokens. A court order freezes 30. The holder can still transfer up to 70.
2. **Seizure.** The court then orders 80 tokens moved. `forcedTransfer` takes the 70 unfrozen tokens and 10 frozen ones. The holder is left with a balance of 20, all of it frozen.
3. **Split.** The fund does a 4-for-1 split. The issuer schedules the multiplier to change from `1e18` to `4e18`. Once the split takes effect, `balanceOf(holder)` is still 20 and `balanceOfUI(holder)` is 80. No balance, transfer, or event amount changes.

### Definitions

```
frozen(a)   = frozen amount of a
unfrozen(a) = balanceOf(a) - frozen(a)   if balanceOf(a) > frozen(a)
            = 0                          otherwise
```

**Protected addresses** are the addresses `burnAt` refuses: the TIP-20 `PROTECTED` system custody addresses active at the current hardfork (including `ReceivePolicyGuard`), the token's own address, and ZonePortal addresses. They can never be frozen, force-debited, or force-credited. Because ZonePortal addresses are protected, pooled zone custody on mainnet cannot be frozen or force-debited.

### Naming

Names follow TIP-20 conventions (`setX` setters, `XUpdated(address indexed updater, …)` events, one role per privileged function), except where an ERC fixes the name. ERC-7943, ERC-8056, and ERC-7572 names are used exactly, because integrators detect and call them by selector.

## Activation

All behavior in this TIP applies from the protocol version assigned to it. Before activation, addresses with the TIP-20A prefix are not precompiles and `createAssetToken` follows unknown-selector behavior.

If contract code or an EIP-7702 delegation already exists at a TIP-20A address, activation is blocked until it is handled.

## Address Space

TIP-20A tokens live at:

```
TIP20A_PREFIX (12 bytes) = 0x20A000000000000000000000
token address           = TIP20A_PREFIX || keccak256(abi.encode(sender, salt))[0:8]
```

This is the TIP-20 derivation with a different prefix. Unlike TIP-20, no range of TIP-20A addresses is reserved, because no protocol-created TIP-20A token exists. `isTIP20(addr)` returns `false` for every TIP-20A address, and `isTIP20A(addr)` returns `false` for every TIP-20 address.

## Differences from TIP-20

A TIP-20A token implements every TIP-20 function, event, and error with the same selector and the same behavior, except for the differences in this table. The unchanged behavior includes roles, `mint`, `burn`, `burnBlocked`, `burnAt`, memos, supply cap, pause, TIP-403 transfer policies (including compound policies), TIP-1028 receive policies, TIP-1022 virtual recipients, `permit`, access-key spending limits, and `logoURI`.

| TIP-20 function | TIP-20A behavior |
|---|---|
| `decimals()` | Returns the value set at creation: 6 or 18. |
| `quoteToken()`, `nextQuoteToken()` | Return `address(0)`. |
| `setNextQuoteToken`, `completeQuoteTokenUpdate` | Revert with `InvalidQuoteToken`. |
| `transfer`, `transferFrom`, `transferWithMemo`, `transferFromWithMemo`, `burn`, `burnWithMemo` | Additionally limited to the sender's unfrozen balance (see [Frozen Tokens](#frozen-tokens)). |
| `burnAt`, `burnBlocked` | May burn frozen tokens and reduce the frozen amount (see [Frozen Tokens](#frozen-tokens)). |
| Reward functions | A TIP-20A token never accrues rewards. The functions behave as on TIP-20 after TIP-1075. |

`currency()` works as on TIP-20: free-form, set at creation, with no ISO-4217 check. The TIP-20 rule that a USD token needs a USD quote token does not apply, because TIP-20A has no quote token.

A TIP-20A token never writes the quote-token and reward fields of TIP-20 storage. Its additional state is listed in [Storage](#storage).

## Roles

| Role | Gates |
|---|---|
| `FREEZE_ROLE` | `setFrozenTokens` |
| `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` | `forcedTransfer` |
| `UI_MULTIPLIER_ROLE` | `setUIMultiplier`, `cancelUIMultiplierUpdate` |

`DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` administers all three, and nobody holds them at creation. `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` also gates `setContractURI` and `setExtraMetadata`, as it gates `setLogoURI`. All TIP-20 roles apply unchanged: `BURN_AT_ROLE` still gates `burnAt`, and `BURN_BLOCKED_ROLE` still gates `burnBlocked`.

Freezing and forced transfer are separate roles, as `burnBlocked` and `burnAt` are on TIP-20, so an issuer can grant the ability to hold funds without the ability to move them.

Role identifiers are in [Role Identifiers](#role-identifiers).

## Token Creation

`TIP20Factory.createAssetToken` creates a TIP-20A token at `getAssetTokenAddress(msg.sender, salt)`. It validates everything before writing any state:

1. Revert `InvalidDecimals` unless `decimals` is 6 or 18.
2. Revert `InvalidTransferPolicyId` unless `transferPolicyId` exists in the TIP-403 registry. The built-in allow-all (`1`) and reject-all (`0`) policies exist.
3. Revert `LogoURITooLong` or `InvalidLogoURI` if `logoURI` fails the TIP-1026 rules. Revert `ContractURITooLong` or `InvalidContractURI` if `contractURI` fails the [Contract URI](#contract-uri) rules.
4. Revert `InvalidUIMultiplier` unless `0 < initialUIMultiplier <= MAX_UI_MULTIPLIER`.
5. Revert `TokenAlreadyExists` if a token exists at the derived address.

It then initializes the token as `createToken` does (name, symbol, currency, supply cap `2^128 - 1`, and `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` granted to `admin`) and binds `transferPolicyId` in the TIP-403 registry. It also sets `decimals`, `uiMultiplier = newUIMultiplier = initialUIMultiplier`, `effectiveAt = 0`, and the two URIs.

Events are emitted in this order:

1. The token's role-grant event.
2. `LogoURIUpdated`, if `logoURI` is non-empty.
3. `ContractURIUpdated`, if `contractURI` is non-empty.
4. `UIMultiplierUpdated(0, initialUIMultiplier, 0)` from the token.
5. `AssetTokenCreated` from the factory.

Binding the policy at creation means a regulated token is never unrestricted, even before its first mint. `initialUIMultiplier` lets an issuer bring a token from another chain with its existing multiplier.

The factory also adds `getAssetTokenAddress(sender, salt)`, which returns the address a `createAssetToken` call would use, and `isTIP20A(token)`, which returns `true` if `token` has the TIP-20A prefix and has been created. The full factory ABI is in [Factory Interface](#factory-interface).

## Frozen Tokens

An account with `FREEZE_ROLE` sets a frozen amount per account. The holder cannot move that many tokens, and the rest of the balance moves normally. The frozen amount may exceed the balance. In that case, tokens that arrive later are also held until the balance exceeds the frozen amount.

Example: balance 100, frozen 30. The holder can transfer up to 70. If the frozen amount is set to 150, the holder can transfer nothing. After receiving 80 more (balance 180), the holder can transfer 30.

### `setFrozenTokens(account, amount)`

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `FREEZE_ROLE`.
2. Revert `ProtectedAddress` if `account` is protected.
3. Revert `VirtualAddressNotAllowed` if `account` is a TIP-1022 virtual address. Virtual addresses hold no balance; their master does.
4. Set `frozen(account) = amount`. Any value is allowed, including 0 and values above the balance.
5. Emit `Frozen(account, amount)`. Return `true`.

`setFrozenTokens` works while the token is paused.

### Effect on Each Operation

| Operation | Debited account | Limited to `unfrozen`? | Changes `frozen`? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `transfer`, `transferWithMemo` | caller | Yes | No |
| `transferFrom`, `transferFromWithMemo` | `from` | Yes | No |
| `burn`, `burnWithMemo` | caller | Yes | No |
| Receive-policy redirect of any of the above (TIP-1028) | sender | Yes, same check as the transfer | No |
| `burnAt`, `burnBlocked` | burned account | No | Yes, see below |
| `forcedTransfer` | `from` | No | Yes, see below |
| `mint`, incoming transfers | none | n/a | No |
| `approve`, `permit` | none | n/a | No |

**Limited operations.** The frozen check runs after the TIP-20 pause, recipient, transfer-policy, and allowance or access-key spending-limit checks, and before the TIP-1028 receive-policy check.

- If `amount <= balanceOf(from)` and `amount > unfrozen(from)`, the call reverts with `ERC7943InsufficientUnfrozenBalance(from, amount, unfrozen(from))`.
- If `amount > balanceOf(from)`, the check passes and the existing `InsufficientBalance` error applies at the balance update.

Because the check runs before the receive-policy check, it also limits a transfer that would be redirected to `ReceivePolicyGuard`.

**Privileged debits.** Let `u = unfrozen(a)` before the debit. If `amount > u`, then before the balance changes:

```
frozen(a) = frozen(a) - (amount - u)
```

The token then emits `Frozen(a, frozen(a))` before the `Transfer` event. Example: balance 100, frozen 30. A `burnAt` of 80 takes 70 unfrozen and 10 frozen tokens. Frozen becomes 20 and the balance becomes 20.

Protected addresses always have `frozen = 0`, so `ReceivePolicyGuard` claims and burns from the guard are unaffected.

## Forced Transfer

`forcedTransfer(from, to, amount)`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE`.
2. Revert `ProtectedAddress` if `from` is protected.
3. Resolve `to` as TIP-20 transfers do (TIP-1022 virtual addresses resolve to their master). Revert `InvalidRecipient` if the resolved target is zero or has the TIP-20 or TIP-20A prefix. Revert `ProtectedAddress` if the resolved target is protected.
4. Revert `ERC7943CannotReceive(to)` if `canReceive(to)` is false.
5. Revert `PolicyForbids` if the resolved target's TIP-1028 receive policy rejects this token from `from`. A forced transfer is never redirected to `ReceivePolicyGuard`, because the guard's recovery path could return seized tokens to `from`.
6. Revert `InsufficientBalance` if `amount > balanceOf(from)`.
7. Apply the [privileged-debit](#effect-on-each-operation) unfreeze rule to `from`.
8. Debit `from` and credit the target. Emit `Transfer(from, to, amount)` (plus the TIP-1022 forwarding `Transfer` for a virtual `to`), then `ForcedTransfer(from, to, amount)`, where `to` is the address as passed. Return `true`.

A forced transfer does not check the pause state, the sender leg of the transfer policy for `from`, allowances, or access-key spending limits.

A lost-wallet recovery is a `forcedTransfer` of the full balance to the holder's new address.

## Permission Views

The three views revert only if they run out of gas. On any other internal error they return `false`.

- `canSend(account)`: the token's TIP-403 transfer policy authorizes `account` in the sender role.
- `canReceive(account)`: `account` is resolved as a transfer recipient (TIP-1022), and the token's TIP-403 transfer policy authorizes the resolved address in the recipient role. An address that fails recipient validation returns `false`.
- `canTransfer(from, to, amount)`: returns `true` only if the token is not paused, `canSend(from)` and `canReceive(to)` are true, and, when `frozen(from) > 0`, `amount <= unfrozen(from)`. With nothing frozen it does not read the balance, as ERC-7943 requires.

These views do not evaluate TIP-1028 receive policies, because a receive-policy rejection redirects the transfer rather than failing it. They do not evaluate allowances or access-key spending limits.

## UI Multiplier

The multiplier tells wallets how to display balances. It never changes balances, transfers, allowances, total supply, the supply cap, or any event amount. A 4-for-1 split is recorded by changing the multiplier from `1e18` to `4e18`: a holder of 100 tokens still holds 100, and `balanceOfUI` shows 400.

The token stores the multiplier in effect now (`uiMultiplier`), a scheduled multiplier (`newUIMultiplier`), and the time the scheduled one takes effect (`effectiveAt`).

```
UI_MULTIPLIER_ONE  = 1e18
MAX_UI_MULTIPLIER  = 1e36
```

`MAX_UI_MULTIPLIER` keeps `balance * multiplier` within `uint256` for any balance up to `2^128`, so `balanceOfUI` and `totalSupplyUI` never overflow.

### Views

These follow the ERC-8056 reference implementation:

- `uiMultiplier()`: `newUIMultiplier` if `block.timestamp >= effectiveAt`, else `uiMultiplier`.
- `newUIMultiplier()`, `effectiveAt()`: the stored values.
- `toUIAmount(raw) = raw * uiMultiplier() / 1e18`, rounded down.
- `fromUIAmount(ui) = ui * 1e18 / uiMultiplier()`, rounded down.
- `balanceOfUI(a) = toUIAmount(balanceOf(a))`. `totalSupplyUI() = toUIAmount(totalSupply())`.

`toUIAmount` and `fromUIAmount` take arbitrary inputs and revert on overflow.

### Scheduling an Update

`setUIMultiplier(newMultiplier, effectiveAtTimestamp)`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `UI_MULTIPLIER_ROLE`.
2. Revert `InvalidUIMultiplier` unless `0 < newMultiplier <= MAX_UI_MULTIPLIER`.
3. Revert `InvalidEffectiveAt` unless `block.timestamp < effectiveAtTimestamp < 2^64`.
4. If no update is pending (`block.timestamp >= effectiveAt`): let `old = newUIMultiplier`. Set `uiMultiplier = old`, `newUIMultiplier = newMultiplier`, and `effectiveAt = effectiveAtTimestamp`. Emit `UIMultiplierUpdated(old, newMultiplier, effectiveAtTimestamp)`.
5. If an update is pending: emit `UIMultiplierUpdateCancelled(newUIMultiplier, effectiveAt)`. Set `newUIMultiplier = newMultiplier` and `effectiveAt = effectiveAtTimestamp`. Emit `UIMultiplierUpdated(uiMultiplier, newMultiplier, effectiveAtTimestamp)`.

`cancelUIMultiplierUpdate()`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `UI_MULTIPLIER_ROLE`.
2. Revert `NoPendingUIMultiplierUpdate` unless `block.timestamp < effectiveAt`.
3. Emit `UIMultiplierUpdateCancelled(newUIMultiplier, effectiveAt)`. Set `newUIMultiplier = uiMultiplier` and `effectiveAt = 0`.

Both functions work while the token is paused, so an issuer can pause trading, apply a corporate action, and unpause.

The earliest a new multiplier can take effect is one second after the current block timestamp. No event is emitted when a scheduled multiplier takes effect; indexers derive that moment from `effectiveAt`.

## Metadata

### Contract URI

```
MAX_CONTRACT_URI_BYTES = 1024
```

`contractURI()` returns the stored string.

`setContractURI(newContractURI)` requires `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`. It reverts `ContractURITooLong` if the value is longer than `MAX_CONTRACT_URI_BYTES`, and `InvalidContractURI` if the value is non-empty and its scheme fails the TIP-1026 `logoURI` scheme rules (`https`, `http`, `ipfs`, or `data`). It then stores the value and emits `ContractURIUpdated()`.

The URI points to, or contains, an ERC-7572 JSON document. ERC-7572 requires only a `name` field. The protocol does not validate the document. The limit is larger than `logoURI`'s 256 bytes so that a small inline `data:` JSON document fits.

`ContractURIUpdated()` has no `updater` parameter, unlike TIP-20's `LogoURIUpdated`, because ERC-7572 defines it that way.

### Extra Metadata

```
MAX_EXTRA_METADATA_KEY_BYTES   = 32
MAX_EXTRA_METADATA_VALUE_BYTES = 256
```

`extraMetadata(key)` returns the value stored under `key`, or the empty string if none is stored.

`setExtraMetadata(key, newValue)`:

1. Revert `Unauthorized` unless the caller holds `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`.
2. Revert `InvalidExtraMetadataKey` if `key` is empty or longer than `MAX_EXTRA_METADATA_KEY_BYTES`.
3. Revert `ExtraMetadataValueTooLong` if `newValue` is longer than `MAX_EXTRA_METADATA_VALUE_BYTES`.
4. If `newValue` is empty, remove the entry under `key`. Otherwise store `newValue` under `key`, replacing any previous value.
5. Emit `ExtraMetadataUpdated(msg.sender, key, newValue)`.

Keys are case-sensitive and compared byte for byte. Entries cannot be listed on-chain; indexers rebuild the set from `ExtraMetadataUpdated` events.

The protocol assigns no meaning to any key. Issuers SHOULD use lowercase keys, and SHOULD store a 12-character ISIN under `isin`.

## Changes to Other Components

- **Precompile routing.** Created addresses with the TIP-20A prefix dispatch to the TIP-20A implementation. `is_precompile` includes them.
- **Code-creation guard.** CREATE, CREATE2, and EIP-7702 delegations that would place code at an address with the TIP-20A prefix are rejected, using the rule TIP-1047 defines for the TIP-20 prefix. This TIP applies that rule to the TIP-20A prefix whether or not TIP-1047 is active.
- **Recipient validation.** TIP-20 and TIP-20A transfers, mints, and forced transfers revert `InvalidRecipient` when the recipient has the TIP-20A prefix, as they already do for the TIP-20 prefix. This is the only behavior change for existing TIP-20 tokens.
- **TIP-403 registry.** Calls that accept a token address (`tokenTransferPolicyId`, receive-policy token filters) accept TIP-20A tokens. A TIP-20A token's policy binding lives only in the registry.
- **Account keychain (TIP-1011).** Selector rules that restrict the recipients of `transfer`, `transferWithMemo`, and `approve` currently require a target with the TIP-20 prefix. They now also accept targets with the TIP-20A prefix. Spending limits already apply to any token and need no change.
- **No change needed.** The TIP Fee Manager, stablecoin DEX, TIP-20 channel reserve (TIP-1034), payment classification (TIP-1045), the implicit approval list (TIP-1035), and ZonePortal token enablement validate tokens by the TIP-20 prefix and therefore reject TIP-20A. Implementations that reuse the TIP-20 token code for TIP-20A MUST keep TIP-20 address validation rejecting TIP-20A addresses.

## Extension Points (Non-Normative)

This TIP reserves no hook slot on the token. The intended path for issuer-defined logic is TIP-403:

1. **EVM callouts.** A future TIP adds a TIP-403 policy type whose authorization is answered by a contract. A TIP-20A token adopts it with `changeTransferPolicyId`. `canSend`, `canReceive`, and `canTransfer` reflect it automatically, because they delegate to TIP-403. No TIP-20A storage or ABI change is needed.
2. **Amount-aware checks**, such as a maximum balance per holder, need a registry call that carries the amount. That is a TIP-403 change, not a token change.

Until then, issuers run their own logic in contracts that act on the token. For example, a compliance contract can administer the token's allowlist policy and hold `FREEZE_ROLE` and `FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` for freezes and seizures, and a corporate-actions contract can hold `UI_MULTIPLIER_ROLE`.

## Interfaces

### Token Interface

```solidity
interface ITIP20A /* is ITIP20 */ {
    // ---------- ERC-7943 (fungible) ----------

    event ForcedTransfer(address indexed from, address indexed to, uint256 amount);
    event Frozen(address indexed account, uint256 amount);

    /// @dev Declared to complete the ERC-7943 ABI. TIP-20A never reverts with these two;
    ///      ordinary transfers keep TIP-20's PolicyForbids and ContractPaused.
    error ERC7943CannotSend(address account);
    error ERC7943CannotTransfer(address from, address to, uint256 amount);

    error ERC7943CannotReceive(address account);
    error ERC7943InsufficientUnfrozenBalance(address account, uint256 amount, uint256 unfrozen);
    error VirtualAddressNotAllowed();

    function forcedTransfer(address from, address to, uint256 amount) external returns (bool result);
    function setFrozenTokens(address account, uint256 amount) external returns (bool result);
    function getFrozenTokens(address account) external view returns (uint256 amount);
    function canSend(address account) external view returns (bool allowed);
    function canReceive(address account) external view returns (bool allowed);
    function canTransfer(address from, address to, uint256 amount) external view returns (bool allowed);

    // ---------- ERC-8056 (scaled UI amount) ----------

    event UIMultiplierUpdated(uint256 oldMultiplier, uint256 newMultiplier, uint256 effectiveAtTimestamp);
    event UIMultiplierUpdateCancelled(uint256 cancelledMultiplier, uint256 cancelledEffectiveAt);

    error InvalidUIMultiplier();
    error InvalidEffectiveAt();
    error NoPendingUIMultiplierUpdate();

    function uiMultiplier() external view returns (uint256);
    function newUIMultiplier() external view returns (uint256);
    function effectiveAt() external view returns (uint256);
    function toUIAmount(uint256 rawAmount) external view returns (uint256);
    function fromUIAmount(uint256 uiAmount) external view returns (uint256);
    function balanceOfUI(address account) external view returns (uint256);
    function totalSupplyUI() external view returns (uint256);
    function setUIMultiplier(uint256 newMultiplier, uint256 effectiveAtTimestamp) external;
    function cancelUIMultiplierUpdate() external;

    // ---------- Metadata ----------

    /// @dev Parameterless, as ERC-7572 defines it.
    event ContractURIUpdated();
    event ExtraMetadataUpdated(address indexed updater, string key, string newValue);

    error ContractURITooLong();
    error InvalidContractURI();
    error InvalidExtraMetadataKey();
    error ExtraMetadataValueTooLong();

    function contractURI() external view returns (string memory);
    function setContractURI(string calldata newContractURI) external;
    function extraMetadata(string calldata key) external view returns (string memory);
    function setExtraMetadata(string calldata key, string calldata newValue) external;

    // ---------- ERC-165 ----------

    function supportsInterface(bytes4 interfaceId) external view returns (bool);

    // ---------- Roles ----------

    function FREEZE_ROLE() external view returns (bytes32);
    function FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE() external view returns (bytes32);
    function UI_MULTIPLIER_ROLE() external view returns (bytes32);
}
```

# Tooling

- **Contract bindings:** add `ITIP20A` and the factory additions to `tempo-contracts` and the SDKs.
- **SDK helpers:** `isTIP20A`, `getAssetTokenAddress`, and a display helper that formats `balanceOfUI` with `decimals()`.
- **Explorers and wallets:** show `balanceOfUI` next to `balanceOf` when `supportsInterface(0xa60bf13d)` is true, and show pending multiplier updates from `newUIMultiplier()` and `effectiveAt()`. Read `decimals()` instead of assuming 6.
- **`tips/verify`:** a reference Solidity implementation and invariant suite for TIP-20A, following the existing TIP-20 tests.

# Threat Model

- **`DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` holder:** fully trusted by holders. Can grant every role, change the transfer policy, and change the contract URI and metadata entries, including identifiers such as the ISIN.
- **`FREEZE_ROLE` holder:** trusted to hold funds only when legally required. Can freeze any amount of any unprotected account, including amounts above its balance and while paused. Cannot move funds.
- **`FORCED_TRANSFER_ROLE` holder:** trusted to move funds only when legally required. Can move any unprotected account's balance to any valid recipient, including while paused. Cannot debit or credit protected addresses, or bypass the recipient's transfer policy or receive policy.
- **`UI_MULTIPLIER_ROLE` holder:** trusted to record corporate actions correctly. A wrong multiplier misleads displays and anything that prices tokens from `uiMultiplier()`, but cannot change any balance.
- **Holders:** untrusted. Cannot move frozen tokens, and cannot avoid a freeze by routing a transfer through a receive-policy redirect.
- **Integrators and DeFi contracts:** must treat `balanceOf` as raw units and apply `uiMultiplier()` only for display or pricing. Contracts holding TIP-20A can be frozen or force-debited like any holder.

# Invariants

1. The sum of all balances equals `totalSupply()`.
2. `setUIMultiplier`, `cancelUIMultiplierUpdate`, `setFrozenTokens`, `setContractURI`, and `setExtraMetadata` never change any balance or `totalSupply()`.
3. A holder-initiated outflow of `amount` from `a` succeeds only if `amount <= unfrozen(a)` at the time of the call.
4. `frozen(a)` changes only through `setFrozenTokens`, `forcedTransfer`, `burnAt`, and `burnBlocked`. The last three only decrease it, and by exactly the part of the debit that exceeded `unfrozen(a)`.
5. Protected addresses always have `frozen = 0`, and `forcedTransfer` never debits or credits them.
6. `0 < uiMultiplier() <= MAX_UI_MULTIPLIER` at all times.
7. If `canTransfer(from, to, amount)` is `true`, a `transfer` or `transferFrom` of `amount` does not revert for pause, transfer-policy, or frozen-balance reasons.
8. TIP-20A tokens are rejected by the fee manager, stablecoin DEX, channel reserve, and ZonePortal token enablement, and are never payment-classified. `isTIP20` is `false` for them.
