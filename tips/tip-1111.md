# TIP-1111: Configurable Account Access Keys

## Abstract

This TIP lets configurable accounts grant and receive access-key permissions. The parent controls the funds and grant; the delegate signs within its permissions using a primitive key or current owner quorum.

Owner rotation and migration preserve regular grants. AccountKeychain enforces their permissions, expiry, spending limits, and counters. Configurable delegates sign directly through their owners; their access keys cannot sign for them.

Each quorum uses at most eight primitive signatures. Combined grant-and-use can require two quorums, plus an optional fee-payer signature.

## Motivation

A treasury needs to give an employee or a smaller multisig limited spending access without giving it unrestricted control. Users who already rely on access keys need this capability before migrating to configurable accounts.

## Assumptions

| Assumption | Consequence if violated |
|---|---|
| [TIP-1011](./tip-1011.md) continues to define permissions and lifecycle, with grant data in AccountKeychain. | A different lifecycle could change the permissions or spending state this TIP preserves. |
| [TIP-1112](./tip-1112.md) root disablement activates with configurable accounts and migration at T14. | A migrated delegate's old root could bypass its current owners. |
| [TIP-1137 admin access-key removal](https://github.com/tempoxyz/tempo/blob/tip/1137/tips/tip-1137.md#existing-admin-keys) activates at T14. | Pre-T14 admin keys could retain spending or key-management authority outside the parent's direct control. |

# Specification

## Overview

The **parent** is the account whose funds are being accessed. The **delegate** is the key or account receiving access. A **grant** records the delegate's permissions on that parent.

A primitive account signs with its **root key**. A configurable account uses an **owner quorum**: owners whose combined weights meet its threshold. TIP-1112 rejects a migrated account's bare root signature in these grant and transaction checks.

1. **Grant:** the parent authorises access using its root key or owner quorum.
2. **Sign:** the delegate approves a transaction using its primitive key or current owner quorum.
3. **Use:** validate the delegate and grant restrictions, then execute as the parent account.

Both primitive and configurable accounts can be parents or delegates.

For example, treasury A grants delegate B $100/day. B has three owners with weight one and a threshold of two. Two owners approve a $40 payment; the grant has $60 remaining for that day. A's owners do not sign the payment. If B grants laptop key C access to B's funds, C cannot use that grant to spend A's funds. B's owners must sign directly.

The grant controls what B may do; B's configuration controls who can approve it. This TIP extends native transaction authorisation. It does not add configurable delegates to the on-chain keychain verification helpers.

## Delegate eligibility

For a configurable delegate:

- Its own primitive owners must sign directly.
- Its access keys cannot sign on its behalf.
- It cannot have EVM code or EIP-7702 delegation.
- Reject a grant where the configurable delegate is the parent itself.

## Granting access

A configurable-delegate grant MUST store the delegate account's address as `key_id`. AccountKeychain identifies it with the [configurable-account key type](#interfaces).

### Authorising the grant

- Register access through the existing transaction-level `key_authorization` field.
- From activation, direct AccountKeychain authorization selectors support configurable parents and enforce the same delegate-type, self-grant, and direct-parent-authority checks. Access-key transactions cannot manage grants.
- The authorization signature is a byte-encoded `TempoSignature::Primitive` or `TempoSignature::ConfigurableAccount`.
- Reject `Keychain` signatures in that field.
- Primitive authorisations keep their existing encoding. Only the primitive parent's current root key may authorise its grants, subject to TIP-1112.
- Reject `key_authorization` with `is_admin = true`.

The configurable parent's own owner quorum signs:

```text
configurable_account_digest(key_authorization.signature_hash(), parent_account, config_version)
```

- The signature carries the parent's address and full configuration, as defined in TIP-1114. Its account must match the account granting access.
- A version-zero parent registers its configuration under TIP-1114 when it authorises a grant.
- Existing expiry, replay, permission, and witness-burning checks still apply. A burned authorization witness cannot be reused.

## Using access

Use the existing V2 keychain signature format:

```text
0x04 || parent_account || access_key_signature
inner_digest = keccak256(0x04 || tx.signature_hash() || parent_account)
```

- A primitive delegate signs `inner_digest` normally.
- A configurable delegate provides a TIP-1114 signature over `configurable_account_digest(inner_digest, delegate_account, delegate_config_version)`.
- Reject a keychain signature inside another keychain signature, as required by the [direct-signing rules](#delegate-eligibility).
- The recovered or named key ID must match the grant. A primitive signature must match the grant's stored type. From T14, a nonzero commitment at that key ID requires a configurable signature from its current owner quorum, regardless of the stored type. TIP-1112 rejects its retired root.
- `verifyKeychain` and `verifyKeychainAdmin` do not accept configurable delegates; see [on-chain signature verification](./tip-1114.md#on-chain-signature-verification).

### Validation and execution

1. Validate the delegate at this transaction's position in the block. Check a primitive delegate's signature and authority. A configurable delegate requires its current configuration and primitive-owner approvals meeting its threshold; a nonzero commitment requires that quorum under TIP-1112.
2. Enforce the selected grant's restrictions.
3. Execute as the parent account.

Ordinary access-key use does not need another signature from the parent's owners. A configurable delegate using its initial configuration is registered under TIP-1114 before calls execute.

## Grant and use in one transaction

- A transaction can authorize an access key and use it immediately.
- The grant and outer transaction signatures are independent approvals. Both must pass validation.
- A configurable parent with code or EIP-7702 delegation is invalid.
- This TIP does not enable multisig signatures in subblocks or authorization lists.

## Owner rotation and revocation

Parent owner rotation preserves regular grants, including their permissions, expiry, limits, spending counters, and revocation state.

Only a primitive parent's current root key or a configurable parent's current owner quorum can revoke its grants. Revoke each unwanted grant separately.

[TIP-1137](https://github.com/tempoxyz/tempo/blob/tip/1137/tips/tip-1137.md#existing-admin-keys) treats pre-T14 admin grants as revoked from T14; rotation or migration cannot restore them.

Delegate owner rotation transfers use of the grant to its new quorum without changing the parent's grant, limits, or spending counters. Subsequent use requires that current quorum; signatures under the previous configuration are invalid.

## Failure and rollback

Grant creation and initial registration follow [TIP-1114's execution boundary](./tip-1114.md#failure-behavior):

| Outcome | State effects | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Inline grant signature or outer signature fails validation | The transaction is invalid. No registration or grant from this transaction is committed. | Correct the failed approval against the current configuration and grant state. |
| A valid, included transaction's execution reverts or halts | Initial registrations and an inline `key_authorization` persist. Grants made through precompile calls roll back with the other user-call changes. Normal fee and nonce rules apply. | Check which grant exists before retrying; a burned authorization witness cannot be reused. |

Expiry, permissions, spending accounting, and revocation retain TIP-1011's rules.

## Bounded work and gas

Count signature checks before performing cryptography.

- Each multisig quorum can contain at most eight primitive signatures.
- A transaction can contain at most two quorums: one for the grant and one for the transaction itself.
- The combined limit is sixteen primitive verifications across the outer signature and any signed key authorization.
- An optional secp256k1 fee-payer signature adds at most one more verification.

The combined verification bound remains a proposal. Confirm it with worst-case benchmarks before approval.

**Gas charges**

- Use TIP-1114's `V` cost for each multisig witness instead of that role's primitive-signature cost.
- Subtract the ordinary outer base-signature charge once. Do not subtract it from signed key authorizations.
- Keep the existing keychain processing and policy charges.
- Charge normal account access for additional delegate accounts.
- Add TIP-1114's initial registration cost for each unregistered configurable signer, once per account.
- Check total intrinsic gas and whether fees are affordable before cryptography.
- Do not add configuration SLOAD charges.

## Changes to other components

### Migration compatibility

From T14, a delegate migrating under [TIP-1113](./tip-1113.md) uses existing regular grants through its current owner quorum without reauthorisation. Bare root use is rejected under TIP-1112. Permissions, expiry, limits, spending counters, and revocation state remain unchanged.

## Interfaces

AccountKeychain adds `ConfigurableAccount = 3` to its key-type enum. Primitive key-type values 0, 1, and 2 MUST remain unchanged.

Before activation, key type `3` and `TempoSignature::ConfigurableAccount` key authorisations MUST remain rejected, including during historical replay.

# Compatibility

- Show the parent, delegate, permissions, and current owners needed to sign.
- Track configurable delegates by their current configuration, including after migration. A grant follows the delegate address; new owners inherit its use.
- Recovery tooling must show active regular grants, including pre-T14 regular grants, and support [revocation per grant](#owner-rotation-and-revocation). Owner rotation must not be shown as revoking them. Show pre-T14 admin grants as revoked under TIP-1137.
- Revalidate affected transactions after configuration changes, grant revocation, or reorgs.

# Observability

| Action | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Grant or revoke access | Existing AccountKeychain authorization, revocation, and witness events, together with the grant's current state. |
| Use access | The transaction result and existing spending accounting. The new key type identifies a configurable delegate; no per-use event is added. |
| Rotate a delegate's owners | Its current configuration commitment and TIP-1109 update events. The parent's grant and spending counters remain unchanged. |

Execution failure does not establish that an inline grant was removed. Inspect grant state using the [rollback rules](#failure-and-rollback) before preparing another authorization.

# Threat Model

| Actor | Trust |
| --- | --- |
| Parent's root key / current owner quorum | The grant must name the parent and pass its current authority checks. Compromise can create unwanted grants or revoke legitimate ones. |
| Delegates | AccountKeychain checks the key ID, signature, permissions, limits, and expiry against one grant. Grants give no parent key-management or owner-rotation authority. Combining unrelated grants and nested keychain signatures are rejected. |
| Configurable delegate's owners | Validation checks the current configuration and quorum. Rotation transfers grant use without the parent's approval; the parent trusts future controllers until expiry or revocation. |
| Wallets | Trusted to show the parent, delegate, and permissions being approved. Misrepresentation can produce valid approval for broader access than intended. |

Parent rotation does not revoke regular grants. TIP-1137 treats pre-T14 admin grants as revoked.

# Invariants

- An included transaction registers every configurable signer, including a parent granting access and a delegate using it, even if execution fails.
- Only the parent's current root key or direct owner quorum can manage its access-key grants.
- Access-key grants provide no key-management or owner-rotation authority.
- Parent owner rotation preserves grants, permissions, expiry, limits, spending counters, and revocation state.
- Delegate rotation preserves grant permissions.
- Delegate rotation preserves spending counters.
- Ordinary use requires only the delegate's approval, not a second parent quorum.
- The grant and validation rules apply to every supported parent/delegate combination, including version-zero accounts and combined grant-and-use transactions.
- Combined grant-and-use transactions perform at most sixteen primitive verifications across the grant and outer signatures.
- An optional secp256k1 fee-payer signature adds at most one verification.
