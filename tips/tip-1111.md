# TIP-1111: Configurable Account Access Keys

## Abstract

This TIP lets configurable accounts grant access keys and act as access keys for other accounts. Access keys give a primitive key or another configurable account limited access to an account's funds. Changing owners and changing access-key permissions remain separate operations. The specification bounds signature verification across granting and using access.

## Motivation

A treasury may require a large owner quorum for unrestricted control while giving an employee or a smaller multisig limited spending access. Access keys let delegates spend within those permissions without collecting the treasury owners' signatures for each transaction. Supporting configurable parents and delegates also lets users retain access-key workflows when migrating to configurable accounts.

## Assumptions

- [TIP-1011](./tip-1011.md) continues to define access-key permissions and lifecycle. Its data remains in AccountKeychain.
- [TIP-1112](./tip-1112.md) adds root-key disablement in T13. T12 retains existing primitive-root authority rules.

# Specification

## Supported relationships

The **parent** is the account whose funds are being accessed. The **delegate** is the key or account receiving access.

| Parent | Supported delegate |
|---|---|
| Primitive account | Primitive key or configurable account |
| Configurable account | Primitive key or configurable account |

For a configurable delegate:

- Its own primitive owners must sign directly.
- Its access keys cannot sign on its behalf.
- It cannot have EVM code or EIP-7702 delegation.
- Reject a grant where the configurable delegate is the parent itself.

For example, treasury A gives multisig B a daily spending limit. B's owners sign the transaction, and A's grant determines how much B can spend. Access keys created by B are not used or checked.

## Granting access

### Delegate identity

- T12 adds `Multisig = 3` to AccountKeychain's key-type enum.
- Hardfork-gate key type `3` and multisig key authorisations so they remain rejected before T12, including during historical replay.
- Existing primitive key-type values 0, 1, and 2 do not change.
- A multisig grant stores the delegate account's address as `key_id`.
- Delegate rotation preserves the grant, spending counters, and limits. The delegate's current owners can use the existing grant.

### Grant authorisation

- Register access through the existing transaction-level `key_authorization` field.
- Direct AccountKeychain authorisation selectors support configurable parents from T12.
- From T12, direct selectors must apply the same delegate-type and self-grant checks defined here, while retaining existing caller permissions.
- The authorisation signature is a byte-encoded `TempoSignature::Primitive` or `TempoSignature::Multisig`.
- Reject `Keychain` signatures in that field.
- Primitive authorisations keep their existing encoding and authority rules.

Owners meeting the configurable parent's approval threshold sign directly:

```text
multisig_digest(key_authorization.signature_hash(), parent_account, config_version)
```

- The signature carries the parent's full configuration, also called its witness, as defined in TIP-1114.
- The account in that signature must match the account granting access.
- A version-zero parent registers its configuration under TIP-1114 when it grants access.
- Existing expiry, replay, permission, and witness-burning checks still apply. A burned authorisation witness cannot be reused.
- No delegate can rotate the parent's owners, even if the delegate has admin permissions.

## Using access

Use the existing V2 keychain signature format:

```text
0x04 || parent_account || access_key_signature
inner_digest = keccak256(0x04 || tx.signature_hash() || parent_account)
```

- A primitive delegate signs `inner_digest` normally.
- A configurable delegate provides a TIP-1114 signature over `multisig_digest(inner_digest, delegate_account, delegate_config_version)`.
- Reject a keychain signature inside another keychain signature.
- The signature type and the recovered or named key ID must match the grant.

### Validation and execution

First validate the delegate:

- For a primitive delegate, check its signature and authority.
- For a configurable delegate, check its configuration at this transaction's position in the block.
- Verify that the configurable delegate's primitive-owner approvals meet its threshold.

Then enforce the selected grant's restrictions and execute as the parent account.

Ordinary access-key use requires only the delegate's approval. A configurable delegate using its initial configuration is registered under TIP-1114 before calls execute.

From T13, if a primitive delegate's key ID has a nonzero configuration hash:

- Its retired primitive root cannot use the old grant.
- The parent must explicitly authorise that account as a multisig delegate.

### Grant and use in one transaction

- A transaction can authorise an access key and use it immediately.
- The grant signature and the transaction's outer signature are independent approvals. Both must pass validation.
- A configurable parent with code or EIP-7702 delegation is invalid.
- This TIP does not enable multisig signatures in subblocks or authorisation lists.

## Verification limits

Count signature checks before performing cryptography.

- Each multisig quorum can contain at most eight primitive signatures.
- A transaction can contain at most two quorums: one for the grant and one for the transaction itself.
- The combined limit is sixteen primitive verifications across the outer signature and any signed key authorisation.
- An optional secp256k1 fee-payer signature adds at most one more verification.

The combined T12 bound remains a proposal. Confirm it with worst-case benchmarks before approval.

## Gas accounting

- Use TIP-1114's `V` cost for each multisig witness instead of that role's primitive-signature cost.
- Subtract the ordinary outer base-signature charge once. Do not subtract it from signed key authorisations.
- Keep the existing keychain processing and policy charges.
- Charge normal account access for additional delegate accounts.
- Add TIP-1114's initial registration cost for each unregistered configurable signer, once per account.
- Check total intrinsic gas and whether fees are affordable before cryptography.
- Do not add configuration SLOAD charges.

# Tooling

- Show the parent, delegate, permissions, and owners needed to sign.
- Track configurable delegates by their current configuration.
- Recovery tooling must identify and revoke compromised access or admin keys; owner rotation preserves their grants.
- Revalidate affected transactions after configuration changes, grant revocation, or reorgs.

# Observability

- Use existing AccountKeychain authorisation, revocation, and witness events.
- Keep existing spending accounting.
- The new key type identifies configurable delegates.
- No per-use event is added.

# Threat Model

Delegation grants limited access to one parent account. Validation must not combine unrelated grants or follow recursive delegation chains.

Rotating a parent's owners preserves its access-key grants. A compromised access or admin key can remain usable after rotation, subject to its existing permissions and expiry, until separately revoked.

# Invariants

- Every configurable parent or delegate that authorises an included transaction has a nonzero configuration hash, including at version zero.
- Execution reverts and halts preserve each signer's initial registration.
- Delegate rotation preserves grant permissions.
- Delegate rotation preserves spending counters.
- Ordinary access-key use requires only the delegate's approval.
- The grant and validation rules apply to every supported parent/delegate combination, including version-zero accounts and combined grant-and-use transactions.
- Combined grant-and-use transactions perform at most sixteen primitive verifications across the grant and outer signatures.
- An optional secp256k1 fee-payer signature adds at most one verification.
