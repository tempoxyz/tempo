# TIP-1150: Per-token Zone Withdrawal Limits

## Abstract

This TIP adds an optional outflow limit to each token a Zone escrows on Tempo.
The `ZonePortal` enforces it when it pays a withdrawal out of escrow. Each limit
is a capacity of `maxAmount` raw token units that refills continuously over
`period` seconds. A withdrawal that does not fit waits in a per-token queue on
Tempo and is paid as capacity returns. It is not rejected or bounced. Limits are
set by the ZoneFactory owner (Tempo), not by the Zone's admin or sequencer. A
limit bounds how fast funds leave. It does not stop a theft: stopping one still
requires pausing the portal.

## Motivation

### Background

A **Zone** is a private chain that settles to Tempo. Its tokens are held in
escrow on Tempo by the Zone's **`ZonePortal`**, one contract per Zone created
by the **ZoneFactory** precompile ([TIP-1091](./tip-1091.md)). The factory's
`owner` is a Tempo-held key; it alone creates Zones. Each portal also has an
**admin** (the Zone's operator), **sequencers** that produce Zone blocks and
settle them, and optional **pause guardians**. The admin can permanently give
up some settings through **capability abdication**. Any of these roles can call
`pause()`, which stops deposits and withdrawal payouts for 30 days.

Sequencers settle Zone blocks on Tempo in **batches**. `submitBatch` checks a
batch's proof and appends the batch's withdrawals to the portal's withdrawal
FIFO, called the **main queue** in this TIP. Sequencers then call
`processWithdrawals` to pay main-queue entries out of escrow in order. An entry
is either a user withdrawal, a plain transfer or a **callback** that sends the
funds through the messenger to a contract with `gasLimit` gas, or a **deposit
bounce-back** (`fallbackNonce == 0`) that refunds a Tempo depositor whose
deposit the Zone rejected. A delivery that fails is returned to the Zone as a
bounce-back deposit. Callbacks may also deposit back into the Zone. Each
processed entry can therefore append at most one deposit to the portal's
**deposit queue**, which holds at most `MAX_UNPROCESSED_DEPOSITS` unprocessed
entries; `processWithdrawals` reverts if a call could overflow it.

### Problem

A Zone's escrow on Tempo holds every user's funds. Today the only thing that
decides what leaves escrow is a batch proof accepted by `submitBatch`. If a
Zone or verifier bug lets an attacker settle a forged batch, or many Zone
accounts are compromised at once, the whole escrow can leave in a few Tempo
blocks. `maxWithdrawalsPerBlock` limits the number of withdrawals, not their
value.

An earlier draft of this TIP enforced the limit in `ZoneOutbox` on the Zone,
rejecting requests over the limit. Asymmetric Research reviewed it with Tempo
on Oct 15 ([notes](https://tempoxyz.enterprise.slack.com/archives/C0C2FSDPPQT/p1792125793796269))
and recommended four changes, which this version adopts:

| Asymmetric Research advice | This TIP |
|---|---|
| Tempo, not the Zone operator, controls the limits, or products will set them as high as possible | Only the ZoneFactory owner can set a limit. The portal admin and sequencers cannot. |
| Per zone per asset is a good starting point, rather than a global limit priced by an oracle | One limit per token per portal, in raw token units |
| Enforce at the L1 portal, so a bad Zone bug cannot bypass the limit | Enforced in `ZonePortal` where funds leave escrow. The Zone is unchanged. |
| Queueing is better UX than blocking | Over-limit withdrawals wait in a per-token queue and are paid in order |
| Monitor it, or the limit can be maxed out and funds stolen unnoticed | Required events and views, and the alerts operators must run ([Observability](#observability)) |

Enforcing on Tempo also removes the Zone hardfork the earlier draft needed. It
uses Tempo's `block.timestamp`, which the sequencer cannot move, and needs no
new prover enclave image.

Limits are per token and in raw units because Zone tokens differ in
denomination and value, and a dollar limit across tokens would need a price
oracle. A token with no limit behaves exactly as before.

## Assumptions

- **The limit authority is honest.** The ZoneFactory owner can raise, lower or
  remove any limit at once. A compromised owner can remove limits, or set them
  so low that withdrawals effectively stop. See [Threat Model](#threat-model).
- **Pause works.** A limit only buys time. Stopping a theft in progress
  requires a pause guardian, sequencer or admin to call `pause()`, which stops
  both payout paths (`processWithdrawals` and the new
  `processQueuedWithdrawals`). The pause ends after `PAUSE_DURATION` (30 days) unless the
  portal runtime is upgraded in that time.
- **Someone watches.** The limit's value depends on an operator noticing
  unusual outflow while the queue holds it. [Observability](#observability)
  lists the alerts this TIP requires.
- **Withdrawal data is public.** Anyone can read queued withdrawals from the
  calldata of the `processWithdrawals` call that queued them, as they can for
  every settled withdrawal today, so anyone can pay them out.

# Specification

## Overview

Each portal keeps, per token, an optional **limit** `(maxAmount, period)` and
a **capacity**: the amount that may leave escrow now. Capacity starts at
`maxAmount`, falls by every amount paid out, and refills at
`maxAmount / period` units per second up to `maxAmount`.

Each portal also keeps, per token, a **withdrawal queue** of withdrawals that
were due to be paid but did not fit. The existing withdrawal FIFO that
`processWithdrawals` drains is called the **main queue** here.

| Operation | Who can call it | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `setWithdrawalLimit` | ZoneFactory owner only | Sets, changes or removes a token's limit |
| `processWithdrawals` | Sequencers (unchanged) | Pays main-queue withdrawals that fit, queues those that don't |
| `processQueuedWithdrawals` | Anyone | Pays a token's queued withdrawals as capacity allows |
| `pause` / `resume` | Unchanged | Pause stops both payout paths |

The trust boundary is the portal: whatever a batch proof says, no more than
the limit allows leaves escrow per token.

## Mechanics

### Limit authority

The limit authority is `IZoneFactory(ZONE_FACTORY_ADDRESS).owner()`, read at
call time. `setWithdrawalLimit` MUST revert with
`NotWithdrawalLimitAuthority` for any other caller, including the portal
admin. Transferring factory ownership transfers this authority for every portal.
Setting a limit is not gated by pause or by any `Capability` abdication.

### Setting a limit

`setWithdrawalLimit(token, maxAmount, period)`:

1. MUST revert with `TokenNotEnabled` if `token` is not enabled on the portal.
2. MUST revert with `InvalidWithdrawalLimit` unless one of these holds:

   ```text
   maxAmount == 0 && period == 0                                        // remove the limit
   maxAmount > 0 && 1 <= period <= MAX_WITHDRAWAL_LIMIT_PERIOD
                 && maxAmount >= period                                 // refills at least 1 unit/s
   ```

   `MAX_WITHDRAWAL_LIMIT_PERIOD` is 7 days.
3. If the token had no limit, capacity becomes `maxAmount`: a new limit starts
   full.
4. If the token had a limit, capacity is first refilled under the old limit,
   then capped at the new `maxAmount`. Raising a limit leaves current capacity
   unchanged and lets it refill faster and higher; lowering one can only
   reduce it.
5. Removing the limit deletes it. Withdrawals already queued stay queued and
   can then be paid in full by `processQueuedWithdrawals`.
6. Emits `WithdrawalLimitSet(token, maxAmount, period)`.

### Refill

Before any read or use of capacity, the portal refills it:

```text
elapsed   = block.timestamp - updatedAt
capacity  = min(maxAmount, capacity + elapsed * maxAmount / period)    // uint256 math, rounded down
updatedAt = block.timestamp
```

The multiplication happens before the division. Rounding down loses less than
one raw unit per refill, which only makes the limit stricter. A token with no
limit has no capacity and is never refilled. Time is Tempo's `block.timestamp`.
Capacity keeps refilling while the portal is paused.

Over any interval of length `T`, at most `maxAmount + T * maxAmount / period`
of a token leaves escrow: a full capacity plus its refill. Within one
`period` that is at most `2 * maxAmount`.

### What counts

Every payout from escrow by `processWithdrawals` or `processQueuedWithdrawals`
counts, at the moment it
is paid:

- user withdrawals, plain or with a callback, including callback value that a
  gateway deposits back into a Zone;
- deposit bounce-backs (main-queue entries with `fallbackNonce == 0`), which
  refund a Tempo depositor. A forged batch could fabricate these, so they are
  limited too. The bounce-back fee paid to the admin counts as part of the
  amount.

A delivery that fails moves nothing out of escrow. Its amount goes back to the
Zone as a bounce-back deposit as today, and any capacity it took is restored,
capped at `maxAmount`. `claimRefund` is not limited: its funds already counted
when the deposit bounce-back was processed. Withdrawals whose `gasLimit`
exceeds `MAX_WITHDRAWAL_GAS_LIMIT` bounce back before the limit applies, as
today.

### Main queue

`processWithdrawals` keeps its access control, ordering and proofs. For each
dequeued withdrawal of token `t`:

1. If `t` has no limit and an empty withdrawal queue, the withdrawal is
   processed exactly as before, with no further limit storage access.
2. If `t` has a limit, the withdrawal has a callback (`gasLimit > 0`), and
   `amount > maxAmount`, it MUST bounce back to the Zone. Callbacks cannot be
   split, and one this large could never be paid.
3. Otherwise, if the withdrawal queue of `t` is empty and `amount` fits in
   capacity, capacity falls by `amount` and the withdrawal is delivered as
   before.
4. Otherwise the withdrawal is appended to the withdrawal queue of `t` as
   `keccak256(abi.encode(withdrawal))` and `WithdrawalQueued` is emitted. No
   funds move and nothing bounces.

Here "withdrawal queue" always means the token's own queue, never the main
queue. Once a token has queued withdrawals, every later withdrawal of that token is
queued behind them, including after its limit is removed, so withdrawals of a
token are paid in main-queue order. Other tokens keep flowing.

### Withdrawal queue

`processQueuedWithdrawals(token, withdrawals)` is permissionless. It is
`whenNotPaused`, takes the existing withdrawal reentrancy lock, and applies the
same deposit-queue capacity check as `processWithdrawals`, since each entry can
append at most one deposit.

The caller supplies queued withdrawals in order from the head. For each, the
portal MUST check that `keccak256(abi.encode(withdrawal))` equals the entry at
the head, reverting with `InvalidWithdrawalProof` otherwise, then refills
capacity. `paid` is the amount of the head entry already paid; `remaining` is
`amount - paid`.

- **Plain withdrawals and deposit bounce-backs** are paid in parts. Each step
  pays `min(remaining, capacity)`, or `remaining` if the token has no limit,
  with the same checks as a main-queue delivery. A deposit bounce-back's fee is
  charged only on its first part, and a part that cannot be delivered is
  parked in `refunds` as today. If a part of a user withdrawal fails, the
  whole `remaining` bounces back to the Zone and the entry is removed. Forced
  exits ([TIP-1012](./tip-1012.md)) of a whole balance are therefore never
  sent back to the Zone because of their size.
- **Callbacks** are paid whole. One larger than `maxAmount` (possible if the
  limit was lowered after it was queued) bounces back to the Zone. Otherwise
  it waits until it fits. Before delivering, the portal MUST revert with
  `InsufficientCallbackGas` if the gas available to the callback frame cannot
  cover `gasLimit` plus a fixed overhead, so a caller cannot force a bounce by
  sending too little gas.
- An entry is removed once fully paid or bounced. Processing stops, without
  reverting, at the first entry that capacity cannot advance. Supplied entries
  after that point are ignored. The call returns the number of entries it
  completed.

`WithdrawalProcessed` is emitted for each delivery attempt with the amount
attempted, so sums over it remain the amount paid or bounced.
`QueuedWithdrawalPaid` records progress through each entry.

### Pause

`pause()` stops both payout paths. Queued withdrawals are not cancelled.
Removing a stolen entry from the queue requires a portal runtime upgrade before
the pause expires.

## Interfaces

`IZonePortal` adds:

```solidity
/// @notice Longest refill period a withdrawal limit may use.
function MAX_WITHDRAWAL_LIMIT_PERIOD() external view returns (uint64);

/// @notice Set, change or remove the outflow limit for `token`.
/// @dev Only the ZoneFactory owner. (0, 0) removes the limit.
/// @param token Enabled token to limit.
/// @param maxAmount Capacity in raw token units, refilled over `period`.
/// @param period Seconds to refill from empty to `maxAmount`.
function setWithdrawalLimit(address token, uint128 maxAmount, uint64 period) external;

/// @notice Current limit and capacity for `token`, refilled to the current block.
/// @return maxAmount Capacity cap, or 0 if the token has no limit.
/// @return period Refill period in seconds.
/// @return available Amount that may leave escrow now.
function withdrawalLimit(address token)
    external
    view
    returns (uint128 maxAmount, uint64 period, uint128 available);

/// @notice Bounds of `token`'s withdrawal queue and the head entry's paid amount.
function queuedWithdrawals(address token)
    external
    view
    returns (uint64 head, uint64 tail, uint128 headPaid);

/// @notice Hash of the queued withdrawal at `index`, or zero if none.
function queuedWithdrawalAt(address token, uint256 index) external view returns (bytes32);

/// @notice Pay `token`'s queued withdrawals in order, as capacity allows.
/// @dev Permissionless. Stops at the first entry capacity cannot advance.
/// @param withdrawals Queued withdrawals in order from the head.
/// @return completed Number of entries fully paid or bounced.
function processQueuedWithdrawals(address token, Withdrawal[] calldata withdrawals)
    external
    returns (uint256 completed);

event WithdrawalLimitSet(address indexed token, uint128 maxAmount, uint64 period);
event WithdrawalQueued(
    address indexed token, uint256 indexed queueIndex, bytes32 withdrawalHash, uint128 amount
);
event QueuedWithdrawalPaid(
    address indexed token, uint256 indexed queueIndex, uint128 paid, uint128 remaining
);

error NotWithdrawalLimitAuthority();
error InvalidWithdrawalLimit();
error InsufficientCallbackGas();
```

Storage is appended after `tokenEnablementCursorInitialized`. No existing slot
moves:

```solidity
struct WithdrawalLimit {
    uint128 maxAmount;  // 0 means no limit
    uint64 period;
    uint64 updatedAt;   // last refill, Tempo seconds
    uint128 available;  // capacity as of updatedAt
}

struct QueuedWithdrawals {
    uint64 head;
    uint64 tail;
    uint128 headPaid;
    mapping(uint256 => bytes32) entries;  // keccak256(abi.encode(Withdrawal))
}

mapping(address token => WithdrawalLimit) internal _withdrawalLimits;
mapping(address token => QueuedWithdrawals) internal _queuedWithdrawals;
```

`TokenConfig` does not change, and the Zone never reads these slots.

# Compatibility

This TIP activates with the T15 Tempo upgrade, which installs a new
`ZonePortal` runtime at `ZONE_PORTAL_IMPL_ADDRESS`. No Zone hardfork, outbox
change or enclave image is needed. Every token starts with no limit, so every
existing portal behaves exactly as before until the limit authority sets one.

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Sequencer | `processWithdrawals` can now leave withdrawals queued on Tempo. The sequencer SHOULD call `processQueuedWithdrawals` for tokens with a backlog whenever capacity allows, reading queued withdrawals from the calldata of the transactions that queued them. |
| Forced exits and users | Anyone can pay out a queued withdrawal, so a stalled sequencer cannot hold queued funds. |
| Wallets and SDKs | A withdrawal can now settle on the Zone and arrive on Tempo later, in parts for plain withdrawals. They SHOULD show `WithdrawalQueued` and the token's `withdrawalLimit` as an expected delay rather than a failure. |
| Indexers and explorers | Index `WithdrawalQueued` and `QueuedWithdrawalPaid`. `WithdrawalProcessed` may now appear several times for one withdrawal, once per part. |
| Zone operations | Tooling for the limit authority MUST support `setWithdrawalLimit` and reading `withdrawalLimit` and the queue. Operators SHOULD set limits for a token in the same Tempo block that enables it. |
| Native portal | A native `ZonePortal` precompile, if adopted, MUST match this behavior. |

Applications that need a payout in one piece within a fixed time SHOULD keep
single withdrawals below the token's `maxAmount` and check capacity first.

# Observability

| Event / view | Fields | Operational question |
|---|---|---|
| `WithdrawalLimitSet` | token, maxAmount, period | Who changed a limit, and to what? |
| `WithdrawalQueued` | token, queueIndex, withdrawalHash, amount | Is outflow above the limit right now? |
| `QueuedWithdrawalPaid` | token, queueIndex, paid, remaining | Is the backlog draining? |
| `WithdrawalProcessed` | to, senderTag, token, amount, success | What left escrow, and what bounced? (existing) |
| `withdrawalLimit` | maxAmount, period, available | How much headroom is left? |
| `queuedWithdrawals` | head, tail, headPaid | How deep is the backlog? |

Operators of each Zone with limits MUST run alerts that page on:

1. any `WithdrawalQueued` for a token;
2. `available` below 20% of `maxAmount`;
3. a queued entry older than one `period`;
4. any `WithdrawalLimitSet`;
5. the portal entering or leaving pause.

Each alert MUST link a runbook that says who can pause the portal and how to
decide whether queued outflow is legitimate.

# Threat Model

**Most consequential attack: a forged batch.** An attacker exploits a Zone,
prover or verifier bug to settle a batch that withdraws the escrow. With limits
set, at most one full capacity of each token leaves at once and then at most
`maxAmount` per `period`. The rest waits in the token's queue, visible on
Tempo, while the alerts fire and someone pauses the portal. A pause stops the
outflow but does not remove the forged entries; that needs a runtime upgrade
within 30 days. Without limits, this TIP changes nothing.

| Actor | Controls | Trusted to | Enforcing check | If compromised or wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Limit authority (ZoneFactory owner) | Every portal's limits | Set limits that leave room for normal flow | `owner()` check in `setWithdrawalLimit` | Can remove limits, or set them so low that exits effectively stop. Cannot move funds. |
| Portal admin | Existing settings | Unchanged | Cannot call `setWithdrawalLimit` | No effect on limits |
| Sequencers | Main-queue order and timing | Unchanged | Limit applies on every payout path | Can choose which withdrawals take capacity first. Cannot exceed it. |
| Any caller of `processQueuedWithdrawals` | When queued entries are paid | Nothing | Hash check at the head. Gas check before callbacks. | Can only pay entries, in order, within capacity |
| Users | Their own withdrawals | Nothing | Shared capacity | One user can use a period's capacity with their own funds, delaying others. That costs fees and capital equal to the limit. |

**Residual risks.**

- **Capacity griefing.** Any user can use up capacity with large legitimate
  withdrawals, delaying other users' payouts by up to the backlog's length.
  The limit bounds total outflow; it does not guarantee any user's latency.
- **Up to 2x in a period.** A full capacity plus a period's refill can leave
  within one `period`.
- **Removing a limit releases the backlog at once.**
- **Custody of the limit key.** The authority can delay every exit from every
  Zone. Who holds it, and whether lowering a limit should take effect only
  after a delay, are open questions for Legal.

## Test Cases

Unless stated otherwise, the token's limit is `maxAmount = 100`,
`period = 100` seconds (1 unit/s), starting full. The test suite MUST cover:

1. **Unlimited:** with no limit, a withdrawal of 1,000 is paid at once.
2. **Exact fit:** a withdrawal of 100 is paid and capacity becomes 0.
3. **Over capacity:** with capacity 40, a withdrawal of 41 is queued and emits
   `WithdrawalQueued`. Nothing bounces.
4. **Paid in parts:** a queued plain withdrawal of 250 is paid by a
   non-sequencer as 100, then 100 after 100 seconds, then 50 after 50 more.
5. **Order within a token:** behind a queued withdrawal of token A, a
   withdrawal of 1 of A is queued while a withdrawal of token B is paid.
6. **Large callback:** a callback withdrawal of 101 bounces back to the Zone.
7. **Callback waits:** with capacity 40, a callback of 60 is queued and paid
   whole once capacity reaches 60. With too little gas, the call reverts with
   `InsufficientCallbackGas` and nothing changes.
8. **Failed delivery:** a withdrawal of 60 to a blocked recipient bounces and
   capacity stays 100.
9. **Deposit bounce-backs:** a deposit bounce-back of 150 pays 100, then 50,
   and the fee is charged once.
10. **Authority:** the portal admin's `setWithdrawalLimit` reverts with
    `NotWithdrawalLimitAuthority`. The factory owner's succeeds.
11. **Invalid limits:** `(100, 0)`, `(0, 60)`, `(50, 60)` and a period above
    7 days revert with `InvalidWithdrawalLimit`.
12. **Lower and raise:** with capacity 80, lowering to 50 sets capacity to 50.
    Raising back to 100 leaves it at 50 plus refill.
13. **New limit starts full:** setting a limit on an unlimited token gives
    capacity equal to `maxAmount`.
14. **Remove:** removing the limit lets the whole backlog be paid in one call.
15. **Pause:** while paused, both payout paths revert, and after 1,000 seconds
    capacity is 100, not more.
16. **Wrong entry:** supplying a withdrawal that is not at the head reverts
    with `InvalidWithdrawalProof`.
17. **Activation:** before T15, the implementation address runs the T13
    runtime. At T15, it runs the new runtime and existing portal storage is
    unchanged.
