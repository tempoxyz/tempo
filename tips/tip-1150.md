# TIP-1150: Per-token Zone Withdrawal Limits

## Abstract

This TIP adds an optional outflow limit for each token a Zone escrows on Tempo: a capacity of `maxAmount` raw token units that every payout from the `ZonePortal`'s escrow spends and that refills over `period` seconds. A plain withdrawal or deposit bounce-back that exceeds the capacity, or that follows unpaid records of its token, waits in a per-token queue on Tempo that anyone can pay as capacity refills, and a callback in either position bounces back to the Zone. Only the ZoneFactory owner sets limits. A limit bounds how fast escrow can drain, which leaves time to pause the portal.

## Motivation

A Zone's `ZonePortal` holds its users' funds in escrow on Tempo ([TIP-1091](./tip-1091.md)). `submitBatch` commits a batch's withdrawals once its proof verifies, and `processWithdrawals` pays them out of escrow. A Zone, prover or verifier bug that lets an attacker settle a forged batch, or a compromise of many Zone accounts, can empty the escrow in a few Tempo blocks. The Zone outbox's `maxWithdrawalsPerBlock` caps how many withdrawals one Zone block may request, whatever their value.

A limit on the value that leaves over time keeps the rest in escrow long enough to pause the portal. The portal enforces it on Tempo, where funds leave escrow, so a Zone bug cannot bypass it, and measures time with Tempo's `block.timestamp`, which a sequencer cannot move. Limits are per token and in raw units because one limit across tokens would need a price oracle. Queueing lets an authorized plain withdrawal finish on Tempo without a second Zone request, and returning a callback lets its sender decide whether to run it later. The ZoneFactory owner, a Tempo-held key, sets limits because a Zone's operator has an incentive to set them as high as possible.

# Specification

## Overview

### Withdrawal flow

A withdrawal requested on a Zone is paid on Tempo in two steps:

1. A sequencer settles a batch of Zone blocks with `submitBatch`. The proof commits to the batch's withdrawals as one hash chain, and the portal appends that hash to its withdrawal FIFO, the **main queue**.
2. Sequencers call `processWithdrawals` with the withdrawals in calldata. The portal checks each one against the hash at the head of the main queue, reverting with `InvalidWithdrawalProof` on a mismatch, and pays it.

Each main-queue **entry** is one of three kinds:

| Kind | Fields | Payment | If it cannot be delivered |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plain withdrawal** | `fallbackNonce != 0`, `gasLimit == 0` | Transferred to its recipient | **Bounces back** to the Zone as a deposit |
| **Callback** | `fallbackNonce != 0`, `gasLimit > 0` | Delivered to a contract through the portal's messenger | Bounces back to the Zone as a deposit |
| **Deposit bounce-back** | `fallbackNonce == 0`, whatever its `gasLimit` | Refunds a deposit the Zone rejected, less a fee paid to the admin | Parked in `refunds` until its recipient calls `claimRefund` |

### Limits and token queues

This TIP gives each token on a portal an optional **limit** `(maxAmount, period)` and a **token queue** of **records**. A limited token has a **capacity**, the amount that may leave escrow now. Capacity starts at `maxAmount`, falls by each amount paid out and refills at `maxAmount / period` units per second up to `maxAmount`. A token has a **backlog** while its token queue holds an unpaid record.

`processWithdrawals` pays an entry that fits in capacity when its token has no backlog. Otherwise it appends a plain withdrawal or deposit bounce-back to its token queue and bounces back a callback. `processQueuedWithdrawals(token, maxEntries)` pays a token queue from its head as capacity allows. These two calls are the **payout paths**. The portal applies the limit on both, whatever the batch proof authorized, and `pause()` stops both. `claimRefund` draws on the same capacity and pays nothing while its token has a backlog.

### Example

Tokens A and B each have `maxAmount = 100` and `period = 100`, so each refills 1 unit per second. A has capacity 40 and B has capacity 100. One `processWithdrawals` call supplies, in main-queue order:

| Entry | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Plain 150 of A | Queued at A's index 0, because it exceeds A's capacity. |
| Plain 70 of B | Paid. B has no backlog and enough capacity. |
| Plain 10 of A | Queued at A's index 1 although it fits, because A has a backlog. |
| Callback 5 of A | Bounces back to the Zone, because A has a backlog. |

A sequencer then sends `processQueuedWithdrawals(A, maxEntries)` as a separate transaction, and anyone else can send it too:

1. In the same block, index 0 waits: its first payment is `min(150, 100) = 100`, more than capacity 40.
2. 60 seconds later, capacity is 100. The call pays 100 toward index 0, leaving 50, and stops with capacity 0.
3. 60 seconds later, capacity is 60. The call pays the last 50 of index 0, then all of index 1, and A's queue is empty.

## Mechanics

### Setting a limit

Only the **limit authority**, `IZoneFactory(ZONE_FACTORY_ADDRESS).owner()` read at call time, can call `setWithdrawalLimit(token, maxAmount, period)`, so transferring factory ownership transfers the authority for every portal. If ownership moves to the zero address or to an address whose key nobody holds, every limit is frozen. The call MUST revert with `NotWithdrawalLimitAuthority` for any other caller, including the portal admin. It works while the portal is paused and after the admin abdicates a `Capability`, the permanent surrender of its power to pause the portal or to change its access policy. It works for a token the admin has not enabled yet.

The call MUST revert with `InvalidWithdrawalLimit` unless `maxAmount == 0 && period == 0`, or `period > 0` and `maxAmount` is a nonzero multiple of `period`, so capacity refills by a whole number of units each second. Otherwise it emits `WithdrawalLimitSet(token, maxAmount, period)` and:

1. For a token with no limit, sets capacity to `maxAmount`.
2. For a token with a limit, refills capacity under the old limit, then caps it at the new `maxAmount`. A raised limit adds capacity only as it refills.
3. For `(0, 0)`, deletes the limit. The next `processQueuedWithdrawals` call can then pay the whole queue.

### Capacity

The portal stores a limited token's capacity as `available`, as of the time `updatedAt`. Before any read or use, it refills capacity:

```text
available = min(maxAmount, available + (block.timestamp - updatedAt) * (maxAmount / period))
updatedAt = block.timestamp
```

`maxAmount / period` is exact because `maxAmount` is a multiple of `period`.

Every amount that leaves escrow on a withdrawal path consumes capacity when it leaves: every payout on either payout path, including value delivered to a callback, every deposit bounce-back in full, including the fee paid to the admin, because a forged batch could fabricate them, and every refund that `claimRefund` pays.

A plain withdrawal or callback whose delivery fails bounces back to the Zone, and the capacity it took is restored, capped at `maxAmount`. A deposit bounce-back whose refund fails parks the unpaid amount in `refunds`. Only the fee the admin received consumes capacity then, and the parked amount consumes capacity when `claimRefund` pays it.

Over any interval of `T` seconds in which the limit does not change, the payout paths and `claimRefund` together therefore move at most `maxAmount + T * maxAmount / period` of the token out of escrow, which is at most `2 * maxAmount` within one `period`.

### Paying from the main queue

An entry with `fallbackNonce != 0` whose `gasLimit` exceeds `MAX_WITHDRAWAL_GAS_LIMIT` bounces back before the limit applies. A deposit bounce-back is never exempt this way, whatever its `gasLimit`.

To every other dequeued entry of token `t`, the portal applies the first rule that matches:

1. If `t` has no backlog and no limit, the entry is delivered.
2. If `t` has no backlog and `amount <= available`, capacity falls by `amount` and the entry is delivered. Capacity is charged before delivery, because a callback can reenter `claimRefund`. If delivery fails, the capacity for the amount that stayed in escrow is restored, capped at `maxAmount`.
3. If the entry is a callback, it MUST bounce back to the Zone. This includes a callback larger than `maxAmount`, which could never be delivered.
4. Otherwise the portal appends a record of the entry at the tail of `t`'s token queue and emits `WithdrawalQueued`.

An entry the same call queued earlier is backlog for the entries after it. While `t` has a backlog, every later plain withdrawal or deposit bounce-back of `t` is queued behind it, even after the limit is removed, and every later callback of `t` bounces back, so entries of one token are paid in main-queue order. Other tokens are not delayed.

A record stores the recipient, `fallbackNonce`, `senderTag` and the unpaid amount `remaining` ([Storage](#storage)), so a caller of `processQueuedWithdrawals` supplies only the token and `maxEntries`.

### Paying from a token queue

`processQueuedWithdrawals(token, maxEntries)` is permissionless, `whenNotPaused` and takes the withdrawal reentrancy lock. It:

1. Runs one bounded [TIP-1171](https://github.com/tempoxyz/tempo/pull/9383) pruning run.
2. Refills capacity.
3. Makes payments on records in order from the head, completing at most `maxEntries` records. It deletes each record it completes and moves the head past it.
4. Stops without reverting at the first record it does not complete.
5. Writes capacity and returns the number of records it completed.

Each **payment** toward a record is `min(remaining, maxAmount)`, or `remaining` for a token with no limit, and is made only once `available` covers it. While the token's limit is unchanged, a record of at most `maxAmount` is therefore paid in one payment, and a larger record in payments of `maxAmount`, each made when capacity is full, followed by its last part, made once `available` covers it. After a limit change, later payments use the new `maxAmount`, and after the limit is removed the remainder is paid in one payment. A payment goes through the same checks as a main-queue delivery. A delivered payment lowers `available` and `remaining` by its amount. A record is complete once it is fully paid, bounced back or parked.

- If a payment toward a plain withdrawal fails, all of `remaining` bounces back to the Zone through the TIP-1171 append path, which exempts withdrawal-generated deposits from the congestion premium. The record is complete, and the bounce consumes no capacity.
- If a payment toward a deposit bounce-back fails, `remaining` minus any fee the admin received in that payment is parked in `refunds`, and the record is complete. Only that fee consumes capacity.
- A deposit bounce-back's fee is `min(calculateBouncebackFee(), payment)` and is attempted once, on the first payment. If the transfer to the admin fails, the fee stays in that payment's refund. `partlyPaid` records that the first payment happened, so later payments carry no fee.

Each payment emits the same event as a main-queue payout, with the payment's amount. A plain withdrawal emits `WithdrawalProcessed`. A deposit bounce-back emits `DepositBounceBack` with the amount net of the fee, or `DepositBounceBackPending` with the amount parked. A bounced remainder emits `WithdrawalBounceBack` and `WithdrawalProcessed` with `success = false`. Each payment also emits `QueuedWithdrawalPaid`, whose `paid` is the amount that left escrow: 0 for a bounced remainder, and only the fee for a parked refund.

### Claiming refunds

While a token has a backlog, `claimRefund(token)` pays nothing, whether or not the token is limited. Queued records are paid before parked refunds, so a refund claim cannot hold capacity below what a queued installment needs. Otherwise, for a token with no limit, `claimRefund(token)` pays the caller's whole parked refund. For a limited token it refills capacity, pays `min(owed, available)`, lowers capacity by the amount paid and leaves the rest owed in `refunds`. `claimRefund` can therefore return less than the amount owed, including 0, and emits `RefundClaimed` with the amount paid, including 0. This applies to every parked refund, whether it was parked from the main queue, from a token queue or before the limit was set.

### Pause

While the portal is paused, both payout paths and `claimRefund` revert, and capacity refills up to `maxAmount`, so resuming releases at most `maxAmount` at once. A pause expires after `PAUSE_DURATION` (30 days), and removing a forged record from a token queue requires a portal runtime upgrade before then.

## Interfaces

`IZonePortal` adds:

```solidity
/// @notice Set, change or remove the outflow limit for `token`.
/// @dev Only the ZoneFactory owner. (0, 0) removes the limit; otherwise period is nonzero and
///      divides maxAmount.
function setWithdrawalLimit(address token, uint128 maxAmount, uint64 period) external;

/// @notice Current limit and capacity for `token`, refilled to the current block.
function withdrawalLimit(address token)
    external
    view
    returns (uint128 maxAmount, uint64 period, uint128 available);

/// @notice Bounds of `token`'s token queue: records from head up to tail are unpaid.
function queuedWithdrawals(address token) external view returns (uint64 head, uint64 tail);

/// @notice The record of `token` at `index`, or all zero if none.
function queuedWithdrawalAt(address token, uint256 index)
    external
    view
    returns (QueuedWithdrawal memory);

/// @notice Pay `token`'s token queue in order, as capacity allows.
/// @param maxEntries Most records to complete.
/// @return completed Number of records fully paid, bounced back or parked in refunds.
function processQueuedWithdrawals(address token, uint256 maxEntries)
    external
    returns (uint256 completed);

event WithdrawalLimitSet(address indexed token, uint128 maxAmount, uint64 period);
event WithdrawalQueued(
    address indexed token,
    uint256 indexed queueIndex,
    address to,
    uint128 amount,
    bytes32 senderTag,
    uint64 fallbackNonce
);
event QueuedWithdrawalPaid(
    address indexed token,
    uint256 indexed queueIndex,
    uint128 paid,      // amount that left escrow in this step
    uint128 remaining  // amount the record still waits for, 0 once complete
);

error NotWithdrawalLimitAuthority();
error InvalidWithdrawalLimit();
```

`(token, queueIndex)` identifies a record. `WithdrawalQueued` carries the record's `senderTag`: the entry's for a plain withdrawal, 0 for a deposit bounce-back. `claimRefund` now returns the amount paid, and `RefundClaimed` carries that amount.

### Storage

Storage is appended after the TIP-1171 fields, after `nextDepositBucketToPrune` in slot 31:

```solidity
struct WithdrawalLimit {
    uint128 maxAmount;  // 0 means no limit; a multiple of period
    uint64 period;
    uint128 available;  // capacity as of updatedAt
    uint64 updatedAt;   // last refill, Tempo seconds
}

struct QueuedWithdrawal {
    address to;
    uint64 fallbackNonce;  // 0 for a deposit bounce-back
    bool partlyPaid;       // a deposit bounce-back's first payment was made
    bytes32 senderTag;     // 0 for a deposit bounce-back
    uint128 remaining;     // amount not yet paid
}

struct QueuedWithdrawals {
    uint64 head;  // index of the next record to pay
    uint64 tail;  // index the next queued record takes
    mapping(uint256 => QueuedWithdrawal) entries;
}

mapping(address token => WithdrawalLimit) internal _withdrawalLimits;     // slot 32
mapping(address token => QueuedWithdrawals) internal _queuedWithdrawals; // slot 33
```

A limit takes two slots: `{maxAmount, period}`, written only by `setWithdrawalLimit`, and `{available, updatedAt}`, written by each refill that changes capacity. A record takes three slots, or two for a deposit bounce-back, whose `senderTag` slot stays empty. Records share the portal's [TIP-1060](./tip-1060.md) storage credits with the main queue and the deposit queue.

# Compatibility

This TIP activates with the T15 Tempo upgrade, which installs a new `ZonePortal` runtime at `ZONE_PORTAL_IMPL_ADDRESS`. Blocks before T15 execute under the T14 portal runtime, or the T13 runtime before T14 activates. It activates in the same fork and runtime as TIP-1171 and relies on its unbounded deposit queue, which accepts a bounce-back from either payout path whatever the queue's length. Every token starts unlimited until the limit authority sets a limit.

| Integration | What changes |
|---|---|
| Sequencer | It SHOULD send `processQueuedWithdrawals` for a token with a backlog once `available >= min(remaining, maxAmount)` for the head record, or at once for a token with no limit, sizing `maxEntries` to its gas budget ([Gas](#gas)). |
| Users and forced exits | Anyone can call `processQueuedWithdrawals`, so a token queue drains while sequencers are down. A [TIP-1012](https://github.com/tempoxyz/tempo/blob/tip/1012/tips/tip-1012.md) forced exit of a whole balance larger than `maxAmount` arrives in several payments, and its size alone never sends it back to the Zone. |
| Wallets, SDKs and applications | A plain withdrawal can arrive on Tempo later, in several payments if it exceeds `maxAmount`. Wallets and SDKs SHOULD show `WithdrawalQueued` as an expected delay. An application that needs a payout at once SHOULD keep each withdrawal at or below capacity. |
| Zone wallets and callback integrators | A callback of a token that lacks capacity or has a backlog bounces back to the Zone. Integrators SHOULD check `withdrawalLimit` and `queuedWithdrawals` before sending one and SHOULD retry a bounced callback once the queue is empty. |
| Refund recipients | A claim pays nothing while the token has a backlog, and for a limited token at most its capacity. Callers of `claimRefund` SHOULD read the return value or `RefundClaimed` and claim again once `queuedWithdrawals` shows an empty token queue and, for a limited token, as capacity refills. |
| Indexers and explorers | `WithdrawalQueued` means an entry was deferred, because capacity was short or because its token already had a backlog. Index it and `QueuedWithdrawalPaid` by `(token, queueIndex)`. `WithdrawalProcessed` and `DepositBounceBack` appear once per payment, so a record larger than `maxAmount` produces several. |
| Zone checker | The bridge-accounting checker fails closed on unknown portal events. Before T15 activates, it MUST decode `WithdrawalLimitSet`, `WithdrawalQueued` and `QueuedWithdrawalPaid` and ignore them for accounting, because they move no funds. Its withdrawal latency measures the time to a withdrawal's first payout or bounce. |
| Limit authority | It can set a token's limit before the admin enables the token, so the token is limited from its first deposit. |

## Gas

The portal creates records in TIP-1060 `Refund` mode, so each new slot pays the full creation cost upfront and end-of-transaction settlement refunds `STORAGE_CREDIT_VALUE` (245,000) per credit the portal holds. Credits lower the gas a transaction uses, while the gas limit a sequencer sets must cover the full cost. Measured with Foundry on the T15 runtime, with cold accounts, no storage credits and recipients whose token balance is zero, excluding calldata and intrinsic gas:

| Step | Gas |
|---|---|
| Queue a plain withdrawal in `processWithdrawals` | 768,704, or 1,025,787 for the first record of a token queue, which also creates its bounds slot |
| Queue a deposit bounce-back in `processWithdrawals` | 518,351, or 775,434 for the first record of a token queue |
| `processWithdrawals` call with one queued plain withdrawal | 1,330,536 |
| Pay a record in `processQueuedWithdrawals` | 281,909 at most, mostly the recipient's new balance slot, which the portal's credits do not cover; 28,249 to bounce one back |
| `processQueuedWithdrawals` call paying one record | 862,135 at most |

From T15 the reference sequencer reserves 1,100,000 gas per plain withdrawal and 1,250,000 per deposit bounce-back in `processWithdrawals`, and 400,000 per record in `processQueuedWithdrawals`, each on top of 1,250,000 per transaction.

# Threat Model

An attacker who exploits a Zone, prover or verifier bug can settle a forged batch that withdraws the whole escrow. With a limit set, the token leaves no faster than the [capacity bound](#capacity) allows, through either payout path or `claimRefund`, and the rest waits in the token queue or in `refunds`, visible on Tempo, until the portal is paused. A pause leaves the forged records queued, and removing them takes a [runtime upgrade](#pause) before the pause expires. A token with no limit is unprotected.

| Actor | Controls | Defense | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Limit authority (ZoneFactory owner) | Every portal's limits | `owner()` check in `setWithdrawalLimit` | Trusted to set limits that leave room for normal flow. Can remove limits, or slow a token to 1 raw unit per second with `(1, 1)`, including during a pause and after the admin abdicates. Cannot move funds. |
| Portal admin | Portal settings, including pause | Cannot call `setWithdrawalLimit` | Trusted to keep pause available. Can abdicate `Capability.PausePortal` or lift any pause with `resume()`. Queued outflow then drains at the limit's rate. |
| Sequencers | Main-queue order and timing | The limit applies on both payout paths | Can choose which withdrawals take capacity first, within capacity. If sequencers stall, anyone can drain token queues. |
| Any caller of `processQueuedWithdrawals` | When queued records are paid, and how many per call | Records written by `processWithdrawals` after its proof check; capacity | Can pay records only in order and within capacity. Callbacks are delivered only from `processWithdrawals`, so these callers never choose when a callback runs. |
| Refund recipients | When their parked refunds are claimed | Capacity in `claimRefund`, which pays nothing while the token has a backlog | Can take capacity up to their parked refunds while the token queue is empty, never ahead of queued records. Total outflow stays within the capacity bound, and pause stops claims. |
| Users | Their own withdrawals | Shared capacity | Can use up a token's capacity with their own funds and delay other users, bounce their callbacks and hold off refund claims, for as long as the backlog lasts, at a cost of fees and capital equal to the limit. Each withdrawal they get queued costs the sequencer its record's storage ([Gas](#gas)). |

Removing a limit releases the token's backlog at once.

# Invariants

| ID | Invariant |
|---|---|
| WL1 | For a token whose limit did not change during an interval of length `T`, the amount that leaves escrow through `processWithdrawals`, `processQueuedWithdrawals` and `claimRefund` during it is at most `maxAmount + T * maxAmount / period`. |
| WL2 | Capacity never exceeds `maxAmount`. |
| WL3 | Only the ZoneFactory owner can change a limit. |
| WL4 | An entry of a token with no limit and no backlog is never queued. |

| WL8 | A failed delivery consumes no capacity: a bounced plain withdrawal or callback, or a parked refund, apart from the bounce-back fee the admin received. |
| WL9 | Pause stops both payout paths and `claimRefund`. Queued records and parked refunds stay in place across it. |
| WL10 | Token queues hold only plain withdrawals and deposit bounce-backs. |
