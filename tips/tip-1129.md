# TIP-1129: EVM2 Native Payment Activation

## Abstract

This proposal defines one fork boundary for EVM2-only Tempo execution and native Earn and Zone payment admission. It binds execution, transaction-pool, builder, RPC, prover, Zone, SDK, and infrastructure behavior to the same activation schedule and bounded work model. It also defines the legacy-state reconciliation and the measurable gates required before a network schedule may be published. This draft does not schedule a network fork.

## Motivation

Replacing an execution engine without a historical replay rule could change old state roots or strand existing vault and Zone balances. Admitting a selector to a payment lane without native dispatch and complete child-call accounting could let arbitrary engine or callback code consume payment capacity without a bound. TIP-1125, TIP-1127, and TIP-1128 specify those components; this TIP makes their activation atomic and testable.

## Assumptions

- Every validator can execute historical blocks with the same consensus results as before this fork. Runtime removal of the former execution engine is permitted only after fixtures, genesis sync, and state-root comparison cover all historical forks.
- EVM2's host, journal, and transaction context support paid external calls from precompiles, including nested calls, reentry, and rollback of all state and logs.
- Zone and Earn activation identities are derived from canonical onchain state and independently published manifests. Neither event indexes nor an RPC cache are consensus authority.
- The proof system and verifier version used for post-fork Zone settlement are configured. A NoProof development certificate is rejected after activation.

## Threat Model

Adversaries may submit malformed ABI, forged portal or adapter bytecode, large access lists, oversized engine data, nested precompile calls, callback loops, proof replays, block-boundary transactions, or transactions that are admitted by a pool but invalid in consensus. A sequencer may omit or duplicate inbox and outbox items. A venue may upgrade code, return unusual values, or revert after a token transfer. The fork must not turn any of these into uncharged execution, partial state, a free general-lane escape, or a settlement accepted under the wrong state root.

# Specification

## Fork and historical execution

The fork name and chain-specific block or timestamp are assigned only after the gates below pass. Activation occurs at the start of an entire Tempo block; the fork's parent executes under pre-fork rules, and the first fork block executes under the new rules. The block header, receipts, state root, logs, and gas accounting at both sides of the boundary are deterministic for every validator. A reorg crossing the boundary recomputes the active rules from the new canonical parent; caches and mempool classifications are invalidated.

At heights before activation, EVM2 runs the historical Tempo rules, including deployed proxy code, preexisting precompile addresses, opcode semantics, fork schedule, and prior gas and receipt rules. A node must sync and independently verify from genesis rather than import a fabricated post-fork snapshot as a substitute for compatibility. A performance snapshot may be used only after its state root is verified against historical execution. No new native portal or vault selector executes in old blocks, even when the node binary includes its code.

At and after activation, EVM2 is the sole transaction execution engine. The removed engine is not linked as a fallback for individual transactions. EVM2's provider dispatches a registered payment selector to native code and a nonpayment or admin selector to the historical account bytecode according to TIP-1127 and TIP-1128. Contract creation, EIP-7702 delegated code, CALLCODE, and DELEGATECALL do not acquire portal or vault authority through address resemblance. Any protocol-specific exception must have a named selector, identity proof, and charged work profile.

## Payment admission and charging

One canonical classifier takes the active fork, transaction envelope, caller, target, selector, canonical ABI length, transaction limits, and the parent state needed to establish the portal or vault identity. The classifier is shared by the pool, builder, block validator, RPC estimator, and prover. A payment-lane transaction cannot be admitted merely because it eventually calls a payment selector: its top-level path and all authorized gateway or engine entrypoints must be enumerated. Existing TIP-20 admission stays subject to TIP-1045's envelope and ABI limits.

The block's payment reservation has independent execution-gas and state-work ceilings, charged for intrinsic work, native work, child EVM2 execution, storage warming and writes, account and code access, returndata copying, logs, proof verification, and failed and reverted paths. A nested call inherits the same remaining reservation; it cannot reset a budget by calling another portal, vault, token, or gateway. Transaction-wide depth, count, input, output, and aggregate work limits are enforced before and during execution. Out-of-budget errors roll back the entire call frame and count consumed work in the receipt. A dependent contract or callback has no payment-lane privilege outside the enumerated parent flow.

Classifiers must use a bounded read profile and charge it. A block validator rechecks classification against the execution parent rather than trusting a pool label. If state changes invalidate an identity or capability, the transaction is evicted or rejected under the active rules; it is never silently rerouted to the general lane. A transaction whose top-level selector is a post-fork payment selector cannot consume general-lane gas as a fallback. Admin and governance selectors are classified separately; their usage is reported separately from the payment-flow zero-general-lane metric.

## Migration and reconciled state

The upgrade has no automatic custody sweep or balance remint. Zone portals retain their deterministic addresses, initialized IDs, token enablement, queue hashes, deposit indices, public backing, pending withdrawals and refunds, and verifier and committee state. Zone chains retain private balances, encrypted note commitments, inbox and outbox cursors, sequencer metadata, and proof obligations. Vault proxies retain their addresses, ERC-1967 implementation slots, venue custody, asset and share-token balances, total share supply, conversion anchors, fee baselines and remainders, recipient credits, and open asynchronous requests. A reconciliation manifest records all of these before activation and compares them after the first post-fork block.

The legacy Earn factory lacks an adapter registry. An audited activation manifest commits its deployments from canonical factory receipts, then verifies each account's proxy runtime, implementation, initialization, asset, share token, engine, custody, and issuer role against fork-parent state. Registration of each account uses a bounded inclusion proof; missing or mismatched accounts are not admitted. Native factory deployments after activation register atomically. Existing Zone identities are checked against factory state plus exact initialized proxy identity at each native call; a scan is not required for normal execution.

The migration test suite includes chains with nonzero private balances, queued deposits, pending withdrawal/refund claims, multiple tokens and Zones, vault gains and losses, accrued and fractional fees, fee credits, open async requests, paused accounts, and a migrated venue. It executes a pre-fork transaction, a first-fork-block transaction, and a reorg across the fork, comparing state roots and account-level reconciliations. A failed registration or migration check prevents native admission without mutating custody. The network schedule cannot be published while an unreconciled position or Zone remains.

## Proof and settlement boundary

The Zone proof statement binds chain ID, Zone ID, fork identifier, previous and next private state roots, inbox and outbox cursors, deposit queue hash, withdrawal and refund commitments, batch height, and verifier version. Native settlement rejects stale or duplicate heights, replayed proofs, unsupported versions, mismatched commitments, and NoProof payloads. Accepted settlement updates the portal queue and custody under the same bounded payment transaction. The Zone prover and L1 verifier must agree on public inputs at the fork boundary and during a reorg; an old-format batch can be settled only under its explicitly versioned historical rule.

## Sequencing and release gates

1. Pin a reviewed EVM2 and Reth integration in Tempo and Zones, replace the former execution engine in runtime dependencies, and pass historical genesis replay plus state-root, receipt, and trace comparison over pre-fork blocks.
2. Publish a deterministic fork schedule for each devnet chain and an activation manifest for existing Earn stacks and Zone identities. Exercise pre-fork, fork, post-fork, and reorg cases with real transactions and nonzero legacy state.
3. Run a configured cryptographic Zone prover and verifier; publish canonical transaction hashes, receipts, private-balance and backing reconciliations, proof public inputs, accepted settlement, and rejected invalid proofs. NoProof mode does not satisfy this gate.
4. Run combined Earn and Zone workloads on the activated devnet: deposits, private transfers, withdrawals, refunds, proof settlement, vault deposits, yield/loss accrual, fee claims, sync and async redemption, direct spending, and gateway composition. Report sustained transactions per second, p50/p95/p99 latency, execution and state work, nested calls, per-lane gas, failed transactions, state-root checks, and a zero general-lane count for those payment flows. Publish the exact command, configuration, code revisions, workload seed, duration, and raw output.
5. Compare the same workload with the existing stack at matched resources, run security and correctness reviews, fix findings, rerun affected tests and measurements, and document unresolved differences before proposing a network schedule.

The first network activation proposal must fill in numeric work ceilings, verifier and proof versions, code commitments, upgrade block or timestamp, rollback or reorg procedure, and links to the reproducible evidence. A draft implementation or a proxy-only devnet does not satisfy these gates.

# Tooling

Node and Zone configuration expose the fork schedule, EVM2/Reth revisions, native identity manifests, registered venue capabilities, work ceilings, and prover/verifier version. SDKs and RPC expose fork-aware transaction building, gas and state-work estimates, native identity status, payment-lane classification, and typed Zone and Earn errors. Infra publishes a runnable local or remote devnet upgrade harness, fixture generator, raw receipts and proofs, metrics dashboard inputs, and a genesis-sync comparison command. The benchmark report distinguishes transactions accepted by the pool from transactions included and settled onchain.

# Observability

Each receipt and trace reports its active fork and lane classification, total transaction execution gas and state work, nested call count, proof-verification work, and native endpoint identity. Migration and registration events name the exact account and manifest root. Zone settlement events identify height, old and new roots, queue cursors, and proof version; existing custody and Earn events retain their historic signatures. A discrepancy alarm compares portal backing to private liability plus pending claims and adapter venue custody to active plus pending share liabilities. Lane metrics count payment selectors that enter general capacity as a hard failure.

# Invariants

- Pre-fork execution produces the same state root and receipt root from genesis on both engine implementations for the compared canonical chain.
- At the fork, every existing Zone balance and vault position is preserved in value and ownership subject only to normal in-block transactions; no position is reset or silently reminted.
- Every post-fork Earn or Zone payment flow has one bounded payment reservation and zero general-lane execution, including dependent TIP-20, engine, verifier, gateway, and callback work.
- A false proof, stale root, duplicate claim, forged endpoint, engine upgrade, or child-call out-of-budget result cannot produce a committed custody or liability change.
- Accepted proof settlement and Earn payout are backed by exact token balance and total-supply deltas and survive independent genesis sync, fresh node replay, and a fork-boundary reorg.
