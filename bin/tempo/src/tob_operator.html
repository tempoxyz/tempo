How a round works

  1. The VRF picks the leader of view v from the certificate of view v−1. Validators broadcast that certificate to the operator too, so the operator verifies it and computes the leader itself, as early as the validators do. No validator announces anything.
  2. The operator immediately flushes the latest quote of every maker to that leader over the authenticated top-of-block channel, then streams each new quote the moment it is signed, until the leader acks Closed. The leader keeps submissions that arrive before its round starts.
  3. A newer quote from a maker replaces its older one at the leader, keeping the maker's slot. Validators that do not lead receive nothing.
  4. After its start-of-block system work, the builder executes the lane first, then fills from the pool. Public transactions pay a far higher tip () and still come after.
  5. The operator reads the lane transactions from the proposed block the leader broadcasts, then reads the block back from a different validator's RPC and checks that they are its first transactions.
